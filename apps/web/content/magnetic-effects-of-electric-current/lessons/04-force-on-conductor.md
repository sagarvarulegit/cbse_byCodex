# 12.3 Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor in a Magnetic Field

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-12-12-force-on-conductor" />

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-12-13-flemings-left-hand-rule" />

A conductor's current creates a field; an external magnetic field interacts
with it and exerts a force.

Activity 12.7 suspends aluminium rod AB between horseshoe-magnet poles.
Reversing current reverses displacement; reversing the field also reverses it.

Force is greatest when current and magnetic field are perpendicular. It
increases with current, field strength, and length of conductor within the
field.

## Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

Stretch left thumb, forefinger, and middle finger mutually perpendicular:

- forefinger: magnetic field `B` (N to S);
- middle finger: conventional current `I`;
- thumb: force/motion `F`.

Devices using this force include motors, loudspeakers, microphones, and
measuring instruments.

## Charged particles

Magnetic force changes the direction of a moving charged particle. Its velocity
and momentum change direction, while speed and mass remain unchanged when only
magnetic force acts.

### NCERT direction cases

- Positive alpha particle moves west and deflects north: field is **upward**,
  option (d).
- Electron beam back wall → front wall, deflected right: conventional current is
  opposite electron motion; Fleming's rule gives field **vertically upward**.

## Three-rule guardrail

- right-hand thumb: field around current;
- Fleming left hand: force on current;
- Fleming right hand: induced current (exercise recall).
