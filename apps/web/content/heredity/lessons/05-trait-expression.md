# 8.2.3 How Do These Traits Get Expressed?

A gene is a section of DNA that provides information for making a protein.
Proteins carry out cell functions and help produce visible traits.

`gene (DNA information) -> protein/enzyme -> cell process -> trait`

## NCERT tallness example

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-8-trait-expression" />

A plant hormone promotes growth. A gene may direct an enzyme needed to make
that hormone.

- An efficient enzyme produces enough hormone -> plant grows tall.
- An altered, less efficient enzyme produces less hormone -> plant remains short.

Thus genes do not “paint” a trait directly; they influence proteins whose
actions produce the trait.

## Why each parent contributes equally

Chromosomes occur in pairs in body cells. The two members of a pair carry genes
for the same traits, one inherited from each parent.

During germ-cell formation:

- chromosome pairs separate;
- each gamete receives one chromosome from each pair;
- therefore a gamete carries one set, not two.

At fertilisation, male and female gametes fuse and restore the normal number of
chromosomes. The offspring therefore receives one gene set from each parent.

## Chromosome-number stability

If germ cells had the full body-cell chromosome number, fertilisation would
double the chromosome number in every generation. Reduction during gamete
formation and restoration at fertilisation keep the species' chromosome number
constant.

In humans:

- body cells: 23 chromosome pairs = 46 chromosomes;
- gametes: 23 single chromosomes;
- zygote: 46 chromosomes after fusion.

## Full-mark answer: equal contribution

“Each parent forms haploid germ cells by separating homologous chromosome
pairs. The male and female gametes contribute one chromosome of every pair.
Their fusion restores the diploid number, so the offspring receives equal
genetic material from both parents.”

## Common mistake

Genes are located on chromosomes and are made of DNA. Do not write that a gene
and a chromosome are the same structure.
