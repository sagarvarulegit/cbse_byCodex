# 12.2.3 Circular Current Loop

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-12-8-12-11-loop-solenoid" />

Each small section produces circular field lines. At the centre, contributions
from all sections point in the same direction and add; large arcs appear nearly
straight and parallel.

Field strength increases with:

- current;
- number of turns `n`;
- closer/smaller loop radius for the same current.

An `n`-turn coil produces about `n` times the field of one turn under the same
conditions. Activity 12.6 displays the pattern with iron filings.

Clockwise current as seen from one face gives field **into** that face; the face
acts south. Anticlockwise gives field **out**; the face acts north.

# 12.2.4 Solenoid

A solenoid is many closely wound turns of insulated copper wire in a cylindrical
shape.

- field pattern resembles a bar magnet;
- one end acts N, the other S;
- inside, lines are parallel and closely spaced: strong, nearly uniform field.

A soft-iron core inside makes a strong temporary **electromagnet**. Strength
increases with current, turns per unit length, and suitable soft-iron core.

## NCERT question block

1. Clockwise loop: field into the table inside; out of the table outside.
2. Uniform field: parallel equally spaced arrowed lines.
3. Field inside a long current-carrying solenoid: **same at all interior
   points**, option (d), in the idealised NCERT model.
