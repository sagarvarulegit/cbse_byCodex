# Chapter 9 NCERT Answer Lab

## Remaining in-text questions and textbook examples

- Convex mirror `R=+32cm`: `f=R/2=+16cm`.
- A concave mirror gives a three-times real image of an object at `u=-10cm`:
  `m=-3=-v/u`, hence `v=-30cm`—30 cm in front.
- Air to water: ray bends toward the normal because light slows in water.
- Glass `n=1.50`: `v=c/n=2.0×10^8m/s`.
- From NCERT Table 9.3, diamond has highest and air the lowest optical density.
- Among water, kerosene and turpentine, light is fastest in water because its
  refractive index is smallest.
- Diamond `n=2.42` means `c/vdiamond=2.42`; light travels `2.42` times faster
  in vacuum than in diamond.
- `1D` is the power of a lens with focal length `1m`.
- Equal-sized real convex-lens image at `v=+50cm` requires `u=-50cm`;
  `f=+25cm`, so `P=+4D`.
- Concave lens `f=-2m`: `P=-0.5D`.

NCERT Example 9.1: convex mirror `R=+3m`, `u=-5m` gives `v=+1.15m` and
`m=+0.23`: virtual, erect, diminished.

NCERT Example 9.2: concave mirror `f=-15cm`, `u=-25cm`, `h=4cm` gives
`v=-37.5cm`, `h'=-6cm`: real, inverted, enlarged.

NCERT Example 9.3: concave lens `f=-15cm`, `v=-10cm` gives `u=-30cm`,
`m=+1/3`: virtual, erect, one-third size.

NCERT Example 9.4: convex lens `f=+10cm`, `u=-15cm`, `h=2cm` gives
`v=+30cm`, `m=-2`, `h'=-4cm`: real, inverted, twice enlarged.

## Exercises 1–6

1. Cannot make a lens: **(d) clay**, because a lens must transmit light.
2. Virtual erect enlarged concave-mirror image: **(d) between P and F**.
3. Same-sized real convex-lens image: **(b) at 2F1**.
4. `f=-15 cm` means concave mirror and concave lens: **(a)**.
5. Always erect for all distances: **(d) plane or convex**.
6. Magnifier: **(c) convex lens, f=5 cm**; short focal length gives more power.

## 7. Erect image with concave mirror

Place object between P and F: distance greater than 0 but less than 15 cm. Image
is behind the mirror, virtual, erect, and enlarged. Draw a parallel ray and a
ray to the pole; extend reflected rays backward.

## 8. Mirrors used

- headlight: concave; source at F gives powerful parallel beam;
- rear-view: convex; wide field, erect diminished image;
- solar furnace: concave; concentrates sunlight at F.

## 9. Half-covered convex lens

Complete but less bright image forms. Every uncovered part receives rays from
every object point; covering half reduces ray number, not image extent.

## 10. Convex lens numerical

`u=-25 cm, f=+10 cm, h=5 cm`; `v=+16.7 cm`, `h'=-3.33 cm`. Real, inverted,
diminished image between F2 and 2F2.

## 11. Concave lens numerical

`f=-15 cm, v=-10 cm`; lens formula gives `u=-30 cm`.

## 12. Convex mirror numerical

`u=-10 cm, f=+15 cm`; mirror formula gives `v=+6 cm`. Virtual, erect,
diminished.

## 13. Plane-mirror magnification `+1`

Positive means erect/virtual; magnitude 1 means image and object have equal size.

## 14. Convex mirror

`h=5 cm, u=-20 cm, R=+30 cm`, so `f=+15 cm`.
`v=+8.57 cm`, `m=+0.429`, `h'=+2.14 cm`: virtual, erect, diminished.

## 15. Concave mirror

`h=7 cm, u=-27 cm, f=-18 cm`.
`v=-54 cm`, so screen is 54 cm in front; `h'=-14 cm`: real, inverted,
enlarged.

## 16–17. Power

16. `P=-2 D -> f=-0.5 m`, concave/diverging.
17. `P=+1.5 D -> f=+0.667 m`, convex/converging.

## Final recall

`R=2f`; mirror `1/f=1/v+1/u`; lens `1/f=1/v-1/u`; mirror `m=-v/u`; lens
`m=v/u`; power `P=1/f(m)`.
