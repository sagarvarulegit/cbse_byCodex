# Reading Skills - Case-based Factual Passage

## chapter goal

Learn how to read a factual passage with data, visuals, examples, or a real-life situation.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the case-based factual reading part of the English curriculum. It gives strategy notes and practice anchors.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Identify the case or situation.
2. Read data and text together.
3. Find evidence for each answer.
4. Explain trends and comparisons.
5. Avoid over-reading the data.

## 1) what makes it case-based

A case-based passage gives information through a situation, report, survey, chart, table, map, or short data set.

The answer must come from the passage and the given visual data.

## 2) reading data

Read the title, labels, units, and source note before interpreting a chart or table.

A number is not meaningful until you know what it measures.

## 3) comparison answers

Use words like higher, lower, increase, decrease, most, least, similar, and different.

Always mention the data point or detail that supports your comparison.

## 4) short answers

Begin with the direct answer. Add one supporting detail from the passage.

Do not turn a one-mark answer into a long paragraph.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Case-based passages combine text with data or a situation.
- Labels and units matter.
- Compare only what the data actually shows.
- Support answers with evidence.
- Keep answers brief and exact.

## common mistakes students make

1. Reading the chart but ignoring the passage.
2. Using numbers without units.
3. Claiming a cause when the data only shows a pattern.

## board tips

- Circle the time period, category names, and units.
- For trend questions, state direction plus evidence.
- For vocabulary, still use sentence context, not chart labels alone.

## short answer practice

1. Why are labels important in a data passage?
2. What is the difference between a trend and a cause?
3. How should you write a comparison answer?
4. Why should the passage and chart be read together?

## mini quiz with answers

1. A case-based passage may include a chart or table.
2. Units tell what a number measures.
3. Evidence is needed for comparisons.
4. A pattern does not always prove a cause.
5. Short answers should be direct.
