# 13.1 Ecosystem—What Are Its Components?

An ecosystem is all interacting organisms in an area together with the
non-living physical environment.

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-13-pond-ecosystem" />

## Components

| Group | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| abiotic | non-living physical factors | temperature, rainfall, wind, water, soil, minerals, sunlight |
| producers | make organic food from inorganic substances using sunlight/chlorophyll | green plants, algae, some bacteria |
| consumers | obtain food directly/indirectly from producers | herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, parasites |
| decomposers | bacteria/fungi break down dead organisms and wastes | bacteria, fungi |

Forests, ponds, and lakes are natural ecosystems. Gardens, crop fields, and
aquaria are human-made ecosystems.

## Why decomposers are essential

- break complex dead matter into simpler substances;
- return nutrients to soil/water for producers;
- prevent accumulation of dead bodies and wastes;
- keep biogeochemical material cycling.

Energy is not recycled by decomposers; it ultimately dissipates as heat.

## NCERT aquarium activities

An aquarium needs space, water, oxygen, food, producers, consumers, and
decomposers. Aquatic plants help supply oxygen/food, but a small aquarium still
needs cleaning because it has limited decomposers, space, and material cycling.
A large natural pond has a much more complete self-regulating community.

Adding an animal that eats the others can disturb the designed balance.
Organisms are interdependent; no group can be removed harmlessly.

## Remember

`ecosystem = biotic community + abiotic environment + interactions`
