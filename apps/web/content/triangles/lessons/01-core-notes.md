# 6.1-6.5 Triangles

The same monument can appear in different photograph sizes without changing its shape. Similarity is the mathematics of that "same shape, scaled size" idea.

## 6.1 Introduction

Congruent figures have the same shape and size. Similar figures have the same shape but may have different sizes. The opening mountain and lamp-post panels motivate indirect measurement through similar triangles.

## 6.2 Similar Figures

Two polygons with the same number of sides are similar when:

1. corresponding angles are equal, and
2. corresponding sides are in the same ratio.

The common ratio is the scale factor.

## 6.3 Similarity of Triangles

For triangles, the two polygon conditions are linked more strongly.

### Activity 2

Figure 6.9 asks you to draw a triangle and mark equal ratios on two sides. Joining the marked points gives a line parallel to the third side.

### Theorem 6.1: Basic Proportionality Theorem

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides, it divides those sides in the same ratio.

In $\triangle ABC$, if $DE\parallel BC$, then

$$\frac{AD}{DB}=\frac{AE}{EC}.$$

Figure 6.10 gives the area-based proof.

### Activity 3 and Theorem 6.2: converse

Figures 6.11-6.12 show the reverse statement: if a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side.

**Examples 1-3** apply BPT and its converse to proportional sides, a trapezium, and an isosceles-triangle proof. Figures 6.13-6.16 match these arguments.

## 6.4 Criteria for Similarity of Triangles

### Activity 4 and Theorem 6.3: AAA/AA

### Writing a similarity statement

Order matters. If $A\leftrightarrow D$, $B\leftrightarrow E$, $C\leftrightarrow F$, write

$$\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF.$$

Then

$$\frac{AB}{DE}=\frac{BC}{EF}=\frac{AC}{DF}.$$

**Examples 4-8** use alternate angles, angle sums, SSS, a shadow model and median ratios. Figures 6.29-6.33 support them.

## 6.5 Summary

- Similar polygons need equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
- BPT and its converse connect parallel lines with proportional side divisions.
- Triangle similarity criteria are AAA/AA, SSS and SAS.

## Common mistakes

- matching sides that are not corresponding
- changing vertex order halfway through a proof
- using SAS with a non-included angle
- using BPT without first stating the parallel lines

## Board tips

- Mark correspondence before writing ratios.
- Give a reason for every angle equality: corresponding, alternate, vertical, or given.
- After proving similarity, cite corresponding sides of similar triangles.

## Quick practice

1. If $DE\parallel BC$, $AD=3$, $DB=2$ and $AE=4.5$, find $EC$.
2. If two triangles have sides $3,4,5$ and $6,8,10$, name the criterion.
3. Why is AAA enough for triangles but not for all polygons?

## Answer check

1. $3/2=4.5/EC$, so $EC=3$.
2. SSS similarity.
3. A triangle's third angle is fixed when two angles are fixed; general polygons have more freedom.

## One-minute revision

Parallel line gives BPT. Equal angles give AA. Three side ratios give SSS. Two side ratios with the included angle give SAS.

## Comic recap

- Scale factor: "I resize every corresponding side equally."
- BPT: "Parallel lines divide two sides proportionally."
- AA: "Two matching angles are enough."
- Correspondence: "Keep my vertex order consistent."
