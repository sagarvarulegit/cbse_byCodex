# For Anne Gregory

## chapter goal

Understand the poem's discussion of physical beauty, inner worth, and love.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "The Sermon at Benares" in `jeff108.pdf`.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Speaker and Anne Gregory.
2. Outer beauty.
3. Inner worth.
4. Human and divine love.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) central question

The poem asks whether a person is loved for appearance or for the inner self.

Anne Gregory's hair becomes the visible sign of physical beauty.

## 2) appearance and self

The poem suggests that human love often gets attracted to outer beauty.

But a person's real worth is deeper than looks.

## 3) divine love

The poem contrasts human attraction with a higher love that can value the soul completely.

This gives the poem its reflective ending.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The poem contrasts outer beauty and inner worth.
- Hair is a symbol of appearance.
- Human love may focus on looks.
- Divine love can see beyond appearance.

## common mistakes students make

1. Saying the poem rejects beauty completely.
2. Missing the contrast between human and divine love.
3. Ignoring the dialogue-like structure.

## board tips

- Use appearance, inner self, and divine love.
- Explain the contrast clearly.
- Keep tone respectful and thoughtful.

## short answer practice

1. What does Anne Gregory's hair represent?
2. What does the poem suggest about human love?
3. How is divine love different?
