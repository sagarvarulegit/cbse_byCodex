# The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

## Hook

Look at a modern map of Europe and it seems natural that France, Germany or Italy should exist as nations. This chapter shows that they were made through revolutions, wars, economic change, culture, symbols and unequal struggles over who belonged.

## Chapter goal

Understand how the idea of the nation grew after the French Revolution, how Germany, Italy and Britain became nation-states, and why nationalism later became linked with imperial rivalry.

## Opening context: Frédéric Sorrieu's vision (unnumbered in the textbook)

In 1848, Sorrieu imagined peoples of Europe and America marching past the Statue of Liberty as free, democratic nations. Broken royal symbols lie below, while Christ, saints and angels watch a brotherhood of nations. It was utopian because several peoples shown did not yet have united democratic nation-states.

## 1 The French Revolution and the Idea of the Nation

The French Revolution transferred sovereignty from the monarch to citizens. Revolutionaries promoted *la patrie* and *le citoyen*, adopted the tricolour, created a National Assembly, composed hymns, took oaths and honoured martyrs. They centralised administration, made uniform laws, ended internal customs duties and promoted French as a common language.

French armies carried these ideas abroad. Napoleon's Civil Code of 1804 ended privileges by birth, established equality before law and protected property. Administrative reform helped business, but taxation, censorship and forced recruitment also produced resistance.

## 2 The Making of Nationalism in Europe

Before the nineteenth century, Europe contained dynastic empires and many small states, not modern nation-states. Different languages and ethnic groups lived under shared rulers.

### 2.1 The Aristocracy and the New Middle Class

Landed aristocrats were socially powerful across regions. Most people were peasants. Industrial growth created towns, workers and educated middle classes. These groups supported ending aristocratic privilege and building national unity.

### 2.2 What did Liberal Nationalism Stand for?

For the middle class, liberalism meant individual freedom, equality before law and representative government. It did not initially include universal suffrage: property-owning men held rights while women and poorer men were excluded. Economic liberalism meant free markets and removal of state barriers. The Zollverein customs union helped unite German states economically.

### 2.3 A New Conservatism after 1815

After Napoleon's defeat, the 1815 Congress of Vienna restored monarchies and redrew borders to contain France. Conservatives valued monarchy, Church, social hierarchy and property, though many accepted modern administration. Regimes censored critical newspapers, books, plays and songs.

### 2.4 The Revolutionaries

Secret societies trained revolutionaries and spread ideas of liberty and nationhood. Giuseppe Mazzini founded Young Italy and Young Europe and believed nations were the natural units of humanity. Conservative rulers feared his democratic republican programme.

## 3 The Age of Revolutions: 1830-1848

Revolutions in 1830 removed the Bourbon rulers in France, helped Belgium break from the Netherlands and encouraged struggles elsewhere. Greece won independence from Ottoman rule with European support.

### 3.1 The Romantic Imagination and National Feeling

Romantic artists and writers used emotion, folk culture, language and history to create national feeling. Herder argued that the true German spirit lived among ordinary people. Polish language, music and religious teaching became weapons against Russian control.

### 3.2 Hunger, Hardship and Popular Revolt

Population growth, unemployment, crowded cities, cheap machine-made imports, feudal dues and food-price rises caused hardship. In 1848, shortages and unemployment drove Paris into revolt. Silesian weavers also rebelled against contractors who reduced their payments.

### 3.3 1848: The Revolution of the Liberals

Educated middle classes demanded constitutions, national unification, press freedom and freedom of association. At Frankfurt, 831 elected representatives prepared a constitution for a German nation under a monarchy, but the Prussian king rejected the crown. Women formed associations and joined politics but were denied voting rights and admitted only as observers.

## 4 The Making of Germany and Italy

After 1848, conservative states used nationalist ideas and military power to build nation-states.

### 4.1 Germany – Can the Army be the Architect of a Nation?

Prussia led unification. Chief Minister Otto von Bismarck used the army and bureaucracy and fought wars with Denmark, Austria and France. In January 1871, German princes proclaimed William I emperor at Versailles. The new state modernised currency, banking, law and administration.

### 4.2 Italy Unified

Italy was divided into seven states. Mazzini's republican uprisings failed, so Sardinia-Piedmont under Victor Emmanuel II and Chief Minister Cavour led unification. Cavour's alliance with France defeated Austria; Garibaldi and his Red Shirts freed southern Italy; Victor Emmanuel II became king of united Italy in 1861. Venetia and Rome joined later.

### 4.3 The Strange Case of Britain

Britain formed gradually, not through one revolution. English wealth and Parliament extended control over Wales, Scotland and Ireland. The 1707 Act of Union created the United Kingdom of Great Britain. Scottish and Irish cultures were suppressed, while the Union Jack, English language and national anthem promoted an English-centred British identity.

## 5 Visualising the Nation

Artists gave the abstract nation a human form. France became Marianne, with the red cap, tricolour and cockade. Germany became Germania, with symbols such as oak leaves, sword, eagle, broken chains and black-red-gold flag. Such allegories made national ideas easy to recognise and remember.

## 6 Nationalism and Imperialism

By the late nineteenth century, nationalism became narrower and more aggressive. The Balkans contained many ethnic groups under a weakening Ottoman Empire. Romantic nationalism, rival territorial claims and interference by Russia, Germany, Britain and Austria-Hungary created repeated conflict. National rivalry and imperialism helped lead Europe towards the First World War.

## Textbook end exercises with answer support

### Write in brief

1. **“Write a note on:”**

   a) **“Guiseppe Mazzini”** — The spelling is printed this way in the exercise; Giuseppe Mazzini was an Italian republican who founded Young Italy and Young Europe and promoted democratic nation-states.

   b) **“Count Camillo de Cavour”** — Sardinia-Piedmont's chief minister, Cavour used diplomacy and an alliance with France to defeat Austria and lead Italian unification under Victor Emmanuel II.

   c) **“The Greek war of independence”** — Greeks revolted against Ottoman rule from 1821. European sympathy, poets and artists supported them; the 1832 Treaty of Constantinople recognised Greece as independent.

   d) **“Frankfurt parliament”** — In 1848, 831 representatives met in St Paul's Church and drafted a constitution for a united German monarchy. The Prussian king rejected its crown; the assembly lost support and was suppressed.

   e) **“The role of women in nationalist struggles”** — Women formed associations, founded newspapers, attended meetings and demonstrations, yet were denied suffrage and allowed into Frankfurt only as observers.

2. **“What steps did the French revolutionaries take to create a sense of collective identity among the French people?”**

   **Answer:** They promoted *la patrie* and *le citoyen*, used the tricolour, elected a National Assembly, created hymns, oaths and martyrs, centralised administration, made uniform laws, ended internal customs duties and promoted standard French.

3. **“Who were Marianne and Germania? What was the importance of the way in which they were portrayed?”**

   **Answer:** They were female allegories of France and Germany. Familiar symbols—the red cap, tricolour and cockade for Marianne; oak leaves, sword, eagle and black-red-gold flag for Germania—made the abstract nation visible and helped people identify with it.

4. **“Briefly trace the process of German unification.”**

   **Answer:** After the Frankfurt parliament failed, Prussia led unification. Bismarck used the army and bureaucracy in wars against Denmark, Austria and France. German princes proclaimed William I emperor at Versailles in January 1871, and the new empire standardised administration and economy.

5. **“What changes did Napoleon introduce to make the administrative system more efficient in the territories ruled by him?”**

   **Answer:** The Civil Code ended birth privileges, established equality before law and protected property. Napoleon simplified administrative divisions, abolished feudalism and guild restrictions, freed peasants from serfdom and improved transport and communication.

### Discuss

1. **“Explain what is meant by the 1848 revolution of the liberals. What were the political, social and economic ideas supported by the liberals?”**

   **Answer:** Educated middle classes demanded national unity, constitutional representative government, civil freedoms and press freedom. They favoured equality before law and free markets, but usually limited voting to property-owning men and excluded women.

2. **“Choose three examples to show the contribution of culture to the growth of nationalism in Europe.”**

   **Answer:** Romantic art such as Delacroix's *Massacre at Chios* created sympathy; Grimm folk tales built German cultural identity; Polish language, Church use and music resisted Russian control. Any three well-explained textbook examples earn credit.

3. **“Through a focus on any two countries, explain how nations developed over the nineteenth century.”**

   **Answer:** Germany unified under Prussia through Bismarck's wars and monarchical proclamation in 1871. Italy united through Mazzini's ideas, Cavour's diplomacy, Sardinia-Piedmont's wars and Garibaldi's southern campaign, completing unity with Rome in 1870.

4. **“How was the history of nationalism in Britain unlike the rest of Europe?”**

   **Answer:** Britain formed through a long process led by English Parliament, wealth and power, not one popular revolution. Union with Scotland and force in Ireland created an English-centred state that imposed symbols and culture on subordinate nations.

5. **“Why did nationalist tensions emerge in the Balkans?”**

   **Answer:** Ottoman decline encouraged rival ethnic groups to claim independence and territory. Historical nationalism made claims overlap, Balkan states competed, and major European powers interfered for strategic advantage, turning local disputes into international tension.

### Project

**“Find out more about nationalist symbols in countries outside Europe. For one or two countries, collect examples of pictures, posters or music that are symbols of nationalism. How are these different from European examples?”**

Record each symbol's date, creator, audience and meaning, then compare it with Marianne or Germania without assuming every national symbol works in the same way.

## Common mistakes students make

- Calling every nineteenth-century state a nation-state.
- Equating liberalism of the period with universal voting rights.
- Saying the Frankfurt parliament united Germany; it failed.
- Mixing up Mazzini, Cavour and Garibaldi's roles.
- Describing British union as equal and peaceful.
- Treating Balkan nationalism without Ottoman decline and great-power rivalry.

## Board-answer tips

- In unification answers, use a clear timeline and name the leading state, leader, method and result.
- Separate liberal political, social and economic ideas.
- For culture, explain how the example built feeling; merely naming a painting or song is incomplete.
- In image answers, decode symbols and connect them to the exact historical moment.
- Do not predict questions; prepare the chapter's cause, comparison and source-based reasoning.

### Answer check

1. It transferred sovereignty from monarch to citizens.
2. It removed customs barriers and built economic unity among German states.
3. To contain France and restore conservative balance after Napoleon.
4. Any two: folk tales, language, music, painting, allegorical symbols.
5. The Prussian king rejected the crown; aristocracy and army opposed it; its social support weakened.
6. Mazzini—republican ideas; Cavour—diplomacy and war from Sardinia-Piedmont; Garibaldi—Red Shirts in the south.
7. English parliamentary power gradually absorbed or subordinated Scotland, Wales and Ireland.
8. Overlapping claims, Ottoman decline, rival Balkan states and major-power interference.

## One-minute revision

- The French Revolution made citizens the source of sovereignty.
- Liberal nationalism demanded freedom and markets but initially excluded many voters.
- Vienna restored conservative order; revolutionaries kept national hopes alive.
- Romantic culture, language and folk traditions built belonging.
- Frankfurt failed; Prussia later united Germany through war.
- Cavour and Garibaldi helped unite Italy under Victor Emmanuel II.
- Britain formed through English-led union and cultural domination.
- Marianne and Germania made nations visible; Balkan rivalry linked nationalism with imperialism.
