# 7.1 Do Organisms Create Exact Copies of Themselves?

Reproduction is not essential for an individual to stay alive, but it maintains
the continuity and population of a species.

## 7.1 DNA copying and cellular apparatus

Chromosomes in the nucleus carry hereditary information as DNA. DNA directs the
formation of proteins, and proteins help determine body design.

The basic sequence of reproduction is:

`copy DNA -> make additional cellular apparatus -> separate the DNA copies -> divide the cell`

Simply pushing a DNA copy out would fail because it would have no organised cell
structure to run life processes.

## Why offspring are similar but not identical

Biochemical copying is very accurate but not absolutely error-free.

- Most DNA copies preserve the parent's body plan.
- A very harmful change may produce a non-functional cell that dies.
- Small survivable differences create variation.

So reproduction provides both:

- **stability:** accurate DNA copying maintains the species' basic design
- **variation:** small copying changes make individuals subtly different

## 7.1.1 The Importance of Variation

A population occupies a particular ecological niche. If the environment changes
drastically, identical organisms may all be vulnerable. Some variants may have a
feature that helps them survive and reproduce.

NCERT example: if temperate water becomes warmer, most bacteria may die, but
heat-resistant variants may survive and multiply.

Variation is therefore beneficial to the **species over time**, but a particular
variation is not guaranteed to help one individual in its present environment.

## Questions and answers

### What is the importance of DNA copying in reproduction?

DNA copying transfers hereditary information and preserves the basic body design
of the species. Its small inaccuracies also generate variation, which supplies
the material for evolution and may improve population survival.

### Why is variation beneficial to a species but not necessarily to an individual?

An environmental change may favour only certain variants. Those individuals
survive and keep the species going, but a variation may be neutral or harmful to
the individual carrying it in the current environment.

## Board-ready reasoning

### Why can inherited changes in proteins alter body design?

DNA contains the information for making proteins. A change in DNA can change a
protein's structure or amount; changed proteins can alter cell structure or
function and eventually the organism's body design.

### Are offspring from asexual reproduction exact copies?

They are genetically very similar because only one parent contributes DNA, but
they are not necessarily perfectly identical because DNA copying can introduce
small variations.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying reproduction is needed for an individual's survival.
2. Saying DNA copies are always completely identical.
3. Claiming every variation is useful.
4. Explaining resemblance without mentioning DNA and proteins.

## Remember

`DNA -> proteins -> body design`

`accurate copying -> stability`

`small copying errors -> variation -> possible survival advantage`
