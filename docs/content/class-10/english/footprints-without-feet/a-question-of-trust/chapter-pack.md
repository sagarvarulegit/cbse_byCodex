# A Question of Trust

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- describe Horace Danby's double life
- explain how the young woman tricks him
- understand irony in a thief being robbed of his plan
- answer questions on trust, crime, and deception

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jefp104.pdf`: Horace's respectable image, annual thefts, Shotover Grange plan, the woman posing as owner, safe robbery, and Horace's arrest.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Horace's respectable image
2. His secret thefts
3. Planning the robbery
4. The young woman's trick
5. Arrest and irony
6. Message

## 1) Horace Danby

Horace appears to be a respectable and successful lock-maker.

Secretly, he robs a safe every year to buy rare books.

## 2) robbery plan

Horace studies Shotover Grange carefully and enters when the servants are away.

He is careful about fingerprints and tools, showing experience.

## 3) the woman

A young woman appears and pretends to be the lady of the house.

She convinces Horace to open the safe for her, claiming she needs the jewels.

## 4) deception

Horace trusts her and opens the safe without gloves.

Later he is arrested because his fingerprints are found. The woman was actually another thief.

## 5) irony and message

The irony is that a thief is deceived by a more clever thief.

The story questions the idea of honour among thieves.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Horace is respectable outside but dishonest inside.
- He steals to buy rare books.
- The young woman pretends to be the owner.
- Horace opens the safe and leaves fingerprints.
- He is trapped by trust in a criminal situation.

## common mistakes students make

1. Thinking the young woman is the real house owner.
2. Calling Horace fully honest because he is polite.
3. Forgetting fingerprints as evidence.

## board tips

- Explain title through misplaced trust.
- In character answers, show Horace's contradiction.
- Mention the twist ending.
