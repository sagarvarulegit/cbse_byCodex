# 13.1.1 Food Chains and Webs

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-13-food-chains-web" />

A food chain is a sequence in which one organism is eaten by the next. Each
step is a trophic level. Arrows point **from food to consumer**, showing energy
flow.

Example:

`grass -> grasshopper -> frog -> snake`

1. producer/first trophic level: grass;
2. primary consumer/second: grasshopper;
3. secondary consumer/third: frog;
4. tertiary consumer/fourth: snake.

A food web is a network of interconnected food chains because most organisms
have several food sources and predators. Multiple pathways improve stability.

## Energy flow and the 10% law

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-13-energy-pyramid" />

- Terrestrial green plants capture about 1% of incident solar energy as food.
- On average, only about 10% of energy at one trophic level becomes body
  material available to the next.
- Most is used in life processes or lost as heat.
- Energy flow is unidirectional: Sun -> producers -> consumers -> decomposers/
  environment. It is not recycled.
- Large losses limit food chains usually to three or four consumer steps.

Example: `10,000 J -> 1,000 J -> 100 J -> 10 J`.

## Why fewer top consumers

Less usable energy is available at each higher level, so ecosystems generally
support fewer organisms/less biomass toward the top.

## Removing a trophic level

Its prey may increase, its predators lose food, competing species shift, and
energy/nutrient relationships are disturbed. The impact differs with the role
and connections of the removed level, but no entire trophic level can be
removed without ecosystem damage.

## NCERT questions

Trophic levels are feeding steps. Decomposers recycle nutrients and clear dead
matter, maintaining ecosystem function.
