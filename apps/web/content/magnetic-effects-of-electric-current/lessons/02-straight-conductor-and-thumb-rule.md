# 12.2 Magnetic Field Due to Current

# 12.2.1 Straight Current-Carrying Conductor

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-12-6-12-7-straight-wire" />

The field lines are concentric circles centred on the wire.

- increase current -> stronger field/greater compass deflection;
- increase distance -> weaker field;
- reverse current -> reverse field direction.

Activity 12.4 places a wire parallel over a compass: north-to-south current
deflects the compass one way; reversing the cells reverses deflection.

Activity 12.5 passes a vertical wire through cardboard; iron filings form
concentric circles.

# 12.2.2 Right-Hand Thumb Rule

Grip the straight conductor with the right hand:

- thumb points in conventional current direction;
- curled fingers show magnetic-field direction.

Memory:

- dot `•`: current out of page -> field anticlockwise;
- cross `×`: current into page -> field clockwise.

Also called Maxwell's corkscrew rule.

## NCERT Example 12.1

For current east to west, apply the right-hand rule. Viewed from the east end,
field circles clockwise; viewed from west, anticlockwise. State directions at
specific above/below points only after fixing the viewing direction.

## Common mistake

Use conventional current, not electron motion, in the right-hand thumb rule.
