# 8.2.2 Rules for Inheritance—Mendel's Contributions

Gregor Mendel selected pea plants because they had clear contrasting traits and
could be self- or cross-pollinated. He used **pure-breeding** parents.

## One trait: tallness

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-8-3-monohybrid-cross" />

### Parental generation

`pure tall TT × pure short tt`

Gametes: `T` and `t`

### First filial generation, F1

All offspring are `Tt` and tall. Tallness is dominant; shortness is recessive.
The recessive trait has not disappeared—it is present as `t`.

### F1 self-cross

`Tt × Tt`

|  | T | t |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | TT | Tt |
| **t** | Tt | tt |

F2 results:

- genotype ratio: `1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt`
- phenotype ratio: `3 tall : 1 short`

## What the cross proves

- Traits are controlled by pairs of factors/alleles.
- The two alleles separate during gamete formation; each gamete receives one.
- A dominant allele expresses in `TT` and `Tt`.
- A recessive allele expresses only in `tt`.
- The recessive trait can reappear unchanged in F2.

## Activity 8.2: how can 1:2:1 be confirmed?

The tall F2 plants include `TT` and `Tt`, which look alike. Self-pollinate them
or perform a test cross with a recessive `tt` plant:

- `TT × tt` -> all tall `Tt`
- `Tt × tt` -> tall and short in `1:1`

Short F2 plants are already known to be `tt`.

## Full-mark working method

Always write:

1. symbol key;
2. parental genotype;
3. gametes;
4. Punnett square;
5. genotype ratio; and
6. phenotype ratio.

## Worked board variant

All violet-flowered offspring are produced from violet × white, while almost
half the offspring are short. If tall `T` dominates short `t` and violet `W`
dominates white `w`, the tall violet parent must be `TtWW`: heterozygous for
height to give half short, but homozygous dominant for flower colour to give all
violet.

## Common mistakes

- `3:1` is a **phenotype** ratio, not genotype.
- `1:2:1` is the **genotype** ratio.
- Dominant does not mean stronger, better, or more common.
