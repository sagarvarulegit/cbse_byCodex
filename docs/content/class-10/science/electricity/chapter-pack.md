# Electricity

## 1) electric current

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

Formula:
`I = Q/t`

SI unit: ampere `A`.

One ampere means one coulomb of charge flows per second.

Conventional current direction is from positive terminal to negative terminal.

## 2) potential difference

Potential difference is work done per unit charge.

Formula:
`V = W/Q`

SI unit: volt `V`.

A cell or battery maintains potential difference in a circuit.

## 3) circuit diagram

A circuit diagram uses symbols for components like cell, battery, switch, bulb, resistor, ammeter, and voltmeter.

Ammeter is connected in series.

Voltmeter is connected in parallel.

## 4) Ohm's law

Ohm's law states:
At constant temperature, current through a conductor is directly proportional to potential difference across it.

Formula:
`V = IR`

Resistance:
`R = V/I`

SI unit of resistance: ohm `ohm`.

## 5) resistance and resistivity

Resistance depends on:
- length of conductor
- area of cross-section
- material
- temperature

Formula:
`R = rho l/A`

Here `rho` is resistivity.

Longer wire has more resistance. Thicker wire has less resistance.

Alloys have high resistivity and are used in heating elements.

## 6) resistors in series and parallel

### series

Same current flows through all resistors.

Equivalent resistance:
`Rs = R1 + R2 + R3`

Series combination increases resistance.

### parallel

Same potential difference across each branch.

Equivalent resistance:
`1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3`

Parallel combination decreases resistance.

Domestic circuits use parallel arrangement.

## 7) heating effect of current

Heat produced:
`H = I2Rt`

This is Joule's law of heating.

Applications:
- electric heater
- fuse
- electric iron
- filament bulb

## 8) electric power

Power is rate of doing electrical work.

Formulae:
- `P = VI`
- `P = I2R`
- `P = V2/R`

SI unit: watt `W`.

Commercial unit of electrical energy:
`1 kWh = 3.6 x 10^6 J`

## exam-friendly summary notes

- `I = Q/t`
- `V = W/Q`
- `V = IR`
- `R = rho l/A`
- Series: resistance adds directly.
- Parallel: reciprocal resistance adds.
- `H = I2Rt`
- `P = VI`

## common mistakes students make

1. Connecting ammeter in parallel.
2. Connecting voltmeter in series.
3. Confusing resistance and resistivity.
4. Forgetting temperature must be constant for Ohm's law.
5. Treating kWh as a unit of power instead of energy.

## board tips

- Write units with every numerical answer.
- For parallel circuits, calculate reciprocals carefully.
- Use `f = 1/P` style thinking only in optics, not electricity.
- In heating questions, square the current.

## solved practice set

### question 1
Find current when `Q = 10 C` flows in `5 s`.

Answer:
`I = Q/t = 10/5 = 2 A`

### question 2
Find resistance if `V = 12 V` and `I = 3 A`.

Answer:
`R = V/I = 12/3 = 4 ohm`

### question 3
Why are domestic appliances connected in parallel?

Answer:
Each gets full voltage and can be switched independently.
