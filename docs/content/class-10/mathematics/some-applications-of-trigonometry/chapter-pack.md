# Some Applications of Trigonometry

This is an original teaching draft for Class 10 Mathematics, Chapter 9. Most student errors here come from wrong diagrams, so the notes keep the picture and the ratio choice together.

- understand line of sight
- identify angle of elevation and angle of depression
- draw the correct right-triangle model
- choose a suitable trigonometric ratio
- solve simple heights-and-distances questions

## 1) line of sight

Line of sight is the straight line from the observer's eye to the object.

If a student sees the top of a tower, the eye-to-top line is the line of sight.

## 2) angle of elevation

When the object is above the observer, the angle made by the line of sight with the horizontal is called angle of elevation.

Examples:
- looking at the top of a building
- looking at a kite in the sky

## 3) angle of depression

When the object is below the observer, the angle made by the line of sight with the horizontal is called angle of depression.

Examples:
- looking down from a lighthouse
- looking from a balcony to a car on the road

### important fact
Horizontal lines at the observer and object level are parallel, so angle of elevation and angle of depression are often equal as alternate interior angles.

## 4) making the right triangle

Most questions are solved by:
1. drawing the situation
2. marking horizontal and vertical lengths
3. identifying the right angle
4. marking the given angle
5. using a trigonometric ratio

### common assumption
Unless otherwise stated, the observer's eye is taken at ground level or neglected for simplicity.

## 5) choosing the correct ratio

Use:
- `tan theta = opposite / adjacent` when height and base are involved
- `sin theta = opposite / hypotenuse` when slant length is involved
- `cos theta = adjacent / hypotenuse` when base and slant length are involved

### solved example
From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is `45 deg`. If the distance from the point to the foot of the tower is `20 m`, find the height of the tower.

Use:

`tan 45 deg = height / 20`

`1 = height / 20`

So height = `20 m`

### another example
If angle of elevation is `30 deg` and base is `10 sqrt(3) m`, then:

`tan 30 deg = height / 10 sqrt(3)`

`1/sqrt(3) = height / 10 sqrt(3)`

Height = `10 m`

## 6) exam-friendly summary notes

### must-know facts
- Line of sight joins eye and object.
- Angle of elevation is measured upward from horizontal.
- Angle of depression is measured downward from horizontal.
- First draw the triangle, then choose the ratio.
- `tan theta` is the most common ratio in this chapter.

### one-line revision points
- picture first, formula second
- horizontal line matters
- height problems usually become right triangles

## 7) common mistakes students make

1. Drawing angle with the vertical instead of the horizontal.
2. Mixing angle of elevation and depression.
3. Choosing the wrong trigonometric ratio.
4. Forgetting units in final answers.
5. Ignoring the observer's height when it is clearly given.

## 8) board tips

- Always draw and label the figure before solving.
- Write what each side represents: height, base, or slant.
- Mention the trigonometric ratio used.
- In final answers, write the unit such as `m`.

## 9) how to remember the chapter

### memory box 1
Elevation means looking up.

### memory box 2
Depression means looking down.

### memory box 3
The angle is always with the horizontal line.

### memory box 4
Heights-and-distances questions are triangle questions in disguise.

## 10) solved practice set

### question 1
Angle of elevation of the top of a tree is `60 deg`. Distance from the tree is `10 m`. Find the height.

Answer:
`tan 60 deg = height / 10`

`sqrt(3) = height / 10`

Height = `10 sqrt(3) m`

### question 2
What is the difference between angle of elevation and angle of depression?

Answer:
Elevation is measured upward from the horizontal.
Depression is measured downward from the horizontal.
