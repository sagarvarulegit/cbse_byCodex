# The Age of Industrialisation

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain proto-industrialisation
- understand the coming of factories
- compare hand labour and machine production
- describe industrialisation in colonies
- explain Indian textiles, factories, workers, and markets

## 1) before industrial revolution

Industrialisation was not a sudden switch from no industry to factories.

Before factories, many goods were produced in rural households through merchant-controlled networks.

## 2) proto-industrialisation

Proto-industrialisation was production for the market before factories became dominant.

Merchants supplied raw materials to rural producers and collected finished goods.

Peasants accepted this work because it gave extra income along with farming.

## 3) coming of factories

Factories brought workers, machines, power, and production under one roof.

Cotton and textiles were among the first major factory industries in Britain.

Factories increased output but did not immediately replace all hand production.

## 4) hand labour and steam power

In Victorian Britain, there was no shortage of labour, so industrialists often used hand labour where machines were costly.

Machines were adopted where they increased efficiency and profit.

Seasonal industries continued to depend on flexible labour.

## 5) workers

Workers faced long hours, unemployment, low wages, and uncertain work.

Jobs often depended on personal contacts and jobbers.

## 6) industrialisation in colonies

Colonial markets were shaped by imperial power.

Indian textiles were once famous globally, but British policies and machine-made imports damaged many weavers.

## 7) Indian textiles and Manchester

The East India Company controlled supplies and pressured weavers.

Later, Manchester cloth entered Indian markets, creating competition for local textiles.

## 8) factories in India

Indian factories grew in the nineteenth century, especially cotton mills in Bombay and jute mills in Bengal.

Indian entrepreneurs invested in trade, shipping, banking, and industry.

During the First World War, imports declined and Indian industries expanded to meet demand.

## 9) market for goods

Advertisements, labels, calendars, and images helped create demand for industrial goods.

Brands used Indian symbols, gods, and royal figures to build trust.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Proto-industrialisation came before factory industry.
- Factories did not replace hand labour instantly.
- Labour abundance slowed machine adoption in some sectors.
- Colonial rule damaged Indian textile producers.
- Indian industry expanded during the First World War.
- Advertisements shaped markets for goods.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing that factories were the first form of industrial production.
2. Assuming machines immediately replaced labour everywhere.
3. Forgetting colonial policies in the decline of Indian textiles.
4. Ignoring advertisements and labels as historical sources.

## board tips

- Define proto-industrialisation clearly.
- In worker questions, mention wages, hours, job insecurity, and jobbers.
- In colonial industry answers, connect British imports with Indian weavers.
