# Mijbil the Otter

## chapter goal

By the end of this unit, a student should be able to:
- describe the author's bond with Mijbil
- explain Mijbil's behaviour and adaptation
- identify humour and observation in animal writing
- revise the poem "Fog"
- answer questions on pets, responsibility, and imagery

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jeff106.pdf`: pet care context, Gavin Maxwell's account of Mijbil, travel from Iraq to London, and the poem "Fog".

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Author's loneliness and decision
2. Mijbil's arrival
3. Behaviour and play
4. Journey and public reaction
5. Human-animal bond
6. Poem: "Fog"
7. Exam practice and recap

## 1) author's situation

After losing his dog, the author feels lonely but does not want another dog.

He gets an otter, later named Mijbil, while he is in Iraq.

## 2) Mijbil's arrival

At first Mijbil is quiet and unfamiliar with the author.

Soon he becomes energetic, playful, and strongly attached to water.

His love of water shows his natural instincts.

## 3) behaviour and play

Mijbil plays with marbles, water, and objects. He is intelligent, curious, and lively.

The author observes him with affection and humour.

## 4) journey and reaction

Transporting Mijbil to London is difficult. The otter causes confusion and surprise.

People misidentify him because they are unfamiliar with otters as pets.

## 5) human-animal bond

The chapter shows that keeping a pet requires patience, care, observation, and responsibility.

It also shows how animals have their own nature and should not be treated as toys.

## 6) poem: Fog

The poem compares fog to a cat.

Fog arrives silently, sits quietly, and then moves away.

The poem uses metaphor, brevity, and visual imagery.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Mijbil is an otter from Iraq.
- He is playful, intelligent, and fond of water.
- The author and Mijbil develop a close bond.
- The travel episode adds humour and tension.
- "Fog" uses a cat metaphor to describe silent movement.

## common mistakes students make

1. Treating Mijbil like an ordinary dog or cat.
2. Forgetting the responsibility involved in pet care.
3. Writing that the poem literally describes a cat.
4. Missing the humour in public reactions to Mijbil.

## board tips

- In animal character answers, use behaviour as evidence.
- For "Fog", mention metaphor and silence.
- Connect pet care with responsibility, not just affection.

## short answer practice

1. Why did the author get an otter?
2. What shows that Mijbil loved water?
3. Why did people react strangely to Mijbil?
4. What does the chapter teach about pets?
5. How is fog compared to a cat?
