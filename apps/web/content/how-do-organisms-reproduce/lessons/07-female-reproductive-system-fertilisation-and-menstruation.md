# 7.3.3 (b) Female Reproductive System

[[figure:figure-7-11-female-reproductive-system]]

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Ovaries | contain immature eggs; after puberty one egg commonly matures and is released each month; also produce hormones |
| Oviducts/Fallopian tubes | carry the egg; usual site of fertilisation |
| Uterus | elastic organ whose thick, blood-rich lining receives the embryo and supports development |
| Cervix | narrow opening between uterus and vagina |
| Vagina | receives sperms and forms the birth passage |

## Fertilisation and implantation

[[figure:figure-7-fertilisation-implantation]]

1. Sperms enter through the vagina and move through the uterus.
2. A sperm may meet the egg in an oviduct.
3. Their nuclei fuse; fertilisation forms a zygote.
4. The zygote divides repeatedly into a ball of cells or embryo.
5. The embryo reaches the uterus and implants in its thick lining.
6. Organ development gradually produces a foetus.

Keep the locations separate:

- **fertilisation:** oviduct/fallopian tube
- **implantation and development:** uterus

## Placenta

[[figure:figure-7-placenta]]

The placenta is a disc-shaped special tissue embedded in the uterine wall. It
has villi on the embryo's side and maternal blood spaces surrounding them.
This large surface area permits exchange:

- glucose and oxygen pass from mother to embryo
- carbon dioxide and metabolic wastes pass from embryo to mother's blood

The two blood supplies lie close for exchange but are not described as mixing
directly.

Development takes about nine months. Rhythmic contraction of uterine muscles
helps deliver the baby.

## 7.3.3 (c) What if the egg is not fertilised?

An unfertilised egg lives for about one day. Each month the uterus has already
formed a thick, spongy, blood-rich lining in case an embryo implants. If
fertilisation does not occur, this lining is no longer needed. It breaks down
and leaves through the vagina as blood and mucus.

This roughly monthly cycle is menstruation and usually lasts about two to eight
days.

## Fertilised versus unfertilised

| Egg fertilised | Egg not fertilised |
| --- | --- |
| zygote forms in oviduct | egg degenerates |
| zygote divides into embryo | thick uterine lining breaks down |
| embryo implants in uterus | blood and mucus leave through vagina |
| placenta develops for exchange | menstruation occurs |

## Board-ready uterus answer

- Before possible implantation, the lining thickens and becomes richly supplied
  with blood.
- After implantation, it remains and supports the embryo; placenta develops.
- Without fertilisation, it breaks down and is discharged during menstruation.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying fertilisation occurs in the uterus.
2. Saying implantation occurs in the ovary.
3. Calling placenta the place where fertilisation occurs.
4. Saying maternal and embryonic blood mix freely.
5. Saying menstruation is the unfertilised egg alone.
