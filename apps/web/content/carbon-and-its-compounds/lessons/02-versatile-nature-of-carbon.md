# 4.2 Versatile nature of carbon

Carbon forms a very large number of compounds because it is tetravalent and shows catenation.

The textbook idea is not only "carbon makes many compounds". The real question is: why does carbon, a small element present in a small amount in nature, become the base of food, clothes, medicines, fuels, plastics, living bodies, and so many other things? The answer is that carbon can build stable structures in many shapes.

## Tetravalency

Tetravalency means the ability to form four bonds.

Carbon has four valence electrons, so it can form four covalent bonds with:
- hydrogen
- oxygen
- nitrogen
- sulphur
- chlorine
- other carbon atoms

This makes many different carbon compounds possible.

Think of tetravalency as four connection points. One carbon atom can connect to four hydrogens, or two oxygens, or one carbon plus several hydrogens, or a carbon chain plus a functional group. Because these four bonds can be used in different combinations, the number of possible compounds becomes very large.

Example:
- in methane, carbon uses all four bonds with hydrogen
- in ethane, each carbon uses one bond with another carbon and three bonds with hydrogen
- in carbon dioxide, carbon uses two double bonds with oxygen

## Preview: single and multiple carbon bonds

This is textbook section 4.2.1.

Saturated carbon compounds have only single bonds between carbon atoms.

Example:
Ethane, `C2H6`, has a single carbon-carbon bond.

The word saturated means the carbon skeleton is already holding the maximum number of hydrogen atoms possible for that carbon arrangement. Since there are only single bonds, no extra atoms can simply add across a double or triple bond.

Unsaturated carbon compounds have at least one double or triple bond between carbon atoms.

Examples:
- Ethene, `C2H4`, has a double bond.
- Ethyne, `C2H2`, has a triple bond.

Unsaturated means there is a multiple bond. A double or triple bond can open up in addition reactions, so these compounds behave differently from saturated hydrocarbons.

## Catenation

Catenation is the ability of an element to form bonds with atoms of the same element.

Carbon shows strong catenation. Carbon atoms can join with other carbon atoms to form:
- long chains
- branched chains
- closed rings

Example:

`C-C-C-C`

This is a carbon chain.

Carbon does this better than most elements because the `C-C` bond is strong. Silicon can also form chains with hydrogen, but the textbook notes that such chains are much shorter and more reactive. Carbon chains are stable enough to form millions of known compounds.

The textbook also asks about cyclopentane.

Cyclopentane has five carbon atoms in a ring. Its formula is `C5H10`.

For a simple ring like cyclopentane, imagine the two ends of a five-carbon chain joining each other. Because the ends join, the compound has fewer hydrogens than an open-chain alkane with five carbons.

## Why carbon compounds are stable

Carbon-carbon bonds are strong.

Carbon is small in size, so the shared electron pairs are held tightly between bonded atoms. This gives carbon compounds good stability.

This is a key "why" answer. When an atom is small, its nucleus can pull the shared pair of electrons strongly. The shared pair stays held between the two atoms, so the covalent bond is strong. Bigger atoms hold shared pairs less tightly, so their bonds are usually weaker.

## 4.2.2 Chains, Branches and Rings

Carbon skeletons can have different shapes.

Straight chain:

`C-C-C-C`

Branched chain:

`C-C-C`

`  |`

`  C`

Ring:

Six carbon atoms can join in a closed ring, as in many cyclic compounds.

Same formula can sometimes have different structures. These are called structural isomers. For example, butane `C4H10` can be a straight chain or a branched chain. Pentane `C5H12` has three structural isomers. This is another reason carbon compounds become so numerous.

## Allotropes of carbon

Allotropes are different physical forms of the same element.

Important carbon allotropes:
- diamond
- graphite
- fullerene

Diamond is very hard because each carbon is strongly bonded to four other carbon atoms.

Graphite is soft and conducts electricity because its carbon atoms are arranged in layers with mobile electrons.

Diamond and graphite teach an important lesson: the element is the same, but the arrangement is different. In diamond, each carbon bonds to four other carbons in a rigid three-dimensional network. In graphite, each carbon bonds to three other carbons in layers. The layers can slide, so graphite is slippery, and the mobile electrons help it conduct electricity.

Fullerene is another allotrope. The textbook mentions `C-60`, where carbon atoms are arranged like a football.

## Questions and answers

### 4.2 Versatile nature of carbon

Question:
What is tetravalency?

Answer:
Tetravalency is the ability of carbon to form four covalent bonds.

Question:
What is catenation?

Answer:
Catenation is the ability of carbon atoms to bond with one another and form long chains, branches, and rings.

Question:
Why is carbon versatile?

Answer:
Carbon is versatile because it has tetravalency, strong carbon-carbon bonds, and catenation.

More to Know:
- Carbon versatility is one reason why the number of carbon compounds is so large.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Catenation

Question:
Why can carbon form long chains?

Answer:
Carbon shows catenation. It can form strong bonds with other carbon atoms.

### Example 2: Graphite

Question:
Why can graphite conduct electricity?

Answer:
Graphite has mobile electrons between its layers, so it can conduct electricity.

### Example 3: Pentane isomers

Question:
How many structural isomers are possible for pentane?

Answer:
Three. Pentane can be drawn as a straight chain, one branch, or a more compact branched structure.

### Example 4: Why carbon, not silicon?

Question:
Why does carbon show catenation better than silicon?

Answer:
Carbon is small, so the carbon-carbon bond is strong and stable. Silicon can form chains, but its chains are shorter and more reactive.

### Example 5: Saturated or unsaturated

Question:
Why is ethyne called unsaturated?

Answer:
Ethyne has a carbon-carbon triple bond. Compounds with double or triple bonds between carbon atoms are called unsaturated.

## Common mistakes

1. Writing catenation as "formation of any bond". It specifically means bonding with the same element.
2. Forgetting that tetravalency means four bonds.
3. Saying diamond and graphite are different elements. They are allotropes of carbon.
4. Calling every hydrocarbon saturated. Ethene and ethyne are unsaturated.
