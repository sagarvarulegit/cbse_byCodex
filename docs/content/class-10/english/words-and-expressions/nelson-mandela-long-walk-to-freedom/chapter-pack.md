# Words and Expressions Unit 2 - Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom

## chapter goal

By the end of this workbook unit, a student should be able to:
- discuss freedom and responsibility
- read speeches and public prose for message and tone
- build vocabulary around character, discipline, and nation-building
- practise grammar, editing, speaking, and writing tasks

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jewe202.pdf`: opening discussion on freedom and responsibility, reading comprehension based on public speech, vocabulary, grammar, editing, listening/speaking, writing, and project work.

## 1) discussion skill

The unit asks students to connect Mandela's idea of freedom with responsibility.

Good answers should show that freedom is not doing anything one wants; it includes duty toward others.

## 2) speech comprehension

Public speeches often have a clear purpose: to inspire, warn, persuade, or guide.

Read for speaker, audience, occasion, main message, and supporting ideas.

## 3) tone and message

Tone may be serious, inspiring, reflective, or urgent.

In this unit, focus on character, discipline, public duty, and national service.

## 4) vocabulary

Use context clues to understand formal words.

Group vocabulary by theme: freedom, duty, discipline, courage, sacrifice, and leadership.

## 5) grammar and editing

Practise sentence links, tense, subject-verb agreement, and correction of errors.

Read the full sentence before choosing an answer.

## 6) speaking and writing

Speaking tasks should be respectful and organised.

Writing tasks may include speeches, paragraphs, or reflections on responsible citizenship.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Freedom and responsibility go together.
- Speeches must be read for audience and purpose.
- Tone tells us the speaker's attitude.
- Formal vocabulary should be understood from context.
- Writing should include examples from public life.

## common mistakes students make

1. Defining freedom as absence of rules.
2. Ignoring the audience of a speech.
3. Writing vague points without examples.
4. Missing formal tone in speech writing.

## board tips

- In speech analysis, write speaker + occasion + message.
- Use connectives like therefore, however, and moreover.
- For responsible freedom, include rights and duties.
