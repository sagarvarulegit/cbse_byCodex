# Chapter 10 NCERT Answer Lab

1. Different-distance focus: **(b) accommodation**.
2. Image forms on **(d) retina**.
3. Least distance: **(c) 25 cm**.
4. Focal-length change: **(c) ciliary muscles**.

## 5. Two corrective powers

- distant: `P=-5.5D -> f=-0.182m` (about `-18.2cm`);
- near: `P=+1.5D -> f=+0.667m` (about `+66.7cm`).

## 6. Myopic far point 80 cm

Concave lens; `u=-∞`, `v=-0.80m`, so `f=-0.80m`, `P=-1.25D`.

## 7. Hypermetropic near point 1 m

Convex lens must form virtual image at `v=-1m` of an object at `u=-0.25m`.
`P=1/f=+3D`. Draw correction bringing final focus onto retina.

## 8. Why not clear below 25 cm?

Ciliary muscles cannot increase curvature/power further; the eye cannot reduce
focal length enough to focus such diverging rays sharply on retina.

## 9. Object moves farther—what happens to image distance?

It stays essentially constant because the retina is fixed. Ciliary muscles
change eye-lens focal length.

## 10. Stars twinkle

Point-source starlight undergoes continuously varying atmospheric refraction,
changing apparent position and brightness.

## 11. Planets do not

They appear extended; fluctuations from many point-source parts average out.

## 12. Astronaut's sky

With no appreciable atmosphere, sunlight is not scattered into the eye; sky is
dark.

## Memory strip

`myopia -> front -> concave`

`hypermetropia -> behind -> convex`

`star point -> twinkles; planet extended -> averages`

`blue scatters more; red scatters least`
