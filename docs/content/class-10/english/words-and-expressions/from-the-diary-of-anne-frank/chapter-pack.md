# Words and Expressions Unit 4 - From the Diary of Anne Frank

## chapter goal

By the end of this workbook unit, a student should be able to:
- discuss isolation, war, and diary writing
- read diary excerpts for feeling and context
- build vocabulary around conflict and survival
- practise grammar, editing, speaking, writing, and project work

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jewe204..pdf`: discussion on Anne Frank's feelings, reading comprehension from a wartime diary excerpt, vocabulary, grammar, editing, listening/speaking, writing, and project work.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Isolation and alienation discussion
2. Diary excerpt reading
3. Historical context
4. Vocabulary
5. Grammar and editing
6. Reflective writing
7. Project work

## 1) discussion skill

The unit asks students to relate to Anne's feelings of isolation and alienation.

Good discussion is sensitive: connect with feelings without exaggerating personal comparison to war suffering.

## 2) diary reading

Diary excerpts show personal experience from inside an event.

Read for speaker's feelings, situation, time, and sensory details.

## 3) historical context

The workbook passage refers to war, siege, hunger, fear, and survival.

Context helps explain why ordinary daily details become emotionally powerful.

## 4) vocabulary

Important vocabulary fields include war, fear, hardship, weather, hunger, and emotional numbness.

Use context clues before checking a dictionary.

## 5) writing

Reflective writing should be personal but organised.

A diary entry should include date/time if required, first-person voice, feelings, and specific details.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Diary writing records personal feelings and events.
- War writing often shows fear, loss, and survival.
- Context is necessary for comprehension.
- Reflective writing needs honesty and structure.
- Vocabulary should be understood in emotional context.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing a diary entry like a formal essay.
2. Ignoring historical context.
3. Using dramatic emotion without specific detail.

## board tips

- For diary entries, use first person naturally.
- In comprehension, identify who is speaking and what situation they face.
- Avoid copying long lines; answer in your own words.
