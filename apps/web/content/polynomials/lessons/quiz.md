# Polynomials chapter quiz

## 1. Zero from a graph

The zeroes of $p(x)$ are the x-coordinates where $y=p(x)$:

1. meets the y-axis
2. meets or touches the x-axis
3. reaches its highest point
4. crosses the origin only

Answer: 2
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 2.2 aligned
Explanation: On the x-axis, $y=0$, so $p(x)=0$.

## 2. Maximum number of zeroes

A cubic polynomial can have at most:

1. one zero
2. two zeroes
3. three zeroes
4. four zeroes

Answer: 3
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 2.2 aligned
Explanation: A degree-$3$ polynomial has at most three zeroes.

## 3. Quadratic sum

For $2x^2-7x+3$, the sum of zeroes is:

1. $-7/2$
2. $7/2$
3. $3/2$
4. $-3/2$

Answer: 2
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 2.3 aligned
Explanation: Sum $=-b/a=-(-7)/2=7/2$.

## 4. Quadratic product

For $3x^2-x-4$, the product of zeroes is:

1. $4/3$
2. $-1/3$
3. $-4/3$
4. $1/3$

Answer: 3
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Exercise 2.2 aligned
Explanation: Product $=c/a=-4/3$.

## 5. Equal zeroes

The zeroes of $4s^2-4s+1$ are:

1. $1/2,1/2$
2. $-1/2,-1/2$
3. $1,-1$
4. $0,1$

Answer: 1
Topic: ncert-exercises-and-revision
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Exercise 2.2 aligned
Explanation: $4s^2-4s+1=(2s-1)^2$.

## 6. Polynomial from zeroes

A monic quadratic with zeroes $2$ and $5$ is:

1. $x^2+7x+10$
2. $x^2-3x+10$
3. $x^2-7x+10$
4. $x^2+3x-10$

Answer: 3
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Section 2.3 aligned
Explanation: $(x-2)(x-5)=x^2-7x+10$.

## 7. No real zero

A quadratic graph entirely above the x-axis has:

1. no real zero
2. one real zero
3. two real zeroes
4. three real zeroes

Answer: 1
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Figures 2.3-2.5 aligned
Explanation: The graph never reaches $y=0$.

## 8. Irrational zeroes

The zeroes of $t^2-15$ are:

1. $15,-15$
2. $\sqrt{15},-\sqrt{15}$
3. $\sqrt{15},\sqrt{15}$
4. $0,15$

Answer: 2
Topic: ncert-exercises-and-revision
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Exercise 2.2 aligned
Explanation: $t^2=15$ gives $t=\pm\sqrt{15}$.
