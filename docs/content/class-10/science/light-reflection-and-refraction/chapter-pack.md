# Light - Reflection and Refraction

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain reflection and refraction of light
- draw ray diagrams for spherical mirrors and lenses
- use sign conventions
- solve numerical problems using mirror formula, lens formula, magnification, and power

## 1) reflection of light

Reflection is bouncing back of light from a surface.

Laws of reflection:
- incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane
- angle of incidence equals angle of reflection

Plane mirror forms a virtual, erect image of the same size.

## 2) spherical mirrors

Two types:
- concave mirror: reflecting surface curves inward
- convex mirror: reflecting surface curves outward

Important terms:
- pole `P`
- centre of curvature `C`
- principal focus `F`
- focal length `f`
- radius of curvature `R`

Relation:
`R = 2f`

## 3) image formation by mirrors

Concave mirror can form real or virtual images depending on object position.

Convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image.

Uses:
- concave mirror: shaving mirror, headlights, solar furnace
- convex mirror: rear-view mirrors

## 4) mirror formula and magnification

Mirror formula:
`1/f = 1/v + 1/u`

Magnification:
`m = -v/u`

Where:
- `u` is object distance
- `v` is image distance
- `f` is focal length

Use the NCERT sign convention carefully.

## 5) refraction of light

Refraction is bending of light when it passes from one medium to another.

Light bends because its speed changes in different media.

When light goes from rarer to denser medium, it bends towards the normal.

When light goes from denser to rarer medium, it bends away from the normal.

Refractive index:
`n = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in medium`

## 6) spherical lenses

Two types:
- convex lens: converging lens
- concave lens: diverging lens

Convex lens can form real or virtual images.

Concave lens always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image.

## 7) lens formula, magnification, and power

Lens formula:
`1/f = 1/v - 1/u`

Magnification:
`m = v/u`

Power:
`P = 1/f`

Here `f` must be in metres. Unit of power is dioptre `D`.

Convex lens has positive power. Concave lens has negative power.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Reflection means bouncing back.
- Refraction means bending due to speed change.
- Concave mirror and convex lens can form real images.
- Convex mirror and concave lens always form virtual, erect, diminished images.
- `R = 2f` for spherical mirrors.
- Use metres for lens power.

## common mistakes students make

1. Mixing mirror and lens formulae.
2. Forgetting sign convention.
3. Using focal length in centimetres while calculating power.
4. Calling every virtual image enlarged.
5. Drawing rays without arrow direction.

## board tips

- Practise ray diagrams repeatedly.
- Write the formula before substituting values.
- Convert centimetres to metres for power.
- State nature, position, and size of image.

## solved practice set

### question 1
A concave mirror has focal length `15 cm`. What is its radius of curvature?

Answer:
`R = 2f = 30 cm`

### question 2
A lens has focal length `+0.5 m`. Find power.

Answer:
`P = 1/f = 1/0.5 = +2 D`

### question 3
Why does light bend during refraction?

Answer:
Its speed changes when it enters another medium.
