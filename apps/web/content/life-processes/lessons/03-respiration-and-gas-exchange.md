# 5.3 Respiration

Respiration releases usable energy from food. The textbook links this to ATP, the energy currency used for many cellular reactions.

Do not confuse breathing with respiration. Breathing is gas exchange at the organism level. Respiration is the cellular breakdown of food to release energy.

## 5.3 Respiration and ATP

Food must be broken down to release energy in a form cells can use.

ATP is used to power many cellular processes. When ATP is broken down, energy is released for activities such as muscle contraction, protein synthesis, and nerve impulse transmission.

Memory line:
`Food stores energy; ATP spends energy.`

## Glucose breakdown pathways

Glucose first breaks down into pyruvate in the cytoplasm.

After that, pyruvate is broken down in different pathways:
- in mitochondria with oxygen: carbon dioxide, water, and more energy
- in yeast without oxygen: ethanol, carbon dioxide, and less energy
- in muscles with lack of oxygen: lactic acid and less energy

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases more energy.

Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen and releases less energy.

Muscle cramps can happen when lack of oxygen leads to lactic acid formation.

## Gas exchange in plants

Plants exchange gases by diffusion through stomata and across other surfaces.

During photosynthesis:
- carbon dioxide is used
- oxygen may be released

During respiration:
- oxygen is used
- carbon dioxide is released

The direction of exchange depends on plant needs and time of day.

## Gas exchange in animals

Animals use different organs for gas exchange:
- aquatic animals may use gills
- terrestrial animals may use lungs or other respiratory surfaces

Aquatic organisms often breathe faster because water has less dissolved oxygen than air.

Terrestrial organisms must keep respiratory surfaces moist while avoiding too much water loss.

## Human respiratory system

Air enters through nostrils and passes through the nasal passage, trachea, bronchi, and lungs.

The trachea has rings of cartilage. These prevent the air passage from collapsing.

Inside the lungs, the bronchi divide into smaller tubes ending in alveoli.

Alveoli are designed for gas exchange:
- very large surface area
- thin walls
- rich blood supply

Oxygen diffuses from alveoli into blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into alveoli.

The lungs always retain some **residual volume of air**. This prevents the
alveoli from collapsing and allows gas exchange to continue between breaths.

## Haemoglobin

Haemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen from lungs to tissues.

If haemoglobin is deficient, less oxygen reaches body cells. A person may feel tired or weak because cells receive less oxygen for energy release.

Carbon dioxide is more soluble in water than oxygen, so it is transported mainly in dissolved form in blood plasma.

## Breathing

When we inhale:
- ribs move up and out
- diaphragm moves down
- chest cavity becomes larger
- air enters lungs

When we exhale:
- ribs move down and in
- diaphragm moves up
- chest cavity becomes smaller
- air moves out

The rings of cartilage in the trachea keep the air passage open and prevent it
from collapsing.

## Questions and answers

Question:
What advantage over an aquatic organism does a terrestrial organism have with regard to obtaining oxygen for respiration?

Answer:
Terrestrial organisms breathe oxygen from air, where oxygen is much more abundant than dissolved oxygen in water. Aquatic organisms must extract oxygen from water, so they often need a faster breathing rate.

Question:
What are the different ways in which glucose is oxidised to provide energy in various organisms?

Answer:
Glucose first breaks into pyruvate in the cytoplasm. Pyruvate may break down into carbon dioxide and water in mitochondria when oxygen is present, into ethanol and carbon dioxide in yeast without oxygen, or into lactic acid in muscles when oxygen is insufficient.

Question:
How is oxygen and carbon dioxide transported in human beings?

Answer:
Oxygen is carried mainly by haemoglobin in red blood cells from the lungs to body tissues. Carbon dioxide is more soluble in water than oxygen, so it is transported mostly in dissolved form in blood plasma.

Question:
How are the lungs designed in human beings to maximise the area for exchange of gases?

Answer:
Inside the lungs, air passages divide into many tiny balloon-like alveoli. The alveoli provide a large surface area, have thin walls, and are surrounded by blood capillaries, making gas exchange efficient.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Aerobic and anaerobic respiration

Question:
What are the differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration?

Answer:
Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases more energy, producing carbon dioxide and water. Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen and releases less energy, producing ethanol and carbon dioxide in yeast or lactic acid in muscles.

### Example 2: Pyruvate

Question:
Where does pyruvate break down to give carbon dioxide, water, and energy?

Answer:
In the mitochondria, when oxygen is available.

### Example 3: Alveoli

Question:
How are alveoli designed to maximise gas exchange?

Answer:
They provide a large surface area, have thin walls, and are surrounded by blood capillaries.

### Example 4: Haemoglobin

Question:
What happens if haemoglobin is deficient?

Answer:
The blood carries less oxygen, so body cells receive less oxygen for respiration and energy release.

## Common mistakes

1. Treating breathing and cellular respiration as the same thing.
2. Saying anaerobic respiration produces more energy.
3. Forgetting mitochondria in aerobic pyruvate breakdown.
4. Writing that alveoli are thick-walled.
5. Ignoring haemoglobin in oxygen transport.

## One-minute revision

Respiration releases energy from food as ATP. Glucose becomes pyruvate in the cytoplasm. With oxygen, pyruvate breaks down in mitochondria to carbon dioxide, water, and more energy. Without oxygen, yeast forms ethanol and carbon dioxide, while muscles may form lactic acid. Alveoli provide a large thin surface for gas exchange, and haemoglobin carries oxygen in blood.
