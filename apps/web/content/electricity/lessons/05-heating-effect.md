# 11.7 Heating Effect of Electric Current

Electrical energy in a resistor becomes heat. Work:

`W = VQ = VIt`.

Using Ohm's law, Joule's law of heating:

`H = I²Rt = VIt = V²t/R`.

At constant R:

- heat ∝ square of current;
- heat ∝ resistance;
- heat ∝ time.

# 11.7.1 Practical Applications

- heater, iron, toaster: high-resistivity alloy element becomes hot;
- electric lamp: tungsten filament becomes white-hot and luminous;
- fuse: low-melting-point wire in series melts when current exceeds its rating,
  opening the circuit.

Tungsten is used for lamp filaments because it has very high melting point,
high resistivity, and can glow at high temperature. The bulb is filled with
chemically inactive gases such as nitrogen/argon to prolong filament life.

Alloys are used in heaters because they have high resistivity, high melting
point, and do not oxidise readily.

## Worked comparison

Two equal wires:

- series equivalent `2R`;
- parallel equivalent `R/2`.

At same voltage and time, `H=V²t/Req`, so:

`Hseries:Hparallel = 1/(2R):1/(R/2) = 1:4`.

## Fuse rule

Fuse is connected in the live wire and in series. Its rating should be just
above normal operating current, not arbitrarily large.
