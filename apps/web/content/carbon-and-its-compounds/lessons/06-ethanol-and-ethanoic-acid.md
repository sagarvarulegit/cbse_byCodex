# 4.4 Some important carbon compounds - ethanol and ethanoic acid

Ethanol and ethanoic acid are two important carbon compounds in the Class 10 textbook.

They look similar because both have two carbon atoms, but their functional groups are different. Ethanol has the alcohol group `-OH`; ethanoic acid has the carboxylic acid group `-COOH`. That one difference changes their smell, acidity, reactions, and uses.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- write the formula of ethanol
- write the formula of ethanoic acid
- list important properties and uses of ethanol
- explain esterification
- describe reactions of ethanoic acid with bases and carbonates

## Ethanol

Ethanol is an alcohol.

Formula:

`C2H5OH`

Properties:
- liquid at room temperature
- mixes with water
- burns with a clean flame
- acts as a solvent

Uses:
- medicines
- tincture iodine
- cough syrups
- fuel mixtures

Ethanol is also the alcohol present in alcoholic drinks. The textbook clearly warns that alcohol consumption harms the body. Large quantities of ethanol slow metabolic processes and depress the central nervous system, causing poor coordination, confusion, drowsiness, and loss of judgement. Pure ethanol can be lethal.

Ethanol is soluble in water in all proportions because its `-OH` group can interact with water.

## Reactions of ethanol

### Reaction with sodium

Ethanol reacts with sodium to form sodium ethoxide and hydrogen gas.

`2Na + 2CH3CH2OH -> 2CH3CH2ONa + H2`

Hydrogen can be tested by bringing a burning splint near the gas. It burns with a pop sound.

Why this reaction matters:
The hydrogen attached to oxygen in the `-OH` group is replaced by sodium. That is why hydrogen gas is released and sodium ethoxide is formed.

### Dehydration to ethene

The textbook also gives this reaction:

When ethanol is heated with excess concentrated sulphuric acid at about `443 K`, water is removed from ethanol.

`CH3CH2OH -> CH2=CH2 + H2O`

This is called dehydration of ethanol.

Concentrated sulphuric acid acts as a dehydrating agent.

Dehydration means removal of water. In ethanol, one hydrogen and one `-OH` are removed in such a way that ethene `CH2=CH2` forms. This connects the alcohol lesson back to unsaturated hydrocarbons.

### Methanol and denatured alcohol

Methanol is poisonous even in small quantities. It can damage the optic nerve and cause blindness.

To prevent misuse, industrial ethanol is often made unfit for drinking by adding poisonous substances such as methanol. Dyes may also be added so it can be identified easily. This is called denatured alcohol.

## Ethanoic acid

Ethanoic acid is a carboxylic acid.

Formula:

`CH3COOH`

Common name:

Acetic acid.

A dilute solution of ethanoic acid in water is called vinegar.

Pure ethanoic acid freezes near room temperature, so it is also called glacial acetic acid.

Ethanoic acid is a weak acid. It does not ionise completely in water.

The textbook gives vinegar as a `5-8%` solution of acetic acid in water, used widely as a preservative in pickles. The word glacial comes from pure ethanoic acid freezing in cold climates because its melting point is about `290 K`.

## Esterification

Ethanol reacts with ethanoic acid in the presence of concentrated sulphuric acid to form an ester.

Esterification:

`ethanoic acid + ethanol -> ester + water`

Esters usually have a pleasant fruity smell.

Concentrated sulphuric acid helps remove water and supports ester formation.

Activity 4.8 uses absolute ethanol, glacial acetic acid, and a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid. The mixture is warmed in a water bath and then poured into water. The pleasant smell comes from the ester.

[[demo:activity-4-8-esterification]]

The reverse idea is also important. When an ester is treated with sodium hydroxide, it gives back alcohol and the sodium salt of a carboxylic acid. This is called saponification because it is used in soap preparation.

## Ethanoic acid with base

Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium hydroxide to form sodium ethanoate and water.

`CH3COOH + NaOH -> CH3COONa + H2O`

This is a neutralisation reaction.

This proves that ethanoic acid behaves like an acid, even though it is weaker than strong mineral acids.

## Ethanoic acid with carbonate

Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium carbonate to produce:
- sodium ethanoate
- carbon dioxide
- water

`2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 -> 2CH3COONa + CO2 + H2O`

Carbon dioxide turns lime water milky.

The textbook also repeats the test with sodium hydrogencarbonate:

`CH3COOH + NaHCO3 -> CH3COONa + CO2 + H2O`

[[demo:activity-4-9-ethanoic-acid-carbonate]]

This carbonate reaction is the best classroom test to distinguish ethanoic acid from ethanol. Ethanoic acid gives brisk bubbles of carbon dioxide with sodium carbonate or sodium hydrogencarbonate. Ethanol does not.

## Questions and answers

### 4.4 Some important carbon compounds - ethanol and ethanoic acid

Question:
What is esterification?

Answer:
Esterification is the reaction of an alcohol with a carboxylic acid to form an ester and water.

Question:
What are the important properties of ethanol?

Answer:
Ethanol is a colourless liquid, mixes with water, and is used as a solvent and fuel.

Question:
Why does ethanoic acid react with carbonates?

Answer:
Ethanoic acid behaves as an acid and reacts with carbonates to produce salt, carbon dioxide, and water.

Think it over:
- The pleasant smell of an ester is one easy way to remember esterification in the lab.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Formula

Question:
Write the formula of ethanol and ethanoic acid.

Answer:
Ethanol is `C2H5OH`. Ethanoic acid is `CH3COOH`.

### Example 2: Ester smell

Question:
Why are esters used in perfumes and flavouring agents?

Answer:
Many esters have a pleasant fruity smell.

### Example 3: Carbon dioxide test

Question:
What happens when ethanoic acid reacts with sodium carbonate?

Answer:
Carbon dioxide gas is released. It turns lime water milky.

### Example 4: Alcohol or carboxylic acid

Question:
How can you distinguish ethanol and ethanoic acid experimentally?

Answer:
Add sodium carbonate or sodium hydrogencarbonate. Ethanoic acid gives carbon dioxide bubbles that turn lime water milky. Ethanol does not give this carbonate reaction.

### Example 5: Dehydration

Question:
What happens when ethanol is heated with excess concentrated sulphuric acid at `443 K`?

Answer:
Water is removed from ethanol, so ethene is formed. Concentrated sulphuric acid acts as a dehydrating agent.

### Example 6: Saponification link

Question:
Why is the reaction of an ester with sodium hydroxide called saponification?

Answer:
The reaction produces alcohol and the sodium salt of a carboxylic acid. Such sodium or potassium salts of long-chain carboxylic acids are soaps, so the reaction is used in soap preparation.

## Common mistakes

1. Mixing ethanol `C2H5OH` with ethanoic acid `CH3COOH`.
2. Forgetting concentrated sulphuric acid in esterification.
3. Calling vinegar pure ethanoic acid.
4. Forgetting the lime-water test for carbon dioxide.
5. Forgetting ethanol dehydration to ethene.
6. Mixing up methanol and ethanol safety.

## Board tips

- Write formulas with functional groups clearly: `C2H5OH` and `CH3COOH`.
- For esterification, mention pleasant smell and concentrated sulphuric acid.
- In carbonate reactions, always name the gas and its test.
- For ethanol reactions, keep sodium reaction and dehydration reaction separate.

## One-minute revision

Ethanol is `C2H5OH`, an alcohol. Ethanoic acid is `CH3COOH`, a carboxylic acid also called acetic acid. Ethanol and ethanoic acid form fruity-smelling esters. Ethanoic acid reacts with carbonates to release carbon dioxide.
