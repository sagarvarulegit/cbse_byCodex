# 7.3.2 Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

The flower contains the reproductive parts of angiosperms.

## Figure 7.7: structure of a flower

[[figure:figure-7-7-flower-section]]

| Part | Structure and role |
| --- | --- |
| Sepals | protect the flower bud; may persist after fruit formation |
| Petals | often attract pollinating animals |
| Stamen | male reproductive part: filament supports the pollen-producing anther |
| Pistil/carpel | female reproductive part: stigma, style, and ovary |
| Stigma | terminal, often sticky surface that receives pollen |
| Style | elongated link between stigma and ovary; pollen tube grows through it |
| Ovary | swollen basal part containing ovules |
| Ovule | contains the egg cell; becomes a seed after fertilisation |

### Unisexual and bisexual flowers

- **Unisexual:** contains either stamens or pistil—papaya, watermelon.
- **Bisexual:** contains both stamens and pistil—Hibiscus, mustard.

## Pollination is not fertilisation

| Pollination | Fertilisation |
| --- | --- |
| transfer of pollen from anther to stigma | fusion of male and female germ cells |
| happens before pollen-tube growth | occurs inside an ovule |
| may use wind, water, or animals | produces a zygote |

### Self- and cross-pollination

- **Self-pollination:** pollen reaches the stigma of the same flower or another
  flower on the same plant.
- **Cross-pollination:** pollen reaches a flower on another plant of the same
  species, commonly through wind, water, or animals.

Cross-pollination can create greater genetic variation because gametes come from
different plants.

## Figure 7.8: from pollination to fertilisation

[[figure:figure-7-8-pollen-germination]]

1. A compatible pollen grain lands on the stigma.
2. It germinates and grows a pollen tube through the style.
3. Male germ cells travel down the pollen tube.
4. The tube enters an ovule in the ovary.
5. A male germ cell fuses with the egg cell.
6. The fertilised egg is the zygote.

If pollen from an unrelated species lands on the stigma, it is generally
incompatible and does not complete normal pollen-tube growth and fertilisation.

## Post-fertilisation changes

[[figure:figure-7-post-fertilisation]]

| Before/at fertilisation | After fertilisation |
| --- | --- |
| zygote | divides repeatedly to form embryo |
| ovule | develops a tough coat and becomes seed |
| ovary | enlarges and ripens into fruit |
| petals, sepals, stamens, style, stigma | usually shrivel and fall; some may persist |

## Activity 7.7 and Figure 7.9: seed and germination

Soak Bengal gram seeds overnight, keep them moist for a day, cut them open, and
identify their parts.

[[figure:figure-7-9-germination]]

- **cotyledons:** store food
- **plumule:** develops into the future shoot
- **radicle:** develops into the future root
- **seed coat:** protects the embryo

Germination is the development of the seed embryo into a seedling under suitable
conditions, including water, air, and appropriate temperature.

## Five-mark answer frame

For "events after pollen lands on a suitable stigma":

`pollen germinates -> pollen tube grows through style -> male germ cell reaches ovule -> fertilisation forms zygote -> embryo forms -> ovule becomes seed -> ovary becomes fruit`

## Common mistakes

1. Saying anther contains ovules; it contains pollen grains.
2. Saying ovary becomes seed; ovary becomes fruit.
3. Calling pollination gamete fusion.
4. Forgetting that fertilisation occurs in an ovule, not on the stigma.
5. Using papaya as a bisexual flower.
