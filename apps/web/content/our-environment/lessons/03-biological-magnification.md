# Biological Magnification

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-13-biomagnification" />

Persistent non-biodegradable chemicals such as some pesticides enter soil or
water, are absorbed by producers, and pass through food chains.

Organisms cannot readily degrade or excrete them. A consumer eats many
organisms from the lower level, so concentration increases progressively at
successive trophic levels. This is **biological magnification**.

Example:

`water -> plankton -> fish -> fish-eating bird/human`

The highest trophic level receives the greatest concentration. Washing food
cannot always remove pesticide already present inside tissues.

## Full-mark distinction

- **10% law:** usable energy decreases upward.
- **Biomagnification:** concentration of a persistent pollutant increases
  upward.

Do not say the quantity of pesticide is “created” at higher levels. Its
concentration rises because it persists and consumers eat many contaminated
organisms.

## Reducing exposure and entry

Use persistent pesticides carefully/replace where possible, prevent industrial
and farm runoff, monitor residues, use integrated pest management, and enforce
safe disposal. Activity 13.3 asks students to debate pesticide residues and
their paths into food.
