# 11.6.2 Resistors in Parallel

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-11-7-parallel-resistors" />

Each resistor is connected across the same two points.

- same potential difference: `V=V1=V2=V3`;
- current divides: `I=I1+I2+I3`;
- `1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3`.

Equivalent parallel resistance is less than the smallest branch resistance.

For two resistors:

`Rp = R1R2/(R1+R2)`.

For `n` equal resistors: `Rp=R/n`.

Activity 11.6 verifies that total current is the sum of branch currents.

## Worked examples

### How many 176 Ω resistors draw 5 A at 220 V?

Required `Req=V/I=44Ω`. Since `176/n=44`, `n=4`.

### Three 6 Ω resistors to obtain 4 Ω

Put two in series: `12Ω`; connect this in parallel with 6 Ω:

`Req=(12×6)/(12+6)=4Ω`.

## Why domestic appliances use parallel

- each receives full supply voltage;
- independent switches and operation;
- failure of one branch does not break others;
- each appliance draws current according to its resistance/power.

## Quick plausibility checks

- series result must exceed largest resistor;
- parallel result must be below smallest;
- two equal resistors give `2R` series and `R/2` parallel.
