# Chapter 4: Globalisation and the Indian Economy

## Hook

Your phone may be designed in one country, use parts from several others, be assembled somewhere else and sold by a company working worldwide. This is not just trade; it is production organised across countries.

## Chapter goal

This chapter explains how multinational corporations spread production, how investment and trade connect markets, why technology and liberalisation speed up globalisation, what the World Trade Organisation does, why the effects in India are unequal, and what can make globalisation fairer.

## PRODUCTION ACROSS COUNTRIES

A multinational corporation, or MNC, owns or controls production in more than one country. MNCs choose locations where labour and other resources are available, production costs are lower, markets are nearby and government policies are favourable.

Earlier, most production stayed within national borders. Today, an MNC can divide design, components, assembly and sales among many countries.

### Spreading of Production by an MNC

This special box traces how a large company can locate different stages of production in different countries. Its key idea is control: the company coordinates decisions even when the work is spread across the world.

### LET’S WORK THIS OUT

## INTERLINKING PRODUCTION ACROSS COUNTRIES

MNCs link production in several ways:

- setting up factories or offices abroad;
- buying an existing local company;
- forming a partnership with a local company;
- placing orders with small producers and controlling quality, price and delivery;
- using local companies as suppliers.

Investment made by an MNC to buy assets such as land, buildings, machines or companies in another country is foreign investment. The purpose is to control production and earn profit.

## FOREIGN TRADE AND INTEGRATION OF MARKETS

Foreign trade gives producers a chance to sell outside their home market and gives buyers access to goods from other countries. Imports create competition between local and foreign producers. Prices, quality and choice in one market begin to affect another.

This linking of markets across countries is market integration.

## WHAT IS GLOBALISATION?

Globalisation is the process of rapid integration or interconnection among countries. It grows through foreign trade, foreign investment, movement of technology and increasingly connected production.

MNCs are central because they organise production internationally. Movement of people between countries also occurs, but restrictions mean it has not expanded as freely as goods, services and investment.

## FACTORS THAT HAVE ENABLED GLOBALISATION

### Technology

Improved transport has lowered the cost and time of moving goods. Telecommunications, computers and the internet let information travel instantly and make it possible to coordinate services across long distances.

### Containers for transport of goods

This special box explains that standard containers can move between ships, railways and trucks with less handling. They reduce transport cost and damage and make long-distance trade faster.

### Using IT in Globalisation

The box shows how information technology lets a service be produced in another country—for example, data processing, customer support or design—while remaining connected to the buyer.

### Liberalisation of foreign trade and foreign investment policy

A trade barrier is a restriction such as a tax or quota on imports. After Independence, India used barriers to protect new domestic producers from foreign competition. From 1991, the government removed many barriers to allow easier import, export and foreign investment. This policy change is liberalisation.

## WORLD TRADE ORGANISATION

The World Trade Organisation, or WTO, makes rules for international trade and seeks liberalisation. Its rules are supposed to apply to all members, yet developed countries have sometimes kept barriers or subsidies while asking developing countries to open markets.

## IMPACT OF GLOBALISATION IN INDIA

Globalisation has produced unequal results.

- Well-off urban consumers often receive more choice, improved quality and lower prices.
- Some Indian companies gain investment, technology and access to global markets.
- Successful large Indian companies may themselves become MNCs.
- New service jobs and supplier opportunities can appear.
- Small producers may lose sales when they cannot match large firms or cheap imports.
- Workers may face temporary jobs, long hours and pressure on wages as firms try to lower costs.

## THE STRUGGLE FOR A FAIR GLOBALISATION

Fair globalisation would create opportunities for all and share benefits better. Government can enforce labour laws, support small producers, use trade and investment barriers when necessary, and work with other developing countries for fairer WTO rules. Employers and MNCs can offer safe conditions, fair wages and stable work. Workers and people’s organisations can act collectively.

## SUMMING UP

Globalisation rapidly integrates countries through trade and investment. MNCs, technology and liberalisation organise production across borders. Benefits have reached many consumers and stronger producers, while many small producers and workers have suffered. Fair rules and public action are therefore essential.

## EXERCISES: Questions and answers

### 1. What do you understand by globalisation? Explain in your own words.

Answer: Globalisation is the rapid integration and interconnection of countries through foreign trade, foreign investment, technology and production organised across borders.

### 2. What were the reasons for putting barriers to foreign trade and foreign investment by the Indian government? Why did it wish to remove these barriers?

Answer: Barriers protected new Indian producers from strong foreign competition after Independence. From 1991, many were removed to increase competition, attract investment, gain technology and connect India more closely with world markets.

### 3. How would flexibility in labour laws help companies?

Answer: It lets companies change worker numbers, use shorter contracts and adjust hours as orders change, reducing costs. For workers, this can also mean less security and fewer benefits.

### 4. What are the various ways in which MNCs set up, control or produce in other countries?

Answer: MNCs set up new units, buy local companies, form partnerships, place orders with small producers, use local suppliers and control technology, quality, price and delivery.

### 5. Why do developed countries want developing countries to liberalise their trade and investment? What do you think should the developing countries demand in return?

Answer: Developed countries seek wider markets and easier investment for their firms. Developing countries should demand equal rules, access to developed markets, reduction of unfair subsidies, and protection for development needs and workers.

### 6. “The impact of globalisation has not been uniform.” Explain this statement.

Answer: Many well-off consumers, skilled workers and large companies gained choice, jobs or markets. Many small producers and workers faced lost business, low wages, temporary work and insecurity. Benefits and costs were therefore uneven.

### 7. How has liberalisation of trade and investment policies helped the globalisation process?

Answer: Removing taxes, quotas and investment restrictions made it easier for goods, services and capital to cross borders. MNCs could invest and connect Indian production with global markets more easily.

### 8. How does foreign trade lead to integration of markets across countries? Explain with an example other than those given here.

Answer: Imports and exports bring producers from different countries into the same market, so price, quality and demand become connected. For example, imported smartphones compete with phones assembled in India while Indian components may be exported to other factories.

### 9. Globalisation will continue in the future. Can you imagine what the world would be like twenty years from now? Give reasons for your answer.

Answer: Production and services may become even more digitally connected, with faster communication and wider global supply chains. The result will also depend on rules for workers, the environment, technology and fair trade.

### 10. Supposing you find two people arguing: One is saying globalisation has hurt our country’s development. The other is telling, globalisation is helping India develop. How would you respond to these arguments?

Answer: Both describe part of the evidence. Globalisation has widened choice, investment, technology and some jobs, but it has also hurt some workers and small producers. A balanced response asks who gains, who loses and how policy can share benefits fairly.

### 11. Fill in the blanks. Indian buyers have a greater choice of goods than they did two decades back. This is closely associated with the process of ______________. Markets in India are selling goods produced in many other countries. This means there is increasing ______________ with other countries. Moreover, the rising number of brands that we see in the markets might be produced by MNCs in India. MNCs are investing in India because _____________ ___________________________________________. While consumers have more choices in the market, the effect of rising _______________ and ______________ has meant greater _________________ among the producers.

Answer: globalisation; integration; they can gain access to a large market and favourable production conditions; competition; imports; competition.

### 12. Match the following. (i) MNCs buy at cheap rates from small producers (a) Automobiles (ii) Quotas and taxes on imports are used to regulate trade (b) Garments, footwear, sports items (iii) Indian companies who have invested abroad (c) Call centres (iv) IT has helped in spreading of production of services (d) Tata Motors, Infosys, Ranbaxy (v) Several MNCs have invested in setting up factories in India for production (e) Trade barriers

Answer: (i)–(b), (ii)–(e), (iii)–(d), (iv)–(c), (v)–(a).

### 13. Choose the most appropriate option. (i) The past two decades of globalisation has seen rapid movements in (a) goods, services and people between countries. (b) goods, services and investments between countries. (c) goods, investments and people between countries. (ii) The most common route for investments by MNCs in countries around the world is to (a) set up new factories. (b) buy existing local companies. (c) form partnerships with local companies. (iii) Globalisation has led to improvement in living conditions (a) of all the people (b) of people in the developed countries (c) of workers in the developing countries (d) none of the above

## Common mistakes

- Defining globalisation as foreign trade only.
- Treating every foreign company as an MNC without checking cross-country control.
- Confusing foreign trade with foreign investment.
- Saying liberalisation removed every barrier.
- Claiming globalisation helped everyone or harmed everyone in the same way.

## Board tips

- Explain a process as a chain: MNC decision → cross-border production or trade → effect on a named group.
- For “not uniform”, write at least one gaining group and one group under pressure.
- Distinguish investment, import and outsourcing with separate examples.
- In WTO answers, discuss fairness using the textbook subsidy example; avoid unsupported predictions.

## One-minute revision

- MNCs own or control production in several countries.
- Foreign investment creates or buys assets abroad; foreign trade links markets.
- MNCs use factories, purchases, partnerships and contracts to organise production.
- Transport, IT and liberalisation speed up globalisation.
- WTO promotes trade rules, but fairness is disputed.
- Consumers and strong firms may gain while small producers and insecure workers may lose.
- Fair globalisation needs rules, protection and collective action.
