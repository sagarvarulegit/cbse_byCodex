# 5.4.1 Transportation in human beings

Transportation moves materials between specialised organs and body cells. In humans, the circulatory system carries oxygen, carbon dioxide, food, hormones, and wastes.

The system has three main parts: heart, blood, and blood vessels.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- trace blood flow through the four chambers of the heart
- explain double circulation
- compare arteries, veins, and capillaries
- explain blood pressure, platelets, and lymph
- identify NCERT Figures 5.10 and 5.11
- avoid common mistakes about oxygenated and deoxygenated blood

## Need for transport

In multicellular organisms, food and oxygen are taken in at specialised places, but every cell needs them.

Similarly, wastes are produced in cells but removed by specialised organs.

So the body needs transport from:
- lungs to cells
- digestive system to cells
- cells to excretory organs
- endocrine glands to target organs

## The heart as a pump

The heart is a muscular organ about the size of a fist.

It has four chambers:
- right atrium
- right ventricle
- left atrium
- left ventricle

The left side handles oxygen-rich blood. The right side handles carbon dioxide-rich blood.

This separation is important because mammals and birds need efficient oxygen supply for high energy demand.

## Blood flow through the heart

Follow the pathway slowly:

1. Oxygen-rich blood from lungs enters the left atrium.
2. Left atrium contracts and sends blood to the left ventricle.
3. Left ventricle pumps blood to the body.
4. Carbon dioxide-rich blood from the body enters the right atrium.
5. Right atrium sends blood to the right ventricle.
6. Right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs for oxygenation.

Valves prevent backward flow when chambers contract.

Ventricles have thicker muscular walls than atria because they pump blood to lungs or body.

## Double circulation

Double circulation means blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle:
- once between heart and lungs
- once between heart and rest of body

It keeps oxygen-rich and carbon dioxide-rich blood separate.

Fishes have two-chambered hearts and single circulation. Amphibians and many reptiles have three-chambered hearts and tolerate some mixing. Birds and mammals have stronger separation.

## Blood vessels

Arteries:
- carry blood away from heart
- have thick, elastic walls
- carry blood under high pressure

Veins:
- bring blood back to heart
- have thinner walls
- have valves to prevent backward flow

Capillaries:
- are the smallest vessels
- have one-cell-thick walls
- allow exchange of materials between blood and cells

Important exception:
Do not write "arteries always carry oxygenated blood." Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from heart to lungs.

## Platelets and clotting

If blood vessels leak after injury, blood loss must be reduced.

Platelet cells help plug leaks by helping blood clot at the point of injury.

Memory line:
`Platelets patch the pipe.`

## Lymph

Lymph is also called tissue fluid.

Some plasma, proteins, and blood cells escape through capillary walls into spaces between cells. This forms lymph.

Lymph:
- is colourless
- contains less protein than plasma
- carries absorbed fat from intestine
- drains excess fluid back into blood through lymph vessels

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Double circulation

Question:
Describe double circulation and why it is necessary.

Answer:
Double circulation means blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle: once through the lungs and once through the body. It keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate, giving efficient oxygen supply.

### Example 2: Valves

Question:
Why are valves present in the heart and veins?

Answer:
Valves prevent backward flow of blood and keep blood moving in the correct direction.

### Example 3: Capillaries

Question:
Why are capillary walls thin?

Answer:
Thin walls allow exchange of gases, nutrients, and wastes between blood and surrounding cells.

### Example 4: Platelets

Question:
What is the role of platelets?

Answer:
Platelets help blood clot at injured points and reduce blood loss.

## Common mistakes

1. Reversing the left and right side of the heart.
2. Saying all arteries carry oxygenated blood.
3. Forgetting valves in blood-flow answers.
4. Writing that capillaries have thick walls.
5. Mixing lymph with urine or digestive juice.

## One-minute revision

Human transport uses heart, blood, and blood vessels. The right side sends carbon dioxide-rich blood to lungs; the left side sends oxygen-rich blood to the body. Double circulation keeps the two streams separate. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins return it, and capillaries allow exchange. Platelets help clotting, and lymph returns tissue fluid and carries absorbed fat.
