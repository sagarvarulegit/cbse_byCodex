# 4.2.2-4.2.4 Hydrocarbons and homologous series

Hydrocarbons are compounds made only of carbon and hydrogen. They may be saturated or unsaturated.

This lesson connects two textbook ideas: first, how to read a hydrocarbon from its bonds; second, how similar compounds can be arranged into a family called a homologous series.

## 4.2.2 Chains, Branches and Rings - hydrocarbon families

A hydrocarbon contains only:
- carbon
- hydrogen

Examples:
- methane, `CH4`
- ethane, `C2H6`
- ethene, `C2H4`
- ethyne, `C2H2`

If a compound contains oxygen, chlorine, nitrogen, sulphur, or any other element, it is still a carbon compound but it is not a hydrocarbon. For example, ethanol `C2H5OH` is not a hydrocarbon because it contains oxygen.

## Saturated hydrocarbons

Saturated hydrocarbons have only single bonds between carbon atoms.

They are called alkanes.

Why "saturated"? Because each carbon has used its four bonds in a way that allows the maximum possible hydrogens for that open chain. There is no double or triple bond waiting to open up and add more atoms.

Examples:
- methane, `CH4`
- ethane, `C2H6`
- propane, `C3H8`
- butane, `C4H10`

General formula for alkanes:

`CnH2n+2`

Use it carefully:
- for methane, `n = 1`, so formula is `CH4`
- for ethane, `n = 2`, so formula is `C2H6`
- for propane, `n = 3`, so formula is `C3H8`

## Unsaturated hydrocarbons

Unsaturated hydrocarbons have at least one double or triple bond between carbon atoms.

Unsaturated compounds have fewer hydrogen atoms than the corresponding saturated compound with the same number of carbon atoms. The missing hydrogens are a clue that a multiple bond is present.

Alkenes have a double bond:

`C=C`

Example:

Ethene, `C2H4`

Alkynes have a triple bond:

`C#C`

Example:

Ethyne, `C2H2`

Quick comparison:

| Two-carbon compound | Bond between carbon atoms | Formula | Family |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ethane | single | `C2H6` | alkane |
| Ethene | double | `C2H4` | alkene |
| Ethyne | triple | `C2H2` | alkyne |

## 4.2.4 Homologous Series

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with:
- the same functional group
- the same general formula
- similar chemical properties
- successive members differing by `CH2`

Example: alkanes

| Compound | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Methane | `CH4` |
| Ethane | `C2H6` |
| Propane | `C3H8` |
| Butane | `C4H10` |

Each next member has one extra carbon and two extra hydrogens.

Memory line:
`Same family, same group, next member adds CH2.`

Why does `CH2` keep appearing? When a carbon chain becomes longer by one carbon atom, the chain usually gains one carbon and two hydrogens while keeping the same family pattern. That is why methane `CH4`, ethane `C2H6`, propane `C3H8`, and butane `C4H10` move in regular steps.

The chemical properties stay similar because the same functional group or same bond family controls the main reactions. But physical properties change gradually because the molecules get heavier. The textbook points out that melting point, boiling point, and solubility show gradation as molecular mass increases.

## How to identify the family

Use this three-step scan:

1. Check elements. Only carbon and hydrogen means hydrocarbon.
2. Check the carbon-carbon bond. Single means alkane, double means alkene, triple means alkyne.
3. Check the formula pattern if needed.

Examples:
- `C3H8` follows `CnH2n+2`, so it is an alkane.
- `C3H6` follows `CnH2n`, so it can be an alkene or a ring compound at this level; if a double bond is shown, call it an alkene.
- `C2H2` follows the alkyne pattern and has a triple bond, so it is ethyne.

## Questions and answers

### 4.2 continued - Hydrocarbons and homologous series

Question:
What is a homologous series?

Answer:
A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with the same functional group and successive members differing by CH2.

Question:
What is the difference between saturated and unsaturated compounds?

Answer:
Saturated compounds have only single bonds between carbon atoms, while unsaturated compounds have at least one double or triple bond.

Question:
Why do successive members of a homologous series differ by CH2?

Answer:
Each next member has one more carbon atom and two more hydrogen atoms than the previous one, so the difference is CH2.

Think it over:
- A small change in bonding can change both the name of a compound and its chemical behaviour.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Saturated or unsaturated

Question:
Is ethene saturated or unsaturated?

Answer:
Ethene is unsaturated because it has a carbon-carbon double bond.

### Example 2: Homologous series

Question:
What is the difference between ethane `C2H6` and propane `C3H8`?

Answer:
The difference is `CH2`, so they are consecutive members of the same homologous series.

### Example 3: Hydrocarbon or not

Question:
Is ethanol `C2H5OH` a hydrocarbon?

Answer:
No. A hydrocarbon contains only carbon and hydrogen. Ethanol contains oxygen also.

### Example 4: Family from formula

Question:
Which family does `C4H10` belong to?

Answer:
It matches the alkane formula `CnH2n+2`. For `n = 4`, `2n + 2 = 10`, so `C4H10` is an alkane.

## Common mistakes

1. Calling ethene an alkane.
2. Forgetting that saturated means only single carbon-carbon bonds.
3. Saying homologous series means compounds with identical formulae.
4. Mixing up ethane, ethene, and ethyne.

## Board tips

- Underline the bond type when classifying a compound.
- For homologous series answers, mention both "same functional group" and "difference of `CH2`".
- Write formulas carefully: ethane `C2H6`, ethene `C2H4`, ethyne `C2H2`.

## One-minute revision

Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen. Alkanes are saturated and have single bonds. Alkenes and alkynes are unsaturated. A homologous series has similar compounds where each next member differs by `CH2`.
