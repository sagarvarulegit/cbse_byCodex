# NCERT Exercises and Chapter Revision

## Exercise 6.1

This three-question set checks the definition of similar figures. Use Figures 6.7-6.8 to test both conditions: equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.

## Exercise 6.2

The ten questions apply BPT and its converse through Figures 6.17-6.21 and several proof diagrams.

For each proof:

1. identify the triangle being used
2. state the parallel line or proportional division
3. write the BPT ratio in the correct order
4. transform it only after the theorem is on the page

The exercise includes a trapezium-diagonal result, midpoint results, a parallelogram construction and a quadrilateral-diagonal proportion.

## Exercise 6.3

The sixteen questions use AAA/AA, SSS and SAS. The printed figures 6.34-6.41 are part of the questions and must be read with their labels.

High-value proof habits:

- For Figure 6.29, use alternate angles and vertically opposite angles to prove $\triangle POQ\sim\triangle SOR$.
- For an intersecting-line diagram, vertical angles often supply the included angle for SAS.
- For a shadow question, the ground and vertical objects create corresponding right angles.
- For medians, replace a full side with twice its half only after naming the midpoint relation.

## Common mistakes

- assuming a line is parallel because it looks parallel
- writing a ratio with numerator and denominator from different correspondence orders
- claiming congruence when only similarity has been shown

## Board-answer checklist

- Given facts copied accurately
- Angle or side correspondence stated
- Correct similarity criterion named
- Required result follows from corresponding parts

## One-minute revision

Most triangle proofs become short after the correspondence is chosen correctly.
