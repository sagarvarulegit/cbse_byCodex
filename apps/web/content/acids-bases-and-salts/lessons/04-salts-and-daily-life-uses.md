# Salts and daily-life uses

Salts are formed when acids and bases react. This chapter then studies important salts made from common salt.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- explain how salts are formed
- connect common salt with useful compounds
- list uses of bleaching powder, baking soda, washing soda, and plaster of Paris
- understand water of crystallisation
- revise Chapter 2 reactions as a connected map

## Salt formation

A salt is an ionic compound often formed by neutralisation.

General form:

`acid + base -> salt + water`

Example:

`HCl + NaOH -> NaCl + H2O`

Sodium chloride is common salt.

## Common salt as a raw material

Common salt is not only used in food. It is also a raw material for important chemicals:
- sodium hydroxide
- bleaching powder
- baking soda
- washing soda

## Bleaching powder

Bleaching powder is used:
- for bleaching cotton and linen
- as a disinfectant for drinking water
- in chemical industries as an oxidising agent

It is prepared from chlorine and dry slaked lime.

## Baking soda

Baking soda is sodium hydrogencarbonate.

Uses:
- baking powder
- antacid
- soda-acid fire extinguishers

On heating, it gives carbon dioxide, which helps cakes and bread rise.

## Washing soda

Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate.

Uses:
- cleaning clothes
- softening hard water
- glass, soap, and paper industries

## Plaster of Paris

Plaster of Paris is calcium sulphate hemihydrate.

Uses:
- plaster casts for fractured bones
- statues and decorative items
- making smooth surfaces

It hardens when mixed with water.

## Water of crystallisation

Some salts contain fixed water molecules in their crystals. This is called water of crystallisation.

Examples:
- copper sulphate crystals are blue because of water of crystallisation
- washing soda is `Na2CO3.10H2O`

## Chapter reaction map

Keep these reaction families together:
- acid + metal -> salt + hydrogen
- acid + carbonate -> salt + water + carbon dioxide
- acid + base -> salt + water
- acid + metallic oxide -> salt + water
- acids and bases show behaviour through ions in water

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Common salt derivative

Which compound is used as an antacid?

Answer:
Baking soda.

### Example 2: Water of crystallisation

Why are copper sulphate crystals blue?

Answer:
They contain water of crystallisation.

### Example 3: Reaction family

What products form when an acid reacts with a carbonate?

Answer:
Salt, water, and carbon dioxide.

## Common mistakes

1. Calling every salt "common salt".
2. Mixing baking soda and washing soda.
3. Forgetting the water in plaster of Paris hardening.
4. Treating water of crystallisation as ordinary wetness.

## Board tips

- Learn one use for each common salt derivative.
- Write formulae only when you are confident.
- In long answers, connect salt preparation with daily use.
- For water of crystallisation, use copper sulphate as the easiest example.

## One-minute revision

- Neutralisation forms salt and water.
- Common salt is a raw material for useful compounds.
- Baking soda helps release carbon dioxide.
- Washing soda softens hard water.
- Plaster of Paris hardens with water.
- Water of crystallisation is fixed water in crystals.
