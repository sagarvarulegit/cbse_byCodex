# Sectors of the Indian Economy

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- classify activities into primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors
- understand GDP and final goods
- explain changes in sectoral importance
- distinguish organised/unorganised and public/private sectors
- explain employment issues and protection of workers

## 1) sectors of economic activities

Primary sector uses natural resources directly. Examples: farming, fishing, mining, forestry.

Secondary sector transforms raw materials into goods. Examples: manufacturing, construction.

Tertiary sector provides services. Examples: transport, banking, teaching, healthcare, trade, communication.

## 2) final goods and GDP

GDP is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a year.

Only final goods are counted to avoid double counting. Intermediate goods are already included in the value of final goods.

## 3) comparing sectors

Sectors can be compared by their share in GDP and their share in employment.

A sector may produce high value but employ fewer people. Another sector may employ many people but produce lower value per worker.

## 4) changes in India

As economies develop, the importance of sectors changes.

In India, the tertiary sector has become the largest producing sector because of growth in transport, communication, banking, education, health, IT, and trade.

But many people still depend on agriculture, so employment has not shifted as fast as production.

## 5) underemployment

Underemployment happens when people appear employed but their work does not use their full time or ability.

Disguised unemployment is common in agriculture, where more people work on land than actually needed.

## 6) organised and unorganised sectors

Organised sector jobs follow government rules, regular wages, fixed hours, paid leave, and social security.

Unorganised sector jobs are often insecure, low paid, and without benefits. Workers need protection.

## 7) public and private sectors

Public sector is owned and managed by the government. It provides essential services and works for public welfare.

Private sector is owned by individuals or companies and usually works for profit.

Some services, like health, education, roads, and electricity, may need government support because they affect everyone.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Primary: natural resource use.
- Secondary: manufacturing and construction.
- Tertiary: services.
- GDP counts final goods and services.
- Tertiary sector is the largest producing sector in India.
- Unorganised workers need protection.

## common mistakes students make

1. Placing mining in secondary sector.
2. Counting intermediate goods in GDP.
3. Confusing underemployment with unemployment.
4. Assuming all government jobs are tertiary and all private jobs are secondary.

## board tips

- Classify activities by what the worker actually does.
- For sector comparison, use GDP and employment separately.
- In unorganised sector answers, mention insecurity and lack of benefits.
