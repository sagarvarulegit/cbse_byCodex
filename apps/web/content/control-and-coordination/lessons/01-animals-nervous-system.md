# 6.1 Animals - Nervous System

Living things do not just move because they are alive. They also detect changes around them and answer them in a controlled way. In animals, that job is done mainly by nervous tissue and muscles.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- explain why control and coordination are needed in animals
- identify receptors, neurons, synapses, and effectors
- trace the path of a nerve impulse through a neuron
- connect taste and smell to the first NCERT activity and Figure 6.1

## 6.1 Animals - Nervous System

Animals use a nervous system to detect a stimulus, carry the message, and produce the correct response. The message travels as an electrical impulse in nerve tissue and then as a chemical signal across the synapse.

Information from the environment is detected by specialised tips of nerve cells. These receptors are usually found in sense organs. Gustatory receptors detect taste. Olfactory receptors detect smell.

### Neuron and impulse

A neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system. Information is acquired at the dendritic tip, travels through the cell body and along the axon, and then leaves the axon ending for onward transmission.

Impulse path:

`receptor -> dendrite -> cell body -> axon -> axon ending -> synapse -> next cell`

The nerve impulse is electrical inside the neuron. At the end of the axon, chemicals are released. These chemicals cross the synapse and start a new impulse in the dendrite of the next neuron. The same idea allows impulses to pass from neurons to muscle cells or gland cells.

## Solved examples

### Example 1

Question:
Why does food taste less clear when your nose is blocked?

Answer:
Taste and smell work together. When the nose is blocked, olfactory receptors cannot help fully, so the brain gets less complete information about flavour.

### Example 2

Question:
Why is the synapse important?

Answer:
It allows one neuron to pass the signal to the next cell using chemicals, so information can continue through the nervous system.

### Example 3

Question:
What is the role of receptors?

Answer:
Receptors detect the stimulus first and start the message that will become a nerve impulse.

## Common mistakes

1. Thinking all movement means growth.
2. Mixing up receptor and effector.
3. Saying the impulse jumps directly from one neuron to the next without a synapse.
4. Forgetting that smell and taste work together.

## Board tips

- In short answers, write the sequence stimulus -> receptor -> impulse -> response.
- Name gustatory and olfactory receptors together when taste and smell are discussed.
- Use the words dendrite, axon, synapse, and impulse in neuron answers.

## One-minute revision

Animals detect changes through receptors and respond through nervous tissue and muscles. A neuron carries an electrical impulse from dendrite to axon. At the synapse, chemicals pass the signal to the next cell. Taste and smell are closely linked because gustatory and olfactory receptors work together.
