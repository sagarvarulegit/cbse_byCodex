## 1) similar figures and similar triangles

Two figures are similar if:
- their corresponding angles are equal
- their corresponding sides are proportional

For triangles, similarity is easier to test than congruence.

### important difference
- congruent triangles: same shape and same size
- similar triangles: same shape, size may differ

## 2) Basic Proportionality Theorem

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle and intersects the other two sides, then it divides those two sides in the same ratio.

In triangle `ABC`, if `DE || BC` with `D` on `AB` and `E` on `AC`, then:

`AD/DB = AE/EC`

### meaning in plain words
A line parallel to one side creates proportional pieces on the other two sides.

### converse
If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then that line is parallel to the third side.

## 3) similarity criteria

### AA similarity
If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.

### SAS similarity
If one angle is equal and the including sides are proportional, the triangles are similar.

### SSS similarity
If corresponding sides are proportional, the triangles are similar.

### board tip
Always write corresponding vertices in the correct order.

## 4) areas of similar triangles

If two triangles are similar, then:

`Area of first triangle / Area of second triangle = (corresponding side)^2`

That means the ratio of areas is the square of the ratio of corresponding sides.

### example
If side ratio is `2:3`, then area ratio is `4:9`.

This is a very common board question.

## 5) Pythagoras theorem

In a right triangle:

`(hypotenuse)^2 = (base)^2 + (perpendicular)^2`

If triangle `ABC` is right-angled at `B`, then:

`AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2`

### solved example
If sides are `6 cm`, `8 cm`, and `10 cm`, check:

`10^2 = 6^2 + 8^2`

`100 = 36 + 64 = 100`

So the theorem holds.

## 6) converse of Pythagoras theorem

If in a triangle:

`(largest side)^2 = (sum of squares of the other two sides)`

then the triangle is right-angled.

Example:
Sides `5`, `12`, `13`

`13^2 = 5^2 + 12^2`

`169 = 25 + 144`

So the triangle is right-angled.

## 7) exam-friendly summary notes

### must-know facts
- Similar triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
- If a line is parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides proportionally.
- AA, SAS, and SSS are the main similarity criteria.
- Ratio of areas of similar triangles is the square of corresponding side ratio.
- Pythagoras theorem works in right triangles only.

### one-line revision points
- similar means same shape
- BPT means parallel line gives equal ratios
- area ratio is squared side ratio

## 8) common mistakes students make

1. Mixing up congruent and similar triangles.
2. Writing side ratios in the wrong order.
3. Forgetting that BPT needs a parallel line.
4. Using Pythagoras theorem in a triangle that is not right-angled.
5. Using side ratio directly for area ratio without squaring it.

## 9) board tips

- Draw the figure neatly and mark equal angles or parallel lines clearly.
- In proof questions, write the reason beside each statement when possible.
- When using similarity, first state which triangles are similar.
- In Pythagoras questions, identify the hypotenuse first.

## 11) solved practice set

### question 1
If `DE || BC` in triangle `ABC`, what relation is formed?

Answer:
`AD/DB = AE/EC`

### question 2
If two triangles have angle-angle equality, what can you say?

Answer:
The triangles are similar by AA criterion.

### question 3
Sides of a triangle are `9`, `12`, `15`. Is it right-angled?

Answer:
`15^2 = 225`
`9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225`

Yes, it is right-angled.
