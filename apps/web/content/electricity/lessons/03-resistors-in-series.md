# 11.6.1 Resistors in Series

Resistors are in series when connected end-to-end in one path.

- same current through each: `I=I1=I2=I3`;
- supply voltage divides: `V=V1+V2+V3`;
- using `V=IR`: `Rs=R1+R2+R3`.

Equivalent series resistance is greater than every individual resistance.

Activities 11.4–11.5 compare the current and potential drops and verify these
relationships.

## Worked example

`0.2+0.3+0.4+0.5+12 = 13.4Ω` across `9V`.

`I=9/13.4=0.672A`. The same 0.672 A flows through the 12 Ω resistor.

## Three 6 Ω resistors to obtain 9 Ω

Put two in parallel: `6||6=3Ω`; connect this 3 Ω equivalent in series with the
third 6 Ω: `3+6=9Ω`.

## Why domestic appliances are not in series

- voltage divides, so appliances do not receive rated mains voltage;
- one switch/failure breaks the entire circuit;
- appliances cannot be operated independently;
- equivalent resistance rises and available current falls.

## Board shortcut

For equal resistors `R` in series: `Req=nR`.
