# 12.1-12.4 Surface Areas and Volumes

When solids are joined, hidden faces stop contributing to outside area, but their space still contributes to volume. That one distinction drives the whole chapter.

## 12.1 Introduction

Figures 12.1-12.3 review the prescribed solids and show combined objects such as a tanker and test tube.

Useful prior formulas:

| Solid | Curved/lateral area | Total area | Volume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cylinder | $2\pi rh$ | $2\pi r(h+r)$ | $\pi r^2h$ |
| cone | $\pi rl$ | $\pi r(l+r)$ | $\frac13\pi r^2h$ |
| sphere | $4\pi r^2$ | same | $\frac43\pi r^3$ |
| hemisphere | $2\pi r^2$ | $3\pi r^2$ | $\frac23\pi r^3$ |
| cuboid | $2h(l+b)$ lateral | $2(lb+bh+hl)$ | $lbh$ |

For a cone, $l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}$.

## 12.2 Surface Area of a Combination of Solids

Only exposed surfaces count. A joined circular face is hidden and must not be counted twice.

Figures 12.4-12.5 show a cylinder with hemispherical ends and a cone joined to a hemisphere.

**Example 1** uses Figure 12.6, a cone on a hemisphere. Its exposed area is CSA of cone plus CSA of hemisphere.

**Example 2** uses Figure 12.7, a hemisphere on a cube. Subtract the covered circular area from the cube's exposed surface, then add the hemisphere's curved area.

## 12.3 Volume of a Combination of Solids

For joined solids, add volumes. For a hollow, depression or removed part, subtract its volume.

**Example 5** uses Figure 12.12, a shed shaped as a cuboid with a half-cylinder roof. Machine and worker volume is subtracted to find air volume.

**Example 6** uses Figure 12.13, a cylindrical glass with a hemispherical bulge. The bulge reduces capacity.

**Example 7** uses Figure 12.14 to compare the volume of a hemisphere-plus-cone toy with a circumscribing cylinder.

## 12.4 Summary

- Surface area counts exposed boundary only.
- Volume counts occupied space.
- Add joined parts and subtract removed or hollow parts.
- Frustum is not a section of the current textbook chapter or active board scope.

## Common mistakes

- adding total surface areas of solids without removing hidden faces
- subtracting a hollow's curved area when that inner surface is exposed
- using diameter as radius
- mixing cubic and square units

## Board tips

- Draw the component solids and mark hidden faces.
- Write one area or volume expression before substituting numbers.
- Convert every length to one unit first.

## Quick practice

1. A hemisphere of radius $r$ sits on a cylinder of the same radius. Which circular face is hidden?
2. Find the volume of a cylinder of radius $3$ cm and height $5$ cm.
3. Does a hemispherical depression increase or decrease exposed surface area?

## Answer check

1. The common circular base between the two solids.
2. $45\pi\text{ cm}^3$.
3. It adds an exposed inner curved surface, though it removes material volume.

## One-minute revision

Area asks, "What can be touched or painted?" Volume asks, "How much space or material is present?"

## Comic recap

- Hidden face: "Do not count me outside."
- Exposed hollow: "My inner curve still counts as surface."
- Volume: "Join means add; remove means subtract."
- Unit checker: "Area is square; volume is cubic."
