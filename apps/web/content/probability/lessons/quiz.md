# Probability chapter quiz

## 1. Theoretical probability

For equally likely outcomes, $P(E)$ is:

1. total outcomes/favourable outcomes
2. favourable outcomes/total outcomes
3. favourable outcomes $+$ total outcomes
4. always $1/2$

Answer: 2
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 14.1 aligned
Explanation: Probability is the fraction of equally likely sample points that favour the event.

## 2. Complement

If $P(E)=0.37$, then $P(\text{not }E)$ is:

1. $0.37$
2. $0.53$
3. $0.63$
4. $1.37$

Answer: 3
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 14.1 aligned
Explanation: Complementary probabilities add to $1$, so $1-0.37=0.63$.

## 3. At least one head

Two fair coins are tossed. The probability of getting at least one head is:

1. $1/4$
2. $1/2$
3. $3/4$
4. $1$

Answer: 3
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 14.1 aligned
Explanation: Of $HH,HT,TH,TT$, three outcomes contain at least one head.

## 4. Playing cards

A card is drawn from a well-shuffled standard deck. The probability of drawing a king is:

1. $1/52$
2. $1/13$
3. $4/13$
4. $1/4$

Answer: 2
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 14.1 aligned
Explanation: There are $4$ kings among $52$ cards, so $4/52=1/13$.

## 5. Two dice sample space

When two distinguishable fair dice are thrown, the number of equally likely ordered outcomes is:

1. $6$
2. $12$
3. $21$
4. $36$

Answer: 4
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Figure 14.3 aligned
Explanation: Each of $6$ results on the first die pairs with $6$ results on the second.

## 6. Sum of seven

The probability that the sum on two fair dice is $7$ equals:

1. $1/12$
2. $1/6$
3. $1/4$
4. $1/3$

Answer: 2
Topic: ncert-exercises-and-revision
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Exercise 14.1 aligned
Explanation: Six ordered pairs sum to $7$, so the probability is $6/36=1/6$.

## 7. Probability bounds

Which statement is always true for an event $E$?

1. $P(E)<0$
2. $0\le P(E)\le1$
3. $P(E)>1$
4. $P(E)=1/2$

Answer: 2
Topic: core-notes
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT Section 14.1 aligned
Explanation: Probability ranges from impossible $(0)$ to certain $(1)$.

## 8. Without replacement

When an object is selected and not replaced before a second selection, what changes?

1. Neither the total nor favourable count
2. The second selection's sample space
3. The first selection's probability after it happens
4. The definition of probability

Answer: 2
Topic: ncert-exercises-and-revision
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Exercise 14.1 aligned
Explanation: Removing the first object changes the available total and may change the favourable count for the second draw.
