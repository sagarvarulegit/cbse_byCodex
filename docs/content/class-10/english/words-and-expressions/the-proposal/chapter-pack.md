# Words and Expressions Unit 9 - The Proposal

## chapter goal

By the end of this workbook unit, a student should be able to:
- use facial expressions and non-verbal communication in drama discussion
- discuss farce, anger, property pride, and relationships
- read passages on anger control
- practise vocabulary, grammar, editing, speaking, writing, and project work

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jewe209..pdf`: facial expression activity, discussion of Chekhov's farce, anger and quarrel prompts, reading comprehension on anger control, vocabulary, grammar, editing, listening/speaking, writing, and project work.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Facial expressions
2. Play discussion
3. Farce and quarrel
4. Anger control reading
5. Vocabulary of emotions
6. Grammar and editing
7. Speaking, writing, and project work

## 1) facial expressions

Drama uses non-verbal communication: face, gesture, pause, tone, and movement.

The unit begins by identifying emotions such as happiness, sadness, anger, surprise, fear, and disgust.

## 2) play discussion

Students discuss how "The Proposal" mocks romance and marriage among property-proud people.

The key issue is that land and dogs become more important than patience and understanding.

## 3) farce and anger

The play is a farce because characters overreact, quarrel, contradict, and lose control over petty matters.

The workbook extends this into anger management and relationship skills.

## 4) reading skill

Read anger-control passages for causes, effects, examples, and advice.

Separate the problem from the solution.

## 5) vocabulary

Vocabulary fields include emotion, expression, quarrel, patience, contradiction, anger, calmness, and relationship.

## 6) speaking and writing

Role-play should use voice and expression, not just words.

Writing may include advice, dialogue, paragraph, or reflection on managing anger.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Facial expressions support drama.
- "The Proposal" is a farce about quarrels and pride.
- Anger control needs listening and patience.
- Role-play requires expression and timing.
- Writing should connect emotion with behaviour.

## common mistakes students make

1. Reading drama without expression.
2. Treating the play as serious romance.
3. Writing about anger without solutions.
4. Ignoring non-verbal communication.

## board tips

- In drama tasks, mark emotion beside each dialogue.
- For anger-control answers, write cause + effect + solution.
- Use examples from Lomov and Natalya's quarrels.

## short answer practice

1. Why are facial expressions important in a play?
2. How is "The Proposal" a farce?
3. What causes the characters to quarrel?
4. How can anger be managed?
