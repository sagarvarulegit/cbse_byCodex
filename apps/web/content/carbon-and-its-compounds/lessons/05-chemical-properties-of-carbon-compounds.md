# Chemical properties of carbon compounds

Carbon compounds show important reactions such as combustion, oxidation, addition, and substitution.

This lesson is about reaction patterns. A good way to study it is to ask: what changes in the molecule? Does oxygen get added? Does hydrogen add across a double bond? Does one atom replace another? That question tells you the reaction type.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- describe combustion of carbon compounds
- explain oxidation of alcohols
- define addition reaction
- define substitution reaction
- connect each reaction with one board-level example

## Combustion

Combustion is burning in oxygen.

Most carbon compounds burn to form:
- carbon dioxide
- water
- heat
- light

Example:

`CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O + heat + light`

For ethanol:

`CH3CH2OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O + heat and light`

If oxygen is limited, carbon compounds may burn with a sooty flame because unburnt carbon particles are produced.

Complete combustion needs enough oxygen. With enough oxygen, carbon becomes carbon dioxide and hydrogen becomes water. If oxygen is not enough, some carbon does not fully burn. It appears as soot, a black deposit of carbon.

That is why the bottom of a cooking vessel can become black when the air holes of a gas or kerosene stove are blocked. The fuel is not getting enough oxygen-rich air, so it burns incompletely.

The textbook also connects carbon fuels with pollution. Coal and petroleum can contain nitrogen and sulphur. On burning, they may form oxides of nitrogen and sulphur, which are major pollutants.

## Oxidation

Oxidation means addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen.

At Class 10 level, remember this key example:

Ethanol can be oxidised to ethanoic acid.

`CH3CH2OH + oxygen -> CH3COOH + water`

Common oxidising agents:
- alkaline potassium permanganate
- acidified potassium dichromate

[[demo:activity-4-5-oxidation-ethanol]]

In Activity 4.5, alkaline potassium permanganate is added drop by drop to warm ethanol. If the oxidising agent is being used up, its colour disappears at first. When excess is added, the colour remains. This tells students that ethanol is being oxidised.

Board answer idea:
The conversion of ethanol to ethanoic acid is oxidation because oxygen is added to ethanol by an oxidising agent.

## Addition reaction

An addition reaction happens when atoms are added to an unsaturated compound.

The word addition is literal: something is added to the molecule. This usually happens at a double or triple bond. The multiple bond opens partly, and new atoms attach.

Example:

Hydrogen adds to ethene in the presence of a nickel catalyst.

`C2H4 + H2 -> C2H6`

This idea is used in hydrogenation of vegetable oils.

Vegetable oils are unsaturated. Hydrogenation converts them into more saturated fats.

Industrial use:
Hydrogenation is used to convert vegetable oils into vanaspati ghee using a nickel catalyst.

The textbook mentions catalysts such as nickel or palladium. A catalyst changes the rate of a reaction without itself being consumed. In hydrogenation, nickel helps hydrogen add to unsaturated carbon chains in vegetable oils.

Health note from the textbook:
Vegetable oils generally contain unsaturated fatty acids, while animal fats generally contain saturated fatty acids. Oils containing unsaturated fatty acids are preferred for cooking.

## Substitution reaction

A substitution reaction happens when one atom or group replaces another atom or group.

The word substitution means "one in place of another". In saturated hydrocarbons, the carbon-carbon bonds are single and fairly unreactive, so they do not usually show addition. But in sunlight, chlorine can replace hydrogen atoms one by one.

Saturated hydrocarbons usually show substitution reactions.

Example:

Methane reacts with chlorine in sunlight.

`CH4 + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + HCl`

One hydrogen atom in methane is replaced by chlorine.

Sunlight is important because it helps start the chlorination reaction. With higher alkanes, many substituted products can form because chlorine may replace hydrogen at different positions.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Sooty flame

Question:
Why can some carbon compounds burn with a sooty flame?

Answer:
They may burn incompletely when oxygen is limited, producing unburnt carbon particles.

### Example 2: Hydrogenation

Question:
What type of reaction converts ethene to ethane?

Answer:
Addition reaction, because hydrogen is added across the double bond.

### Example 3: Chlorination of methane

Question:
Why is chlorination of methane called substitution?

Answer:
One hydrogen atom of methane is replaced by a chlorine atom.

### Example 4: Ethyne welding

Question:
Why is oxygen mixed with ethyne for welding instead of using air?

Answer:
Oxygen gives complete combustion and a very hot flame. Air contains less oxygen, so ethyne burns less completely and gives a sooty flame.

### Example 5: Black vessel bottom

Question:
While cooking, why does the bottom of a vessel sometimes become black?

Answer:
The fuel is burning incompletely because the air holes are blocked or oxygen supply is limited. Unburnt carbon particles form soot and blacken the vessel.

### Example 6: Addition or substitution

Question:
Which reaction is expected from `C3H6`: addition or substitution?

Answer:
`C3H6` is unsaturated, so it can undergo addition reactions.

## Common mistakes

1. Writing combustion without oxygen.
2. Calling hydrogenation a substitution reaction.
3. Forgetting that sunlight is needed for methane-chlorine substitution.
4. Saying oxidation always means only burning.

## Board tips

- For reaction-type questions, write the equation and then name the reaction.
- For addition reactions, mention "unsaturated compound" and "double/triple bond".
- For substitution, mention replacement of one atom by another.

## Answer check

1. Carbon dioxide and water.
2. Alkaline potassium permanganate or acidified potassium dichromate.
3. Addition of hydrogen to an unsaturated compound.
4. Substitution reaction.
5. Oxygen is added to ethanol to form ethanoic acid.
6. Incomplete combustion produces unburnt carbon particles, or soot.
7. `C3H6` and `C2H2`, because they are unsaturated.
8. Add bromine water or alkaline potassium permanganate; unsaturated compounds decolourise them.
9. Hydrogen adds across a double or triple bond in an unsaturated compound.
10. Nickel acts as a catalyst.
11. Chlorine replaces hydrogen atoms one by one in saturated methane.

## One-minute revision

Combustion needs oxygen and gives carbon dioxide, water, heat, and light. Oxidation can turn ethanol into ethanoic acid. Addition happens with unsaturated compounds. Substitution happens when one atom replaces another, as in methane and chlorine in sunlight.
