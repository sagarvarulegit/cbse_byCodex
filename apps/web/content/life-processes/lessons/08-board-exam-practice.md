# Latest verified 5-mark pattern: 2026

## 1. Stomata and photosynthesis, or nephron and selective reabsorption

### Option A

(a) Draw labelled diagrams of an open and a closed stomatal pore.  
(b) List the main events of photosynthesis.  
(c) Write a balanced equation for photosynthesis.

**Full-mark answer:**

[[figure:figure-5-3-stomata]]

Required labels: guard cells, stomatal pore and chloroplasts. Show swollen
guard cells around a wide open pore and shrunken guard cells around a closed
pore.

Main events:

1. chlorophyll absorbs light energy
2. light energy changes into chemical energy and water molecules split into
   hydrogen and oxygen
3. carbon dioxide is reduced to carbohydrates

Equation:

`6CO2 + 12H2O --sunlight/chlorophyll--> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O`

### Option B

(a) Draw and label at least four parts of a nephron.  
(b) State the role of the capillary cluster inside Bowman's capsule.  
(c) Define selective reabsorption.

**Full-mark answer:**

[[figure:figure-5-14-nephron]]

Any four correct labels earn the diagram-label marks: glomerulus, Bowman's
capsule, afferent arteriole, efferent arteriole, tubule, capillary network and
collecting duct.

- The capillary cluster, or glomerulus, filters blood under pressure to remove
  wastes and small dissolved substances.
- Selective reabsorption is the return of useful substances such as glucose,
  amino acids, vitamins, required salts and most water from the filtrate to
  blood.

**Official marking insight:** The 2026 marking scheme awards separate marks for
the diagram, four labels, filtration and the complete definition of selective
reabsorption. A diagram alone cannot earn all five marks.

# Excretion and kidney patterns

## 2. Nitrogenous wastes, glomerulus and urine path

Name two nitrogenous wastes removed from human blood. Name the capillary
cluster in Bowman's capsule. Show the path of urine until it leaves the body.

**Full-mark answer:**

- nitrogenous wastes: urea and uric acid
- capillary cluster: glomerulus
- path: `collecting ducts in kidney -> ureter -> urinary bladder -> urethra`

## 3. Kidney case study and dialysis

A person has kidney failure and receives dialysis.

(a) Name the artery carrying blood to a kidney and the capillary cluster in
Bowman's capsule.  
(b) Name the urine-storage organ and state whether its emptying is controlled
by hormones or nerves.  
(c) State the two major processes that convert filtrate into urine.

**Full-mark answer:**

- renal artery; glomerulus
- urinary bladder; nervous control
- filtration at the glomerulus and selective reabsorption along the tubule

Dialysis removes wastes through a semipermeable membrane, but unlike a nephron
it does not perform the nephron's complete selective reabsorption.

[[figure:figure-5-dialysis]]

## 4. Why is only a small fraction of filtered fluid excreted?

Kidneys may filter a very large volume of fluid daily, but only about one or
two litres may leave as urine. Explain.

**Full-mark answer:** The initial filtrate contains water and many useful small
substances. Nephron tubules selectively reabsorb all glucose and amino acids,
required salts and most water into surrounding capillaries. Only nitrogenous
wastes, excess salts and excess water remain as urine. The amount of water
reabsorbed changes with body need and dissolved waste load.

## 5. Alveolus and nephron comparison

State one structural similarity and one functional difference between alveoli
and nephrons.

**Full-mark answer:**

- Similarity: both provide a large, thin exchange surface closely associated
  with a rich capillary network.
- Difference: alveoli exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide by diffusion;
  nephrons pressure-filter blood and selectively reabsorb useful substances to
  form urine.

# Photosynthesis and stomata patterns

## 6. Sunlight is essential for photosynthesis

Write the experimental steps that verify that sunlight is essential for
photosynthesis.

**Full-mark answer:**

1. Keep a healthy potted plant in darkness for about three days to destarch it.
2. Cover part of a leaf on both sides with opaque black paper.
3. Keep the plant in sunlight for a few hours.
4. Pluck the leaf, boil it in water, then heat it in alcohol using a water bath
   to remove chlorophyll.
5. Rinse and add iodine solution.
6. The exposed part turns blue-black; the covered part does not.
7. Therefore, starch forms only in the part receiving light, proving that
   sunlight is essential for photosynthesis.

Safety point: heat alcohol in a water bath because it is inflammable.

## 7. Carbon dioxide is essential for photosynthesis

Explain the two-bell-jar experiment and identify the experimental control.

**Full-mark answer:**

1. Destarch two similar potted plants.
2. Place each under an airtight bell jar in equal light.
3. Keep potassium hydroxide in one jar; it absorbs carbon dioxide.
4. The second jar has no potassium hydroxide and is the control.
5. After exposure, test one leaf from each plant for starch.
6. The control leaf turns blue-black more strongly; the leaf without available
   carbon dioxide does not.
7. Therefore, carbon dioxide is essential for photosynthesis.

[[demo:activity-5-2-carbon-dioxide]]

## 8. Chlorophyll is essential

State the observation and conclusion of the variegated-leaf activity.

**Full-mark answer:** After destarching and exposing the plant to light, only
the parts of the leaf that were green turn blue-black with iodine. The
non-green parts remain pale. Starch formed only where chlorophyll was present;
therefore chlorophyll is essential for photosynthesis.

## 9. Desert-plant carbon dioxide strategy

How can some desert plants photosynthesise while keeping stomata closed during
the day?

**Full-mark answer:** They open stomata at night, take in carbon dioxide and
store it in an intermediate carbon compound, often in vacuoles. During the
day, when stomata remain closed to reduce water loss, the compound releases
carbon dioxide in chloroplasts and absorbed light energy drives
photosynthesis.

## 10. Stomatal control

Why does a stomatal pore open when water enters guard cells and close when
water leaves them?

**Full-mark answer:** Water entry makes guard cells turgid and changes their
shape so the pore opens. Water loss makes them flaccid or shrunken, bringing
the cells closer and closing the pore. This balances carbon-dioxide intake
against water loss.

# Digestion and nutrition patterns

## 11. Small-intestine assertion trap

Assertion: Villi are finger-like projections rich in blood capillaries.  
Reason: Villi complete digestion of food.

**Answer:** The assertion is true, but the reason is false. Villi increase the
surface area for **absorption** of already digested food; digestive enzymes
complete digestion in the small intestine.

## 12. Final products of digestion

Write the final products of digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

**Answer:**

- carbohydrates -> glucose or other simple sugars
- proteins -> amino acids
- fats -> fatty acids and glycerol

## 13. Peristalsis, bile and pancreatic juice

(a) Name the movement along the gut and state its function.  
(b) Where is bile stored? Give two roles.  
(c) State the substrates of trypsin and lipase.

**Full-mark answer:**

- Peristalsis is rhythmic contraction and relaxation of gut muscles; it pushes
  food forward and mixes it with digestive juices.
- Bile is stored in the gall bladder. It neutralises the acidic food from the
  stomach and emulsifies fats into small droplets.
- Trypsin digests proteins; lipase digests emulsified fats.

## 14. Dual role of pancreas

A pancreatic duct is blocked. How does this affect the two functions of the
pancreas?

**Full-mark answer:** Pancreatic digestive enzymes cannot reach the small
intestine, so digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats is impaired.
Hormones such as insulin are endocrine secretions released directly into
blood, so a duct blockage does not directly block their release.

## 15. Nutrition in Amoeba

Write the correct sequence of nutrition in Amoeba.

**Answer:** `pseudopodia surround food -> food vacuole forms -> complex food is
digested -> simple nutrients diffuse into cytoplasm -> undigested material is
expelled through the cell surface`

[[figure:figure-5-5-amoeba-nutrition]]

## 16. Chewing versus salivation

State two differences between chewing food and salivation on seeing food.

**Answer:**

| Chewing | Salivation on seeing food |
|---|---|
| voluntary muscular action | involuntary neurally controlled response |
| mechanical breakdown that increases food surface area | secretion that moistens food and begins starch digestion through amylase |

# Respiration patterns

## 17. ATP, glycolysis and residual air

(a) Why is ATP called the energy currency of the cell?  
(b) What is the first step common to aerobic and anaerobic respiration, and
where does it occur?  
(c) Why do lungs retain residual air?

**Full-mark answer:**

- Energy released from food is stored in ATP; ATP breakdown supplies usable
  energy for endothermic cellular processes.
- Glucose breaks into the three-carbon molecule pyruvate in the cytoplasm.
- Residual air prevents alveoli from collapsing and permits continuous gas
  exchange between breaths.

## 18. Complete respiration pathway

Write the fate of pyruvate in oxygen, in yeast without oxygen and in
oxygen-deficient muscle cells.

**Answer:**

[[figure:figure-5-8-glucose-breakdown]]

- oxygen in mitochondria -> carbon dioxide + water + much energy
- no oxygen in yeast -> ethanol + carbon dioxide + less energy
- insufficient oxygen in muscles -> lactic acid + less energy

The human anaerobic end product remains a three-carbon compound because
pyruvate undergoes incomplete breakdown; it is not fully oxidised to carbon
dioxide.

## 19. Respiratory-system diagram and design

Draw the human respiratory system and explain the roles of alveoli and
respiratory pigment.

**Full-mark answer:**

[[figure:figure-5-9-respiratory-system]]

Required labels: nasal passage, trachea, bronchi, lungs and alveoli.

- Alveoli provide a vast, thin, moist, capillary-rich exchange surface.
- Haemoglobin in red blood cells binds oxygen and transports it from lungs to
  tissues. Carbon dioxide is carried mainly dissolved in plasma because it is
  more soluble in water.
- Cartilage rings keep the trachea open.

## 20. Aquatic versus terrestrial respiration

Why is the breathing rate of aquatic organisms usually faster?

**Answer:** Water contains much less dissolved oxygen than air, so an aquatic
organism must pass more water over its respiratory surface to obtain enough
oxygen. Terrestrial organisms obtain oxygen more readily from oxygen-rich air.

# Transportation patterns

## 21. Trace blood through the human heart

Name the vessels bringing oxygenated and deoxygenated blood to the heart. Trace
deoxygenated blood from its receiving chamber to the lungs.

**Full-mark answer:**

- pulmonary veins bring oxygenated blood from lungs to the left atrium
- venae cavae bring deoxygenated blood from the body to the right atrium
- pathway: `vena cava -> right atrium -> right ventricle -> pulmonary artery -> lungs`

[[figure:figure-5-10-heart]]

## 22. Evolution of circulation

Complete the comparison.

| Organisms | Chambers | Type |
|---|---:|---|
| fishes | ? | ? |
| amphibians and most reptiles | ? | ? |
| birds and mammals | ? | ? |

**Answer:**

- fishes: two chambers, single circulation
- amphibians and most reptiles: three chambers, incomplete double circulation
  with some mixing
- birds and mammals: four chambers, complete double circulation

Complete double circulation is necessary in birds and mammals because their
high energy demand requires efficient delivery of fully oxygenated blood.

## 23. Arteries, veins, capillaries and lymph

Why are vein walls thinner than artery walls? State two correct facts about
lymph.

**Full-mark answer:** Veins carry blood at lower pressure, so they do not need
thick elastic walls; valves prevent backflow. Lymph is colourless tissue fluid
containing some plasma, proteins and white blood cells. Lymph vessels return it
to larger veins and transport absorbed fats from the intestine.

## 24. What happens if xylem is removed or a blood vessel is cut?

**Answer:**

- Removing xylem stops the upward transport of water and minerals, causing
  leaves and other parts above the cut to wilt and eventually die.
- At a cut blood vessel, platelets help form a clot that plugs the leak and
  reduces blood loss.

## 25. How does water reach leaves?

**Full-mark answer:** Root cells actively absorb mineral ions; water follows
into the root and enters continuous xylem vessels and tracheids. Root pressure
can push water upward over short distances. Evaporation from leaf cells through
stomata creates transpiration pull, which is the main upward force in tall
plants. Cohesion maintains the continuous water column.

## 26. Transpiration: a necessary evil

Give one harmful effect and two benefits of transpiration.

**Answer:** It is an "evil" because excessive water loss can cause wilting.
It is necessary because it produces transpiration pull for upward water and
mineral transport and helps cool the plant.

[[figure:activity-5-8-transpiration]]

## 27. Translocation through phloem

Explain how food reaches non-photosynthetic parts of a plant.

**Full-mark answer:** Sucrose is actively loaded into phloem sieve tubes using
ATP. Water enters by osmosis, creating pressure that pushes the food solution
from a source, such as a leaf, to a sink, such as a root, fruit, seed or growing
bud. At the sink, food is unloaded and water pressure falls. Transport can be
upward or downward according to source and sink positions.

# Plant excretion pattern

## 28. Plants have no specialised excretory system. How do they remove wastes?

**Full-mark answer:** Plants diffuse gaseous wastes through stomata and other
surfaces, lose excess water by transpiration, store wastes in vacuoles, store
them in leaves that later fall, deposit resins and gums in old xylem, and
release some substances into surrounding soil.

# Final Full-mark Checklist

For a 3- or 5-mark answer:

1. match the number of marking points to the marks
2. use exact biological terms rather than vague words such as "cleans" or
   "moves"
3. write direction and location for every pathway
4. state the control, observation and conclusion for an experiment
5. draw neat diagrams with outward label lines and no crossing
6. include units only where needed, such as `mm Hg` for blood pressure
7. check every `OR` choice before selecting the one you can answer completely
