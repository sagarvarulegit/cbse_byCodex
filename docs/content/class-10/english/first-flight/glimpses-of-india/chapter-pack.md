# Glimpses of India

## chapter goal

By the end of this unit, a student should be able to:
- explain the cultural picture in "A Baker from Goa"
- describe Coorg's landscape, people, and traditions
- explain the tea legends and Assam setting in "Tea from Assam"
- revise the poem "The Trees"
- answer questions on travel writing, culture, and symbolism

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jeff105.pdf`: "A Baker from Goa", "Coorg", "Tea from Assam", and the poem "The Trees".

## 1) A Baker from Goa

The lesson remembers the traditional Goan baker, called the pader.

Bread is linked with festivals, marriages, daily life, and childhood memories.

The baker's arrival, dress, bamboo staff, and basket create a living picture of old Goan culture.

## 2) Coorg

Coorg, or Kodagu, is described as a beautiful region of rainforests, coffee, spices, and martial traditions.

The people are known for hospitality and bravery. The region is linked with nature, adventure, and cultural pride.

## 3) Tea from Assam

Rajat and Pranjol travel through Assam's tea gardens.

The lesson mixes observation with legends about tea's discovery in China and India.

It shows how travel can turn ordinary scenes into learning.

## 4) travel writing style

The unit uses sensory details: sound, smell, movement, landscape, and local customs.

It helps students see India as diverse and culturally rich.

## 5) poem: The Trees

The poem imagines trees inside a house moving out into the forest.

It can be read as a poem about nature reclaiming its space and also as a symbol of freedom from confinement.

The poem uses personification and strong visual images.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Goa section preserves memory of traditional bakers.
- Coorg is linked with coffee, nature, bravery, and hospitality.
- Assam section presents tea gardens and tea legends.
- The unit celebrates India's diversity.
- "The Trees" uses trees as symbols of freedom and return to nature.

## common mistakes students make

1. Mixing details of Goa, Coorg, and Assam.
2. Forgetting the cultural importance of bread in Goa.
3. Writing only scenery for Coorg and missing people/traditions.
4. Taking "The Trees" only literally.

## board tips

- Organise answers by sub-section.
- Use place + feature + cultural meaning.
- In poem answers, mention personification.
