# Control and Coordination chapter quiz

Answer these quick questions after revising the chapter notes.

## 1. Receptor

Which part detects a stimulus first?

1. receptor
2. synapse
3. cerebellum

Answer: 1
Topic: animal-nervous-system
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT in-text aligned
Explanation: Receptors in sense organs detect the stimulus and start the message.

## 2. Synapse

The gap between two neurons is called a:

1. dendrite
2. synapse
3. axon

Answer: 2
Topic: animal-nervous-system
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: The synapse is the gap where chemicals pass the signal from one neuron to the next.

## 3. Reflex

A reflex action is:

1. a slow planned action
2. a quick automatic response
3. a type of digestion

Answer: 2
Topic: voluntary-involuntary-reflex-actions
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT in-text aligned
Explanation: Reflex actions are fast, automatic responses to a stimulus.

## 4. Brain

Which part of the brain maintains posture and balance?

1. fore-brain
2. medulla
3. cerebellum

Answer: 3
Topic: voluntary-involuntary-reflex-actions
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT in-text aligned
Explanation: The cerebellum controls posture, balance, and precision of voluntary actions.

## 5. Plant hormone

Which of the following is a plant hormone?

1. Insulin
2. Cytokinin
3. Thyroxin

Answer: 2
Topic: plant-tropic-movements-hormones
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Cytokinin is a plant hormone that promotes cell division.

## 6. Phototropism

Why does a shoot bend towards light?

1. Water moves into the root only
2. Auxin makes cells on the shady side grow longer
3. The shoot stops growing

Answer: 2
Topic: plant-tropic-movements-hormones
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT in-text aligned
Explanation: Auxin accumulates on the shady side and makes those cells elongate more.

## 7. Hormone

Which hormone prepares the body for an emergency?

1. adrenaline
2. oestrogen
3. cytokinin

Answer: 1
Topic: animal-hormones
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT in-text aligned
Explanation: Adrenaline increases heart rate and breathing rate and readies the body for fight or flight.

## 8. Thyroxin

Why is iodised salt advisable?

2. It helps the thyroid make thyroxin
3. It stops reflex actions

Answer: 2
Topic: animal-hormones
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT in-text aligned

## 9. Insulin

What does insulin regulate?

1. blood sugar level
2. blood pressure only
3. light response in shoots

Answer: 1
Topic: animal-hormones
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Insulin helps keep blood sugar level under control through feedback.

## 10. Control system

What is the main need for a control and coordination system in an organism?

1. to detect changes and respond correctly
2. to make leaves green
3. to stop all movement

Answer: 1
Topic: animal-nervous-system
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Organisms need control systems to sense stimuli, respond correctly, and keep body parts working together.

## 11. Electrical signalling

Which is a limitation of electrical impulses in animals?

1. they reach only cells connected by nervous tissue
2. they always reach every cell through blood
3. they can be produced continuously without reset time

Answer: 1
Topic: animal-nervous-system
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2025-2026 board family
Explanation: An electrical impulse follows connected neurons, and a neuron also needs reset time after transmitting one.

## 12. Peripheral nervous system

The peripheral nervous system consists mainly of:

1. brain and spinal cord
2. cranial and spinal nerves
3. medulla and cerebellum only

Answer: 2
Topic: human-brain
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2024 board family
Explanation: Cranial nerves arise from the brain and spinal nerves arise from the spinal cord.

## 13. Motor neuron

A motor neuron carries an impulse:

1. from a receptor to the CNS
2. from the CNS to an effector
3. across the blood to every cell

Answer: 2
Topic: animal-nervous-system
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2026 board family
Explanation: Motor neurons carry instructions from the CNS to muscles or glands.

## 14. Hind-brain

Which set contains only parts of the hind-brain?

1. pons, cerebellum, medulla

3. hypothalamus, cerebrum, medulla

Answer: 1
Topic: human-brain
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2025 board family

## 15. Sensitive plant

Movement of touch-me-not leaves is:

1. directional and growth-related
2. non-directional and growth-independent
3. caused by voluntary muscles

Answer: 2
Topic: plant-tropic-movements-hormones
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2023-2026 board family
Explanation: The leaf cells change water content quickly; the response does not depend on growth.

## 16. Tendril

A tendril coils around a support mainly because:

1. both sides stop growing equally
2. the side away from contact grows faster
3. the touching side grows faster than the opposite side

Answer: 2
Topic: plant-tropic-movements-hormones
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2026 board family
Explanation: Unequal growth, faster away from the support, makes the tendril curve around it.

## 17. Hydrotropism

Answer: 1
Topic: plant-tropic-movements-hormones
Difficulty: easy
Source: CBSE 2026 board case
Explanation: Roots use positive hydrotropism to reach water and dissolved minerals.

## 18. Hormone imbalance

Excess growth hormone during childhood may cause:

1. goitre
2. gigantism
3. diabetes

Answer: 2
Topic: animal-hormones
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2023 board family
Explanation: The pituitary secretes growth hormone; its over-secretion in childhood can cause gigantism.

## 19. Feedback

Which sequence correctly describes insulin feedback?

1. blood sugar rises -> more insulin -> blood sugar falls -> insulin release reduces
2. blood sugar rises -> less insulin -> blood sugar rises further
3. blood sugar falls -> unlimited insulin release

Answer: 1
Topic: animal-hormones
Difficulty: hard
Source: CBSE 2023-2026 board family
Explanation: Feedback adjusts hormone release according to the current blood-sugar level.

## 20. Nervous versus hormonal

Which comparison is correct?

1. nervous messages travel only as hormones in blood
2. nervous responses are usually rapid; hormonal responses are usually slower and longer-lasting
3. hormonal messages act only at synapses

Answer: 2
Topic: animal-hormones
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2023 five-mark family
Explanation: Nervous impulses are fast and precise, while hormones travel in blood and often act more slowly and persistently.
