# Board Exam Practice

These recurring answer families cover the distinct Heredity demands screened in
the local 2023-2026 CBSE paper archive. The candidate pool also contained
adjacent reproduction questions; only Chapter 8 demands are retained here.

## 1. Why variation helps a species

DNA-copying changes and sexual recombination produce variants. If conditions
change, a suitable variant may survive and reproduce. Thus variation protects
the species over time, though it may not benefit every individual.

## 2. Monohybrid cross—3 or 5 marks

Show `TT × tt`, gametes `T` and `t`, all F1 `Tt`, then `Tt × Tt`. Punnett square
gives `TT, Tt, Tt, tt`; genotype `1:2:1`, phenotype `3 tall:1 short`. Conclude
dominance, recessiveness, and segregation.

## 3. Why the recessive trait reappears

F1 `Tt` plants carry `t`, although `T` masks it. During gamete formation the
alleles separate. Fusion of two `t` gametes produces `tt`, so shortness
reappears in F2.

## 4. Identify genotype from offspring

- A dominant-looking parent producing recessive offspring must be heterozygous.
- A test cross with `tt` gives all dominant offspring from `TT`, but `1:1` from
  `Tt`.

## 5. Genotype versus phenotype

Genotype is the allele combination, e.g. `TT` or `Tt`; phenotype is the
expressed form, e.g. tall. `TT` and `Tt` have different genotypes but the same
phenotype.

## 6. Dihybrid cross—5 marks

For NCERT `RRyy × rrYY`, F1 is `RrYy`, round-yellow. F1 produces `RY, Ry, rY,
ry`. Selfing gives `9 round-yellow : 3 round-green : 3 wrinkled-yellow : 1
wrinkled-green`. New combinations prove independent inheritance.

## 7. Monohybrid versus dihybrid

Monohybrid studies one trait and commonly yields `3:1`; dihybrid studies two and
yields `9:3:3:1` under complete dominance and independent assortment.

## 8. Dominant does not mean common

Dominance describes expression in a heterozygote. It says nothing about whether
the allele is better, stronger, or more frequent in a population.

## 9. Gene to trait

Gene/DNA information directs protein formation. A protein or enzyme changes a
cell process; the process produces the visible trait. In peas, an enzyme affects
growth-hormone production and hence height.

## 10. Equal contribution from parents

Chromosome pairs separate during gamete formation. Each haploid gamete carries
one set. Fertilisation combines one maternal and one paternal set, so both
parents contribute equally.

## 11. Constant chromosome number

Gametes contain half the body-cell chromosome number. Fusion restores the
diploid number. Without reduction, chromosome number would double in every
generation.

## 12. Human sex determination—diagram answer

Mother `XX` makes only X eggs. Father `XY` makes X and Y sperm equally. X sperm
gives `XX` female; Y sperm gives `XY` male. Probability is `1:1`, and the
fertilising sperm determines sex.

## 13. Genetic and environmental sex determination

Human sex is genetic (`XX/XY`). In some reptiles incubation temperature
determines sex. Some snails can change sex.

## 14. Mismatched chromosomes

Human males have a dissimilar sex-chromosome pair `XY`; females have a similar
pair `XX`.

## 15. Blood-group reasoning

One A-group father and O-group mother producing an O-group child does not by
itself establish which trait is dominant. State that more controlled inheritance
evidence is needed; do not guess from frequency.

## 16. Light-eye family reasoning

Light-eyed parents and children do not prove dominance. Parental phenotypes
alone do not reveal every allele; analyse additional generations or crosses.

## 17. Contrasting pea traits

Examples: tall/short stem, round/wrinkled seed, yellow/green seed, violet/white
flower. Write a true contrasting pair, not two unrelated traits.

## 18. Case-study checklist

When a question supplies a cross:

1. assign symbols from the stated dominance;
2. infer parental genotypes;
3. list gametes;
4. construct the square;
5. state genotype and phenotype ratios;
6. answer the biological conclusion in words.

## Full-mark self-check

Before finishing, verify symbols are defined, capital/lower-case alleles are
consistent, F1 and F2 are not mixed, and each ratio is labelled genotype or
phenotype.
