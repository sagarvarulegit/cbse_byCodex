# Agriculture

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain types of farming in India
- distinguish cropping seasons
- identify major food and non-food crops
- connect crops with climate and soil conditions
- explain technological and institutional reforms in agriculture

## 1) importance of agriculture

Agriculture is a primary activity. It produces food and raw materials for many industries.

A large share of India’s population depends on agriculture directly or indirectly.

## 2) types of farming

Primitive subsistence farming uses simple tools, family labour, and natural fertility. Shifting cultivation is a form of this in some regions.

Intensive subsistence farming uses small landholdings, high labour, and high inputs to produce more from limited land.

Commercial farming focuses on sale in the market. It uses modern inputs and may include plantation farming.

Plantation farming is a single-crop, large-scale system linked with processing industries and transport networks.

## 3) cropping seasons

Kharif crops are grown with the monsoon and harvested in autumn. Examples: rice, maize, cotton, jute, groundnut.

Rabi crops are sown in winter and harvested in spring. Examples: wheat, barley, gram, mustard.

Zaid crops are grown between rabi and kharif seasons. Examples: watermelon, cucumber, fodder crops.

## 4) major food crops

Rice needs high temperature, high humidity, and plenty of water.

Wheat needs cool growing season, bright sunshine at ripening, and moderate rainfall or irrigation.

Millets such as jowar, bajra, and ragi are hardy coarse grains.

Pulses are important protein sources and often help restore soil fertility through nitrogen fixation.

## 5) major non-food crops

Sugarcane needs hot and humid climate and is used for sugar, jaggery, and related products.

Oilseeds provide edible oils and raw material for industry.

Tea and coffee are beverage crops grown under specific climate and soil conditions.

Cotton grows well in black soil and warm climate.

Jute grows in fertile floodplains and is called the golden fibre.

## 6) reforms in agriculture

Technological reforms include high-yielding varieties, fertilisers, irrigation, mechanisation, and improved seeds.

Institutional reforms include land reforms, consolidation of holdings, cooperatives, crop insurance, credit support, and minimum support price.

## 7) agriculture and national economy

Agriculture supports food security, rural employment, agro-based industries, exports, and domestic markets.

Challenges include small holdings, dependence on monsoon, low productivity in some areas, and market uncertainty.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Farming types: primitive subsistence, intensive subsistence, commercial, plantation.
- Seasons: kharif, rabi, zaid.
- Rice needs heat and water; wheat needs cool season and sunshine.
- Cotton is linked with black soil; jute with fertile floodplains.
- Reforms improve productivity and farmer security.

## common mistakes students make

1. Mixing kharif and rabi crop examples.
2. Writing all millets as one crop without examples.
3. Forgetting that pulses restore nitrogen.
4. Confusing commercial farming with intensive subsistence farming.

## board tips

- For crop questions, write climate + soil + region/use.
- Use examples for each farming type.
- In reform answers, separate technological and institutional reforms.
