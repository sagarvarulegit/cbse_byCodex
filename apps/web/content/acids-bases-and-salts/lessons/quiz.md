## 1. One red litmus strip

Three solutions are an acid, a base and distilled water. Red litmus turns blue
in one solution. What should be done next to identify the other two?

1. Add phenolphthalein to both
2. Use the newly blue litmus to test both remaining solutions
3. Smell both solutions directly
4. Evaporate both solutions

Answer: 2
Topic: indicators-and-neutralisation
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT opening in-text question aligned
Explanation: The base first turns red litmus blue. That blue strip then turns red in the acid and remains unchanged in distilled water.

## 2. Phenolphthalein clue

## 11. Safe dilution

Why must concentrated acid be added slowly to water rather than water being
added to concentrated acid?

1. Water makes the acid stronger
2. Acid floats on water
3. Water stops all ion formation
4. Dilution releases heat, and sudden local heating can cause splashing and burns

Answer: 4
Topic: ph-and-ions-in-solution
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Activity 2.10 and in-text question aligned
Explanation: Acid/base dilution is highly exothermic. Slow addition to a larger amount of water spreads the heat more safely.
