# Life Processes

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain why living organisms need life processes
- compare autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition
- describe human digestion, respiration, transport, and excretion
- explain transport in plants and humans

## 1) what are life processes?

Living organisms need energy and raw materials to maintain their body.

Important life processes:
- nutrition
- respiration
- transport
- excretion

Diffusion alone is not enough in large organisms because all cells are not directly exposed to the environment.

## 2) nutrition

Nutrition is the process of taking in food and using it.

### autotrophic nutrition

Green plants make food by photosynthesis.

Equation:
`6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2`

Conditions:
- sunlight
- chlorophyll
- carbon dioxide
- water

Stomata help in gas exchange. Guard cells control opening and closing.

### heterotrophic nutrition

Organisms depend on other organisms for food.

Types:
- holozoic nutrition
- saprophytic nutrition
- parasitic nutrition

## 3) nutrition in human beings

Main path:
mouth -> oesophagus -> stomach -> small intestine -> large intestine -> anus

Important points:
- saliva contains salivary amylase
- stomach has hydrochloric acid and pepsin
- bile emulsifies fats
- pancreatic juice digests carbohydrates, proteins, and fats
- small intestine completes digestion and absorbs food
- large intestine absorbs water

## 4) respiration

Respiration releases energy from food.

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases more energy.

Equation:
`Glucose + Oxygen -> Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy`

Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen and releases less energy.

In muscles, lack of oxygen can produce lactic acid and cramps.

In yeast, anaerobic respiration produces ethanol and carbon dioxide.

## 5) transportation

### in human beings

Blood transports oxygen, carbon dioxide, food, hormones, and wastes.

Heart has four chambers:
- right atrium
- right ventricle
- left atrium
- left ventricle

Double circulation keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate.

Blood vessels:
- arteries carry blood away from heart
- veins carry blood towards heart
- capillaries exchange materials

### in plants

Xylem transports water and minerals.

Phloem transports food.

Transpiration helps pull water upward and cools the plant.

## 6) excretion

Excretion removes harmful metabolic wastes.

Human excretory system:
- kidneys
- ureters
- urinary bladder
- urethra

Nephrons filter blood and form urine.

Plants remove wastes by:
- storing in leaves, bark, or vacuoles
- releasing through stomata
- forming gums and resins

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Photosynthesis makes glucose using sunlight and chlorophyll.
- Small intestine is the main site of digestion and absorption.
- Aerobic respiration releases more energy than anaerobic respiration.
- Human heart has four chambers.
- Xylem carries water; phloem carries food.
- Nephron is the functional unit of kidney.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing respiration and breathing as the same thing.
2. Forgetting bile does not contain digestive enzymes.
3. Mixing xylem and phloem functions.
4. Saying arteries always carry oxygenated blood without exception.
5. Forgetting small intestine absorbs digested food.

## board tips

- Label diagrams neatly: digestive system, heart, nephron.
- In photosynthesis answers, mention all raw materials and conditions.
- For transport, use arrows to show direction of blood flow.
- In excretion, explain filtration and urine formation simply.

## solved practice set

### question 1
Why is diffusion insufficient in multicellular organisms?

Answer:
Their bodies are large and all cells are not in direct contact with the environment. Diffusion would be too slow.

### question 2
What is the role of bile?

Answer:
Bile makes the medium alkaline and emulsifies fats into small droplets.

### question 3
What is double circulation?

Answer:
Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle, once for lungs and once for the body.
