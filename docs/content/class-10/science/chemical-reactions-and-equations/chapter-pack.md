## 1.1 Chemical Equations

A chemical reaction forms one or more new substances. NCERT uses change in state, colour, temperature and evolution of gas as observable evidence.

Activity 1.1 must be performed with teacher assistance, suitable eyeglasses and the burning magnesium kept away from the eyes. Activities 1.2 and 1.3 establish precipitate formation, gas evolution and temperature change.

`Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide` **(1.1)**

Reactants are written on the left and products on the right.

## 1.1.1 Writing a Chemical Equation

`Mg + O₂ → MgO` **(1.2)**

Equation (1.2) is skeletal because its oxygen atom counts are unequal.

## 1.1.2 Balanced Chemical Equations

The law of conservation of mass requires equal numbers of every atom on the two sides.

`Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂` **(1.3)**

Hit-and-trial sequence:

`Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂` **(1.4)**  
`Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂` **(1.5)**  
`Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂` **(1.6)**  
`Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂` **(1.7)**  
`3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂` **(1.8)**  
`3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂` **(1.9)**  
`3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)` **(1.10)**

Only coefficients may change. Use the smallest whole numbers. In equation (1.10), water is steam.

`CO(g) + 2H₂(g) →[340 atm] CH₃OH(l)` **(1.11)**

`6CO₂(aq) + 12H₂O(l) →[Sunlight, chlorophyll] C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) + 6O₂(aq) + 6H₂O(l)` **(1.12)**

**❓ QUESTIONS**

**1. Why should a magnesium ribbon be cleaned before burning in air?**  
*Answer:* To remove the magnesium oxide coating and expose fresh magnesium.

**2. Write the balanced equation for the following chemical reactions.**

**(i) Hydrogen + Chlorine → Hydrogen chloride**  
*Answer:* `H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl`

**(ii) Barium chloride + Aluminium sulphate → Barium sulphate + Aluminium chloride**  
*Answer:* `3BaCl₂ + Al₂(SO₄)₃ → 3BaSO₄ + 2AlCl₃`

**(iii) Sodium + Water → Sodium hydroxide + Hydrogen**  
*Answer:* `2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂`

**3. Write a balanced chemical equation with state symbols for the following reactions.**

**(i) Solutions of barium chloride and sodium sulphate in water react to give insoluble barium sulphate and the solution of sodium chloride.**  
*Answer:* `BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)`

**(ii) Sodium hydroxide solution (in water) reacts with hydrochloric acid solution (in water) to produce sodium chloride solution and water.**  
*Answer:* `NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)`

## 1.2 Types of Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions break existing bonds and form new bonds. Atoms do not disappear, appear from nowhere or change into atoms of another element.

## 1.2.1 Combination Reaction

Activity 1.4 forms one product and releases heat:

`CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat` **(1.13)**

The whitewashing Do You Know? box explains the shiny calcium carbonate layer:

`Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)` **(1.14)**

`C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)` **(1.15)**  
`2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)` **(1.16)**  
`CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(g)` **(1.17)**  
`C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) + 6O₂(aq) → 6CO₂(aq) + 6H₂O(l) + Energy` **(1.18)**

Combination reactions form one product. Exothermic reactions release energy.

## 1.2.2 Decomposition Reaction

In Activity 1.5, `FeSO₄·7H₂O` first loses water. Ferrous sulphate then decomposes:

`2FeSO₄(s) →[Heat] Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g)` **(1.19)**

`CaCO₃(s) →[Heat] CaO(s) + CO₂(g)` **(1.20)**

This thermal decomposition is important in cement manufacture.

`2Pb(NO₃)₂(s) →[Heat] 2PbO(s) + 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g)` **(1.21)**

Water electrolysis is unnumbered in NCERT:

`2H₂O(l) →[Electricity] 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)`

`2AgCl(s) →[Sunlight] 2Ag(s) + Cl₂(g)` **(1.22)**  
`2AgBr(s) →[Sunlight] 2Ag(s) + Br₂(g)` **(1.23)**

The silver salt reactions were used in black-and-white photography. The barium hydroxide-ammonium chloride activity feels cold and demonstrates an endothermic reaction.

**❓ QUESTIONS**

**1. A solution of a substance ‘X’ is used for whitewashing.**

**(i) Name the substance ‘X’ and write its formula.**  
*Answer:* Calcium oxide, `CaO`.

**(ii) Write the reaction of the substance ‘X’ named in (i) above with water.**  
*Answer:* `CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat`

**2. Why is the amount of gas collected in one of the test tubes in Activity 1.7 double of the amount collected in the other? Name this gas.**  
*Answer:* Water produces hydrogen and oxygen in a 2:1 ratio. Hydrogen is collected in double amount.

## 1.2.3 Displacement Reaction

Activity 1.9 compares three nails and test tubes A/B before and after 20 minutes.

`Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)` **(1.24)**  
`Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)` **(1.25)**  
`Pb(s) + CuCl₂(aq) → PbCl₂(aq) + Cu(s)` **(1.26)**

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element.

## 1.2.4 Double Displacement Reaction

`Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)` **(1.27)**

The reactants exchange ions. Insoluble `BaSO₄` is a precipitate.

Recall Activity 1.2: yellow `PbI₂` forms in `Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2KNO₃(aq)`. This is also double displacement and precipitation.

## 1.2.5 Oxidation and Reduction

`2Cu + O₂ →[Heat] 2CuO` **(1.28)**  
`CuO + H₂ →[Heat] Cu + H₂O` **(1.29)**  
`CuO + H₂ →[Heat] Cu + H₂O` **(1.30)** — `H₂` is oxidised; `CuO` is reduced.  
`ZnO + C → Zn + CO` **(1.31)**  
`MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + 2H₂O + Cl₂` **(1.32)**

Hydrochloric acid loses hydrogen and is oxidised to chlorine. Manganese dioxide loses oxygen and is reduced to manganese chloride; the oxygen combines with hydrogen to form water. Oxidation is gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen. Reduction is loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.

**❓ QUESTIONS**

**1. Why does the colour of copper sulphate solution change when an iron nail is dipped in it?**  
*Answer:* Iron displaces copper and pale-green iron sulphate forms, so the blue colour fades.

**2. Give an example of a double displacement reaction other than the one given in Activity 1.10.**  
*Answer:* `Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2KNO₃(aq)`

**3. Identify the substances that are oxidised and the substances that are reduced in the following reactions.**

**(i) `4Na(s) + O₂(g) → 2Na₂O(s)`**  
*Answer:* Sodium is oxidised; oxygen is reduced.

**(ii) `CuO(s) + H₂(g) → Cu(s) + H₂O(l)`**  
*Answer:* Hydrogen is oxidised; copper oxide is reduced.

## 1.3 Have You Observed the Effects of Oxidation Reactions in Everyday Life?

## 1.3.1 Corrosion

Corrosion is attack on a metal by moisture, acids or other substances around it. Iron rusts reddish-brown, silver turns black and copper develops a green coating. Corrosion damages vehicles, bridges, railings and ships.

## 1.3.2 Rancidity

Oxidation changes the smell and taste of fats and oils. Antioxidants, airtight containers, cooling and nitrogen flushing slow rancidity. In the Activity 1.1 recall, magnesium gains oxygen and is oxidised.

## Common mistakes

- Changing formulae while balancing.
- Attaching NCERT numbers to the wrong equations.
- Calling Activity 1.2 Figure 1.2.
- Omitting states or reaction conditions.
- Naming a reaction without its observation or reason.

## Board tips

- Write formulae, balance, add states, add conditions, then check atoms.
- In activity answers, state observation before inference.
- In redox answers, name both oxidised and reduced substances.
- Answer every requested example and subpart.

## One-minute revision

- Combination: many to one; decomposition: one to many.
- Displacement replaces an element; double displacement exchanges ions.
- Oxidation and reduction occur together.
- Corrosion attacks metals; rancidity oxidises fats and oils.
