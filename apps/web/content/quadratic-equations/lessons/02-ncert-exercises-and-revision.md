# NCERT Exercises and Chapter Revision

## Exercise 4.1

First simplify each equation and compare it with $ax^2+bx+c=0$. The exercise then asks you to form quadratic equations for:

- a rectangular plot of area $528\text{ m}^2$
- two consecutive positive integers with product $306$
- Rohan's mother's age and the age three years later
- a train travelling $480$ km with a changed speed and time

Do not solve these situation equations until the question asks you to.

## Exercise 4.2

The factorisation exercise includes six direct equations and five applications.

Key application answers:

- two numbers with sum $27$ and product $182$: $13$ and $14$
- two consecutive positive integers whose squares sum to $365$: $13$ and $14$
- right triangle with altitude $7$ cm less than its base and hypotenuse $13$ cm: base $12$ cm, altitude $5$ cm
- pottery production problem: $50$ articles; cost per article Rs $2$

## Exercise 4.3

Use $D=b^2-4ac$ for every nature-of-roots question.

- If $D>0$, calculate both real roots.
- If $D=0$, write the repeated root once and state that it is equal.
- If $D<0$, stop: there is no real root.

Application checks:

- rectangular mango grove with length twice breadth and area $800\text{ m}^2$: breadth $20$ m, length $40$ m
- consecutive ages whose product was $48$ three years ago: present ages $9$ and $8$ years
- rectangular park with perimeter $80$ m and area $400\text{ m}^2$: square of side $20$ m

## 4.5 Summary check

1. Can you recognise a quadratic after simplification?
2. Can you factor by splitting the middle term?
3. Can you use the formula without losing the sign of $b$?
4. Can you reject a root that breaks the situation?

## Common mistakes

- treating a non-quadratic equation as quadratic before simplifying
- forgetting units in the final application answer
- accepting both roots when only a positive measurement is possible

## Board-answer checklist

- Equation in standard form
- Correct method shown
- Both roots considered
- Context and units stated

## One-minute revision

Form the equation carefully; the algebra can only be as correct as the model.

## Comic recap

- Situation: "Translate me into $ax^2+bx+c=0$."
- Factors: "Set each of us equal to zero."
- Formula: "Keep the entire discriminant under the square root."
- Final answer: "Check sign, meaning and units."
