# The Making of a Global World

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- trace global connections before modern globalisation
- explain trade, migration, technology, and disease in world history
- describe the nineteenth-century world economy
- understand indentured labour and colonial trade
- explain the Great Depression and post-war world order

## 1) pre-modern world

Global connections are not new. Travellers, traders, pilgrims, and migrants moved goods, ideas, skills, money, and diseases across regions.

The modern global world grew from these older connections.

## 2) silk routes and food travels

Silk routes connected Asia, Europe, and North Africa through trade and culture.

Food also travelled globally. Potatoes, maize, tomatoes, chillies, and other crops changed diets and economies after contact between continents.

## 3) conquest and disease

European conquest of the Americas was helped not only by weapons but also by diseases like smallpox, which devastated local populations.

Trade, conquest, and disease were connected parts of early global history.

## 4) nineteenth-century economy

The nineteenth-century world economy involved three flows:

- flow of goods
- flow of labour
- flow of capital

Industrial Europe needed food and raw materials. Colonies and other regions were reorganised to supply them.

## 5) technology and colonialism

Railways, steamships, telegraphs, and refrigeration changed trade and movement.

Technology made long-distance transport faster and cheaper, but colonial power shaped who benefited.

## 6) rinderpest and Africa

Rinderpest was a cattle plague that spread in Africa and destroyed cattle wealth.

It weakened livelihoods and helped European colonisers control land and labour.

## 7) indentured labour

Indentured labourers from India migrated to plantations in places such as the Caribbean, Fiji, Mauritius, and other colonies.

They signed contracts, but conditions were often harsh and close to forced labour.

## 8) Indian trade and colonialism

Colonial rule changed India's trade pattern. India exported raw materials and food grains and imported manufactured goods.

This supported British industry while hurting many Indian producers.

## 9) Great Depression and post-war order

The Great Depression of the 1930s caused falling production, unemployment, and trade decline.

After the Second World War, institutions linked with the Bretton Woods system tried to stabilise the world economy.

Decolonised countries later demanded a fairer international economic order.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Globalisation has a long history.
- Nineteenth-century economy had flows of goods, labour, and capital.
- Technology helped global trade but worked within imperial power.
- Indentured labour connected India to global plantations.
- Great Depression deeply affected trade, jobs, and agriculture.
- Bretton Woods shaped post-war economic recovery.

## common mistakes students make

1. Thinking globalisation began only recently.
2. Forgetting labour and capital flows.
3. Treating technology as neutral without colonial context.
4. Confusing indentured labour with voluntary modern migration.

## board tips

- For global flows, always write goods, labour, and capital.
- Use one example each for food travel, disease, and migration.
- In Great Depression answers, mention effects on workers and farmers.
