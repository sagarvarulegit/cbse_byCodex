# 8.2.4 Sex Determination

Different organisms use different mechanisms:

- In some reptiles, the incubation temperature of fertilised eggs determines
  sex.
- Some animals, such as snails, can change sex.
- In humans, sex is determined genetically by sex chromosomes.

## Human sex determination

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-8-6-sex-determination" />

Humans have 23 chromosome pairs:

- 22 pairs are autosomes;
- 1 pair is the sex-chromosome pair.

| Parent | Sex chromosomes | Gametes produced |
|---|---|---|
| Mother | `XX` | all eggs carry `X` |
| Father | `XY` | half sperm carry `X`, half carry `Y` |

- `X egg + X sperm -> XX` female child
- `X egg + Y sperm -> XY` male child

The probability is approximately `50% female : 50% male` for each fertilisation.
The father contributes the X or Y chromosome that determines the child's sex.
It is scientifically wrong to blame the mother.

## Why chromosomes are mismatched in males

The sex-chromosome pair in a human male consists of two different chromosomes,
X and Y. In a female it consists of two similar X chromosomes.

## Full-mark diagram answer

Write the parental chromosomes, show the mother's single egg type `X`, the
father's two sperm types `X` and `Y`, and show both products `XX` and `XY` with
equal probability.

## Common traps

- Sex is not chosen by a parent.
- “Father determines sex” means the type of fertilising sperm is decisive; it
  does not imply conscious control.
- Every pregnancy has an independent probability; earlier children do not
  alter the next outcome.
