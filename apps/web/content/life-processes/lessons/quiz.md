# Life Processes chapter quiz

Answer these questions after revising the Chapter 5 notes. They follow the NCERT exercise and key in-text checks.

## 1. Kidney system

The kidneys in human beings are part of the system for:

1. nutrition
2. respiration
3. excretion
4. transportation

Answer: 3
Topic: excretion-in-human-beings-and-plants
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Kidneys remove soluble nitrogenous wastes from blood, so they are part of the excretory system.

## 2. Xylem function

Xylem in plants is responsible for:

1. transport of water
2. transport of food
3. transport of amino acids
4. transport of oxygen only

Answer: 1
Topic: transportation-in-plants
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Xylem carries water and minerals from roots to other parts of the plant.

## 3. Autotrophic nutrition

Autotrophic nutrition in green plants requires:

1. carbon dioxide and water only
2. chlorophyll only
3. sunlight only
4. carbon dioxide, water, chlorophyll, and sunlight

Answer: 4
Topic: life-processes-and-nutrition-in-plants
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Photosynthesis needs carbon dioxide, water, sunlight, and chlorophyll.

## 4. Pyruvate breakdown

The breakdown of pyruvate to carbon dioxide, water, and energy takes place in:

1. cytoplasm
2. mitochondria
3. chloroplast
4. nucleus

Answer: 2
Topic: respiration-and-gas-exchange
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: When oxygen is present, pyruvate is broken down in mitochondria to release more energy.

## 5. Fats digestion

Where does fat digestion mainly take place in humans?

1. mouth
2. oesophagus
3. small intestine
4. large intestine

Answer: 3
Topic: heterotrophic-and-human-nutrition
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Bile emulsifies fats in the small intestine, and lipase breaks them into fatty acids and glycerol.

## 6. Saliva

Saliva helps digestion because it contains:

1. salivary amylase
2. bile
3. haemoglobin
4. urea

Answer: 1
Topic: heterotrophic-and-human-nutrition
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Salivary amylase starts digestion of starch into simpler sugar.

## 7. Anaerobic respiration

Yeast can respire anaerobically to produce:

1. lactic acid only
2. ethanol and carbon dioxide
3. oxygen and water
4. urea and salts

Answer: 2
Topic: respiration-and-gas-exchange
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: In yeast, anaerobic breakdown of pyruvate produces ethanol and carbon dioxide.

## 8. Alveoli design

Alveoli are suitable for gas exchange because they:

1. have thick walls and no blood supply
2. have large surface area, thin walls, and many capillaries
3. store bile
4. carry food through the gut

Answer: 2
Topic: respiration-and-gas-exchange
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Large surface area, thin walls, and rich blood supply allow fast diffusion of gases.

## 9. Haemoglobin deficiency

A deficiency of haemoglobin mainly reduces the blood's ability to carry:

1. oxygen
2. bile
3. starch
4. undigested food

Answer: 1
Topic: respiration-and-gas-exchange
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Haemoglobin in red blood cells transports oxygen from lungs to tissues.

## 10. Double circulation

Double circulation means:

1. blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle
2. food passes twice through the stomach
3. water passes twice through xylem
4. urine passes twice through the bladder

Answer: 1
Topic: transportation-in-human-beings
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Blood passes once through the heart-lung loop and once through the heart-body loop.

## 11. Phloem transport

Translocation in phloem requires energy because:

1. food is loaded into sieve tubes using ATP
2. water evaporates from leaves
3. blood pressure pushes it
4. alveoli exchange gases

Answer: 1
Topic: transportation-in-plants
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT in-text aligned
Explanation: Phloem transport uses ATP to load sugars into conducting tissue.

## 12. Nephron function

In a nephron, Bowman's capsule mainly:

1. stores bile
2. collects filtrate from blood
3. pumps blood to lungs
4. absorbs carbon dioxide from air

Answer: 2
Topic: excretion-in-human-beings-and-plants
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT in-text aligned
Explanation: Filtrate formed from blood enters Bowman's capsule before useful substances are reabsorbed.

## 13. Plant excretion

Which option is a correct plant excretion method?

1. storing wastes in cellular vacuoles or falling leaves
2. filtering blood through nephrons
3. making urine in a bladder
4. using haemoglobin

Answer: 1
Topic: excretion-in-human-beings-and-plants
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT exercise aligned
Explanation: Plants may store wastes in vacuoles, leaves that fall off, resins, gums, or release some wastes into soil.

## 14. Activity 5.2 control

In the two-bell-jar experiment, potassium hydroxide is placed beside one plant to:

1. produce oxygen

Topic: life-processes-and-nutrition-in-plants

## 15. Guard cells

A stomatal pore closes when:

1. water enters and guard cells swell
2. water leaves and guard cells shrink
3. chlorophyll enters the pore
4. xylem transports sucrose

Answer: 2
Topic: life-processes-and-nutrition-in-plants
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT Figure 5.3; CBSE 2023–2026 recurring MCQ
Explanation: Loss of water makes guard cells shrink, which closes the stomatal pore.

## 16. Villi assertion

Assertion: Villi in the small intestine are richly supplied with blood capillaries.  
Reason: Villi complete digestion of food.

1. both are true and the reason explains the assertion
2. both are true but the reason does not explain the assertion
3. assertion is true but reason is false
4. assertion is false but reason is true

Answer: 3
Topic: heterotrophic-and-human-nutrition
Difficulty: hard
Source: CBSE 2023 assertion-reason family
Explanation: Villi increase surface area for absorption of digested food; they do not themselves complete digestion.

## 17. Human anaerobic end product

When oxygen is insufficient in human muscle cells, pyruvate is converted into:

1. ethanol and carbon dioxide
2. lactic acid
3. carbon dioxide and water
4. glucose and oxygen

Answer: 2
Topic: respiration-and-gas-exchange
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE 2023–2026 recurring MCQ
Explanation: In oxygen-deficient muscle cells, incomplete breakdown of pyruvate produces the three-carbon compound lactic acid and less energy.

## 18. Vessel exception

Which vessel carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart?

1. aorta
2. pulmonary artery
3. pulmonary vein
4. renal vein

Answer: 2
Topic: transportation-in-human-beings
Difficulty: medium
Source: CBSE heart-pathway family
Explanation: The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs.

## 19. Lymph

Which statement about lymph is correct?

1. it contains many red blood cells and enters large arteries
2. it is colourless tissue fluid and its vessels finally return it to veins
3. it pumps blood under high pressure
4. it transports water through xylem

Answer: 2
Topic: transportation-in-human-beings
Difficulty: hard
Source: CBSE 2025 lymph MCQ family
Explanation: Lymph is colourless tissue fluid with some plasma, proteins and white blood cells; lymph vessels return it to the venous bloodstream.

## 20. Selective reabsorption

Which set is normally reabsorbed from nephron filtrate into blood?

1. blood cells and large plasma proteins only
2. glucose, amino acids, required salts and most water
3. only urea and uric acid
4. carbon dioxide and bile

Answer: 2
Topic: excretion-in-human-beings-and-plants
Difficulty: hard
Source: CBSE 2024–2026 nephron marking schemes
Explanation: Useful solutes and the water required by the body are selectively returned to blood; wastes and excess substances remain to form urine.
