# 3.2.2 What happens when Metals react with Water?

## Activity 3.10 / Figure 3.3

> Teacher-assisted safety: use only small samples. Sodium and potassium react
> violently. Never touch or collect them casually.

1. Test metal samples separately with cold water.
2. Test non-reacting samples with hot water.
3. Pass steam over the samples that still do not react.
4. Record fire, floating, gas and relative reaction rate.

Figure 3.3 passes steam over a heated metal sample and collects the gas over
water in an inverted gas jar.

## General reactions

`Metal + Water → Metal oxide + Hydrogen`

If the oxide dissolves:

`Metal oxide + Water → Metal hydroxide`

## Exact reactivity pattern with water

| Metal(s) | Condition and observation | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K, Na | Violent with cold water; exothermic heat ignites H₂ | Hydroxide + H₂ |
| Ca | Less violent with cold water; H₂ bubbles make it float | Ca(OH)₂ + H₂ |
| Mg | No reaction with cold water; reacts with hot water and floats due to H₂ bubbles | Mg(OH)₂ + H₂ |
| Al, Zn, Fe | Do not react with cold or hot water; react with steam | Metal oxide + H₂ |
| Pb, Cu, Ag, Au | Do not react with water | No reaction |

### Equations

`2K(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g) + heat`

`2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g) + heat`

`Ca(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g)`

`Mg(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Mg(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g)` (hot water)

`2Al(s) + 3H₂O(g) → Al₂O₃(s) + 3H₂(g)`

`3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)`

# 3.2.3 What happens when Metals react with Acids?

## Activity 3.11

[[demo:activity-3-11-metals-with-acid]]

Clean all samples except sodium and potassium. Put them in separate test tubes
containing dilute hydrochloric acid. Keep thermometer bulbs dipped in the acid
and compare:

- rate of bubble formation;
- highest temperature rise;
- decreasing reactivity.

`Metal + Dilute acid → Salt + Hydrogen`

### Equations with dilute hydrochloric acid

`Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂`

`2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂`

`Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂`

`Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂`

NCERT’s observed order is:

`Mg > Al > Zn > Fe`

Magnesium produces bubbles fastest and shows the greatest temperature rise.
Copper gives no bubbles and no temperature change because it does not react
with dilute HCl.

## Nitric acid exception

Metals generally do **not** evolve hydrogen with nitric acid. HNO₃ is a strong
oxidising agent: it oxidises the hydrogen produced to water and is reduced to
nitrogen oxides such as N₂O, NO or NO₂.

Exception: magnesium and manganese evolve H₂ with **very dilute** HNO₃.

## Do You Know — aqua regia

**Aqua regia** is a freshly prepared, highly corrosive mixture of concentrated
hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in a **3:1 ratio**. It dissolves
gold and platinum even though neither acid can do so alone.

# 3.2.4 How do Metals react with Solutions of other Metal Salts?

## Activity 3.12 / Figure 3.4

[[demo:activity-3-12-metal-salt-solutions]]

Put:

- a clean copper wire in iron(II) sulphate solution;
- a clean iron nail in copper(II) sulphate solution.

Compare after 20 minutes.

Iron displaces copper:

`Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)`

Evidence:

- blue CuSO₄ solution becomes pale green FeSO₄;
- a reddish-brown copper deposit appears on the iron nail.

Copper does not displace iron from FeSO₄, so that test tube shows no reaction.

## Displacement rule

`Metal A + salt solution of B → salt solution of A + metal B`

This happens only if A is more reactive than B. Therefore, iron is more
reactive than copper.

# 3.2.5 The Reactivity Series

The reactivity or activity series lists metals in decreasing order of
reactivity.

## Table 3.2 — complete series

| Order | Symbol | Metal | Relative position |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | K | Potassium | Most reactive |
| 2 | Na | Sodium |  |
| 3 | Ca | Calcium |  |
| 4 | Mg | Magnesium |  |
| 5 | Al | Aluminium |  |
| 6 | Zn | Zinc | Reactivity decreases downward |
| 7 | Fe | Iron |  |
| 8 | Pb | Lead |  |
| — | [H] | [Hydrogen] | Reference position |
| 9 | Cu | Copper |  |
| 10 | Hg | Mercury |  |
| 11 | Ag | Silver |  |
| 12 | Au | Gold | Least reactive |

Memory ladder: **Please Stop Calling Me A Zebra; Instead Learn How Copper
Mercury Silver Gold.** Use it only to recall the order; write proper names in
the exam.

## What the series predicts

- Higher metal displaces a lower metal from its compound.
- Metals above hydrogen usually liberate H₂ from dilute acids.
- Metals high in the series react more readily with water/oxygen, but
  protective oxide layers can affect visible rate.
- Extraction method depends on reactivity; this is developed in Section 3.4.

# NCERT Questions — after 3.2

## 1. Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil?

### Answer

Sodium reacts vigorously with oxygen and moisture and may catch fire. Kerosene
keeps it away from air and water.

## 2. Write equations for the reactions of

(i) iron with steam  
(ii) calcium and potassium with water.

### Answer

`3Fe + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂`

`Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂`

`2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂ + heat`

## 3. Four metals A, B, C and D were tested with FeSO₄, CuSO₄, ZnSO₄ and AgNO₃.

The observations show:

- A: no reaction with FeSO₄; displacement with CuSO₄
- B: displacement with FeSO₄; no reaction with ZnSO₄
- C: no reaction with FeSO₄, CuSO₄ or ZnSO₄; displacement with AgNO₃
- D: no reaction with any solution

### (i) Which is most reactive?

**B**.

### (ii) What happens when B is added to CuSO₄?

B displaces copper. Copper deposits and the blue colour of CuSO₄ changes
according to the colour of B’s sulphate solution.

### (iii) Decreasing order

`B > A > C > D`

Reason: a metal that displaces another from its salt lies above it.

## 4. Which gas is produced when dilute HCl is added to a reactive metal? Write the reaction of iron with dilute H₂SO₄.

### Answer

Hydrogen gas:

`Fe(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)`

## 5. What happens when zinc is added to iron(II) sulphate solution?

### Answer

Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it displaces iron. Grey iron deposits and
zinc sulphate forms:

`Zn(s) + FeSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Fe(s)`

## Exam decision path

1. Identify the reacting metal.
2. Locate it relative to the other metal or hydrogen.
3. Predict “displacement” or “no reaction.”
4. Balance the equation.
5. Add visible evidence if the question asks for observation.

## Common mistakes

- Writing hydrogen at the top of the metal series as though it were a metal.
- Saying calcium floats because it is less dense; NCERT says H₂ bubbles stick
  to its surface.
- Forgetting the HNO₃ exception.
- Writing iron(III) sulphate for iron with dilute sulphuric acid; the expected
  salt is FeSO₄.

## One-minute revision

Water reactions range from violent cold-water reactions to no reaction.
Metals above hydrogen usually release H₂ from dilute acids. A more reactive
metal displaces a less reactive one from its salt. Table 3.2 is the prediction
tool for all three patterns.
