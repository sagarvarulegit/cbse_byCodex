# 2.4 More about Salts

The salt on a dining table is also a raw material for sodium hydroxide,
bleaching powder, baking soda and washing soda. One common substance can lead
to an entire family of useful chemicals.

## 2.4 More about Salts

Salts form in several reactions in this chapter. Their properties depend on
the ions they contain and on the acid and base from which they are formed.

Salts with the same positive ion belong to one family. `NaCl`, `Na2SO4`,
`NaNO3` and `Na2CO3` are sodium salts. Salts with the same negative ion also
form a family. `NaCl` and `KCl` are chloride salts.

[JARGON: Salt family | Salts sharing the same positive ion or the same negative ion | Sodium chloride and sodium sulphate belong to the sodium-salt family]

| Parent acid and base | Expected salt nature | pH relation | Examples from Activity 2.14 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strong acid + strong base | Neutral | About `7` | `NaCl`, `KNO3` |

## 2.4.3 Chemicals from Common Salt

Sodium chloride (`NaCl`) is the salt used in food. Seawater contains it along
with other salts. Solid deposits also occur underground. Their large crystals
are often brown because of impurities and are called rock salt. Ancient rock
salt beds formed when seas dried up.

Common salt was also an important symbol in India's freedom struggle. Mahatma
Gandhi's Dandi March challenged the British salt law.

### Common salt - a raw material for chemicals

Common salt is used to make sodium hydroxide, baking soda, washing soda,
bleaching powder and other chemicals.

### Sodium hydroxide and the chlor-alkali process

When electricity passes through brine, or aqueous sodium chloride, it
decomposes:

`2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) -> 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g)`

The process is called chlor-alkali because it produces chlorine and the alkali
sodium hydroxide.

- chlorine gas forms at the anode
- hydrogen gas forms at the cathode
- sodium hydroxide solution forms near the cathode

### Bleaching powder

Chlorine reacts with dry slaked lime. The current 2026-27 NCERT text
represents bleaching powder as `Ca(ClO)2` and notes that its actual composition
is complex:

`2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2 -> Ca(ClO)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O`

Bleaching powder is used:

1. to bleach cotton and linen, wood pulp and washed clothes
2. as an oxidising agent in chemical industries
3. to make drinking water free from germs

> [!NOTE]
> Some older school sources use `CaOCl2` for bleaching powder. For a
> source-based answer, use the representation required by the question. These
> notes follow the current local NCERT reprint.

### Baking soda

Baking soda is sodium hydrogencarbonate, `NaHCO3`. It is a mild,
non-corrosive basic salt.

It is prepared using sodium chloride:

`NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 -> NH4Cl + NaHCO3`

On heating:

`2NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2`

Baking powder is a mixture of baking soda and a weak edible acid such as
tartaric acid. On heating or adding water, carbon dioxide forms:

`NaHCO3 + H+ -> CO2 + H2O + sodium salt of acid`

The carbon dioxide makes bread or cake rise and become soft and spongy.
Tartaric acid also prevents the bitter taste that sodium carbonate could
produce.

Uses of baking soda:

1. in baking powder
2. as an antacid
3. in soda-acid fire extinguishers

### Washing soda

Heating baking soda gives sodium carbonate. Recrystallising sodium carbonate
with water gives washing soda:

`Na2CO3 + 10H2O -> Na2CO3.10H2O`

Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate and is basic.

Uses of washing soda:

1. glass, soap and paper industries
2. manufacture of sodium compounds such as borax
3. domestic cleaning
4. removal of permanent hardness of water

## 2.4.4 Are the Crystals of Salts really Dry?

### Activity 2.15

1. Heat a few blue copper sulphate crystals in a dry boiling tube.
2. Observe the colour of the crystals and droplets inside the tube.
3. Add `2-3` drops of water to the heated sample.
4. Observe whether the blue colour returns.

The crystals turn white on heating because their water of crystallisation is
removed. Water droplets appear in the tube. Adding water restores the blue
colour.

[JARGON: Water of crystallisation | The fixed number of water molecules present in one formula unit of a salt | `CuSO4.5H2O` contains five water molecules per formula unit]

- hydrated copper sulphate: `CuSO4.5H2O`
- washing soda: `Na2CO3.10H2O`
- gypsum: `CaSO4.2H2O`

The `10H2O` in washing soda is a fixed part of the crystal formula. It does not
mean that the crystal is wet to touch.

### Plaster of Paris

Heating gypsum at `373 K` removes part of its water and forms calcium sulphate
hemihydrate, or Plaster of Paris:

`2CaSO4.2H2O --373 K-> 2CaSO4.1/2H2O + 3H2O`

Plaster of Paris is a white powder. Doctors use it to support fractured bones.
It is also used for toys, decoration and smooth surfaces.

When mixed with water, it changes back to hard gypsum:

`CaSO4.1/2H2O + 1 1/2H2O -> CaSO4.2H2O`

The half water molecule means that two formula units of `CaSO4` share one
water molecule.

**❓ QUESTIONS**

**1. What is the common name of the compound Ca(ClO)2?**

*Answer:* Bleaching powder.

**2. Name the substance which on treatment with chlorine yields bleaching powder.**

*Answer:* Dry slaked lime, `Ca(OH)2`.

**3. Name the sodium compound which is used for softening hard water.**

*Answer:* Washing soda, `Na2CO3.10H2O`.

**4. What will happen if a solution of sodium hydrocarbonate is heated? Give the equation of the reaction involved.**

*Answer:* Sodium hydrogencarbonate decomposes to form sodium carbonate, water
and carbon dioxide:

`2NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2`

**5. Write an equation to show the reaction between Plaster of Paris and water.**

*Answer:*

`CaSO4.1/2H2O + 1 1/2H2O -> CaSO4.2H2O`

The product is hard gypsum.

## Common mistakes

1. Inventing a textbook section `2.5`. The current chapter ends at `2.4.4`.
2. Calling every salt neutral.
3. Swapping the chlor-alkali electrodes: chlorine is at the anode; hydrogen is
   at the cathode.
4. Confusing baking soda (`NaHCO3`) with washing soda
   (`Na2CO3.10H2O`).
5. Writing gypsum and Plaster of Paris as the same formula.
6. Saying water of crystallisation makes a crystal physically wet.
7. Omitting the water and carbon dioxide products when baking soda is heated.

## Board tips

- Preparation answers should include the raw material, condition, balanced
  equation and product name.
- Chlor-alkali answers need all three products and both electrodes.
- For baking powder, explain why carbon dioxide makes the food rise.
- For Plaster of Paris storage, explain that moisture changes it into hard
  gypsum.
- Use the chemical representation required by the current source or the
  question's own convention.

## One-minute revision

- Salt pH depends on the parent acid and base.
- Brine electrolysis gives `Cl2`, `H2` and `NaOH`.
- Baking soda is `NaHCO3`; washing soda is `Na2CO3.10H2O`.
- Water of crystallisation is fixed in a crystal formula.
- Gypsum is `CaSO4.2H2O`; Plaster of Paris is `CaSO4.1/2H2O`.
- Preparation, observation, equation and use together make a complete answer.
