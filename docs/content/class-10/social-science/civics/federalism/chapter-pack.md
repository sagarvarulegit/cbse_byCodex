# Federalism

## 1) what is federalism?

Federalism is a system where power is divided between a central authority and constituent units such as states or provinces.

Both levels have their own jurisdiction and authority.

## 2) key features

Key features include:

- two or more levels of government
- same citizens governed by different levels for different matters
- constitutionally specified powers
- constitutional provisions cannot be changed by one level alone
- courts interpret the constitution
- revenue sources for each level
- aim to protect unity and regional diversity

## 3) types of federations

Coming-together federations are formed when independent states join to form a bigger unit. Examples commonly include the USA, Switzerland, and Australia.

Holding-together federations are formed when a large country divides power between central and regional governments. India is an example.

## 4) Indian federal structure

India has Union, State, and Concurrent Lists.

Union List: subjects of national importance like defence and foreign affairs.

State List: subjects of state importance like police and agriculture.

Concurrent List: subjects where both Union and State can make laws, such as education and forests.

Residuary subjects belong to the Union government.

## 5) language policy

India did not impose one national language. Hindi is an official language, and English continues for official purposes.

States were reorganised largely on linguistic basis, which helped strengthen unity by respecting diversity.

## 6) centre-state relations

Federalism in India became stronger through democratic politics, coalition governments, regional parties, and respect for state governments.

The central government cannot easily dismiss state governments for political reasons as before.

## 7) local government

Decentralisation means taking power away from central and state governments and giving it to local governments.

Panchayats work in rural areas. Municipalities and municipal corporations work in urban areas.

Local government helps people participate directly in decision-making.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Federalism divides power between levels of government.
- India is a holding-together federation.
- Union, State, and Concurrent Lists divide subjects.
- Language policy respects diversity.
- Decentralisation created a third tier of government.

## common mistakes students make

1. Calling India a unitary country only.
2. Confusing Union List and Concurrent List.
3. Writing Hindi is the national language.
4. Forgetting local government as a third tier.

## board tips

- Write key features in bullet points.
- In India questions, mention lists and constitutional status.
- For language policy, use careful wording: official language, not national language.
