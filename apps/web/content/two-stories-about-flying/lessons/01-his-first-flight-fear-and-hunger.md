# His First Flight: fear and hunger

The young seagull is stuck on the ledge because he is afraid. Hunger makes the problem sharper, but the story shows that fear is not the same as laziness.

## Story context

This part of the chapter follows a young seagull who wants food but does not want to fly. He watches the sea, the sky, and the family around him, but fear keeps him fixed in one place.

The key point is simple: he is afraid, not lazy. He wants to move, but he cannot cross the fear barrier until hunger and a real chance force action.

## Key idea

Fear can freeze a person or an animal. Hunger can create pressure, but pressure alone does not remove fear.

The lesson teaches that courage often begins after the first movement. The young seagull learns by acting, not by thinking about action only.

## Important moments

- The young seagull stays away from the edge.
- His family watches him and calls him out.
- He feels hunger and looks at the food.
- He notices that the family will not feed him from a distance.
- The story builds toward one brave attempt.

## Character or speaker insight

The young seagull is nervous, unsure, and physically trapped by his own fear. He is not refusing work or being lazy.

The family pressure is frustrating, but it also shows that the seagull must take responsibility for his own movement.

## Solved examples

### Example 1

Question: Why does the young seagull stay on the ledge?

Answer: He stays because he is afraid of flying and falling.

### Example 2

Question: What makes his hunger stronger?

Answer: He sees food but cannot reach it, so hunger becomes a real push.

### Example 3

Question: Is the seagull lazy?

Answer: No. He is afraid, and the story repeatedly shows that fear is the main problem.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying the seagull is lazy.
2. Forgetting that fear is the first barrier.
3. Missing the role of hunger.
4. Writing only one-word answers for why he does not fly.
5. Confusing family pressure with the final courage.

## Board tips

- Use the words fear, hunger, and courage in the answer.
- Give one reason first, then the result.
- Keep answers short and direct.
- Mention that action starts after pressure builds.

## Quick practice

1. Why does the young seagull refuse to fly?
2. How does hunger affect him?
3. Why is he not lazy?
4. What does the family do around him?
5. What is the main lesson of this story part?

## Answer check

1. He refuses to fly because he is afraid.
2. Hunger makes him want food and pushes him to act.
3. He is not lazy because fear, not carelessness, keeps him still.
4. The family watches him and pressures him to move.
5. The main lesson is that fear can be overcome when action finally begins.

## One-minute revision

- The young seagull is afraid.
- Hunger adds pressure.
- Fear is not laziness.
- The family watches and waits.
- One brave action changes everything.

## Comic recap

- Seagull Scout: "Fear is strong, but hunger is stronger today."
- Family Caller: "Come on, one step can start the flight."
- Sea Mirror: "Look at me, then trust yourself."
- Brave Wings: "Action begins where fear ends."

## Remember this line

The young seagull is afraid, but hunger and one brave move help him learn that he is not lazy.
