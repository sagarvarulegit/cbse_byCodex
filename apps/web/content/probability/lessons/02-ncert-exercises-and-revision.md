# NCERT Exercises and Chapter Revision

## Exercise 14.1

The twenty-five printed questions cover:

- probability statements and equally likely outcomes
- complements and valid probability values
- candies, birthdays, coloured balls and marbles
- coin, die, card and two-dice experiments
- fish, spinner and letter cards
- defective items with and without replacement
- a dartboard area model
- experimental probability tables
- three-coin outcomes and die-parity arguments

### Counting templates

**One die:** $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.

**Two different coins:** $\{HH,HT,TH,TT\}$.

**Two distinguishable dice:** $36$ ordered pairs $(i,j)$.

**Standard deck:** $52$ cards, $4$ suits, $13$ per suit, $26$ of each colour.

**Without replacement:** after the first draw, both the favourable count and total count may change.

### Examination-scope boundary

Question 20, the dartboard-area problem, carries the textbook's star marking for "not from the examination point of view." Keep that one question as enrichment. The two-dice table and argument questions remain useful sample-space reasoning practice.

## Common mistakes

- treating a second draw without replacement as if the first object returned
- counting unordered two-dice totals instead of $36$ ordered outcomes
- confusing empirical frequency with theoretical probability

## Board-answer checklist

- Experiment and sample space clear
- Outcomes equally likely or assumption stated
- Favourable count correct
- Final fraction simplified and within $[0,1]$

## One-minute revision

Most mistakes come from the denominator. Build the full sample space before counting the event.

## Comic recap

- Denominator: "Count every possible elementary outcome."
- Numerator: "Count only favourable outcomes."
- Replacement rule: "Tell me whether the first object returns."
- Reasonableness check: "Your answer must lie from zero to one."
