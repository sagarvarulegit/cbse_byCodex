# 5.2.2-5.2.4 Heterotrophic and human nutrition

Heterotrophs depend directly or indirectly on autotrophs for food. Their food is usually complex, so it must be broken into simpler molecules before it can be absorbed and used.

This lesson follows the textbook path from food strategies in organisms to the human alimentary canal.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- compare saprophytic, holozoic, and parasitic nutrition
- explain nutrition in Amoeba and Paramoecium
- trace food through the human alimentary canal
- state the roles of saliva, hydrochloric acid, pepsin, mucus, bile, pancreatic juice, villi, and large intestine
- explain Activity 5.3 on saliva and starch

## 5.2.2 Heterotrophic nutrition

Heterotrophs use complex food prepared by other organisms. Enzymes break complex substances into simpler ones.

Main strategies:
- saprophytic nutrition: food is broken down outside the body and absorbed
- holozoic nutrition: food is taken in and digested inside the body
- parasitic nutrition: food is taken from a host without immediately killing it

Textbook examples:
- fungi such as bread moulds, yeast, and mushrooms break food outside the body
- animals usually take in food and digest it internally
- Cuscuta, ticks, lice, leeches, and tapeworms use parasitic nutrition

## 5.2.3 How do organisms obtain their nutrition?

Amoeba uses temporary finger-like extensions called pseudopodia. These extensions surround a food particle and form a food vacuole.

Inside the food vacuole:
1. complex food is broken into simpler substances
2. useful substances diffuse into the cytoplasm
3. undigested material moves to the surface and is thrown out

Paramoecium has a more definite shape. Food enters at a specific spot and is moved there by cilia present on the cell surface.

Memory line:
`Amoeba grabs with pseudopodia; Paramoecium sweeps with cilia.`

## 5.2.4 Nutrition in human beings

Food passes through the alimentary canal:

`mouth -> oesophagus -> stomach -> small intestine -> large intestine -> anus`

The canal is a long tube, but each region has a special function.

## Mouth

In the mouth:
- teeth crush food into smaller particles
- saliva wets food and helps smooth movement
- salivary amylase begins starch digestion
- tongue mixes food with saliva

Salivary amylase breaks starch into simple sugar.

Dental caries:
If food particles and sugar remain in the mouth, bacteria can produce acid. Acid gradually damages tooth enamel and causes dental caries. Clean teeth reduce this risk.

## Oesophagus and peristalsis

Food moves from the mouth to the stomach through the oesophagus.

The lining of the canal has muscles that contract rhythmically to push food forward. This movement is called peristalsis.

Peristalsis occurs all along the gut, so food moves in a regulated way.

## Stomach

The stomach has muscular walls that mix food with gastric juice.

Gastric glands release:
- hydrochloric acid
- pepsin
- mucus

Hydrochloric acid:
- makes the medium acidic
- helps pepsin act on proteins
- helps kill many microbes entering with food

Pepsin digests proteins.

Mucus protects the stomach lining from acid under normal conditions.

## Small intestine

The small intestine is the site of complete digestion and absorption.

It receives:
- bile from the liver and gall bladder
- pancreatic juice from the pancreas
- intestinal juice from the intestinal wall

Bile:
- makes the food medium alkaline
- emulsifies fats into smaller droplets

Pancreatic juice contains enzymes for carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.

The walls of the small intestine have finger-like projections called villi. Villi increase surface area for absorption. Absorbed food enters blood and is carried to all cells.

Herbivores often have a longer small intestine because cellulose in grass is harder to digest. Carnivores such as tigers have a shorter small intestine because meat is easier to digest.

## Large intestine

The large intestine absorbs water from undigested food.

The remaining waste is removed through the anus.

## Questions and answers

Question:
What are the differences between autotrophic nutrition and heterotrophic nutrition?

Answer:
Autotrophic nutrition uses simple inorganic materials such as carbon dioxide and water to make food using an external energy source. Heterotrophic nutrition uses complex food prepared by other organisms and breaks it into simpler substances with enzymes.

Question:
Where do plants get each of the raw materials required for photosynthesis?

Answer:
Plants get carbon dioxide from the air mainly through stomata. They absorb water from the soil through roots. Sunlight comes from the Sun, and chlorophyll is present in chloroplasts in green parts of the plant.

Question:
What is the role of the acid in our stomach?

Answer:
Hydrochloric acid makes the stomach medium acidic so pepsin can digest proteins properly. It also helps kill many microbes that enter with food.

Question:
What is the function of digestive enzymes?

Answer:
Digestive enzymes act as biological catalysts. They break complex food molecules such as carbohydrates, proteins, and fats into simpler molecules that can be absorbed and used by the body.

Question:
How is the small intestine designed to absorb digested food?

Answer:
The inner lining of the small intestine has many finger-like projections called villi. Villi greatly increase the surface area for absorption and are richly supplied with blood vessels that carry absorbed food to body cells.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Saliva

Question:
What is the role of saliva in digestion?

Answer:
Saliva wets food and contains salivary amylase, which begins the digestion of starch into simple sugar.

### Example 2: Bile

Question:
How are fats digested in our body?

Answer:
Bile emulsifies large fat globules into small droplets in the small intestine. Pancreatic enzymes then digest the fats more effectively.

### Example 3: Villi

Question:
Why does the small intestine have villi?

Answer:
Villi increase the surface area for absorption of digested food into the blood.

### Example 4: Amoeba

Question:
How does Amoeba obtain nutrition?

Answer:
Amoeba surrounds food with pseudopodia, forms a food vacuole, digests food inside it, absorbs useful substances into cytoplasm, and throws undigested material out.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying saliva fully digests all food.
2. Forgetting that bile has no digestive enzyme but helps fat digestion.
3. Writing that absorption mainly happens in the stomach.
4. Mixing oesophagus with trachea.
5. Forgetting villi in absorption answers.

## One-minute revision

Heterotrophs need complex food to be broken into simpler molecules. Amoeba uses pseudopodia and a food vacuole; Paramoecium uses cilia to move food. In humans, digestion starts in the mouth with salivary amylase, proteins begin digestion in the stomach, complete digestion and absorption happen in the small intestine, and water is absorbed in the large intestine.
