# The Black Aeroplane: unknown helper

This lesson keeps the mystery alive. The black aeroplane appears, helps the pilot, and then disappears without a fixed explanation.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- explain the role of the black aeroplane
- describe the safe landing
- use the control room reveal in an answer
- keep the mystery without inventing certainty
- write a short inference-based response

## Story context

The pilot is lost in the storm and then a black aeroplane seems to guide him. Later, the control room says no other aeroplane was seen.

That gap between the rescue and the explanation is the main mystery of the story.

## Key idea

The story does not give a final clear answer. Good answers should respect that uncertainty.

We can say that the pilot was helped, but we should not force one fixed explanation if the text does not confirm it.

## Important moments

- The black aeroplane appears in the storm.
- The pilot follows it for safety.
- He lands safely after the guidance.
- The control room says no other aeroplane was seen.
- The mystery remains open.

## Character or speaker insight

The pilot is thankful, confused, and aware that the rescue does not fully make sense.

The control room gives the factual reveal, but it does not solve the mystery completely.

## Solved examples

### Example 1

Question: What helps the pilot land safely?

Answer: The black aeroplane seems to guide him through the storm.

### Example 2

Question: What does the control room say?

Answer: It says no other aeroplane was seen.

### Example 3

Question: Why should we keep the answer uncertain?

Answer: Because the story preserves mystery and does not confirm one fixed explanation.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying the mystery is fully solved.
2. Inventing a clear identity for the helper.
3. Forgetting the control room reveal.
4. Missing the safe landing.
5. Writing an answer that ignores uncertainty.

## Board tips

- Mention both the help and the mystery.
- Use the control room reveal as evidence.
- Avoid unsupported guesses.
- Say the story remains unexplained if the question asks about the ending.

## Quick practice

1. What appears in the storm?
2. How does the black aeroplane help?
3. What does the control room reveal?
4. Why is this part of the story mysterious?
5. How should you answer without inventing certainty?

## Answer check

1. A black aeroplane appears in the storm.
2. It guides the pilot toward safety.
3. The control room says no other aeroplane was seen.
4. It is mysterious because the rescue happens, but the helper is never clearly explained.
5. You should say the rescue is unexplained and keep the mystery intact.

- The explanation stays uncertain.
- Mystery is part of the lesson.
