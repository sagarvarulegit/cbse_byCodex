# How do Organisms Reproduce?

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain why reproduction is important
- compare asexual and sexual reproduction
- describe reproduction in unicellular organisms, plants, and humans
- understand reproductive health and contraception basics

## 1) why reproduction matters

Reproduction makes new individuals of the same species.

It is not needed for survival of one organism, but it is needed for survival of the species.

DNA copying is central to reproduction.

Small copying errors create variation.

## 2) importance of variation

Variation helps a species survive changing environments.

If all organisms were exactly identical, one disease or environmental change could harm all of them.

Sexual reproduction creates more variation than asexual reproduction.

## 3) asexual reproduction

Asexual reproduction uses one parent and produces very similar offspring.

### fission

One organism splits into two or more organisms.

Example:
Amoeba shows binary fission.

### fragmentation

Body breaks into fragments and each fragment grows into a new organism.

Example:
Spirogyra.

### regeneration

Some organisms can regrow body parts. If broken pieces grow into complete organisms, it becomes reproduction.

Example:
Planaria.

### budding

A small bud grows on the parent and separates.

Example:
Hydra.

### vegetative propagation

New plants grow from root, stem, or leaf.

Examples:
potato, sugarcane, rose.

### spore formation

Spores are tiny reproductive bodies protected by thick walls.

Example:
Rhizopus.

## 4) sexual reproduction

Sexual reproduction involves two parents and fusion of male and female gametes.

Fusion of gametes is fertilisation.

The fertilised egg is called zygote.

Advantages:
- more variation
- better chance of survival in changing conditions

## 5) sexual reproduction in flowering plants

Flower parts:
- sepals
- petals
- stamens
- carpel or pistil

Stamen is male reproductive part. It makes pollen.

Carpel has stigma, style, and ovary.

Pollination is transfer of pollen from anther to stigma.

Fertilisation happens inside ovule.

After fertilisation:
- ovule becomes seed
- ovary becomes fruit

## 6) reproduction in human beings

### male reproductive system

Main parts:
- testes
- vas deferens
- seminal vesicles
- prostate gland
- penis

Testes produce sperms and testosterone.

### female reproductive system

Main parts:
- ovaries
- oviducts
- uterus
- vagina

Ovaries produce eggs and female sex hormones.

Fertilisation usually occurs in the oviduct.

Embryo implants in the uterus.

Placenta helps exchange nutrients, oxygen, and wastes between mother and embryo.

## 7) reproductive health

Contraception helps prevent unwanted pregnancy and can reduce spread of sexually transmitted infections depending on method.

Examples:
- barrier methods
- oral pills
- intrauterine devices
- surgical methods

## exam-friendly summary notes

- DNA copying produces similarity and variation.
- Asexual reproduction needs one parent.
- Sexual reproduction creates more variation.
- Pollination is pollen transfer; fertilisation is gamete fusion.
- Ovule becomes seed; ovary becomes fruit.
- Placenta supports embryo development.

## common mistakes students make

1. Confusing pollination with fertilisation.
2. Saying regeneration is always reproduction.
3. Forgetting testes produce both sperm and testosterone.
4. Saying fertilisation occurs in uterus instead of oviduct.
5. Mixing contraception with reproductive health only for females.

## board tips

- Use diagrams for flower and human reproductive systems.
- Define zygote, embryo, pollination, and fertilisation clearly.
- Compare asexual and sexual reproduction in a table.
- In variation questions, connect to survival.

## solved practice set

### question 1
Why is variation useful?

Answer:
Variation helps some individuals survive when the environment changes.

### question 2
What happens after fertilisation in a flower?

Answer:
The ovule becomes seed and the ovary becomes fruit.

### question 3
Where does fertilisation occur in humans?

Answer:
Usually in the oviduct.
