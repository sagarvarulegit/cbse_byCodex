# Chapter 8 NCERT Answer Lab

## In-text questions after 8.1

### 1. Trait A occurs in 10% and B in 60% of an asexual population. Which arose earlier?

Trait B probably arose earlier and accumulated through more generations,
provided neither trait has a major survival advantage.

### 2. How does variation promote survival?

Environmental change does not affect every variant equally. A suitable variant
may survive and reproduce, maintaining the species.

## In-text questions after 8.2

### 1. How do Mendel's experiments show dominance and recessiveness?

Pure tall `TT × tt` short gives all tall `Tt` F1, so tall is dominant. Shortness
reappears as `tt` in F2, showing it is recessive, not destroyed.

### 2. How do Mendel's experiments show independent inheritance?

A two-trait cross gives new F2 combinations and the ratio `9:3:3:1`, showing
that the allele pair for one trait can assort independently of the other.

### 3. A man with blood group A and a woman with blood group O have a daughter with O. Is A or O dominant?

No. One family result is insufficient. The father could carry an unexpressed O
allele, but the observation alone does not establish which allele is dominant;
more inheritance evidence is needed.

### 4. How is a child's sex determined in humans?

All eggs carry X. Half the sperm carry X and half Y. X sperm produces XX
female; Y sperm produces XY male, with equal probability.

# Exercises

## 1. Tall violet × short white gives all violet but nearly half short. Tall parent's genotype?

**(c) `TtWW`.** Half short requires the tall parent to be `Tt`; all violet
requires it to supply dominant `W` to every offspring, so it is `WW`.

## 2. Light-eyed children have light-eyed parents. Can we say light eyes are dominant or recessive?

No. The same observation can be consistent with more than one parental
genotype. A controlled cross or wider pedigree evidence is needed.

## 3. Project to find the dominant coat colour in dogs

Choose dogs with known, contrasting coat colours; record parents and offspring
over many controlled matings; compare reciprocal crosses; follow F1 and F2 or
pedigrees; and identify the colour expressed in heterozygous offspring. Use a
large sample and ethical breeding records.

## 4. How do both parents contribute equally?

Each gamete contains one chromosome from each pair. At fertilisation, one
haploid set from the mother and one from the father unite, so each supplies half
the offspring's chromosomes and gene copies.

# One-page memory map

- variation accumulates across generations;
- genes transmit inherited traits;
- `TT × tt -> all Tt`;
- `Tt × Tt -> 1:2:1 genotype, 3:1 phenotype`;
- dihybrid F2 -> `9:3:3:1`;
- `gene -> protein -> trait`;
- gametes have one chromosome set;
- mother `XX`, father `XY`, child `XX` or `XY`.
