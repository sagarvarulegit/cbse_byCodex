# Power-sharing

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain why power-sharing is important in democracy
- compare Belgium and Sri Lanka
- distinguish majoritarianism from accommodation
- identify forms of power-sharing
- explain prudential and moral reasons for power-sharing

## 1) meaning of power-sharing

Power-sharing means distributing power among different organs, levels, groups, or parties so that no single person or group controls everything.

It is a basic principle of democracy because democracy involves participation and consent.

## 2) Belgium

Belgium has Dutch-speaking, French-speaking, and German-speaking communities.

The capital Brussels has a different language composition from the country as a whole, creating a special challenge.

Belgium handled diversity through accommodation and constitutional arrangements.

## 3) Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka has Sinhala-speaking majority and Tamil-speaking minority communities.

Majoritarian policies favoured Sinhala language and Buddhist identity, creating alienation among Tamils.

This led to conflict and showed the danger of ignoring minority demands.

## 4) Belgian accommodation

Belgium shared power through equal representation of Dutch and French-speaking ministers in the central government, strong regional governments, special arrangements for Brussels, and community government.

This reduced conflict by recognising diversity.

## 5) why power-sharing is desirable

Prudential reason: power-sharing reduces social conflict and protects political stability.

Moral reason: power-sharing is democratic because people affected by decisions should have a voice in those decisions.

## 6) forms of power-sharing

Major forms:

- among organs of government: legislature, executive, judiciary
- among levels of government: union, state, local
- among social groups: communities and minorities
- among political parties and pressure groups: coalitions, alliances, movements

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Belgium followed accommodation; Sri Lanka followed majoritarianism.
- Power-sharing reduces conflict and deepens democracy.
- Prudential reason is practical; moral reason is democratic principle.
- Horizontal sharing is among organs.
- Vertical sharing is among levels.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing power-sharing weakens democracy.
2. Confusing horizontal and vertical power-sharing.
3. Forgetting community government in Belgium.
4. Mixing prudential and moral reasons.

## board tips

- For comparison answers, use Belgium vs Sri Lanka columns.
- Define majoritarianism clearly.
- Write both prudential and moral reasons when asked "why desirable".
