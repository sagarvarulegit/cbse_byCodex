# Fire and Ice

## chapter goal

Understand how the poem uses fire and ice as symbols for destructive human emotions.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "A Letter to God" in `jeff101.pdf`. It summarises ideas without copying poem lines.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Main idea.
2. Fire as desire.
3. Ice as hatred.
4. Tone and brevity.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) main idea

The poem imagines two ways the world could be destroyed.

It quickly turns this large idea into a warning about emotions inside human beings.

## 2) fire

Fire suggests desire, greed, passion, and uncontrolled wanting.

When desire becomes extreme, it can harm people and society.

## 3) ice

Ice suggests hatred, coldness, cruelty, and lack of feeling.

Hatred can destroy as surely as uncontrolled desire.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Fire and ice are symbols.
- Fire is linked with desire.
- Ice is linked with hatred.
- The poem is brief but serious.
- The warning is about destructive emotions.

## common mistakes students make

1. Explaining the poem only as science.
2. Forgetting that both fire and ice are dangerous symbols.
3. Writing that one emotion is harmless.

## board tips

- Use symbol plus meaning in answers.
- Mention the universal warning.
- Do not over-explain with unsupported stories.

## short answer practice

1. What does fire symbolise?
2. What does ice symbolise?
3. Why is the poem powerful despite being short?

## mini quiz with answers

1. Fire suggests desire.
2. Ice suggests hatred.
3. Both symbols show destruction.
4. The poem warns against extreme emotions.
5. The tone is serious and compact.
