# 11.4 Ohm's Law

At constant temperature, current through a conductor is directly proportional
to potential difference across it:

`V ∝ I`, so `V = IR`.

Resistance `R=V/I`; unit ohm `Ω`. `1Ω` allows `1A` at `1V`.

Activity 11.1 varies current with a rheostat, records V and I, and plots V
against I. A straight line through origin confirms Ohm's law; its slope
`ΔV/ΔI = R`.

## Activity 11.2: component behaviour

Compare a nichrome wire and torch bulb. A filament's temperature changes, so
its V–I relationship need not remain perfectly linear. Ohm's law requires
unchanged physical conditions, especially temperature.

# 11.5 Factors on Which Resistance Depends

Activity 11.3 shows:

- `R ∝ l` for same material and area;
- `R ∝ 1/A` for same material and length;
- resistance depends on material.

Combining:

`R = ρl/A`

`ρ` is resistivity, unit `Ω m`; it depends on material and temperature, not the
wire's length or area.

## Materials

- metals such as copper/aluminium: low resistivity, good conductors;
- alloys such as nichrome: higher resistivity, high melting point, resist
  oxidation—useful heating elements;
- insulators: very high resistivity.

## Graph and scaling tricks

- double length -> double R;
- double diameter -> area ×4 -> R/4;
- slope of V-versus-I graph is R;
- slope of I-versus-V graph is `1/R`.

## NCERT numerical pattern

`V=12V`, `I=2.5mA=0.0025A`; `R=V/I=4800Ω=4.8kΩ`.
