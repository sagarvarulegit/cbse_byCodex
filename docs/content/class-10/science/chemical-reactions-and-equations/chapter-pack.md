# Chemical Reactions and Equations

## 1.1 Chemical equations

A chemical reaction happens when one or more substances change into new substances.

Signs of a chemical reaction:
- change in state
- change in colour
- gas formation
- temperature change
- precipitate formation

Example:
`Mg + O2 -> MgO`

Magnesium burns in oxygen and forms magnesium oxide.

## 1.1 continued - Balancing chemical equations

A chemical equation represents a reaction using symbols and formulae.

Reactants are written on the left. Products are written on the right.

Example:
`Zn + H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2`

This means zinc reacts with sulphuric acid to form zinc sulphate and hydrogen gas.

## 1.2.1-1.2.2 Combination and decomposition reactions

A chemical equation must obey the law of conservation of mass.

That means atoms are not created or destroyed. The number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides.

Example:
`H2 + O2 -> H2O` is not balanced.

Balanced form:
`2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O`

### easy method

1. Count atoms on both sides.
2. Balance one element at a time.
3. Do not change formulae. Only change coefficients.
4. Check again at the end.

Combination reaction: two or more reactants combine to form one product.

Example:
`CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2`

Decomposition reaction: one compound breaks into simpler substances.

Example:
`CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2`

Decomposition may happen due to heat, light, or electricity.

## 1.2.3-1.2.4 Displacement and double displacement reactions

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.

Example:
`Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu`

Two compounds can exchange ions.

Example:
`Na2SO4 + BaCl2 -> BaSO4 + 2NaCl`

`BaSO4` is a white precipitate.

## 1.2.5-1.3 Oxidation, reduction, and effects in daily life

Oxidation means gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen.

Reduction means loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.

Example:
`CuO + H2 -> Cu + H2O`

In this reaction:
- `CuO` is reduced to `Cu`
- `H2` is oxidised to `H2O`

Oxidation and reduction happen together, so the reaction is called a redox reaction.

## 1.3 Effects of oxidation in daily life

### corrosion

Corrosion is the slow damage of metals due to reaction with air, water, or chemicals.

Rusting of iron is the most common example.

Prevention:
- painting
- oiling or greasing
- galvanisation
- alloying

### rancidity

Rancidity is the spoilage of fats and oils due to oxidation.

Prevention:
- use airtight containers
- keep food away from light and heat
- add antioxidants
- flush packets with nitrogen

## exam-friendly summary notes

- A balanced equation has equal atoms of each element on both sides.
- Combination: many reactants, one product.
- Decomposition: one reactant, many products.
- Displacement depends on reactivity.
- Precipitation is often a double displacement reaction.
- Oxidation and reduction occur together.

## common mistakes students make

1. Changing chemical formulae while balancing.
2. Forgetting state symbols when asked.
3. Calling every gas-forming reaction a decomposition reaction.
4. Confusing oxidation with reduction.
5. Writing corrosion only for iron, though other metals also corrode.

## board tips

- Balance equations neatly and show final check.
- Mention the visible observation when a reaction asks for identification.
- In redox questions, clearly state which substance is oxidised and which is reduced.
- Use examples with correct formulae.

## solved practice set

### question 1
Balance: `Fe + H2O -> Fe3O4 + H2`

Answer:
`3Fe + 4H2O -> Fe3O4 + 4H2`

### question 2
Name the reaction: `2Pb(NO3)2 -> 2PbO + 4NO2 + O2`

Answer:
Thermal decomposition reaction.

### question 3
Why is rancidity an oxidation reaction?

Answer:
Fats and oils react with oxygen and get spoiled. Since oxygen is involved, it is oxidation.
