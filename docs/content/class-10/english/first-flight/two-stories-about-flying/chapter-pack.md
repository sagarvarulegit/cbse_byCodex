# Two Stories about Flying

## chapter goal

By the end of this unit, a student should be able to:
- explain how fear is overcome in "His First Flight"
- describe the mystery and suspense in "The Black Aeroplane"
- compare literal and emotional meanings of flying
- revise "How to Tell Wild Animals" and "The Ball Poem"
- answer inference questions on courage, risk, loss, and humour

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jeff103.pdf`: "His First Flight", "The Black Aeroplane", "How to Tell Wild Animals", and "The Ball Poem".

1. "His First Flight": fear and hunger

## 1) His First Flight

The young seagull is afraid to fly, though his siblings have already flown.

His parents scold, threaten, and tempt him because they know he must overcome fear to survive.

Hunger finally pushes him to dive toward his mother and discover that his wings can support him.

## 2) meaning of the first flight

The first flight is not only physical. It is a moment of self-confidence.

The story shows that fear often disappears only when we act.

## 3) The Black Aeroplane

A pilot flies into storm clouds while returning to England.

His instruments fail, he is lost, and then a mysterious black aeroplane guides him safely.

At the end, he learns no other plane was seen. This creates mystery.

## 4) theme of mystery

The story can be read as a miracle, imagination under stress, or an unexplained rescue.

Its suspense comes from danger, darkness, failed instruments, and the unknown pilot.

## 5) poem: How to Tell Wild Animals

This humorous poem gives playful and exaggerated ways to identify wild animals.

It uses rhyme, irony, and comic danger to make learning funny.

## 6) poem: The Ball Poem

The poem is about a boy losing his ball and learning the first lesson of loss.

The ball symbolises childhood attachments. The poem teaches that some losses cannot simply be replaced by money.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The seagull learns courage through action.
- The pilot is saved by an unexplained black aeroplane.
- "How to Tell Wild Animals" uses humour and exaggeration.
- "The Ball Poem" is about accepting loss and growing up.

## common mistakes students make

1. Saying the seagull was lazy instead of afraid.
2. Giving a fixed explanation for the black aeroplane when the story keeps it mysterious.
3. Taking the wild animal poem literally.
4. Writing that the boy only loses a toy in "The Ball Poem".

## board tips

- In courage answers, show fear before action and confidence after action.
- In mystery answers, preserve the uncertainty.
- For poems, identify symbol and tone.

## short answer practice

1. Why was the young seagull afraid?
2. How did his mother make him fly?
3. Why is the black aeroplane mysterious?
4. How does humour work in "How to Tell Wild Animals"?
5. What does the ball symbolise?

## mini quiz with answers

1. Hunger helps the young seagull take his first flight.
2. The pilot is flying to England.
3. The black aeroplane guides him through the storm.
4. "How to Tell Wild Animals" is humorous.
5. The lost ball symbolises loss and growing up.
