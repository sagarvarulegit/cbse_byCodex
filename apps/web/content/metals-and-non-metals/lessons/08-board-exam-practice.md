# Board Exam Practice — Verified Questions

These questions were checked against the original paper pages and official
CBSE marking schemes. Write each answer before reading the platform marking
points. For an **OR** question, attempt one complete option only.

## PYQ 1 — 2023, 31/1/1, Q5, 1 mark

A metal X is used in the thermit process. When X burns in air, it gives an
amphoteric oxide Y. Identify X and Y.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-1]]

### Platform marking point

**X = aluminium; Y = aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃.** Aluminium powder reduces iron
oxide in the thermit reaction, and Al₂O₃ reacts with acids as well as bases.

## PYQ 2 — 2023, 31/1/1, Q37, 4 marks

Metals form oxides with different behaviour and reactivity.

(a) What happens when copper is heated in air? Give the equation.  
(b) Why are some metal oxides called amphoteric? Give one example.  
(c) Complete:

`Na₂O + H₂O → ?`

`Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → ?`

**OR:** answer the source subpart about sulphur burning in oxygen, the gas,
nature and action on dry litmus.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-2]]

### Platform marking points

Main option:

- copper develops black CuO: `2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO`;
- amphoteric oxides react with both acids and bases; Al₂O₃ or ZnO;
- `Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH`;
- `Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O`.

OR option:

- `S + O₂ → SO₂`;
- gas is sulphur dioxide, an acidic oxide;
- dry litmus shows no change because water is needed to produce H⁺ ions.

## PYQ 3 — 2023, 31/2/1, Q28, 3 marks

Using equations, give the two main differences between roasting and
calcination. How is the metal reduced from the product formed?

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-3]]

### Platform marking points

- Roasting: sulphide ore, excess air:
  `2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂`.
- Calcination: carbonate ore, limited/no air:
  `ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂`.
- Reduce the oxide, for example:
  `ZnO + C → Zn + CO`.

The official scheme also accepts suitable Hg/Cu/Ca/Pb examples.

## PYQ 4 — 2023, 31/1/1, Q17, Assertion–Reason

Assertion: Copper sulphate solution turns colourless when lead is added.  
Reason: Lead is more reactive than copper and displaces it.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-4]]

### PYQ 4 answer check

### Platform marking points

The **Assertion is false but the Reason is true**. Lead can displace copper,
but sparingly soluble lead sulphate forms a coating and the solution does not
simply become colourless as claimed.

## PYQ 5 — 2024, 31/3/1, Q27, 3 marks

Give reasons:

(a) Zinc oxide is amphoteric.  
(b) Sodium is stored in kerosene.  
(c) Metals generally do not evolve hydrogen with nitric acid.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-5]]

### Platform marking points

- ZnO reacts with both acids and bases to give salt and water.
- Sodium reacts vigorously with air/moisture and may catch fire; kerosene
  isolates it.
- HNO₃ is a strong oxidising agent and oxidises the produced H₂ to water.

One precise reason for each part earns the three marks.

## PYQ 6 — 2024, 31/3/1, Q28, 3 marks

Attempt either:

**A.** State, with reason, the reduction process used to obtain mercury,
copper and sodium from their compounds.

**OR**

**B.** Explain the changed appearance of silver, copper and iron after
exposure to atmospheric air.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-6]]

### Platform marking points

Option A:

- Hg: heat HgO formed by roasting cinnabar; the oxide decomposes.
- Cu: roast Cu₂S and use self-reduction with Cu₂O.
- Na: electrolytic reduction of molten NaCl because sodium is too reactive for
  carbon reduction.

Option B:

- silver: black Ag₂S;
- copper: green basic copper carbonate;
- iron: brown flaky hydrated iron oxide (rust).

## PYQ 7 — 2025, 31/4/3, Q22, 2 marks

Attempt either:

**A.** Show calcium chloride formation by electron transfer. Atomic numbers:
Ca = 20, Cl = 17.

**OR**

**B.** Justify that aluminium oxide is amphoteric using equations.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-7]]

### Platform marking points

Option A:

`Ca (2,8,8,2) → Ca²⁺ (2,8,8) + 2e⁻`

`2Cl (2,8,7) + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ (2,8,8)`

`Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → CaCl₂`

Option B:

`Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O`

`Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O`

## PYQ 8 — 2025, 31/1/2, Q30, 3 marks

Attempt either:

**A.** With an activity, explain the conditions under which iron rusts.

**OR**

**B.** Name two metals that react violently with cold water, list three
observations, and test the gas evolved.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-8]]

### Platform marking points

Option A:

- Tube A: nails + water + air → rust.
- Tube B: boiled water under oil, no dissolved air → no rust.
- Tube C: dry air over anhydrous CaCl₂, no water → no rust.
- Conclusion: both air and water are necessary.

Option B:

- any two of sodium, potassium and lithium;
- violent reaction, much heat, evolved hydrogen catches fire;
- hydrogen gives a pop with a burning splint.

## PYQ 9 — 2025, 31/1/2, Q31, 3 marks

(a) Justify that displacement reactions help extract middle-activity metals,
using two examples.  
(b) Why can carbon not reduce oxides of high-activity metals?

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-9]]

### Platform marking points

Any two correct aluminothermic displacement examples, such as:

`3MnO₂ + 4Al → 3Mn + 2Al₂O₃ + heat`

`Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + heat`

High-activity metals such as Na, Mg and Ca have greater affinity for oxygen
than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce their oxides.

## PYQ 10 — 2025, 31/3/3, Q34, 5 marks

Attempt either:

**A.** From K, Ca, Al, Cu, Ag and Fe, select the metal that: does not react
with oxygen at high temperature; forms a protective oxide at room
temperature; catches fire in air; becomes coated with black oxide when hot.
Then define amphoteric oxide with Al₂O₃ equations and define an alkali.

**OR**

**B.** Give equations for extraction of mercury from cinnabar and copper from
its sulphide ore. Name the corrosion coatings on silver and copper.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-10]]

### Platform marking points

Option A:

- Ag, Al, K, Cu respectively;
- amphoteric definition;
- both Al₂O₃ equations with acid and base;
- alkali = water-soluble base; NaOH or KOH.

Option B:

- `2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂`;
- `2HgO → 2Hg + O₂`;
- `2Cu₂S + 3O₂ → 2Cu₂O + 2SO₂`;
- `2Cu₂O + Cu₂S → 6Cu + SO₂`;
- silver sulphide and basic copper carbonate.

## PYQ 11 — 2026, 31/3/2, Q25, 2 marks

Comment, with reasons, on the physical nature and melting/boiling points of
ionic compounds.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-11]]

### Platform marking points

- They are hard solids but generally brittle because strong electrostatic
  attraction holds oppositely charged ions in a rigid lattice.
- They have high melting and boiling points because much energy is needed to
  overcome strong inter-ionic attraction.

## PYQ 12 — 2026, 31/3/2, Q28, 4 marks

An electrochemical process refines copper, zinc, tin, nickel, silver and gold.

(a) Define anode mud.  
(b) Name the anode and cathode for copper.  
(c) Attempt either copper extraction from Cu₂S with equations **or** draw and
label copper electrolytic refining.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-12]]

### Platform marking points

- Anode mud: insoluble impurities settling below the anode.
- Anode: impure copper rod; cathode: pure copper strip.
- Extraction option: the two Cu₂S/Cu₂O equations in PYQ 10.
- Diagram option must label the impure Cu anode, pure Cu cathode, acidified
  CuSO₄ electrolyte and anode mud.

## PYQ 13 — 2026, 31/4/1, Q27, 3 marks

Attempt either:

**A.** Name the processes that convert a carbonate ore and a sulphide ore to
oxides; write the thermit equation; name the anode and cathode in copper
refining.

**OR**

**B.** Show extraction of zinc from either a sulphide or carbonate ore and
explain why HNO₃ usually gives no H₂ with metals.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-pyq-13]]

### Platform marking points

Option A:

- carbonate → calcination; sulphide → roasting;
- `Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + heat`;
- impure-copper anode and pure-copper cathode.

Option B:

- sulphide route: roasting then `ZnO + C → Zn + CO`; or
- carbonate route: calcination then the same reduction;
- HNO₃ oxidises produced hydrogen to water.

# Latest official SQP practice — 2025–26

## SQP 1 — Q25, 2 marks

In Activity 3.5, an aluminium wire is clamped, a pin is attached with wax and
the clamped end is heated.

(a) If silver replaces aluminium, will the observation change? Why?  
(b) Will the wire melt? Why?

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-sqp-1]]

### Official marking points

- The pin still falls, but sooner because silver conducts heat better than
  aluminium.
- The wire does not melt because metals have high melting points under the
  activity conditions.

## SQP 2 — Q26, 3 marks

Attempt either:

**A.** Element X is stored in kerosene, cannot be extracted using a reducing
agent and forms an ionic chloride. Can it be stored in water? Identify X,
name the extraction process and write electrode equations.

**OR**

**B.** Explain the green colour of old copper domes; decide whether iron
should replace copper; explain why thin copper sheets suit domes.

### Write before checking

[[response:mnm-sqp-2]]

### Official marking points

Option A:

- cannot store in water; it reacts violently;
- X is sodium (potassium also accepted);
- electrolytic reduction of molten NaCl;
- `Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na` and `2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻`.

Option B:

- basic copper carbonate forms by corrosion;
- do not replace with iron: rust flakes away, while copper’s green layer
  protects the underlying metal;
- copper is malleable and can form thin shaped sheets.
