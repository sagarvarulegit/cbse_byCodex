# 7.3.3 Reproduction in Human Beings

Humans reproduce sexually. Reproductive tissues mature during adolescence as
the rate of general body growth begins to slow. This period is called puberty.

## Puberty and sexual maturation

Changes common to many boys and girls include:

- hair growth in armpits and the genital region
- thinner hair on arms, legs, or face
- oilier skin and pimples
- new awareness of one's own body and others

Changes described in girls include breast development, darkening around the
nipples, and the start of menstruation.

Changes described in boys include thicker facial hair, voice cracking, and
maturation of the penis.

These changes occur gradually and at different ages and rates. Differences
between individuals are normal.

## 7.3.3 (a) Male Reproductive System

[[figure:figure-7-10-male-reproductive-system]]

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Testes | produce sperms and testosterone |
| Scrotum | holds testes outside the abdomen at a temperature lower than normal body temperature, needed for sperm formation |
| Vas deferens | carries sperms away from each testis |
| Seminal vesicles | add nutrient-containing fluid that supports sperm transport |
| Prostate gland | adds fluid to semen and helps make sperm transport easier |
| Urethra | common passage for urine and semen, though not at the same time |
| Penis | delivers semen into the female reproductive tract |

Testosterone helps regulate sperm formation and produces several puberty changes
in boys.

## Sperm structure and pathway

A sperm is a tiny male germ cell containing genetic material. Its long tail
helps it swim towards the female germ cell.

Board-ready pathway:

`testis -> vas deferens -> urethra -> penis -> vagina -> uterus -> oviduct`

The seminal vesicles and prostate add fluid along the route; the fluid provides
nutrition and improves transport.

## Why testes are outside the abdominal cavity

Sperm formation requires a temperature lower than the normal body temperature.
The scrotum provides this cooler location.

## Two functions of testes

1. formation of male germ cells or sperms
2. secretion of testosterone, which supports sperm formation and male puberty
   changes

## Common mistakes

1. Saying sperms are formed in the seminal vesicles.
2. Saying the scrotum makes testosterone.
3. Omitting lower temperature from the scrotum explanation.
4. Saying seminal fluid contains no nutrition.
5. Writing that urine and sperm pass together.
