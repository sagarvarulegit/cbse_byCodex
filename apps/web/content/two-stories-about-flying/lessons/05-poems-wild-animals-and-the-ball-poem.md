# Poems: Wild Animals and The Ball Poem

This page covers two poems from the same First Flight unit. One uses humour and exaggeration in How to Tell Wild Animals. The other uses loss and growing up in The Ball Poem.

## Story context

How to Tell Wild Animals is a playful poem that uses funny clues to describe animals. The Ball Poem is quieter and more thoughtful, focusing on a child who loses a ball and begins to understand loss.

Together, the poems show very different tones, but both are easy to ask about in exams.

## Key idea

The first poem depends on humour and exaggeration. The second poem depends on feeling, symbol, and maturity.

If you remember the tone, symbol, and theme, most questions become easier.

## Important moments

- The poet describes animals in a funny way.
- The exaggeration creates humour.
- The child loses the ball.
- The loss makes the child think deeply.
- Growing up begins with understanding that some things cannot return.

## Character or speaker insight

In How to Tell Wild Animals, the speaker sounds playful and joking.

In The Ball Poem, the speaker sounds serious, reflective, and gentle because the poem is about loss and growing up.

## Solved examples

### Example 1

Question: Why is How to Tell Wild Animals funny?

Answer: It uses humour and exaggerated clues to describe animals.

### Example 2

Question: What does the ball stand for in The Ball Poem?

Answer: It stands for loss, responsibility, and growing up.

### Example 3

Question: Why are the tones different?

Answer: One poem is playful, while the other is thoughtful and emotional.

## Common mistakes

1. Treating How to Tell Wild Animals like a serious science poem.
2. Forgetting the exaggeration.
3. Saying The Ball Poem is only about a toy.
4. Missing the idea of loss and growing up.
5. Mixing the tones of the two poems.

## Board tips

- Name the poem before giving the point.
- Use humour for the first poem and loss for the second.
- Mention symbol when discussing the ball.
- Keep tone words clear: playful, humorous, reflective, serious.
