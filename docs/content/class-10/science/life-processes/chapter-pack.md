## Chapter mastery map

### 5.1 What are Life Processes?

Students must explain:

- visible and molecular movement as evidence of life
- continuous maintenance of organised living structures
- why diffusion is too slow for large multicellular bodies
- roles of nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion

### 5.2 Nutrition

Students must:

- compare autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition
- write the board-ready photosynthesis equation:
  `6CO2 + 12H2O --sunlight/chlorophyll--> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O`
- list absorption of light, conversion to chemical energy with water splitting,
  and reduction of carbon dioxide
- explain chlorophyll, carbon-dioxide and sunlight investigations with
  controls, observations and conclusions
- describe stomata and guard-cell movement
- explain saprophytic, holozoic and parasitic strategies
- sequence nutrition in Amoeba and the human alimentary canal
- name the source and role of saliva, hydrochloric acid, pepsin, mucus, bile,
  pancreatic amylase, trypsin and lipase
- distinguish digestion from absorption and explain villi

### 5.3 Respiration

Students must:

- distinguish breathing from cellular respiration
- explain ATP as usable cellular energy
- trace glucose to pyruvate in cytoplasm and all three pyruvate pathways
- explain why aerobic respiration yields more energy
- describe lime-water and yeast activities
- compare aquatic and terrestrial oxygen intake
- explain cartilage rings, residual air, alveoli and haemoglobin
- describe inhalation and exhalation using ribs, diaphragm and chest volume

### 5.4 Transportation

Students must:

- trace blood through all four heart chambers and named vessels
- explain valves, ventricular wall thickness and double circulation
- compare fishes, amphibians/reptiles, birds and mammals
- compare arteries, veins and capillaries and know pulmonary exceptions
- explain platelets, blood pressure and lymph
- trace water and minerals through continuous xylem channels
- distinguish root pressure from transpiration pull
- explain ATP-dependent phloem loading and source-to-sink translocation

### 5.5 Excretion

Students must:

- label kidneys, renal vessels, ureters, bladder and urethra
- label a nephron and explain glomerular filtration
- explain selective reabsorption and urine-volume regulation
- trace urine to the urethra and state nervous control of bladder emptying
- explain dialysis and its limitation
- list all NCERT plant-waste routes
- treat the organ-donation box respectfully and accurately

## Full-mark answer rules

1. Use one biological marking point per available mark.
2. Name both the structure and its function.
3. For a pathway, include direction, vessel/tissue and start/end locations.
4. For an activity, write setup, control, observation, conclusion and safety
   where relevant.
5. For a diagram, draw neat outlines and place readable labels outside it.
6. For comparisons, use the exact basis requested in the question.
7. Do not write that bile contains a digestive enzyme, that all arteries carry
   oxygenated blood, or that villi complete digestion.

## Known high-risk board traps now covered

- Activity 5.2 is the KOH/carbon-dioxide investigation, not the sunlight design
  prompt below it.
- The glucose-to-pyruvate step occurs in cytoplasm for both aerobic and
  anaerobic respiration.
- Human muscle anaerobic respiration forms lactic acid, not ethanol.
- Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart.
- Lymph returns to larger veins and normally has no red blood cells.
- Renal artery brings waste-bearing oxygenated blood to kidneys.
- Blood cells and large proteins do not enter normal glomerular filtrate.
- Emptying of the bladder is under nervous, not hormonal, control.
- Phloem loading uses ATP; xylem ascent does not use ATP in that way.
- Plants have several excretion strategies, not only gaseous diffusion.
