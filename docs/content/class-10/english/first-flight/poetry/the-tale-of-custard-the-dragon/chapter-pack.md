# The Tale of Custard the Dragon

## chapter goal

Understand how a comic ballad shows that real courage appears in action.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "Madam Rides the Bus" in `jeff107.pdf`.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Characters.
2. Custard's fear.
3. Pirate episode.
4. Humour and ballad style.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) characters

Belinda and her pets present themselves as brave, while Custard seems timid.

This sets up comic contrast.

## 2) Custard's courage

When real danger appears, Custard acts bravely.

The poem shows that true courage is proved by action, not boasting.

## 3) humour

The poem uses rhyme, repetition, exaggeration, and playful character names.

Its funny tone does not hide the message about real bravery.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Custard is called cowardly but acts bravely.
- Other characters boast more than they prove.
- The pirate episode reveals true courage.
- The poem is a comic ballad.

## common mistakes students make

1. Calling Custard a coward after the ending.
2. Ignoring humour and rhyme.
3. Forgetting the contrast between words and action.

## board tips

- Use courage in action as the main answer point.
- Mention comic ballad features.
- Compare Custard with the boasting pets.

## short answer practice

1. Why is Custard underestimated?
2. How does Custard prove courage?
3. What makes the poem humorous?

## mini quiz with answers

1. Custard is a dragon.
2. He proves brave during danger.
3. The poem uses humour.
4. Boasting is not the same as courage.
5. It has ballad-like storytelling.
