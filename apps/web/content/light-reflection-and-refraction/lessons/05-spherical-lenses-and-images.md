# 9.3.3 Refraction by Spherical Lenses

- Convex lens: thicker at centre; converges parallel rays.
- Concave lens: thinner at centre; diverges parallel rays.
- Optical centre `O`: central point; a ray through O emerges undeviated.
- Principal axis: line through centres of curvature.
- Each lens has two foci, `F1` and `F2`.
- Focal length `f = OF`; aperture is effective lens diameter.

# 9.3.4 Image Formation by Lenses

## Convex lens

| Object | Image | Size | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| infinity | F2 | point | real, inverted |
| beyond 2F1 | F2–2F2 | diminished | real, inverted |
| at 2F1 | at 2F2 | same | real, inverted |
| F1–2F1 | beyond 2F2 | enlarged | real, inverted |
| at F1 | infinity | highly enlarged | real, inverted |
| O–F1 | same side as object | enlarged | virtual, erect |

## Concave lens

At infinity: point-sized virtual erect image at F1. At any finite distance:
virtual, erect, diminished image between O and F1.

## Activities 9.11–9.13

- Focus a distant object with convex lens to estimate focal length.
- Move an object through all convex-lens positions and tabulate images.
- Observe the always virtual, erect, diminished concave-lens image.

## Half-covered lens question

Covering half a convex lens still produces the **complete image**, because rays
from every object point pass through every uncovered part. The image becomes
dimmer because less light passes; its size and position remain unchanged.
