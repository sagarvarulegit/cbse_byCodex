# Chapter 3: Money and Credit

## Hook

A shoe-maker wants wheat, but the farmer does not want shoes. How can they trade? Money breaks this deadlock. Credit can then help people produce more—but the wrong loan can also trap them in debt.

## Chapter goal

This chapter explains why money is accepted in exchange, how currency and bank deposits work, how banks connect savers with borrowers, why credit may help or harm, how loan terms differ, and why formal credit and Self Help Groups matter for fair development.

## MONEY AS A MEDIUM OF EXCHANGE

In barter, each person must want exactly what the other person offers. This is called double coincidence of wants. Money removes this condition because a person can sell for money and then use the money to buy something else.

Money acts as an intermediate step in exchange: `goods or service → money → another good or service`.

## MODERN FORMS OF MONEY

Modern money includes currency and deposits with banks. Unlike old commodity money, notes and coins do not need to be made of something valuable. Their acceptance rests on legal authority and public trust.

### Currency

In India, the Reserve Bank of India issues currency notes on behalf of the Central Government. No other individual or organisation is allowed to issue currency. The law makes the rupee a legal medium of payment, so it cannot be refused for settling transactions in India.

### Deposits with Banks

People keep spare money in bank accounts because it is safe and earns interest. Demand deposits can be withdrawn whenever the account holder needs them. They are money because payments can be made directly from them.

## LOAN ACTIVITIES OF BANKS

Banks keep only a small part of deposits as cash for daily withdrawals. They use the major part to give loans. In this way, banks connect people who have surplus money with people who need money.

Banks pay interest to depositors and charge a higher interest on loans. The difference between these rates is an important source of bank income.

## TWO DIFFERENT CREDIT SITUATIONS

Credit is an agreement in which a lender supplies money, goods or services now and the borrower promises repayment later. Its result depends on risk and whether the activity earns enough to repay the loan.

### (1) Festival Season

Salim takes credit to obtain raw material for shoe production before the festival season. Sales go well, so he repays the loan and earns a profit. Credit helps him meet working-capital needs and raises his income.

### (2) Swapna’s Problem

Swapna borrows for cultivation, but crop failure prevents repayment. The debt grows and she must sell land. Credit makes her worse off because risk turns the loan into a debt trap.

## TERMS OF CREDIT

Every loan has conditions. The main terms of credit are the interest rate, collateral, documentation, repayment period and method of repayment.

Collateral is an asset owned by the borrower—such as land, a building, a vehicle, livestock or bank deposits—that the lender may claim if the loan is not repaid.

## FORMAL SECTOR CREDIT IN INDIA

Formal lenders include banks and cooperatives. Informal lenders include moneylenders, traders, employers, relatives and friends.

The Reserve Bank of India supervises formal lending. Banks must keep a minimum cash balance and report how much they lend, to whom and at what interest rate. Informal lenders are not supervised in the same way and may charge very high interest.

Affordable formal credit matters because high borrowing costs leave less income for the borrower and can increase debt.

Richer households are more likely to obtain bank credit because they can provide documents and collateral. Poor households often turn to lenders who know them personally, even though interest may be higher and conditions harsher.

## SELF-HELP GROUPS FOR THE POOR

A typical Self Help Group has 15–20 members, often women from one neighbourhood. Members save regularly and can borrow small amounts from the group. After showing regular saving and repayment, the group may obtain a bank loan without each member offering separate collateral.

Members decide the purpose, amount, interest and repayment schedule together. SHGs give timely credit, support self-employment, build financial self-reliance and create a space to discuss health, nutrition and domestic violence.

## SUMMING UP

Money makes exchange easier, deposits connect money with banking, and bank loans support economic activity. Credit can create income or deepen hardship depending on risk and loan terms. Development requires more formal lending and a fairer share of low-cost credit for poor households.

## EXERCISES: Questions and answers

### 1. In situations with high risks, credit might create further problems for the borrower. Explain.

Answer: If the activity fails, the borrower earns too little to repay principal and interest. New borrowing, loss of collateral or sale of assets may follow, creating a debt trap.

### 2. How does money solve the problem of double coincidence of wants? Explain with an example of your own.

Answer: Money separates sale from purchase. A tailor can sell clothes for money and use the money to buy rice, even if the rice seller does not want clothes.

### 3. How do banks mediate between those who have surplus money and those who need money?

Answer: Banks accept deposits from savers, keep a small cash reserve and lend the rest to borrowers. They pay interest on deposits and charge interest on loans.

### 4. Look at a 10 rupee note. What is written on top? Can you explain this statement?

Answer: It carries the promise to pay the bearer the sum of ten rupees, signed by the RBI Governor. It means RBI guarantees the note’s value, and it is accepted as legal currency.

### 5. Why do we need to expand formal sources of credit in India?

Answer: Formal credit is supervised and usually cheaper. Expanding it can reduce dependence on costly moneylenders, prevent exploitation and support production and development.

### 6. What is the basic idea behind the SHGs for the poor? Explain in your own words.

Answer: Members pool small regular savings, lend from the common fund, make decisions together and later link with banks. Group responsibility helps people without collateral get affordable loans.

### 7. What are the reasons why the banks might not be willing to lend to certain borrowers?

Answer: A borrower may lack collateral, regular income, documents, a clear repayment record or a low-risk purpose. A bank may fear that the loan will not be repaid.

### 8. In what ways does the Reserve Bank of India supervise the functioning of banks? Why is this necessary?

Answer: RBI requires banks to keep a minimum cash balance and receive reports on lending, borrowers and interest rates. It checks that banks follow rules and lend to important sectors. Supervision protects depositors and supports fair credit.

### 9. Analyse the role of credit for development.

Answer: Credit supplies working capital and funds investment, farming, trade, education or enterprise. It supports development when returns allow repayment. With crop failure, high interest or unfair terms, it can instead create debt.

### 10. Manav needs a loan to set up a small business. On what basis will Manav decide whether to borrow from the bank or the moneylender? Discuss.

Answer: Manav should compare interest, collateral, documents, processing time, repayment schedule, reliability and any tied conditions. A regulated bank loan is usually preferable when he is eligible and can obtain it on time.

### 11. In India, about 80 per cent of farmers are small farmers, who need credit for cultivation. (a) Why might banks be unwilling to lend to small farmers? (b) What are the other sources from which the small farmers can borrow? (c) Explain with an example how the terms of credit can be unfavourable for the small farmer. (d) Suggest some ways by which small farmers can get cheap credit.

Answer: (a) Small farmers may lack collateral, documents and assured income because crops can fail. (b) They may borrow from moneylenders, traders, employers, relatives, friends, cooperatives or SHGs. (c) A trader may charge high interest and force the farmer to sell the crop to him at a low price. (d) Expand rural banks and cooperatives, simplify access, support SHGs and provide timely low-interest institutional loans.

### 12. Fill in the blanks: (i) Majority of the credit needs of the _________________ households are met from informal sources. (ii) ___________________ costs of borrowing increase the debt-burden. (iii) __________________ issues currency notes on behalf of the Central Government. (iv) Banks charge a higher interest rate on loans than what they offer on __________. (v) _______________ is an asset that the borrower owns and uses as a guarantee until the loan is repaid to the lender.

Answer: (i) poor; (ii) high; (iii) Reserve Bank of India; (iv) deposits; (v) collateral.

### 13. Choose the most appropriate answer. (i) In a SHG most of the decisions regarding savings and loan activities are taken by (a) Bank. (b) Members. (c) Non-government organisation. (ii) Formal sources of credit does not include (a) Banks. (b) Cooperatives. (c) Employers.

Answer: (i) (b) Members. (ii) (c) Employers.

## Common mistakes

- Saying barter is impossible; it works when there is double coincidence of wants.
- Treating only notes and coins as money and forgetting demand deposits.
- Saying every loan is helpful or every loan is harmful.
- Confusing collateral with interest.
- Calling moneylenders formal lenders because they lend regularly.

## Board tips

- In a credit answer, name the purpose, risk, terms and final result.
- Compare formal and informal sources using regulation, interest, collateral and access.
- Use a short example to explain double coincidence of wants or a debt trap.
- Do not claim that all informal credit is identical; say its terms can be costly or unfair and it lacks formal supervision.

## One-minute revision

- Money removes the need for double coincidence of wants.
- Currency and demand deposits are modern forms of money.
- Banks connect depositors and borrowers.
- Credit helps when income from the activity covers repayment.
- Interest, collateral, documents and repayment form the terms of credit.
- RBI supervises banks; informal lenders are not supervised in the same way.
- SHGs use small savings and group responsibility to widen access.
