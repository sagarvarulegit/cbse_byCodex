# 9.3 Refraction of Light

Refraction is the change in direction of light when it passes obliquely from one
transparent medium to another because its speed changes.

- rarer to optically denser -> bends toward normal;
- denser to optically rarer -> bends away from normal;
- normal incidence -> travels undeviated.

Activities 9.7–9.9: a submerged pencil appears bent, a coin appears raised after
water is poured, and a line under glass appears displaced.

# 9.3.1 Rectangular Glass Slab

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-9-10-glass-slab" />

At the first face the ray bends toward the normal; at the parallel second face
it bends away by an equal opposing amount. The emergent ray is therefore
parallel to the incident ray but shifted sideways—**lateral displacement**.

## Laws of refraction

1. Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
2. For a given colour and media, `sin i / sin r = constant`.

This constant is the refractive index of medium 2 relative to medium 1:

`n21 = sin i / sin r = v1/v2`

Absolute refractive index: `n = c/v`, where `c` is speed in vacuum and `v` in
the medium. Higher `n` means lower light speed and greater optical density.

## Relative refractive index

If `nA` and `nB` are absolute indices:

`nBA = nB/nA = vA/vB`

Optical density is not the same as mass density. Kerosene may be optically
denser than water even though its mass density is lower.

## Worked answer

If light speed in glass is `2 × 10^8 m/s`,

`n = 3 × 10^8 / 2 × 10^8 = 1.5`.

## Activity 9.10 record

Trace the slab, use four vertical pins, mark incident/refracted/emergent paths,
draw normals, measure `i` and `r`, and observe parallel emergence plus lateral
shift.
