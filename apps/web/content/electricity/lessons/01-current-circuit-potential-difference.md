# 11.1 Electric Current and Circuit

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-11-1-basic-circuit" />

Electric current is rate of flow of charge:

`I = Q/t`

Unit ampere: `1 A = 1 C/s`. `1 mA=10^-3 A`, `1 μA=10^-6 A`.

Conventional current is taken from positive to negative terminal outside the
source, opposite to electron flow. An ammeter measures current and is connected
**in series**, with its positive terminal toward the source's positive side.

## NCERT questions

- Circuit: continuous closed conducting path through which current flows.
- `1 C` equals charge of about `6 × 10^18` electrons.
- `Q=0.5C`, `t=10min=600s`: `I=8.33×10^-4A = 0.833mA`.

# 11.2 Electric Potential and Potential Difference

Potential difference is work done per unit charge:

`V = W/Q`

`1 V = 1 J/C`. A cell/battery maintains potential difference. A voltmeter is
connected **in parallel** across the component.

NCERT:

- `V=12V`, `Q=2C`: `W=VQ=24J`.
- A 6 V battery supplies 6 J per coulomb.

# 11.3 Circuit Diagram

Use standard symbols for cell, battery, open/closed key, wire junction,
crossing without junction, bulb, resistor, variable resistance/rheostat,
ammeter, and voltmeter.

## Fast wiring check

`battery -> key -> ammeter -> resistor -> back to battery`

Connect voltmeter across the resistor. A closed switch is necessary for
continuous current.

## Common mistakes

- Ammeter parallel can draw excessive current.
- Voltmeter in series does not measure the component's potential difference.
- Current is not “used up”; charge rate is conserved in a series path.
