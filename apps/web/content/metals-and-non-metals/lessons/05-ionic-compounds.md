# Ionic compounds

Metals and non-metals can form ionic compounds by transfer of electrons.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- explain ion formation
- describe how sodium chloride forms
- list properties of ionic compounds
- explain conductivity in solid and aqueous states

## Ion formation

Metals usually lose electrons and form positive ions.

Non-metals usually gain electrons and form negative ions.

Example:

`Na -> Na+ + e-`

`Cl + e- -> Cl-`

Opposite charges attract, so `Na+` and `Cl-` form sodium chloride.

## Ionic compound properties

Ionic compounds usually:
- are solid and hard
- have high melting and boiling points
- dissolve in water
- do not conduct electricity in solid state
- conduct electricity when molten or dissolved in water

## Conductivity rule

Use this board-answer pattern:

`Solid ionic compound -> ions fixed -> no conduction`

`Molten or aqueous ionic compound -> ions mobile -> conduction`

For sodium chloride:

`NaCl(aq) -> Na+(aq) + Cl-(aq)`

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Solid sodium chloride

Question:
Why does solid sodium chloride not conduct electricity?

Answer:
Its ions are fixed in the crystal lattice and cannot move freely.

### Example 2: Sodium chloride solution

Question:
Why does sodium chloride solution conduct electricity?

Answer:
It contains mobile ions, `Na+` and `Cl-`, which carry charge.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying ionic compounds always conduct electricity.
2. Forgetting the difference between solid and aqueous states.
3. Calling sodium chloride a covalent compound.
4. Saying electrons move freely in ionic solutions instead of ions.

## Board tips

- For conductivity answers, write "mobile ions".
- For high melting point, mention strong electrostatic attraction.
- Do not skip state symbols if the question compares solid and aqueous samples.

## Quick practice

1. What ion does sodium form?
2. What ion does chlorine form?
3. Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?
4. Why does `NaCl(aq)` conduct electricity?

## Answer check

1. `Na+`.
2. `Cl-`.
3. Strong attraction between oppositely charged ions.
4. It has mobile ions.

## One-minute revision

Ionic compounds form by electron transfer. They have strong attraction between ions, so melting points are high. They conduct only when ions are mobile.

## Comic recap

- Chlorine: "I gain one electron."
- Lattice Lock: "Solid ions cannot move."
- Aqueous Ion: "In water, I can carry current."
