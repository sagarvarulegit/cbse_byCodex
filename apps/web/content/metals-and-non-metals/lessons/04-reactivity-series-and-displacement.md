# Reactivity series and displacement

The reactivity series helps predict how metals react with water, acids, and salt solutions.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- compare metals using the reactivity series
- predict reactions with water and dilute acids
- explain displacement from salt solutions
- use visible changes like bubbles and colour change as evidence

## Reactivity series

A useful order for Class 10 is:

`K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Hg, Ag, Au`

Metals near the top are more reactive. Metals near the bottom are less reactive.

## Reaction with water

Very reactive metals like sodium react vigorously with cold water.

Less reactive metals react slowly or need hot water or steam.

Copper does not react with water under ordinary conditions.

## Reaction with dilute acids

Metals above hydrogen can displace hydrogen from dilute acids.

Example:

`Mg + 2HCl -> MgCl2 + H2`

Copper is below hydrogen, so it does not normally react with dilute hydrochloric acid.

## Displacement reactions

A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution.

Example:

`Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu`

Iron is more reactive than copper, so iron displaces copper from copper sulphate solution.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Iron and copper sulphate

Question:
What happens when iron is placed in copper sulphate solution?

Answer:
Iron displaces copper. The blue solution becomes greenish and reddish-brown copper deposits.

### Example 2: Copper and dilute acid

Question:
Why does copper not produce hydrogen with dilute HCl?

Answer:
Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so it cannot displace hydrogen from dilute acid.

## Common mistakes

1. Treating all metals as equally reactive.
2. Forgetting hydrogen's position in the series.
3. Saying copper displaces iron from iron sulphate.
4. Ignoring colour change evidence in salt solution reactions.

## Board tips

- Always compare positions in the reactivity series before predicting displacement.
- If a metal is above hydrogen, it can usually release hydrogen from dilute acid.
- For `CuSO4`, mention blue-to-green change and copper deposit when iron is added.

## Quick practice

1. Which is more reactive: iron or copper?
2. Can copper displace hydrogen from dilute HCl?
3. What is deposited when iron reacts with copper sulphate?
4. Why does sodium react strongly with water?

## Answer check

1. Iron.
2. No.
3. Copper.
4. Sodium is very high in the reactivity series.

## One-minute revision

The reactivity series is the prediction tool. More reactive metals displace less reactive metals from salt solutions, and metals above hydrogen can release hydrogen from dilute acids.
