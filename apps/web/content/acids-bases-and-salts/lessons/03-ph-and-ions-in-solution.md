# 2.2-2.3.1 Ions, Strength and pH

Glucose and hydrochloric acid both contain hydrogen. Yet only one behaves as
an acid. The difference is not the presence of the letter `H` in a formula. The
difference is the ions produced in water.

The bulb glows in acid and alkali solutions because ions carry electric current
through the solution. It does not glow in glucose or alcohol solutions because
those substances do not produce the required ions in water.

Acids produce hydrogen ions, `H+(aq)`, in solution. The positive ion common to
all the acids in this activity is `H+`. Glucose and alcohol contain hydrogen
but do not release `H+` ions in water, so they are not acids.

### Figure 2.4: Preparation of HCl gas

[[demo:activity-2-9-dry-wet-hcl-litmus]]

Label key:

1. Dry sodium chloride in a test tube
2. Dropping funnel containing concentrated sulphuric acid
3. Delivery tube
4. Dry blue litmus paper
5. Wet blue litmus paper

Dry hydrogen chloride gas does not change dry blue litmus. Wet blue litmus
turns red because water allows hydrogen ions to form.

Teacher note: In very humid weather, the gas must be passed through a guard
tube containing calcium chloride to dry it.

`HCl + H2O -> H3O+ + Cl-`

A hydrogen ion cannot exist alone in water. It combines with a water molecule
to form a hydronium ion:

`H+ + H2O -> H3O+`

Therefore, write the acidic ion as `H+(aq)` or `H3O+`.

Bases produce hydroxide ions in water:

`NaOH(s) -> Na+(aq) + OH-(aq)`

`KOH(s) -> K+(aq) + OH-(aq)`

`Mg(OH)2(s) -> Mg2+(aq) + 2OH-(aq)`

### Do You Know: base and alkali

All alkalis are bases, but all bases are not alkalis. An alkali is a base that
dissolves in water. Alkalis are bitter, soapy and corrosive. Never taste or
touch them.

At ion level, neutralisation is:

`H+(aq) + OH-(aq) -> H2O(l)`

The remaining positive and negative ions form the salt.

**❓ QUESTIONS**

**1. Why do HCl, HNO3, etc., show acidic characters in aqueous solutions while solutions of compounds like alcohol and glucose do not show acidic character?**

*Answer:* `HCl` and `HNO3` ionise in water and produce `H+(aq)` or `H3O+`
ions. Alcohol and glucose contain hydrogen but do not produce these ions in
water, so their solutions do not show acidic character.

**2. Why does an aqueous solution of an acid conduct electricity?**

*Answer:* An acid produces mobile ions in water. These ions carry electric
current through the solution.

**3. Why does dry HCl gas not change the colour of the dry litmus paper?**

*Answer:* Without water, `HCl` cannot produce `H+(aq)` or `H3O+` ions. Dry
litmus therefore does not show the acidic colour change.

**4. While diluting an acid, why is it recommended that the acid should be added to water and not water to the acid?**

*Answer:* Dilution is highly exothermic. Adding acid slowly to a larger volume
of water lets the heat spread safely. Adding water to concentrated acid may
cause sudden heating, splashing, burns and breakage of the container.

**5. How is the concentration of hydronium ions (H3O+) affected when a solution of an acid is diluted?**

*Answer:* The hydronium-ion concentration per unit volume decreases.

**6. How is the concentration of hydroxide ions (OH-) affected when excess base is dissolved in a solution of sodium hydroxide?**

*Answer:* The hydroxide-ion concentration increases because the added base
produces more `OH-` ions in the solution.

## 2.3 How Strong are Acid or Base Solutions?

Litmus tells whether a sample is acidic or basic. A universal indicator gives
different colours at different hydrogen-ion concentrations and helps us
estimate the strength.

[JARGON: pH | A number that indicates the acidic or basic nature of a solution by its hydrogen-ion concentration | Lemon juice has a low pH, while sodium hydroxide has a high pH]

The `p` in pH comes from the German word *potenz*, meaning power.

### Figure 2.6: Variation of pH with H+(aq) and OH-(aq) concentration

[[demo:figure-2-6-ph-scale]]

#### Reading Figure 2.6

| pH range | Nature | Ion trend |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0` to below `7` | Acidic | More `H+`; lower pH means stronger acidic character |
| `7` | Neutral | Balanced acidic and basic character |
| Above `7` to `14` | Basic | More `OH-`; higher pH means stronger basic character |

Higher hydronium-ion concentration means lower pH.

The textbook colour strip is a rough guide, not an exact laboratory
measurement. Anchor examples are:

- gastric juice: strongly acidic, about pH `1`
- lemon juice: acidic, about pH `2.2`
- pure water: neutral, pH `7`
- milk of magnesia: basic, about pH `10`
- sodium hydroxide: strongly basic, near pH `14`

Acids of the same concentration can produce different numbers of `H+` ions.
An acid that produces more `H+` ions is stronger. One that produces fewer is
weaker. Strong and weak bases are compared in the same way using the number of
`OH-` ions produced.

## 2.3.1 Importance of pH in Everyday Life

### Are plants and animals pH sensitive?

The human body works within a narrow pH range of about `7.0` to `7.8`. Living
organisms can survive only within suitable pH ranges.

Rainwater with pH below `5.6` is called acid rain. It can lower the pH of river
water and make survival difficult for aquatic life.

### Do You Know: acids on another planet

The atmosphere of Venus contains thick white and yellowish clouds of sulphuric
acid. Such conditions are hostile to life as we know it.

### Activity 2.12

[[demo:activity-2-12-soil-ph]]

1. Put about `2 g` soil in a test tube.
2. Add `5 mL` water and shake.
3. Filter the mixture.
4. Test the filtrate with universal-indicator paper.
5. Compare the soil pH with the plants growing at the collection site.

Plants need a suitable soil pH. Acidic soil may be treated with quick lime
(`CaO`), slaked lime [`Ca(OH)2`] or chalk (`CaCO3`) to reduce its acidity.

### pH in our digestive system

The stomach produces hydrochloric acid for digestion. Too much acid causes
pain and irritation during indigestion. Mild bases called antacids neutralise
the excess acid. Magnesium hydroxide, or milk of magnesia, is one such antacid.

### pH change as the cause of tooth decay

Tooth decay starts when mouth pH falls below `5.5`. Tooth enamel contains
calcium hydroxyapatite, a crystalline form of calcium phosphate and the
hardest substance in the body. Acids made by bacteria from food particles can
corrode it. Cleaning the mouth after eating and using a generally basic
toothpaste help neutralise the acid.

### Self-defence by animals and plants

A bee sting leaves an acid that causes pain and irritation. A mild base such
as baking soda can give relief. Nettle stinging hairs inject methanoic acid.

### Do You Know: nature provides a neutralisation option

Dock plants often grow near nettles. Rubbing a dock leaf on a nettle sting is a
traditional remedy, suggesting that the dock provides a neutralising effect.

**❓ QUESTIONS**

**1. You have two solutions, A and B. The pH of solution A is 6 and pH of solution B is 8. Which solution has more hydrogen ion concentration? Which of this is acidic and which one is basic?**

*Answer:* Solution A has more hydrogen ions because its pH is lower. A is
acidic because its pH is below `7`. B is basic because its pH is above `7`.

**2. What effect does the concentration of H+(aq) ions have on the nature of the solution?**

*Answer:* A higher `H+(aq)` concentration makes a solution more acidic and
lowers its pH.

**3. Do basic solutions also have H+(aq) ions? If yes, then why are these basic?**

*Answer:* Yes. Basic aqueous solutions also contain some `H+(aq)` ions, but
their `OH-` ion concentration is greater than their `H+` ion concentration.
Therefore, they show basic character.

**4. Under what soil condition do you think a farmer would treat the soil of his fields with quick lime (calcium oxide) or slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) or chalk (calcium carbonate)?**

*Answer:* The farmer would use these basic substances when the soil is too
acidic. They neutralise excess soil acidity and raise the pH towards a range
suitable for crops.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying any compound containing hydrogen is an acid.
2. Writing that dry `HCl` changes dry litmus.
3. Adding water directly to concentrated acid.
4. Reading pH backwards: lower pH means greater acidic character.
5. Confusing acid strength with amount of solution.
6. Saying basic solutions contain no `H+` ions.
7. Writing acid rain as any rain below pH `7`; the NCERT threshold is below
   `5.6`.

## Board tips

- For aqueous-acid questions, write both "ions form in water" and
  "`H+(aq)`/`H3O+` causes acidic character."
- For dilution, state the safety reason: the process is highly exothermic.
- For pH comparisons, first identify the lower pH, then state that it has
  greater hydrogen-ion concentration.
- Daily-life answers should use the exact threshold when given: body pH
  `7.0-7.8`, acid rain below `5.6`, tooth decay below `5.5`.

## One-minute revision

- Acids produce `H+(aq)` or `H3O+`; bases produce `OH-` in water.
- An alkali is a water-soluble base.
- Acid/base dilution is exothermic: add acid to water.
- Lower pH means more acidic; higher pH means more basic.
- pH affects rivers, soil, digestion, teeth and stings.
- Complete answers use a mechanism, not only a memorised pH number.
