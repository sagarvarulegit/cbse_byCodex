# 7.3.3 (d) Reproductive Health

Physical sexual maturation does not automatically mean that a person's body,
mind, or life situation is ready for sexual activity or parenthood. Reproductive
health includes informed, responsible decisions, freedom from pressure,
prevention and treatment of disease, and safe medical care.

## Sexually transmitted infections

NCERT examples:

- bacterial: gonorrhoea, syphilis
- viral: warts, HIV-AIDS

A condom provides a physical covering and helps reduce transmission of many
sexually transmitted infections. No other method in this chapter is presented as
providing the same barrier protection.

## Why contraception may be adopted

- to avoid an unwanted pregnancy
- to space births
- to protect the physical and mental health of parents
- to allow responsible family planning
- condoms also help reduce transmission of many STIs

## Methods of contraception

| Category | Example | How it prevents pregnancy | Important limitation/side effect from NCERT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barrier | condom or vaginal covering | prevents sperm from reaching egg | only condoms also help reduce many STI transmissions |
| Hormonal | oral pills | alter hormonal balance so eggs are not released and fertilisation cannot occur | may cause side effects because hormone balance changes |
| Intrauterine device | loop or Copper-T in uterus | prevents pregnancy within the uterus | may cause irritation and side effects; does not protect against STIs |
| Surgical—male | vasectomy | vas deferens is blocked, preventing sperm transfer | surgery must be performed safely |
| Surgical—female | tubectomy | fallopian tubes are blocked, preventing egg-sperm meeting | surgery must be performed safely |

## Copper-T question

Copper-T helps prevent pregnancy but does **not** protect a person from sexually
transmitted infections because it is inside the uterus and does not block
exchange of infectious body fluids.

## Surgical methods

- **Vasectomy:** vas deferens is blocked in the male.
- **Tubectomy:** fallopian tubes are blocked in the female.

In each case, gamete transport is interrupted and fertilisation is prevented.
Surgery can cause infection or other problems if not performed properly.

## Prenatal sex determination

Prenatal sex determination for sex selection is prohibited by law. Sex-selective
abortion of female foetuses harms the female-male ratio and society. The sex of
a child is not the mother's fault; that mechanism is studied in Chapter 8.

## Medical safety note

Contraceptive choice and STI diagnosis belong with qualified health
professionals. Platform notes explain the NCERT mechanisms; they do not replace
individual medical advice.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying Copper-T protects against STIs.
2. Saying oral pills physically block sperm.
3. Swapping vasectomy and tubectomy.
4. Claiming contraception is only a woman's responsibility.
5. Treating prenatal sex selection as lawful.
