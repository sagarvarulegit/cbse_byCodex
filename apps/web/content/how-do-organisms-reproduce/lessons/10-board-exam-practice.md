# Board Exam Practice

These 28 patterns close the distinct Chapter 7 marking demands found in the
local 2023-2026 CBSE archive.

# Latest verified five-mark patterns: 2026

## 1. Male germ-cell formation and path - 5 marks

(i) Name the sperm-forming organ and the condition required.  
(ii) Give the sperm pathway within the male system.  
(iii) State the feature that helps a sperm move.

**Full-mark answer:**

- testes; a temperature lower than normal body temperature, provided by the
  scrotum
- `testes -> vas deferens -> urethra`
- long tail

The official scheme awards the organ and condition separately, two marks for the
path, and one for the tail.

## 2. Contraception and STIs - 5 marks

(i) Name two non-surgical methods a woman can use.  
(ii) Name the male and female surgical methods and state what is blocked.  
(iii) Give one bacterial and one viral STI.

**Full-mark answer:**

- any two: female barrier/condom, oral pills, Copper-T/loop
- vasectomy—vas deferens blocked; tubectomy—fallopian tube blocked
- bacterial—gonorrhoea or syphilis; viral—warts or HIV-AIDS

## 3. Post-fertilisation in a flower OR vegetative propagation - 5 marks

### Option A

- zygote divides to embryo
- ovule develops tough coat and becomes seed
- ovary enlarges and ripens into fruit
- sepals/petals/stamens/style/stigma usually shrivel and fall
- germination is development of the embryo into a seedling under suitable
  conditions

### Option B

Sugarcane reproduces by vegetative propagation. Advantages: earlier
flowering/fruiting, propagation without viable seeds, preservation of parental
traits, and rapid multiplication. Examples include rose, grape, banana, orange,
and jasmine. Regeneration cannot occur in all animals because complex,
specialised tissues and organs cannot be reconstructed from arbitrary pieces.

# Asexual-reproduction patterns

## 4. Amoeba versus Leishmania binary fission

Both form two daughter cells. Amoeba can divide in any plane because it lacks a
fixed body axis. Leishmania divides longitudinally in a definite orientation
related to its flagellum and elongated body.

## 5. Leishmania versus Plasmodium

- Leishmania—binary fission in a definite longitudinal plane; two daughters.
- Plasmodium—multiple fission; many daughter cells form simultaneously.

## 6. Hydra's two asexual processes

- Regeneration: specialised cells in a cut piece proliferate and differentiate
  to rebuild a whole Hydra.
- Budding: repeated cell division at one site forms a bud; it matures and
  detaches.

## 7. Regeneration: Planaria but not a complex animal

Planaria has specialised cells able to proliferate and differentiate into all
needed tissues in an organised sequence. A complex animal's highly specialised
cells, tissues, and organs cannot normally be rebuilt from an arbitrary body
piece; therefore regeneration is not its reproductive method.

## 8. Fragmentation in Spirogyra

At maturity the simple filament breaks into smaller fragments; each grows into
a complete Spirogyra. Fragmentation is unsuitable for complex multicellular
organisms because their organs must remain in a definite arrangement.

## 9. Yeast and Rhizopus activity reasoning

Yeast multiplies in warm sugar solution because sugar supplies energy and water
supports metabolism. Rhizopus fails to grow on dry bread because spores need
moisture to germinate.

## 10. Rhizopus structure and spore advantage

Hyphae form the thread-like body; upright sporangiophores carry sporangia
containing many spores. Thick walls protect spores, their large number aids
dispersal, and they germinate on moist surfaces.

## 11. Vegetative propagation definition and advantages

It is formation of new plants from root, stem, or leaf. Write any two:
earlier flowering/fruiting; propagation of seedless plants; preservation of
desirable parental traits; rapid multiplication.

## 12. Bryophyllum, potato, money plant, tissue culture

- Bryophyllum—leaf-margin buds fall and root.
- Potato—only a tuber piece with an eye/bud sprouts.
- Money plant—a cutting needs a node.
- Tissue culture—explant -> callus -> hormone medium -> plantlets -> soil.

# DNA, variation, and sexual-reproduction patterns

## 13. DNA, proteins, and altered body design

DNA carries instructions for proteins. A DNA change may change a protein;
different proteins can change cell function and hence body design.

## 14. Variation and heat-resistant bacteria

Copying changes create variants. If water temperature rises, most bacteria may
die, but heat-resistant variants survive and multiply. Variation therefore
supports species survival under environmental change.

## 15. Asexual offspring assertion-reason

Asexual offspring usually show little variation because one parent supplies the
DNA and copying is highly accurate. However, DNA copies are not always perfectly
identical, so the statement "generated DNA is not similar to the original" is
false.

## 16. Why chromosome number remains constant

Meiosis halves chromosome number during gamete formation. Fertilisation fuses
two gametes and restores the full number in the zygote. Humans: `23 + 23 = 46`.

## 17. Gamete versus zygote

Gametes are haploid reproductive cells carrying DNA from one parent. A zygote is
the diploid first cell formed by gamete fusion; it combines both parental DNA
sets and develops into the new individual.

# Flowering-plant patterns

## 18. Flower parts and functions

- sepals—protect bud; may persist
- petals—attract pollinators
- anther—produces pollen
- stigma—receives pollen
- style—pollen-tube path
- ovary—contains ovules; becomes fruit
- ovule—contains egg; becomes seed

## 19. Self- versus cross-pollination

Self-pollination transfers pollen within the same flower or plant. Cross-
pollination transfers it between flowers on different plants of the same
species. Cross-pollination creates greater genetic variation, improving a
species' chance of surviving change.

## 20. Pollination versus fertilisation

Pollination is pollen transfer from anther to stigma and occurs before pollen-
tube growth. Fertilisation is male-female gamete fusion inside an ovule and
forms a zygote.

- radicle—future root
- cotyledons—stored food

# Human-reproduction patterns

## 24. Male organs and placenta - 5 marks

- testis—sperm and testosterone
- scrotum—lower temperature
- vas deferens—sperm transport
- seminal vesicle/prostate—nutritive transport fluid
- placenta—disc in uterine wall, villi and maternal blood spaces; oxygen and
  glucose to embryo, wastes to mother

## 25. Female organs and exact sites

- ovary—eggs/hormones
- oviduct—usual fertilisation site
- uterus—implantation and development
- vagina—sperm entry/birth passage

## 26. Fertilised versus unfertilised egg

If fertilised, a zygote forms in the oviduct, divides into an embryo, implants
in the uterus, and develops with placental support. If not, the egg degenerates
and the thick uterine lining breaks down and leaves as blood and mucus during
menstruation.

## 27. Puberty, testes, and contraception

Puberty is the adolescent period when reproductive tissues mature. In boys,
voice cracks and facial hair grows. Testes lie in the scrotum for a cooler
temperature. Any three contraceptive categories may be written; Copper-T is not
a male method.

## 28. Five-situation analysis

1. Rhizopus spores reach moist bread -> germinate into new hyphae.
2. Bryophyllum leaf falls on wet soil -> marginal buds root into new plants.
3. Unrelated-species pollen reaches stigma -> incompatibility prevents normal
   fertilisation.
4. Copper-T is placed in uterus -> pregnancy is prevented, but STI protection
   is not provided.
5. Spirogyra breaks on maturity -> fragments grow into new filaments.

## Final examiner checklist

- organism and reproductive mode correctly paired
- every pathway shown with arrows
- pollen transfer kept separate from gamete fusion
- fertilisation and implantation sites kept separate
- hormone/organ/duct functions stated precisely
- contraceptive method matched to its mechanism
- placenta described by structure, two-way exchange, and large area
- post-fertilisation fate of zygote, ovule, ovary, and floral parts complete
