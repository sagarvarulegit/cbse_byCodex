# The Sermon at Benares

## chapter goal

By the end of this unit, a student should be able to:
- explain Buddha's early life and search for enlightenment
- retell Kisa Gotami's story
- understand the lesson about death and grief
- revise the poem "For Anne Gregory"
- answer questions on wisdom, acceptance, and appearance

## 1) Buddha's background

Siddhartha Gautama was born a prince and lived a protected life.

After seeing sickness, old age, death, and a monk, he left royal life to understand suffering.

## 2) enlightenment

After years of wandering, he attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree.

He became known as the Buddha, the Awakened One.

## 3) Kisa Gotami

Kisa Gotami loses her only son and is unable to accept his death.

She asks people for medicine to bring him back.

## 4) mustard seed lesson

Buddha asks her to bring a handful of mustard seeds from a house where no one has died.

She goes from house to house and realises that every family has faced death.

This experience teaches her the universality of death more deeply than a direct lecture would.

## 5) teaching

Buddha teaches that death is natural and unavoidable.

Wisdom lies in accepting reality and overcoming selfish grief.

## 6) poem: For Anne Gregory

The poem discusses whether someone can be loved for inner qualities rather than physical beauty.

The speaker suggests that human love often focuses on appearance, while only divine love can love the soul completely.

Theme: true worth is deeper than external beauty.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Buddha left royal life after seeing suffering.
- Kisa Gotami learns that death comes to every home.
- The mustard seed task teaches through experience.
- The lesson is about acceptance of death.
- "For Anne Gregory" contrasts appearance and inner self.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing that Buddha actually wanted mustard seeds for medicine.
2. Treating Kisa Gotami as foolish instead of grief-stricken.
3. Missing the experiential nature of Buddha's lesson.
4. Saying the poem rejects beauty; it questions shallow love.

## board tips

- In Buddha answers, use words like suffering, enlightenment, acceptance.
- Explain why the mustard seed condition matters.
- In the poem, separate physical beauty and inner worth.

## short answer practice

1. What sights changed Siddhartha's life?
2. Why did Kisa Gotami go to Buddha?
3. What condition did Buddha place for the mustard seeds?
4. What did Kisa Gotami realise?
5. What does "For Anne Gregory" say about love?
