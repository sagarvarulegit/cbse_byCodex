# Board Exam Practice

## Recurring demand families

1. Calculate current or charge using `I=Q/t`.
2. Explain conventional current versus electron flow.
3. Define potential difference and joule/coulomb.
4. Correct ammeter-series and voltmeter-parallel wiring/polarity.
5. Draw/repair a circuit using standard symbols.
6. State Ohm's law with constant-temperature condition.
7. Read V–I graph; distinguish slope for V-I versus I-V axes.
8. Calculate resistance and convert mA/μA.
9. Use `R=ρl/A`; compare length, area, diameter, material.
10. Distinguish resistance and resistivity.
11. Derive and calculate series equivalent.
12. Derive and calculate parallel equivalent.
13. Design a target resistance from equal resistors.
14. Explain domestic parallel connection.
15. Apply Joule's law in any equivalent form.
16. Compare heat at same V/current/time.
17. Explain tungsten, nichrome/alloy, and fuse choices.
18. Calculate `P=VI`, `I²R`, or `V²/R`.
19. Interpret appliance ratings and changed supply voltage.
20. Calculate electrical energy and convert J/kWh.
21. Find number of appliances within a current limit.
22. Case study combining circuit, meter, resistance, power, and energy.

## Five-mark numerical answer frame

Write given values with SI conversion -> choose formula -> substitute with unit
-> calculate -> sanity-check -> answer the physical question in words.

## Graph scoring

Label axes and units; choose uniform scale; plot points; draw best-fit line; use
two well-separated points on the line for slope; state `R` with Ω.

## High-value traps

- Diameter doubled means area four times, not twice.
- In series current is same; in parallel voltage is same.
- Parallel equivalent is below the smallest branch.
- Fuse protects against excessive current; it is not an ordinary switch.
- kW measures power; kWh measures energy.
- A bulb's listed power applies at its rated voltage.
