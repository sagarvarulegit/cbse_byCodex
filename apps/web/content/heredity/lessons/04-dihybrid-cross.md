# Two traits together

Mendel crossed pea plants differing in seed shape and seed colour.

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-8-5-dihybrid-cross" />

NCERT Figure 8.5 uses:

`round green RRyy × wrinkled yellow rrYY`

- `R` round dominates `r` wrinkled.
- `Y` yellow dominates `y` green.
- All F1 are round yellow `RrYy`.

F1 plants make four gametes: `RY`, `Ry`, `rY`, `ry`.

Selfing `RrYy × RrYy` gives the F2 phenotypic ratio:

`9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green`

## Why new combinations matter

Round-yellow and wrinkled-green are combinations not present in the NCERT
parental pair. Their appearance shows that seed shape and seed colour can be
inherited independently.

This is the **independent inheritance of traits**: allele pairs for different
traits assort into gametes independently in this cross.

## Exam comparison

| Cross | Traits studied | F1 | Important F2 ratio | Conclusion |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Monohybrid | 1 | dominant phenotype | `3:1` | dominance and segregation |
| Dihybrid | 2 | both dominant phenotypes | `9:3:3:1` | independent inheritance |

## Board-ready explanation

“All F1 plants were round-yellow, showing that round and yellow are dominant.
Selfing F1 produced four F2 phenotypes, including two new combinations, in the
ratio 9:3:3:1. Therefore the two pairs of traits are inherited independently.”

## Remember

Do not claim `9:3:3:1` for every two-trait situation. It applies here because
the alleles show complete dominance and assort independently.
