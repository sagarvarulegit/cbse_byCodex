# Control and Coordination

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain nervous control in animals
- describe reflex action, brain, spinal cord, and nerves
- understand plant movements
- explain hormones in animals and plants

## 1) why coordination is needed

Organisms must respond to changes in their surroundings.

Stimulus means a change that causes a response.

Example:
Touching a hot object is a stimulus. Pulling the hand away is a response.

## 2) nervous system

Main parts:
- brain
- spinal cord
- nerves

Neuron is the structural and functional unit of nervous system.

Parts of neuron:
- dendrite
- cell body
- axon
- nerve ending

Nerve impulse travels from dendrite to cell body to axon.

## 3) reflex action

A reflex action is a quick, automatic response to a stimulus.

Example:
Pulling hand away from a hot object.

Reflex arc path:
receptor -> sensory neuron -> spinal cord -> motor neuron -> effector

Reflex actions are fast because the spinal cord can respond before the brain fully processes the event.

## 4) human brain

Main parts:
- forebrain
- midbrain
- hindbrain

Forebrain controls thinking, memory, voluntary actions, and sensory processing.

Cerebellum helps in balance and posture.

Medulla controls involuntary actions like breathing and heartbeat.

Brain is protected by skull. Spinal cord is protected by vertebral column.

## 5) coordination in plants

Plants do not have nerves, but they respond using chemical coordination and growth movements.

### tropic movements

Directional growth response to stimulus.

Examples:
- phototropism: response to light
- geotropism: response to gravity
- hydrotropism: response to water
- chemotropism: response to chemicals

### immediate response

Touch-me-not leaves fold quickly due to water movement in cells, not growth.

## 6) hormones

Hormones are chemical messengers.

Important animal hormones:
- adrenaline: emergency response
- thyroxine: metabolism
- insulin: blood sugar control
- growth hormone: body growth
- testosterone and oestrogen: reproductive changes

Plant hormones:
- auxin: growth, phototropism
- gibberellin: stem growth
- cytokinin: cell division
- abscisic acid: growth inhibition, wilting response

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Nervous control is fast and electrical.
- Hormonal control is slower and chemical.
- Reflex action protects the body.
- Brain has specialised parts.
- Plants respond through hormones and growth movements.
- Auxin causes bending towards light.

## common mistakes students make

1. Saying reflex action is controlled only by the brain.
2. Mixing sensory and motor neurons.
3. Calling all plant movements growth movements.
4. Forgetting adrenaline is secreted in emergencies.
5. Confusing insulin with thyroxine.

## board tips

- Draw reflex arc with arrows.
- Mention both nervous and hormonal coordination in animals.
- For plant tropism, name the stimulus.
- In brain questions, connect part to function.

## solved practice set

### question 1
Why are reflex actions important?

Answer:
They protect the body by giving quick automatic responses before serious damage occurs.

### question 2
What is phototropism?

Answer:
Growth movement of a plant part in response to light.

### question 3
What is the role of insulin?

Answer:
Insulin regulates blood sugar level.
