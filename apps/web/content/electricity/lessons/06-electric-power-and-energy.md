# 11.8 Electric Power

Power is rate of electrical energy consumption:

`P=W/t=VI=I²R=V²/R`.

Unit watt: `1W=1V×1A`; `1kW=1000W`.

Electrical energy:

`E=Pt=VIt`.

Commercial unit:

`1 kWh = 3.6×10^6 J`.

One “unit” on an electricity bill means 1 kWh, not 1 kW.

## Rating meaning

A `220V,100W` bulb is designed to consume 100 W at 220 V.

Its resistance `R=V²/P=484Ω`. At 110 V, assuming R constant:

`P=110²/484=25W`.

## Worked comparisons

- 250 W TV for 1 h: `250Wh=0.25kWh`;
- 1200 W toaster for 10 min: `1200×1/6=200Wh=0.20kWh`.

The TV uses more energy.

Heater `R=44Ω`, `I=5A`: heat-development rate
`P=I²R=25×44=1100W`.

## Formula selection

- know V and I -> `P=VI`;
- know I and R -> `P=I²R`;
- know V and R -> `P=V²/R`;
- energy bill -> kW × h.

## Common mistake

Power is a **rate** in W; energy is power × time in J or kWh.
