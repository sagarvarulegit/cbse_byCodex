## One-page memory map

`stimulus -> receptor -> sensory neuron -> CNS -> motor neuron -> effector -> response`

- **Nervous control:** fast electrical impulses along connected neurons
- **Reflex arc:** quick pathway commonly through the spinal cord
- **Brain:** fore-brain thinks; cerebellum balances; medulla controls several
  involuntary actions
- **Plant coordination:** immediate water-content change or slow directional growth
- **Hormonal control:** chemical messengers act through target cells
- **Feedback:** hormone release rises or falls according to the body's need

## 6.1 in-text questions

### 1. What is the difference between a reflex action and walking?

A reflex action is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus, commonly completed
through a spinal reflex arc without waiting for conscious thought. Walking is a
voluntary action: the fore-brain decides to act and sends instructions to
voluntary muscles.

### 2. What happens at the synapse between two neurons?

When an electrical impulse reaches the axon ending, it causes chemicals to be
released. They diffuse across the synaptic gap, reach the dendrite of the next
neuron, and start a new electrical impulse. This arrangement makes transmission
mainly one-way.

### 3. Which part of the brain maintains posture and equilibrium?

The cerebellum, a part of the hind-brain, maintains posture and equilibrium and
improves the precision of voluntary movements.

### 4. How do we detect the smell of an agarbatti?

Olfactory receptors in the nose detect its chemicals and create nerve impulses.
The impulses reach the olfactory area of the fore-brain, where the smell is
recognised.

### 5. What is the role of the brain in reflex action?

The spinal cord completes the immediate response through the reflex arc, while
the sensory information also travels to the brain. The brain becomes aware of
the event, interprets it, and can guide later action.

## 6.2 in-text questions

### 1. What are plant hormones?

Plant hormones are chemical substances made in one part of a plant that move or
diffuse to another part and coordinate growth, development, and responses to the
environment.

### 2. Sensitive-plant movement versus a shoot bending towards light

| Sensitive plant | Shoot towards light |
| --- | --- |
| Non-directional response to touch | Directional response to light |
| Quick | Slow |
| Growth-independent | Growth-related |
| Cells change water content | Unequal cell elongation causes bending |

### 3. Give an example of a growth-promoting plant hormone.

Auxin promotes cell elongation. Gibberellin promotes stem growth, and cytokinin
promotes cell division; any one correctly named example answers the question.

### 4. How do auxins promote the growth of a tendril around a support?

Touch creates unequal growth. The side touching the support grows more slowly,
while the side away from it grows faster under auxin's influence. The unequal
growth makes the tendril bend and coil around the support.

### 5. Design an experiment to demonstrate hydrotropism.

Place similar germinated seeds in moist sawdust on a sieve and allow roots to
emerge. Then keep the region above or to one side of the root tips moist while
the other region is comparatively dry. Keep a similar setup uniformly moist as
a comparison, and keep light, temperature, and time the same. Roots in the test
setup bend towards the moisture source; this directional growth towards water
demonstrates positive hydrotropism.

## 6.3 in-text questions

### 1. How does chemical coordination take place in animals?

Endocrine glands release hormones directly into blood. Blood carries them
throughout the body, but only target cells with suitable receptors respond.
Hormones coordinate processes such as growth, metabolism, puberty, emergency
response, and blood-sugar control.

### 2. Why is iodised salt advisable?

Iodised salt supplies iodine, which the thyroid gland needs to make thyroxin.
Thyroxin regulates carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism for balanced
growth. Iodine deficiency may reduce thyroxin production and cause goitre.

### 3. How does the body respond when adrenaline enters the blood?

- heart beats faster, sending more oxygen to muscles
- breathing rate rises through diaphragm and rib-muscle action
- small arteries to skin and the digestive system constrict
- more blood is diverted to skeletal muscles

Together these changes prepare the body to fight or run.

### 4. Why are some patients with diabetes given insulin injections?

Their pancreas may not produce enough effective insulin. Without enough insulin,
blood sugar stays abnormally high. The injection supplies the missing hormone
and helps regulate blood-sugar level under medical supervision.

## What you have learnt

- Control and coordination in our body use the nervous system and hormones.
- Nervous responses may be reflex, voluntary, or involuntary.
- Nervous tissue transmits messages as electrical impulses.
- Sense organs gather information; muscles and glands act as effectors.
- Plants and animals both use chemical coordination.
- Hormones are made in one part and act on target cells elsewhere.
- Feedback mechanisms regulate the timing and amount of hormone release.

## NCERT Exercises

### Exercise 1

**Which is a plant hormone: insulin, thyroxin, oestrogen, or cytokinin?**

**Answer:** Cytokinin. It promotes cell division and is concentrated in rapidly
dividing regions such as fruits and seeds.

### Exercise 2

**The gap between two neurons is called what?**

**Answer:** A synapse.

### Exercise 3

**The brain is responsible for thinking, heartbeat regulation, balancing the
body, or all of these?**

**Answer:** All of these. The fore-brain supports thinking, the medulla regulates
heartbeat, and the cerebellum maintains balance.

### Exercise 4

**What is the function of receptors? What problems may arise if they do not work
properly?**

Receptors detect specific environmental changes and start sensory nerve
impulses. If a receptor fails, the matching stimulus may not be detected. A
person may fail to feel heat or pain, detect smell, taste food, hear, or see
normally. The nervous system then lacks the input required for an appropriate
response, so danger or injury may go unnoticed.

### Exercise 5

**Draw a neuron and explain its function.**

[[figure:figure-6-1-neuron]]

Label dendrites, cell body with nucleus, axon, and axon ending. Add an arrow:

`dendrite -> cell body -> axon -> axon ending`

Dendrites acquire information and a chemical reaction starts an electrical
impulse. It travels through the cell body and axon. At the axon ending, chemical
transmitters cross a synapse and start a new impulse in the next cell.

### Exercise 6

**How does phototropism occur in plants?**

Auxin is made at the shoot tip. With light from one side, auxin moves towards the
shaded side. Higher auxin concentration makes those cells elongate more than
cells on the lit side. This unequal growth bends the shoot towards light,
showing positive phototropism.

### Exercise 7

**Which signals will be disrupted in a spinal-cord injury?**

Reflex-arc messages passing through the injured region may be disrupted. Sensory
impulses from body parts below the injury may not reach the brain, and motor
instructions from the brain may not reach their muscles or glands. The exact
loss depends on the location and severity of the injury.

### Exercise 8

**How does chemical coordination occur in plants?**

Plant cells make hormones such as auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin, and abscisic
acid. They diffuse from where they are made to where they act and control cell
elongation, cell division, growth inhibition, and directional responses. For
example, unequal auxin distribution bends a shoot towards light.

### Exercise 9

**Why does an organism need a control and coordination system?**

Different cells and organs must detect changes, exchange information, select an
appropriate response, and act together. Without coordination, unrelated or
conflicting actions would occur and the organism could not protect itself,
obtain resources, maintain internal conditions, or survive changing
surroundings.

### Exercise 10

**How are involuntary and reflex actions different?**

| Involuntary action | Reflex action |
| --- | --- |
| Occurs without conscious choice and may continue rhythmically | Rapid automatic response to a particular stimulus |
| Commonly controlled by mid-brain or hind-brain centres | Commonly completed through a spinal reflex arc |
| Example: breathing, salivation, blood pressure | Example: withdrawing a hand from heat |

### Exercise 11

**Compare nervous and hormonal control in animals.**

| Nervous control | Hormonal control |
| --- | --- |
| Message is an electrical impulse within a neuron | Message is a chemical hormone |
| Carried through connected neurons | Carried by blood |
| Very rapid | Usually slower |
| Acts on precise connected effectors | Can reach widely, but acts only on target cells |
| Usually short-lived | Often longer-lasting and persistent |

### Exercise 12

**How does movement in a sensitive plant differ from movement in our legs?**

The sensitive plant has no specialised nervous or muscle tissue. Touch
information travels cell to cell, and leaf cells alter their water content,
changing shape and folding without growth. Leg movement is produced when motor
neurons carry impulses to muscles; muscle proteins change arrangement, the
muscle fibres shorten, and bones move.
