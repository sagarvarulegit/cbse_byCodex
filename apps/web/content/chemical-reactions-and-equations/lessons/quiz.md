# Chemical Reactions and Equations chapter quiz

Answer these quick questions after revising the chapter notes.

## 1. Reaction

Topic: chemical-equations
Difficulty: Easy
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 1, Chemical reactions

A chemical reaction is:

1. a change that produces new substances
2. only a physical shape change
3. only a temperature reading

Answer: 1
Explanation: A chemical reaction changes the starting substances into new substances with different properties.

## 2. Equation balancing

Topic: balancing-equations
Difficulty: Easy
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 1, Chemical equations

Chemical equations are balanced to follow:

1. law of conservation of mass
2. law of motion
3. law of reflection

Answer: 1
Explanation: Balanced equations keep the number of atoms of each element equal on both sides, matching conservation of mass.

## 3. Combination

Topic: combination-and-decomposition-reactions
Difficulty: Medium
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 1, Types of chemical reactions

In a combination reaction:

1. one product forms from two or more reactants
2. one reactant always breaks apart into two products
3. nothing changes at all

Answer: 1
Explanation: Combination reactions join two or more reactants to form a single product, such as calcium oxide reacting with water.

## 4. Oxidation

Topic: oxidation-and-reduction
Difficulty: Medium
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 1, Oxidation and reduction

Oxidation often means:

1. gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen
2. loss of oxygen only
3. no exchange at all

Answer: 1
Explanation: In Class 10 terms, oxidation is usually identified by gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen.

## 5. Displacement

Topic: displacement-and-double-displacement-reactions
Difficulty: Medium
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter 1, Displacement reactions

In a displacement reaction:

1. a more reactive element replaces a less reactive one
2. all reactants disappear
3. only gases are produced

Answer: 1
Explanation: A more reactive element can displace a less reactive element from its compound, which is why the reactivity series matters.
