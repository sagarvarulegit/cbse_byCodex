# The Proposal

## chapter goal

By the end of this unit, a student should be able to:
- explain the plot and comic conflict of the play
- describe Lomov, Natalya, and Chubukov
- understand farce, exaggeration, and dramatic irony
- answer questions on marriage, property, pride, and humour

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jeff109.pdf`: Russian wedding context and Anton Chekhov's one-act farce "The Proposal".

1. Setting and purpose of visit

## 1) setting

The play is set in Chubukov's drawing room.

Lomov visits formally to ask for Natalya's hand in marriage.

## 2) Lomov

Lomov is nervous, unhealthy, and overly sensitive.

He wants to propose, but his pride and anxiety pull him into arguments.

## 3) Natalya

Natalya is eager for marriage but also proud and argumentative.

Before she knows about the proposal, she quarrels fiercely with Lomov.

When she learns why he came, she wants him called back.

## 4) Chubukov

Chubukov is Natalya's father. He is happy about the proposal but quickly joins the quarrels.

He is comic because he blesses the match and insults Lomov almost at the same time.

## 5) quarrels

The first quarrel is about ownership of Oxen Meadows.

The second quarrel is about whose dog is better: Guess or Squeezer.

These petty arguments almost destroy the marriage proposal.

## 6) farce and satire

The play is a farce: it uses exaggerated situations, quick quarrels, physical reactions, and absurd behaviour.

It satirises wealthy families who care more about property, pride, and status than love.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Lomov comes to propose to Natalya.
- The proposal is interrupted by petty quarrels.
- Oxen Meadows and the dogs become comic issues.
- Chubukov is both excited and quarrelsome.
- The play is a farce and satire on upper-class marriage.

## common mistakes students make

1. Treating the play as a romantic love story.
2. Forgetting the two main quarrels.
3. Describing Lomov only as sick, not proud and nervous.
4. Missing the satire on property and social status.

## board tips

- In play answers, mention dialogue and stage comedy.
- For character questions, use behaviour during quarrels.
- Define farce as exaggerated comedy.

## short answer practice

1. Why does Lomov visit Chubukov?
2. Why do Lomov and Natalya quarrel over Oxen Meadows?
3. What is the second quarrel about?
4. How does Chubukov create humour?
5. Why is the play called a farce?

## mini quiz with answers

3. Oxen Meadows is the first quarrel.
4. Guess and Squeezer are dogs.
5. Chekhov wrote the play.

Panel 3: Dog Debate begins: "Guess is better!" "No, Squeezer is!"
