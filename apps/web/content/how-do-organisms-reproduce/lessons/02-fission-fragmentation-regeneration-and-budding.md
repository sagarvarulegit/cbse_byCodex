# 7.2 Modes of Reproduction Used by Single Organisms

Asexual reproduction creates a new generation from one parent, without gamete
fusion. The method depends on the organism's body design.

## Activities 7.1 and 7.2

### Activity 7.1: budding in yeast

1. Dissolve about 10 g sugar in 100 mL water.
2. Take 20 mL in a test tube and add a pinch of yeast.
3. Plug with cotton and keep warm.
4. After 1-2 hours, mount a drop with a coverslip and observe.

Observation: small buds appear on parent yeast cells and may separate.

### Activity 7.2: bread mould

Wet bread and keep it in a cool, moist, dark place. Observe it with a magnifying
glass for a week. Cottony hyphae grow across it; later, upright structures with
dark sporangia appear.

## 7.2.1 Fission

In unicellular organisms, cell division itself produces new individuals.

### Binary fission

One parent forms two daughter cells.

[[figure:figure-7-1a-amoeba-fission]]

- **Amoeba:** divides in any plane because it has no fixed body axis.

[[figure:figure-7-1b-leishmania-fission]]

- **Leishmania:** divides lengthwise in a definite plane related to its
  flagellum and body orientation.

Activity 7.3 compares a permanent slide of Amoeba with one showing binary
fission.

### Multiple fission

One parent produces many daughter cells simultaneously.

[[figure:figure-7-2-plasmodium-fission]]

Example: the malarial parasite **Plasmodium**.

| Binary fission | Multiple fission |
| --- | --- |
| one parent forms two daughters | one parent forms many daughters |
| Amoeba, Leishmania | Plasmodium |

## 7.2.2 Fragmentation

Activity 7.4 uses dark-green pond water containing filamentous **Spirogyra**.
One or two filaments are mounted in glycerine and observed.

On maturity, a Spirogyra filament breaks into smaller fragments. Each fragment
grows into a new filament.

**Textbook correction to remember:** Spirogyra shows fragmentation. Hydra does
not; Hydra shows budding and can regenerate.

## Why fragmentation does not suit all multicellular organisms

Complex organisms have specialised cells organised into tissues, organs, and
organ systems in fixed positions. A random fragment does not contain or
reconstruct all these arrangements. Cell-by-cell division or fragmentation is
therefore impractical.

## 7.2.3 Regeneration

[[figure:figure-7-3-planaria-regeneration]]

In simple organisms such as Planaria and Hydra, specialised cells proliferate at
a cut surface. The new mass of cells differentiates in an organised sequence
called development, forming tissues and a complete organism.

Regeneration is **not the same as normal reproduction**: most organisms do not
normally depend on being cut into pieces to reproduce.

## 7.2.4 Budding

[[figure:figure-7-4-hydra-budding]]

Repeated cell division at one specific site on Hydra forms an outgrowth or bud.
The bud grows, develops tentacles, matures, and detaches as a new individual.

### Hydra's two relevant processes

- **Regeneration:** a cut piece rebuilds missing parts.
- **Budding:** a normal reproductive bud grows from one site and detaches.

## Rapid comparison

| Mode | Key event | NCERT example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binary fission | one cell -> two cells | Amoeba, Leishmania |
| Multiple fission | one cell -> many cells | Plasmodium |
| Fragmentation | mature filament breaks; pieces grow | Spirogyra |
| Regeneration | specialised cells rebuild a complete body | Planaria, Hydra |
| Budding | local outgrowth grows and detaches | Hydra, yeast |

## Common mistakes

1. Writing Hydra for fragmentation.
2. Calling every repair process reproduction.
3. Saying Amoeba has a fixed plane of division.
4. Confusing Plasmodium multiple fission with budding.
5. Calling Rhizopus hyphae spores; spores are inside sporangia.

## Board tip

When a process is shown in a diagram, write three items: **mode, organism,
visible evidence**. Example: "Hydra—budding—a side outgrowth grows and
detaches."

## Comic recap

- Leishmania: "My flagellum fixes the direction."
- Spirogyra: "A fragment becomes a filament."
- Hydra: "I regenerate after injury and bud for reproduction."
