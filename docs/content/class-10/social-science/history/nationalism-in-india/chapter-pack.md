# Nationalism in India

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain how nationalism grew under colonial rule
- describe the impact of the First World War
- understand satyagraha, Non-Cooperation, and Civil Disobedience
- identify social groups in national movements
- explain symbols of collective belonging

## 1) colonialism and nationalism

Indian nationalism grew through the shared experience of colonial rule.

Different groups suffered in different ways, but anti-colonial struggle helped connect them.

## 2) First World War effects

The war led to increased taxes, rising prices, forced recruitment, crop failures, and hardship.

These conditions created anger against colonial rule.

## 3) satyagraha

Satyagraha was Mahatma Gandhi's method of non-violent resistance based on truth.

It aimed to fight injustice without hatred or violence.

Early satyagraha movements included Champaran, Kheda, and Ahmedabad.

## 4) Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh

The Rowlatt Act allowed detention without trial and was strongly opposed.

The Jallianwala Bagh massacre in 1919 shocked the country and intensified anti-British feeling.

## 5) Khilafat and Non-Cooperation

Gandhiji linked the Khilafat issue with Non-Cooperation to build Hindu-Muslim unity.

Non-Cooperation included surrendering titles, boycotting schools, courts, councils, foreign goods, and promoting swadeshi.

The movement was withdrawn after the Chauri Chaura incident because it turned violent.

## 6) differing strands

In towns, students, teachers, lawyers, and merchants joined boycotts.

In the countryside, peasants linked swaraj with relief from high rents and oppression.

In plantations, workers interpreted swaraj as freedom to move and return home.

## 7) Civil Disobedience and Salt March

The Salt March of 1930 challenged the British salt law. Salt became a powerful symbol because it was used by rich and poor alike.

Civil Disobedience included breaking colonial laws, refusing taxes, boycotts, and protests.

## 8) participants and limits

Different groups joined for different reasons: peasants, industrial workers, business classes, women, and students.

The movement had limits. Dalit leaders, some Muslim political groups, and other communities had concerns about representation, social justice, and separate electorates.

## 9) collective belonging

Nationalism spread through symbols, songs, images, folklore, flags, and the idea of Bharat Mata.

These symbols helped people imagine the nation as a shared community.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Satyagraha means non-violent struggle for truth.
- Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh intensified national anger.
- Non-Cooperation was linked with Khilafat.
- Salt March made Civil Disobedience a mass movement.
- Different groups joined with different expectations.
- National symbols helped create collective belonging.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing that every group joined for the same reason.
2. Forgetting why the Salt March was symbolic.
3. Mixing Non-Cooperation with Civil Disobedience.
4. Ignoring the limits of national unity.

## board tips

- Use movement names with dates where possible.
- In social group questions, write group + reason + form of participation.
- For symbols, explain how they created emotional unity.
