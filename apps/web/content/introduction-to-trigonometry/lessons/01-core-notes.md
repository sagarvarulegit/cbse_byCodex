# 8.1-8.5 Introduction to Trigonometry

You can measure a tower without climbing it. A right triangle stores the needed relationship between an angle and its side lengths.

## 8.1 Introduction

Figures 8.1-8.3 show the Qutub Minar, opposite river banks and a balloon. Each situation can be modelled with right triangles and ratios.

## 8.2 Trigonometric Ratios

For acute angle $A$ in right triangle $ABC$, right-angled at $B$:

$$\sin A=\frac{BC}{AC},\quad \cos A=\frac{AB}{AC},\quad \tan A=\frac{BC}{AB},$$

$$\cosec A=\frac{AC}{BC},\quad \sec A=\frac{AC}{AB},\quad \cot A=\frac{AB}{BC}.$$

So,

$$\cosec A=\frac1{\sin A},\quad\sec A=\frac1{\cos A},\quad\cot A=\frac1{\tan A},$$

$$\tan A=\frac{\sin A}{\cos A},\quad\cot A=\frac{\cos A}{\sin A}.$$

The side names depend on the chosen angle. The hypotenuse does not change.

Figures 8.4-8.7 show why triangles with the same acute angle have the same ratios. Figures 8.8-8.13 support Examples 1-5 and Exercise 8.1.

## 8.3 Trigonometric Ratios of Some Specific Angles

Figures 8.14-8.16 derive $45^\circ$, $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. Figures 8.17-8.18 explain the limiting values at $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.

### Table 8.1

| $A$ | $0^\circ$ | $30^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\sin A$ | $0$ | $1/2$ | $1/\sqrt2$ | $\sqrt3/2$ | $1$ |
| $\cos A$ | $1$ | $\sqrt3/2$ | $1/\sqrt2$ | $1/2$ | $0$ |
| $\tan A$ | $0$ | $1/\sqrt3$ | $1$ | $\sqrt3$ | not defined |
| $\cosec A$ | not defined | $2$ | $\sqrt2$ | $2/\sqrt3$ | $1$ |
| $\sec A$ | $1$ | $2/\sqrt3$ | $\sqrt2$ | $2$ | not defined |
| $\cot A$ | not defined | $\sqrt3$ | $1$ | $1/\sqrt3$ | $0$ |

**Examples 6-8** apply standard values and the facts $\sin A=\cos(90^\circ-A)$ and $\cos A=\sin(90^\circ-A)$.

## 8.4 Trigonometric Identities

Figure 8.21 applies Pythagoras' theorem and derives:

$$\sin^2A+\cos^2A=1,$$

$$1+\tan^2A=\sec^2A,$$

$$1+\cot^2A=\cosec^2A.$$

An identity is true for every angle for which both sides are defined. It is not an equation to solve for one angle.

**Examples 9-12** express ratios in terms of one ratio and prove identities by simplifying one side into the other.

## 8.5 Summary

- Six ratios compare the sides of a right triangle relative to an acute angle.
- Table 8.1 gives the five standard-angle values.
- Pythagoras' theorem gives the three identities.

## Common mistakes

- choosing opposite and adjacent before marking the reference angle
- writing values at $90^\circ$ that require division by zero
- treating $\sin^2A$ as $\sin(A^2)$
- proving an identity by assuming the statement to be proved

## Board tips

- Sketch and mark the right angle and reference angle.
- In an identity, begin with the more complicated side.
- Convert everything to sine and cosine if the simplification stalls.

## Quick practice

2. Evaluate $2\sin^230^\circ+\cos^260^\circ$.
3. Express $\sec^2A-1$ in terms of $\tan A$.
