# Development

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain why development goals differ
- connect income with non-income goals
- compare countries or states using per capita income and other indicators
- understand public facilities and sustainability

## 1) different people, different goals

Development means improvement, but people do not always want the same improvement.

A landless rural labourer, a rich farmer, an urban student, and an industrialist may have different goals because their life situations are different.

Sometimes one person’s development may harm another person. For example, a dam may provide electricity but displace people.

## 2) income and other goals

Income is important because it helps people buy goods and services.

But people also value security, respect, freedom, equal treatment, health, education, clean environment, and dignity.

So development cannot be judged only by money.

## 3) national development

National development means thinking about what is fair and beneficial for the whole country.

It requires comparing different goals and deciding which goals are more just, inclusive, and sustainable.

## 4) comparing countries and states

Average income, or per capita income, is commonly used to compare countries and states.

Formula:
`Per capita income = total income / total population`

Average income is useful but hides inequality. Two countries can have the same average income but very different income distribution.

## 5) income and other criteria

Better indicators include literacy rate, infant mortality rate, net attendance ratio, life expectancy, and Human Development Index.

Health and education show whether people actually enjoy better quality of life.

## 6) public facilities

Public facilities are services provided collectively, such as schools, hospitals, roads, sanitation, public transport, and safe drinking water.

Many development goals require public facilities because individuals cannot arrange them alone.

## 7) sustainability

Sustainable development means using resources in a way that does not damage future generations’ ability to live well.

Overuse of groundwater, fossil fuels, forests, and minerals can create future crises.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Development goals vary from person to person.
- Income matters, but non-income goals also matter.
- Per capita income helps comparison but hides inequality.
- Public facilities improve quality of life.
- Sustainable development protects future generations.

## common mistakes students make

1. Defining development only as more income.
2. Forgetting that goals can conflict.
3. Treating average income as a perfect measure.
4. Mixing literacy rate with attendance ratio.

## board tips

- Use examples when explaining different goals.
- In comparison answers, mention both income and non-income indicators.
- For sustainability, write one resource example.
