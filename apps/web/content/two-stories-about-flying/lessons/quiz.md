# Two Stories about Flying chapter quiz

Answer these questions after revising the chapter notes.

## 1. Fear on the ledge

Topic: his-first-flight-fear-and-hunger
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, His First Flight

Why does the young seagull stay on the ledge at the beginning?

1. He wants to sleep through the day.
2. He is afraid of flying and falling.
3. He has already eaten enough fish.
4. He is waiting for a storm.

Answer: 2
Explanation: The first barrier is fear. The lesson warns against calling the seagull lazy because fear, not carelessness, keeps him fixed on the ledge.

## 2. Hunger as pressure

Topic: his-first-flight-fear-and-hunger
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, His First Flight

What does hunger do for the young seagull?

1. It makes him forget the sea.
2. It solves his fear without action.
3. It pushes him toward the food and the first attempt.
4. It makes his family leave forever.

Answer: 3
Explanation: Hunger creates pressure because the food is visible but out of reach. That pressure helps move him toward action.

## 3. The mother's role

Topic: his-first-flight-courage-and-family-pressure
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, His First Flight

Why does the mother seagull bring food close but not simply feed him?

1. She has forgotten where he is.
2. She wants him to stop flying forever.
3. She wants him to hide from the family.
4. She tempts him toward the edge so he will try flying.

Answer: 4
Explanation: The mother uses food as firm pressure. Her action creates the moment where he must choose between hunger and fear.

## 4. Courage after action

Topic: his-first-flight-courage-and-family-pressure
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, His First Flight

Which sequence best matches the seagull's change?

1. Fear, pressure, first dive, confidence.
2. Confidence, sleep, hunger, fear.
3. Storm, instrument failure, landing, mystery.
4. Prize, school, apology, flight.

Answer: 1
Explanation: The lesson sequence is fear first, then pressure from hunger and family, then the first dive, and finally growing confidence.

## 5. Storm danger

Topic: the-black-aeroplane-storm-and-mystery
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, The Black Aeroplane

What changes the mood of The Black Aeroplane from confident to dangerous?

1. A school bell.
2. A lost ball.
3. Dark storm clouds and failed guidance.
4. A funny animal description.

Answer: 3
Explanation: The storm and the unreliable instruments create danger and suspense during the flight to England.

## 6. Instrument problem

Topic: the-black-aeroplane-storm-and-mystery
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, The Black Aeroplane

Why are the instruments important in the storm section?

1. They prove the pilot is at home.
2. They should guide the plane, so their failure increases suspense.
3. They turn the plane into a bird.
4. They explain who the helper is.

Answer: 2
Explanation: The instruments matter because they are supposed to guide the pilot. When they do not help clearly, the danger feels stronger.

## 7. The mystery

Topic: the-black-aeroplane-unknown-helper
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, The Black Aeroplane

Why does the ending remain mysterious?

1. The narrator never lands.
2. The storm was not real.
3. The control room says no other aeroplane was seen.
4. The narrator refuses to fly to England.

Answer: 3
Explanation: The pilot seems to be guided by a black aeroplane, but the control room later says no other aeroplane was seen.

## 8. Safe inference

Topic: the-black-aeroplane-unknown-helper
Difficulty: hard
Source: NCERT First Flight, Two Stories about Flying, The Black Aeroplane

Which answer is safest for an exam question about the helper?

1. The story keeps the helper unexplained, so the mystery should remain open.
2. The helper is certainly the pilot's brother.
3. The helper is certainly a teacher from school.
4. The helper is certainly a wild animal.

Answer: 1
Explanation: The text does not confirm one fixed identity for the helper. A good answer respects the uncertainty instead of inventing certainty.

## 9. Humour in Wild Animals

Topic: poems-wild-animals-and-the-ball-poem
Difficulty: easy
Source: NCERT First Flight poem, How to Tell Wild Animals

What creates humour in How to Tell Wild Animals?

1. A completely serious scientific tone.
2. A list of railway stations.
3. A hidden aeroplane guide.
4. Funny and exaggerated clues about animals.

Answer: 4
Explanation: The poem is playful because it uses exaggerated clues. It should not be treated like a serious science description.

## 10. Symbol in The Ball Poem

Topic: poems-wild-animals-and-the-ball-poem
Difficulty: medium
Source: NCERT First Flight poem, The Ball Poem

What does the lost ball mainly suggest in The Ball Poem?

1. Only a sports prize.
2. Loss, responsibility, and growing up.
3. The young seagull's first meal.
4. The pilot's compass.

Answer: 2
Explanation: The lesson presents the ball as a symbol of loss and maturity. The child begins to understand that some things cannot simply return.
