# 9.1-9.2 Some Applications of Trigonometry

Height-and-distance problems are not a new set of formulas. They are right triangles hidden inside towers, ladders, shadows, buildings, bridges and balloons.

## 9.1 Heights and Distances

The line from an observer's eye to the object is the line of sight.

- angle of elevation: line of sight is above the observer's horizontal
- angle of depression: line of sight is below the observer's horizontal

Figures 9.1-9.3 define these three ideas. Corresponding horizontal lines are parallel, so an angle of depression equals the matching angle of elevation.

### Reliable method

1. Draw the horizontal through the observer.
2. Mark the line of sight and angle.
3. Add observer height or platform height separately.
4. Name known and unknown sides.
5. Choose the ratio that uses only those sides.
6. Solve and attach units.

**Example 1**

A tower is viewed from $15$ m away at $60^\circ$. Figure 9.4 gives

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{h}{15},$$

so $h=15\sqrt3$ m.

**Example 2** reaches a repair point $1.3$ m below the top of a $5$ m pole. The ladder length is about $4.28$ m and its foot is about $2.14$ m from the pole.

**Example 3** adds the observer's $1.5$ m eye height after finding the vertical distance from eye level, giving a $30$ m chimney.

**Example 4** uses two observation points on one line to find a $7.32$ m flagstaff and the distance from the nearer point.

**Example 5** uses two angles of elevation and a shadow difference to find a tower height $20\sqrt3$ m.

**Example 6** uses angles of depression to the top and bottom of an $8$ m building and finds the other building's height and separation.

**Example 7** uses the bridge height and two depression angles to find the river width $3(\sqrt3+1)$ m.

## 9.2 Summary

- line of sight joins observer and object
- elevation looks up from the horizontal
- depression looks down from the horizontal
- solve the drawn right triangle with trigonometric ratios

## Common mistakes

- measuring elevation from the vertical instead of the horizontal
- forgetting observer or building height
- assuming a diagram is to scale
- using an angle of depression without transferring it through parallel horizontals

## Board tips

- Draw and label before calculating.
- Keep exact surd values until the final line.
- State any common textbook assumption such as level ground or vertical object.

## Quick practice

1. From $20$ m away, the angle of elevation of a tower is $45^\circ$. Find its height.
2. An observer $1.6$ m tall sees a top at $45^\circ$ from $18$ m away. Find the object's height.
3. Why does the matching elevation angle equal the depression angle?

## Answer check

1. $20$ m.
2. $18+1.6=19.6$ m.
3. The observer and object horizontals are parallel, and the line of sight is a transversal.
