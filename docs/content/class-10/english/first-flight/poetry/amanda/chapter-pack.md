# Amanda!

## chapter goal

Understand the contrast between adult instructions and Amanda's private world of imagination.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "From the Diary of Anne Frank" in `jeff104.pdf`.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Adult instructions.
2. Amanda's imagination.
3. Contrast.
4. Theme of freedom.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) adult voice

Amanda is repeatedly told what to do and what not to do.

The instructions show control, correction, and pressure.

## 2) Amanda's imagination

Amanda escapes into imagined worlds where she feels free, silent, and unbothered.

Her imagination shows her need for space.

## 3) contrast

The poem moves between outer control and inner freedom.

This contrast helps us understand Amanda's loneliness and desire for independence.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The poem shows a child under constant correction.
- Amanda uses imagination as escape.
- Contrast is central.
- The poem asks adults to understand children gently.

## common mistakes students make

1. Blaming only Amanda without seeing pressure.
2. Ignoring the fantasy sections.
3. Writing that all discipline is bad.

## board tips

- Mention both adult concern and child pressure.
- Use contrast in answers.
- Explain imagination as escape, not laziness.

## short answer practice

1. Why does Amanda escape into imagination?
2. What does the adult voice show?
3. How does contrast work in the poem?
