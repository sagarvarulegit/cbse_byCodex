# Chemical Reactions and Equations chapter quiz

Answer all questions after revising the six lessons. Work out equations on paper before selecting an option.

## 1. Evidence of reaction

Topic: chemical-equations
Difficulty: Easy
Source: NCERT Chapter 1, opening observations after Activities 1.1-1.3

Which observation is listed by NCERT as evidence that a chemical reaction may have occurred?

1. evolution of a gas
2. change in the size of a container
3. movement of a test tube
4. change in handwriting

Answer: 1
Explanation: NCERT lists change in state, colour, temperature and evolution of a gas as evidence of a chemical reaction.

## 2. Figure 1.2

Topic: chemical-equations
Difficulty: Easy
Source: NCERT Activity 1.3 and Figure 1.2

NCERT Figure 1.2 belongs to which activity?

1. lead nitrate mixed with potassium iodide
2. zinc reacting with dilute sulphuric acid
3. quicklime mixed with water
4. iron nails dipped in copper sulphate

Answer: 2
Explanation: Figure 1.2 shows hydrogen formation when zinc reacts with dilute sulphuric acid in Activity 1.3.

## 3. Skeletal equation

Topic: chemical-equations
Difficulty: Easy
Source: NCERT Section 1.1.1, equation (1.2)

Why is `Mg + O₂ → MgO` called a skeletal equation?

1. It contains no element symbols.
2. It contains no reactants.
3. Its atom counts are not equal on the two sides.
4. It describes only a physical change.

Answer: 3
Explanation: There are two oxygen atoms on the left but one on the right, so equation (1.2) is unbalanced.

## 4. Conservation of mass

Topic: balancing-equations
Difficulty: Easy
Source: NCERT Section 1.1.2, balanced chemical equations

Why must a chemical equation be balanced?

1. To make every formula shorter
2. To remove physical-state symbols
3. To change products into reactants
4. To obey the law of conservation of mass

Answer: 4
Explanation: Equal atom counts on both sides show that mass has neither been created nor destroyed.

## 6. Combination pattern

Topic: combination-and-decomposition-reactions
Difficulty: Easy
Source: NCERT Section 1.2.1, combination reaction

Which pattern defines a combination reaction?

1. One reactant always forms one element.
2. Two or more reactants form a single product.
3. Two compounds exchange ions.
4. One element replaces another.

Answer: 2
Explanation: A combination reaction forms one product from two or more reactants.

## 7. Balancing sodium and water

Topic: balancing-equations
Difficulty: Medium
Source: NCERT first Questions block, Question 2(iii)

Which is the correctly balanced equation for sodium reacting with water?

1. `Na + H₂O → NaOH + H₂`
2. `2Na + H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂`
3. `2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂`
4. `Na + 2H₂O → NaOH + 2H₂`

Answer: 3
Explanation: The coefficients 2, 2, 2 and 1 give equal Na, H and O atom counts.

## 11. Electrolysis volumes

## 13. Displacement observation

Topic: displacement-and-double-displacement-reactions
Difficulty: Medium
Source: NCERT Activity 1.9 and equation (1.24)

Why does the blue colour of copper sulphate solution fade when iron nails are immersed?

1. Iron displaces copper and pale-green iron sulphate forms.
2. Copper sulphate evaporates completely.
3. Iron changes into lead.
4. The solution becomes pure water.

Answer: 1
Explanation: More reactive iron displaces copper, producing iron sulphate and a copper coating on the nails.

## 15. Redox identification

Topic: oxidation-and-reduction
Difficulty: Medium
Source: NCERT equation (1.29)

In `CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O`, which statement is correct?

1. CuO is oxidised and H₂ is reduced.
2. Both CuO and H₂ are oxidised.
3. H₂ is oxidised and CuO is reduced.
4. Neither reactant changes.

Answer: 3
Explanation: Hydrogen gains oxygen, while copper oxide loses oxygen.

## 18. Two-reaction classification

Topic: displacement-and-double-displacement-reactions
Difficulty: Hard
Source: NCERT Sections 1.2.3-1.2.4

How should these reactions be classified in order? `Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu`; `Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl`

1. combination, decomposition
2. displacement, double displacement
3. double displacement, displacement
4. oxidation only, combination

Explanation: Zinc replaces copper in the first; ions exchange and a precipitate forms in the second.

## 19. Complete oxidation answer

Topic: oxidation-and-reduction
Difficulty: Hard
Source: NCERT Exercise 16

Which pair correctly gives two examples of oxidation by gain of oxygen?

1. `CuO → Cu` and `PbO → Pb`
2. `AgCl → Ag` and `CaCO₃ → CaO`
3. `2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO` and `2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO`
4. `H₂O → H₂` and `FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃`

Answer: 3
Explanation: Magnesium and copper each gain oxygen to form their oxides.

## 20. Rancidity reasoning

Topic: ncert-exercises-and-revision
Difficulty: Hard
Source: NCERT Section 1.3.2 and Exercise 19

Why are oily food packets flushed with nitrogen?

1. Nitrogen changes oil into water.
2. Nitrogen increases the packet temperature.
3. Nitrogen makes oxidation faster.
4. Nitrogen limits contact with oxygen and delays rancidity.

Answer: 4
Explanation: Rancidity is caused by oxidation of fats and oils; replacing oxygen with nitrogen slows that process.
