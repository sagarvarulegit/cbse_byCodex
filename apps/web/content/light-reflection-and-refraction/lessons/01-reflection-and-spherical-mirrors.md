# 9.1 Reflection of Light

Light reflected from an object enters our eyes and makes the object visible.
Ray optics treats light as travelling in straight lines.

## Laws of reflection

1. Angle of incidence `i =` angle of reflection `r`.
2. Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal at the point of incidence lie in
   the same plane.

A plane mirror forms an image that is virtual, erect, same-sized, laterally
inverted, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

# 9.2 Spherical Mirrors

- **Concave mirror:** reflecting surface curves inward; converging mirror.
- **Convex mirror:** reflecting surface bulges outward; diverging mirror.

## NCERT terms

| Symbol | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `P` | pole | centre of reflecting surface |
| `C` | centre of curvature | centre of the sphere of which mirror is a part |
| `R = PC` | radius of curvature | radius of that sphere |
| principal axis | line through P and C | normal to mirror at P |
| `F` | principal focus | point where parallel rays meet/appear to diverge |
| `f = PF` | focal length | pole-to-focus distance |
| aperture | — | diameter of reflecting surface |

For a small-aperture spherical mirror: `R = 2f`.

## Activities 9.1 and 9.2

- A shining spoon's inner surface behaves approximately as a concave mirror;
  its outer surface as a convex mirror.
- A concave mirror converges sunlight to a bright image of the Sun near F.
  Concentrated energy can burn paper.

**Safety:** never look directly at the Sun or at its mirror reflection.

## First question block

1. Principal focus of a concave mirror: point on the principal axis where rays
   parallel to the axis meet after reflection.
2. If `R = 20 cm`, then `f = R/2 = 10 cm`.
3. An erect enlarged image is produced by a concave mirror when the object is
   between P and F.
4. Convex rear-view mirrors give an erect, diminished image and a wide field of
   view.
