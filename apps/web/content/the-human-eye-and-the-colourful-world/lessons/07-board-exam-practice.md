# Board Exam Practice

## Recurring demand families

1. Label cornea, iris, pupil, lens, ciliary muscles, retina, optic nerve.
2. Explain accommodation for near versus distant objects.
3. State normal near and far points.
4. Diagnose myopia from a near far-point or blurred blackboard.
5. Give both myopia causes and concave correction.
6. Give both hypermetropia causes and convex correction.
7. Explain presbyopia and bifocals when two defects coexist.
8. Calculate corrective power from a far point.
9. Calculate hypermetropic correction from abnormal near point.
10. Trace prism refraction and identify `i,r,e,A,D`.
11. Define dispersion, spectrum, VIBGYOR; compare red/violet deviation.
12. Explain Newton's inverted-prism recombination.
13. Explain rainbow sequence: refraction/dispersion, internal reflection,
    refraction.
14. Explain star twinkling and planet non-twinkling.
15. Explain advanced sunrise, delayed sunset, apparent flattening.
16. Define Tyndall effect with one example.
17. Explain blue sky and astronaut's dark sky.
18. Explain red sunrise/sunset and use of red danger signals.
19. Distinguish dispersion, atmospheric refraction, and scattering.
20. Distinguish scattering from reflection: particle scattering redirects
    incident light in many directions; reflection obeys a definite surface
    normal and `i=r`.

## Five-mark defect answer frame

Name defect -> what is clear/blurred -> focus relative to retina -> two causes
-> correcting lens with reason -> labelled before/after ray diagram.

## Numerical frame

Convert cm to metre -> assign `u` and desired virtual `v` -> use
`1/f=1/v-1/u` -> `P=1/f(m)` -> state lens type from sign.

## Assertion/reason traps

- Pupil controls amount of light; ciliary muscles control focal length.
- Image distance does not change appreciably because retina is fixed.
- Stars twinkle because they are point sources, not because they emit
  fluctuating light.
- The sky is blue due scattering, not reflection of oceans.
- Red is least scattered and least deviated; violet is most strongly scattered
  and most deviated in the chapter's contexts.
