# Oxides and chemical properties

Metals and non-metals behave differently when they react with oxygen. The nature of the oxide often tells us whether it is basic or acidic.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- write simple metal and non-metal oxide reactions
- explain why metal oxides are generally basic
- explain why non-metal oxides are generally acidic
- connect litmus changes with oxide nature

## Metals with oxygen

Metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides.

Example:

`2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO`

Metal oxides are generally basic. Some metal oxides can also be amphoteric, but the main Class 10 rule is:

`metal oxide -> usually basic`

## Non-metals with oxygen

Non-metals react with oxygen to form non-metal oxides.

Example:

`S + O2 -> SO2`

Non-metal oxides are generally acidic.

Burn magnesium and sulphur, dissolve the product in water, and test with litmus. Magnesium oxide behaves as a basic oxide, while sulphur dioxide gives an acidic solution.

## Litmus clues

Use this simple map:

- Basic solution turns red litmus blue.
- Acidic solution turns blue litmus red.
- Magnesium oxide in water is basic.
- Sulphur dioxide in water is acidic.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Magnesium burning

Question:
What forms when magnesium burns in air?

Answer:
Magnesium oxide forms.

`2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO`

### Example 2: Sulphur dioxide

Question:
Why does sulphur dioxide turn moist blue litmus red?

Answer:
It forms an acidic solution in water.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying all oxides are basic.
2. Forgetting that sulphur is a non-metal.
3. Writing `MgO2` instead of `MgO`.
4. Using dry litmus when the gas needs moisture to show acidic behaviour.

## Board tips

- For oxide nature, always mention litmus colour change.
- Write balanced equations when the question asks for reactions with oxygen.
- Use "generally" for metal and non-metal oxide rules because exceptions exist.

## One-minute revision

Metals form oxides that are generally basic. Non-metals form oxides that are generally acidic. Use litmus change as visible evidence.
