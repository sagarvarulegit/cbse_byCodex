# Chapter 6: Manufacturing Industries

Cotton becomes yarn, yarn becomes cloth, and cloth becomes a shirt. Every step adds value. Manufacturing connects farms, mines, workers, transport, markets and services.

## Chapter goal

- Classify industries on the textbook bases.
- Understand the location and challenges of major agro- and mineral-based industries.
- Explain industrial pollution and practical controls.

## Importance of Manufacturing

Manufacturing is production in large quantities after processing raw materials into more valuable products.

It supports development because it:

- modernises agriculture and supplies farm inputs
- creates jobs in secondary and tertiary sectors
- reduces dependence on agricultural income
- helps reduce unemployment and poverty
- supports balanced regional development
- expands trade and earns foreign exchange
- turns raw materials into higher-value finished goods

Agriculture and industry depend on each other. Farms supply raw materials; industries supply pumps, fertilisers, machines, transport and markets.

## Agro-Based Industries

### Textile Industry

The textile industry has a complete value chain from fibre to garments and is important for employment, industrial production and foreign exchange.

Cotton spinning is concentrated mainly in Maharashtra, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu, while weaving is decentralised to use traditional skills and designs. Handloom, powerloom and mill sectors all contribute.

Khadi and hand-spun yarn support home-based employment. The textbook asks why Gandhi valued spinning and why mills should not remove the livelihood of handloom and powerloom workers.

### Jute Textiles

Most jute mills lie along the Hugli River in West Bengal. Location factors include nearby jute fields, inexpensive water transport, rail and road links, abundant water for processing, labour and Kolkata's banking, insurance and port services.

### Sugar Industry

Sugar mills are near sugarcane fields because cane is bulky and quickly loses sugar content. Many mills have shifted towards Maharashtra and other southern and western states because of higher sucrose content, a cooler climate, longer crushing season and successful cooperatives.

## Mineral-Based Industries

### Iron and Steel Industry

Iron and steel is a basic industry because other industries depend on its machinery and material. Its heavy raw materials make transport cost important. Plants need iron ore, coking coal, limestone, manganese, water, power, labour and market links.

The Chotanagpur plateau has a major concentration because raw materials, labour, transport and markets are relatively close.

### Aluminium Smelting

Aluminium is light, corrosion-resistant, malleable and a good conductor. Bauxite is refined into alumina and then smelted into aluminium. Smelting requires a large, regular electricity supply, so power availability strongly affects location.

### Chemical and Fertiliser Industries

Inorganic chemicals include sulphuric acid, nitric acid, alkalis and soda ash. Organic chemicals include petrochemicals used for synthetic fibres, rubber, plastics, dyes and medicines.

Fertiliser plants produce nitrogenous, phosphatic and complex fertilisers. Phosphate and potash inputs may need imports, so raw materials and port links matter.

### Cement Industry

Cement uses limestone, silica, alumina and gypsum. Plants need heavy raw materials, coal, electricity, rail transport and access to markets.

### Automobile, Information Technology and Electronics Industries

Automobile centres include Delhi-Gurugram, Mumbai-Pune, Chennai, Kolkata, Lucknow, Indore, Hyderabad, Jamshedpur and Bengaluru. Electronics and IT produce devices, communication systems, hardware and software and have created employment with less material pollution than many heavy industries.

## Industrial Pollution and Environmental Degradation

| Type | Main source or effect |
| --- | --- |
| Air | Smoke, dust, gases and particles from factories and thermal plants |
| Water | Organic and inorganic waste, dyes, acids, salts, metals and hot water |
| Land | Glass, chemicals, packaging, salts, ash and other solid waste |
| Noise | Machinery, generators, compressors, drills and construction |

Thermal pollution occurs when hot industrial or power-plant water enters rivers before cooling. Noise can damage hearing and affect heart rate and blood pressure.

## Control of Environmental Degradation

Industries can reduce harm by:

- reusing and recycling water
- harvesting rainwater
- treating effluent through primary, secondary and tertiary stages
- controlling groundwater withdrawal
- fitting smoke stacks with electrostatic precipitators, filters, scrubbers and inertial separators
- using cleaner fuel and quieter, well-maintained machinery
- reducing waste and maximising equipment use
- creating green belts and restoring land

The NTPC box shows measures such as equipment optimisation, ash-water recycling, green belts, pollution control, ash management and ecological monitoring.

## NCERT exercise questions and answers

### 1. Multiple choice questions

**(i) Which one of the following industries uses bauxite as a raw material?**

*Answer:* (a) Aluminium Smelting.

**(ii) Which one of the following industries manufactures telephones, computer, etc.?**

*Answer:* (b) Electronic.

### 2. Answer the following briefly in not more than 30 words

**(i) What is manufacturing?**

*Answer:* Manufacturing is producing goods in large quantities by processing raw materials into more valuable finished products.

**(ii) What are basic industries? Give an example.**

*Answer:* Basic industries supply raw material or machinery to other industries. The iron and steel industry is a basic industry.

### 3. Write the answers of the following questions in 120 words

**(i) How do industries pollute the environment?**

*Answer:* Industries pollute air through smoke, dust, gases and fine particles. They pollute water with organic waste, acids, dyes, salts, metals, oil and hot water. Solid wastes such as ash, glass, chemicals and packaging degrade land and may contaminate groundwater. Machinery, drills, generators and construction create noise that affects hearing and health. Mining and industrial growth can also remove vegetation and increase pressure on land and water. Pollution is therefore not one problem but a combination of air, water, land, thermal and noise damage.

**(ii) Discuss the steps to be taken to minimise environmental degradation by industry?**

*Answer:* Industries should reuse water, harvest rain and treat effluent before discharge. Primary, secondary and tertiary treatment removes solids, organic matter and harmful chemicals. Filters, scrubbers and electrostatic precipitators can reduce air pollution. Cleaner fuels, efficient machines, silencers and proper maintenance reduce smoke, energy waste and noise. Solid waste and ash need safe recycling or disposal. Green belts, restored land and continuous monitoring help protect nearby ecosystems. Environmental rules must be followed and results made accountable.

## Common mistakes

- Calling manufacturing any act of selling or transport.
- Classifying an industry on one basis when the question asks another.
- Saying cotton weaving and spinning have exactly the same location pattern.
- Forgetting electricity as a major aluminium-smelting input.
- Listing pollution types without their source or effect.

## Board tips

- For location questions, use raw material, power, labour, transport, market and water.
- Explain why iron and steel is basic instead of only naming it.
- In pollution answers, pair each pollutant with one control.

## One-minute revision

- Manufacturing adds value to raw materials.
- Industry and agriculture support each other.
- Classify by raw material, role, capital, ownership or weight.
- Iron and steel is basic; aluminium is power-intensive.
- Pollution can affect air, water, land, temperature and noise levels.
- Prevent, treat, recycle and monitor.
