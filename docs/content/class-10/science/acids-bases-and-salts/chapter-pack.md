- compare solutions on the pH scale and apply pH to daily life;
- classify salt families and salt pH;
- explain the chlor-alkali process and common-salt derivatives;
- explain water of crystallisation and Plaster of Paris;
- answer all NCERT in-text questions and Exercises 1-15.

## 2.1 Understanding the Chemical Properties of Acids and Bases

Indicator map:

| Indicator | Acid | Base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue litmus | Turns red | No change |
| Red litmus | No change | Turns blue |
| Phenolphthalein | Colourless | Pink |
| Methyl orange | Red | Yellow |
| Onion, vanilla, clove | Odour remains | Odour becomes difficult to detect |

Natural indicators include litmus, turmeric, red cabbage and coloured petals.
Synthetic indicators include methyl orange and phenolphthalein.

## 2.1.1 Acids and Bases in the Laboratory

Use an indicator instead of tasting a sample. In the opening textbook problem,
red litmus first identifies the base. The newly blue strip then identifies the
acid; distilled water causes no change.

## 2.1.2 How do Acids and Bases React with Metals?

`Acid + Metal -> Salt + Hydrogen gas`

`Zn + H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2`

Hydrogen burns with a pop sound.

Some metals react with strong bases:

`2NaOH + Zn -> Na2ZnO2 + H2`

The salt is sodium zincate. This is not a rule for every metal.

## 2.1.3 How do Metal Carbonates and Metal Hydrogencarbonates React with Acids?

`Metal carbonate/Metal hydrogencarbonate + Acid -> Salt + CO2 + H2O`

`Na2CO3 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + H2O + CO2`

`NaHCO3 + HCl -> NaCl + H2O + CO2`

Lime-water sequence:

`Ca(OH)2 + CO2 -> CaCO3 + H2O`

White `CaCO3` makes the solution milky.

`CaCO3 + H2O + CO2 -> Ca(HCO3)2`

Excess carbon dioxide forms soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate, so the milkiness
disappears.

## 2.1.4 How do Acids and Bases React with each other?

`Base + Acid -> Salt + Water`

`NaOH + HCl -> NaCl + H2O`

This is neutralisation.

## 2.1.5 Reaction of Metallic Oxides with Acids

`CuO + 2HCl -> CuCl2 + H2O`

The black solid dissolves and a blue-green solution forms. Metallic oxides are
basic because they react with acids to form salt and water.

## 2.1.6 Reaction of a Non-metallic Oxide with Base

`Ca(OH)2 + CO2 -> CaCO3 + H2O`

Carbon dioxide behaves like an acid when it reacts with a base. Non-metallic
oxides are acidic in this chapter.

## 2.2 What do all Acids and all Bases have in Common?

Acids conduct in water because they produce mobile `H+(aq)`/`H3O+` ions.
Glucose and alcohol contain hydrogen but do not produce these ions, so they are
not acids.

Bases produce `OH-(aq)` ions. An alkali is a base that dissolves in water.

## 2.2.1 What Happens to an Acid or a Base in a Water Solution?

`HCl + H2O -> H3O+ + Cl-`

`H+ + H2O -> H3O+`

`H+ + OH- -> H2O`

Dry HCl does not change dry blue litmus because water is needed for ion
formation. Wet blue litmus turns red.

Dilution lowers ion concentration per unit volume. Mixing a concentrated acid
or base with water releases much heat.

Caution: Always add acid slowly to water with constant stirring.

## 2.3 How Strong are Acid or Base Solutions?

| pH | Nature |
| --- | --- |
| Below `7` | Acidic |
| `7` | Neutral |
| Above `7` | Basic |

Lower pH means greater hydrogen-ion concentration. At the same concentration,
a strong acid produces more `H+` ions than a weak acid.

## 2.3.1 Importance of pH in Everyday Life

- Human body: suitable pH about `7.0-7.8`.
- Acid rain: rainwater pH below `5.6`.
- Soil: acidic soil can be treated with quick lime, slaked lime or chalk.
- Digestion: antacids such as milk of magnesia neutralise excess stomach acid.
- Teeth: enamel begins to corrode when mouth pH falls below `5.5`.
- Stings: baking soda can neutralise acid from a bee sting; nettle injects
  methanoic acid.

## 2.4 More about Salts

## 2.4.1 Family of Salts

Salts with the same positive ion or negative ion form a family. `NaCl` and
`Na2SO4` are sodium salts; `NaCl` and `KCl` are chloride salts.

## 2.4.2 pH of Salts

| Parent acid and base | Salt nature |
| --- | --- |
| Strong acid + strong base | Neutral |
| Strong acid + weak base | Acidic |
| Weak acid + strong base | Basic |

## 2.4.3 Chemicals from Common Salt

Brine electrolysis:

`2NaCl + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2`

Chlorine forms at the anode, hydrogen at the cathode and sodium hydroxide near
the cathode.

Bleaching powder:

`2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2 -> Ca(ClO)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O`

The current NCERT reprint represents bleaching powder as `Ca(ClO)2` while
noting that its actual composition is complex.

Baking soda:

`NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 -> NH4Cl + NaHCO3`

`2NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2`

Washing soda:

`Na2CO3 + 10H2O -> Na2CO3.10H2O`

## 2.4.4 Are the Crystals of Salts really Dry?

Water of crystallisation is the fixed number of water molecules in one formula
unit:

- hydrated copper sulphate: `CuSO4.5H2O`
- washing soda: `Na2CO3.10H2O`
- gypsum: `CaSO4.2H2O`
- Plaster of Paris: `CaSO4.1/2H2O`

Heating gypsum at `373 K` forms Plaster of Paris. Adding water changes it back
to hard gypsum.

## Common mistakes students make

1. Writing a non-existent section `2.5`.
2. Confusing the hydrogen pop test with the carbon-dioxide lime-water test.
3. Saying all compounds containing hydrogen are acids.
4. Adding water to concentrated acid.
5. Reading pH in the wrong direction.
6. Calling every salt neutral.
7. Swapping chlorine and hydrogen electrodes.
8. Confusing baking soda, washing soda, gypsum and Plaster of Paris.

## Board tips

- Write observation, inference and balanced equation.
- Answer every subpart before stopping.
- For pH, connect the number to hydrogen-ion concentration.
- For preparation questions, include raw materials, conditions and products.
- For chlor-alkali, name all three products and both electrodes.
- Use the formula convention stated by the current source or question.

## Solved practice set

### Question 1

Why does dry HCl gas not turn dry blue litmus red?

Answer: Water is needed for `HCl` to form `H+(aq)` or `H3O+` ions. Without
these ions, acidic behaviour is not shown.

### Question 2

Why is fizzing faster when magnesium reacts with hydrochloric acid than with
the same concentration of acetic acid?

Answer: Hydrochloric acid is strong and produces more hydrogen ions than weak
acetic acid, so hydrogen gas forms faster.

### Question 3

Name the products of the chlor-alkali process and their locations.

Answer: Chlorine at the anode, hydrogen at the cathode and sodium hydroxide
solution near the cathode.

## One-minute revision

- Indicator -> observation -> conclusion.
- Metal -> hydrogen; carbonate -> carbon dioxide.
- Acidity and basicity require ions in water.
- Lower pH -> more `H+`.
- Common salt -> chlor-alkali chemicals, baking soda and washing soda.
- Hydrated salts contain a fixed number of water molecules.
