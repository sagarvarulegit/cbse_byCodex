## 1) determiners

Determiners come before nouns. They show number, quantity, possession, or reference.

Check whether the noun is singular, plural, countable, or uncountable before choosing a determiner.

## 2) tenses

Tense shows time and completion. Do not choose tense by one word only.

Read the full sentence and look for time clues, sequence, and meaning.

## 3) modals

Modals show ability, permission, possibility, advice, duty, or necessity.

Choose the modal that matches the speaker's intention.

## 4) subject-verb concord

The verb must agree with the real subject, not the closest noun.

Ignore extra phrases between subject and verb while checking agreement.

## 5) reported speech

Reported speech changes speaker words into a reporting sentence.

Check reporting verb, pronoun, tense, word order, and punctuation.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Determiners depend on noun type.
- Tense depends on time, sequence, and meaning.
- Modals express intention.
- Subject and verb must agree.
- Reported speech needs pronoun and tense control.

## common mistakes students make

1. Matching the verb with the nearest noun.
2. Using a modal that sounds polite but changes meaning.
3. Forgetting question word order in reported speech.

## board tips

- Read the whole sentence before choosing.
- Cross out extra phrases to find the subject.
- In editing, check one grammar target at a time.
