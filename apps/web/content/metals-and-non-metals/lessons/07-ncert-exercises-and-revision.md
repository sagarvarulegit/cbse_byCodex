# What You Have Learnt

- Elements are classified as metals and non-metals.
- Metals are generally lustrous, malleable, ductile and good conductors of heat
  and electricity. They are solid at room temperature except mercury.
- Metals lose electrons to non-metals and form positive ions.
- Metal oxides are generally basic; Al₂O₃ and ZnO are amphoteric.
- Different metals show different reactivities with water and dilute acids.
- The activity series arranges common metals in decreasing reactivity.
- Metals above hydrogen displace hydrogen from dilute acids.
- A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt.
- Metals occur in the free state or as compounds.
- Extracting a metal from its ore and refining it is metallurgy.
- An alloy is a homogeneous mixture of metals, or a metal and non-metal.
- Long exposure of metal surfaces such as iron to moist air causes corrosion.
- Non-metals are generally neither malleable nor ductile and are poor
  conductors; graphite conducts electricity.
- Non-metals gain electrons from metals and form negative ions.
- Non-metal oxides are acidic or neutral.
- Non-metals do not displace hydrogen from dilute acids; they react with
  hydrogen to form hydrides.

# NCERT Exercises

Attempt every question first. Then use the answer as a marking checklist.

## 1. Which pair will give a displacement reaction?

(a) NaCl solution and copper metal  
(b) MgCl₂ solution and aluminium metal  
(c) FeSO₄ solution and silver metal  
(d) AgNO₃ solution and copper metal

### Answer

**(d)**. Copper is more reactive than silver:

`Cu(s) + 2AgNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s)`

The other metal in each pair lies below the metal present in the salt and
cannot displace it.

## 2. Which method is suitable for preventing an iron frying pan from rusting?

(a) Applying grease  
(b) Applying paint  
(c) Applying a coating of zinc  
(d) All of the above

### Answer

**(c) Applying a coating of zinc.** Grease and paint would not be suitable for
a frying surface used for heating food. Zinc coating protects iron from air and
water.

## 3. An element reacts with oxygen to give a high-melting compound that is soluble in water. The element is likely to be

(a) calcium  (b) carbon  (c) silicon  (d) iron

### Answer

**(a) calcium.** Calcium forms ionic calcium oxide, which has a high melting
point and reacts with/dissolves in water to form calcium hydroxide:

`2Ca + O₂ → 2CaO`

`CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂`

## 4. Food cans are coated with tin and not zinc because

(a) zinc is costlier than tin  
(b) zinc has a higher melting point than tin  
(c) zinc is more reactive than tin  
(d) zinc is less reactive than tin

### Answer

**(c) zinc is more reactive than tin.** If the coating reacts with food acids,
zinc would be attacked more readily. Tin is less reactive and better protects
the food.

## 5. You are given a hammer, battery, bulb, wires and switch.

### (a) How could you distinguish metals from non-metals?

### Answer

1. **Hammer test:** hammer the sample. A metal generally flattens because it is
   malleable; a solid non-metal generally breaks because it is brittle.
2. **Circuit test:** place the sample in the circuit. A glowing bulb indicates
   electrical conduction, generally a metal property.

### (b) Assess the usefulness of these tests.

### Answer

They are useful preliminary tests but not perfectly reliable because there are
exceptions. Graphite is a non-metal that conducts electricity, while some
metals are soft or brittle under particular conditions. Use several properties
and chemical evidence rather than one test alone.

## 6. What are amphoteric oxides? Give two examples.

### Answer

Amphoteric oxides react with both acids and bases to form salt and water.
Examples: aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) and zinc oxide (ZnO).

`Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O`

`Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O`

## 7. Name two metals that displace hydrogen from dilute acids and two that do not.

### Answer

Magnesium and zinc displace hydrogen because they lie above hydrogen in the
activity series. Copper and silver do not because they lie below hydrogen.

## 8. In electrolytic refining of a metal M, what are the anode, cathode and electrolyte?

### Answer

- Anode: thick slab of impure metal M.
- Cathode: thin sheet of pure metal M.
- Electrolyte: solution of a soluble salt of M.

Pure M deposits on the cathode; insoluble impurities form anode mud.

## 9. Sulphur powder is heated and the gas is collected in an inverted test tube.

### (a)(i) Action on dry litmus

No change. Acidic behaviour requires water.

### (a)(ii) Action on moist litmus

Moist blue litmus turns red because SO₂ dissolves in water to form sulphurous
acid.

### (b) Balanced equation

`S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)`

In water:

`SO₂(g) + H₂O(l) → H₂SO₃(aq)`

## 10. State two ways to prevent rusting of iron.

### Answer

Any two with explanation:

- paint/oil/grease to block air and water;
- galvanise with zinc;
- chrome-plate;
- alloy iron to make stainless steel.

## 11. What type of oxides form when non-metals combine with oxygen?

### Answer

Non-metals generally form **acidic or neutral oxides**. SO₂ and CO₂ are acidic;
CO and NO are neutral.

## 12. Give reasons.

### (a) Platinum, gold and silver are used for jewellery.

They are lustrous, malleable and ductile, and are so unreactive that they resist
corrosion.

### (b) Sodium, potassium and lithium are stored under oil.

These alkali metals react vigorously with oxygen and moisture and may catch
fire. Oil isolates them from air and water.

### (c) Aluminium is highly reactive, yet used for cooking utensils.

Aluminium conducts heat and quickly forms a thin, adherent Al₂O₃ layer that
prevents further reaction and corrosion.

### (d) Carbonate and sulphide ores are usually converted to oxides during extraction.

Metal oxides are easier to reduce to metals than sulphides or carbonates.
Sulphides are roasted and carbonates are calcined.

## 13. Why do lemon or tamarind juice clean tarnished copper vessels?

### Answer

Tarnished copper carries a green layer of basic copper carbonate. Acids in
lemon or tamarind react with and dissolve this basic layer, exposing clean
copper.

## 14. Differentiate metals and non-metals using chemical properties.

### Answer

| Chemical behaviour | Metals | Non-metals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| With oxygen | Usually form basic oxides; some amphoteric | Usually form acidic or neutral oxides |
| Electron behaviour | Lose electrons; form cations | Gain electrons from metals; form anions |
| With dilute acids | Metals above H release H₂ | Do not displace H₂ |
| With water | Reactive metals form oxide/hydroxide + H₂ | Generally do not react this way |
| Salt solutions | More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal | Do not show metal-displacement behaviour |
| With each other | Transfer electrons to non-metals | Accept electrons from metals, forming ionic compounds |

Use “generally” because exceptions exist.

## 15. A fake goldsmith dips gold bangles in a solution. They shine but lose weight. Identify the solution.

### Answer

He used **aqua regia**, a freshly prepared 3:1 mixture of concentrated HCl and
concentrated HNO₃. It dissolves some gold from the surface, restoring shine but
reducing the bangles’ mass.

## 16. Why is copper used for hot-water tanks and not steel?

### Answer

Copper is less reactive and does not react with water or steam, so it resists
corrosion in a hot-water tank. Iron in steel can react with steam and can rust
in the presence of air and water.
