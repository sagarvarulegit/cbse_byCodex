# The Ball Poem

## chapter goal

Understand how the loss of a ball teaches a child about loss, responsibility, and growing up.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "Two Stories about Flying" in `jeff103.pdf`.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. The lost ball.
2. Why replacement is not enough.
3. Growing up.
4. Tone and symbol.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) the lost ball

A boy loses his ball and feels deep grief.

To an adult, the ball may seem small. To the child, it holds memory and attachment.

## 2) no easy replacement

Buying another ball cannot replace the first loss.

The poem shows that some losses must be understood, not simply replaced.

## 3) growing up

The child begins learning that life includes loss.

This knowledge is painful but important for maturity.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The ball is a symbol of childhood attachment.
- Loss teaches responsibility and maturity.
- Replacement cannot remove emotional loss.
- The tone is serious and reflective.

## common mistakes students make

1. Saying the poem is only about a toy.
2. Ignoring the emotional value of the ball.
3. Treating replacement as the solution.

## board tips

- Mention symbol when explaining the ball.
- Connect loss with growing up.
- Keep the answer sensitive and thoughtful.

## short answer practice

1. Why is the ball important to the boy?
2. Why is buying another ball not enough?
3. What lesson does the boy begin to learn?
