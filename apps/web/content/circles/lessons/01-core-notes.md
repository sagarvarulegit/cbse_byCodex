# 10.1-10.4 Circles

A wheel touches the road at one point. At that exact point, the radius and road make a right angle. This chapter turns that observation into two powerful theorems.

## 10.1 Introduction

Figure 10.1 compares:

- a non-intersecting line
- a secant meeting the circle at two points
- a tangent meeting the circle at one point

## 10.2 Tangent to a Circle

A tangent is a line that intersects a circle at exactly one point. That point is the point of contact.

### Activity 1

Rotate a line through a point on the circle. As the two secant intersections come together, the line reaches the tangent position. Figure 10.3(i) records the sequence.

### Activity 2

Draw parallel lines that meet a circle in chords. The chord length decreases as the line moves away from the centre. At the limiting position, the two chord endpoints coincide and the line is tangent. Figure 10.3(ii) records this.

Figure 10.2 gives the well-pulley example; Figure 10.4 gives the wheel-road example.

### Theorem 10.1

The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

In Figure 10.5, if $XY$ is tangent at $P$ and $OP$ is the radius, then

$$OP\perp XY.$$

The proof compares $OP$ with the distance from $O$ to every other point of the tangent line.

## 10.3 Number of Tangents from a Point on a Circle

### Activity 3

Figure 10.6 shows:

- point inside the circle: no tangent
- point on the circle: one tangent
- point outside the circle: two tangents

### Theorem 10.2

The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

If $PQ$ and $PR$ are tangents from $P$, then $PQ=PR$. The proof uses congruent right triangles $\triangle OPQ$ and $\triangle OPR$.

## 10.4 Summary

- Tangent meets the circle once.
- Radius is perpendicular to tangent at contact.
- From an external point, the two tangent lengths are equal.

## Common mistakes

- drawing the radius to a point other than the point of contact
- saying every line perpendicular to any radius is tangent
- using equal-tangent theorem when the segments do not start at the same external point
- confusing tangent length with distance from external point to centre

## Board tips

- Mark every right angle produced by a radius and tangent.
- State equal radii and the shared hypotenuse when proving tangent triangles congruent.
- Write tangent pairs from each external vertex before simplifying a circumscribed figure.

## Quick practice

1. How many tangents can be drawn from a point inside a circle?
2. If $PA$ and $PB$ are tangents from $P$ and $PA=7$ cm, find $PB$.
3. If $OP=13$ cm and radius $=5$ cm, find tangent length $PT$.

## Answer check

1. None.
2. $7$ cm.
3. $PT=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=12$ cm.

## One-minute revision

Contact gives a right angle. One external point gives equal tangent lengths.

## Comic recap
