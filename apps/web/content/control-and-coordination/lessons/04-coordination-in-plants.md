# 6.2 Coordination in Plants

Plants do not have nerves or muscles, but they still respond to their surroundings in controlled ways. Some responses are quick and do not involve growth. Others are slow and depend on growth.

## 6.2 Coordination in Plants

Plants use chemical and electrical-chemical signals from cell to cell. Since there is no specialised nervous tissue, the message is not carried by neurons the way it is in animals.

## 6.2.1 Immediate Response to Stimulus

The sensitive plant, Mimosa or chhui-mui, folds its leaves very quickly when touched. No growth is involved in this movement. The plant must be changing shape by changing the amount of water in cells.

## 6.2.2 Movement Due to Growth

Some plant movements are caused by growth. Tendrils circle around a support because the side in contact with the support grows more slowly than the side away from it.

[[figure:figure-6-tendril-thigmotropism]]

Directional growth responses are called tropic movements.

- phototropism: movement in response to light
- geotropism: movement in response to gravity
- hydrotropism: movement in response to water
- chemotropism: movement in response to chemicals

The shoot bends towards light, while the root bends away from light. Roots grow downwards and shoots grow upwards because of geotropism.

Plant hormones help control this growth:
- auxin helps cells on the shady side of a shoot grow longer
- gibberellin helps stem growth
- cytokinin helps cell division
- abscisic acid inhibits growth and can cause wilting

The pollen tube growing towards an ovule is a good example of chemotropism.

### Activity 6.2 and Figure 6.5

1. Fill a conical flask with water and cover its neck with wire mesh.
2. Keep two or three freshly germinated bean seeds on the mesh.
3. Put the flask inside a cardboard box that is open on one side. Face the open side towards light from a window.
4. After two or three days, observe that the shoots bend towards light and the roots grow away from light.
5. Turn the flask so the shoots are away from light and the roots are towards light. Leave it undisturbed for a few days.
6. Compare the old portions with the new growth. The old portions keep their direction, while the new tips respond to light.

### Figure 6.6: Plant showing geotropism

When a potted plant is placed sideways, gravity changes the direction of its new growth. The shoot bends upward, away from gravity, so it is **negatively geotropic**. The roots bend downward, towards gravity, so they are **positively geotropic**.

## Questions and answers

Question:
What are plant hormones?

Answer:
Plant hormones are chemical substances made in one part of the plant that help control growth, development, and responses to the environment.

Question:
How is the movement of leaves of the sensitive plant different from the movement of a shoot towards light?

Answer:
The sensitive plant moves quickly without growth, while the shoot bends slowly because growth happens more on one side than the other.

Question:
Give an example of a plant hormone that promotes growth.

Answer:
Auxin is an example of a plant hormone that promotes growth.

Question:
How do auxins promote the growth of a tendril around a support?

Answer:
The side of the tendril touching the support grows more slowly than the other side, so the tendril curves and wraps around the support.

Question:
Design an experiment to demonstrate hydrotropism.

Answer:
Place a sieve carrying moist sawdust and freshly germinated seeds at a slant.
Keep the sawdust uniformly moist at first; the roots grow down through the holes
because of gravity. Then arrange the moist sawdust above the root tips and keep
the lower side comparatively dry. After some time the root tips bend back
towards the moist sawdust, even against their earlier downward direction. Keep
a similar control uniformly moist. Its roots continue mainly with the gravity
response. Bending specifically towards the water source demonstrates positive
hydrotropism.

Use similar seedlings and keep light, temperature, and duration the same; change
only the position of available moisture.

## Solved examples

### Example 1

Question:
Why does a shoot bend towards light?

Answer:
Auxin collects on the shady side of the shoot and makes those cells grow longer, so the shoot bends towards the light.

### Example 2

Question:
Why do roots grow downward?

Answer:
Roots show positive geotropism, so they grow in the direction of gravity.

### Example 3

Question:
Why is the sensitive plant movement called an immediate response?

Answer:
Because it happens quickly without growth, mainly by changes in water content in cells.

## Common mistakes

1. Treating all plant movement as growth.
2. Saying plant responses use nerves like animals.
3. Forgetting the term tropic movement.
4. Mixing up phototropism and geotropism.
5. Leaving out abscisic acid when plant hormones are listed.

## Board tips

- When answering plant movement questions, state the stimulus first.
- For tendrils, write that the side near the support grows more slowly.
- In a hydrotropism experiment, include an unequal moisture source, a comparison
  or control, the observation that roots bend towards water, and the conclusion.
- Mention auxin for phototropism and gibberellin for stem growth.

## One-minute revision

Plants coordinate through chemical signals and growth responses. Mimosa gives an immediate non-growth response. Shoots, roots, and tendrils show tropic movement. Auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin, and abscisic acid help control plant growth and response.
