# Chapter 1: Resources and Development

## Resources and their classification

Anything in the environment becomes a resource only when it can satisfy human needs and is:

- technologically accessible
- economically feasible
- culturally acceptable

Resources are not simply free gifts of nature. Nature supplies materials, but people use technology and institutions to turn those materials into resources.

| Basis | Types | Easy meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | Biotic and abiotic | From living things or non-living things |
| Exhaustibility | Renewable and non-renewable | Can be renewed in a useful time or takes a very long time to form |
| Ownership | Individual, community, national and international | Who owns or controls it |
| Status of development | Potential, developed, stock and reserves | How far it has been surveyed and can be used |

[TIP: In a classification answer, name the basis first. Then name and explain both types.]

## Development of Resources

Careless resource use creates three linked problems:

- Resources are depleted to satisfy the greed of a few.
- Resources collect in a few hands, widening the gap between rich and poor.
- Indiscriminate exploitation causes global warming, ozone-layer depletion, pollution and land degradation.

Sustainable development means development without damaging the environment or reducing the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

The 1992 Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit addressed environmental protection and socio-economic development. Agenda 21 called for global cooperation and asked every local government to prepare its own local Agenda 21.

## Resource Planning

India needs planning because resources are unevenly distributed. Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh are rich in minerals and coal. Arunachal Pradesh has abundant water but limited infrastructure. Rajasthan has strong solar and wind potential but limited water. Ladakh has rich culture but lacks water, infrastructure and some vital minerals.

Balanced planning is therefore needed at national, state, regional and local levels.

## Resource Planning in India

Resource planning has three textbook stages:

1. Identify and inventory resources through surveying, mapping and qualitative and quantitative estimation.
2. Build a planning structure with suitable technology, skills and institutions.
3. Match resource-development plans with overall national-development plans.

Resources alone do not guarantee development. Technology, the quality of human resources, institutions and historical experience also matter.

## Conservation of Resources

Irrational consumption and over-use create social, economic and environmental problems. Gandhiji's message was that the earth has enough for everyone's need, but not for anyone's greed.

Key milestones named in the textbook are the Club of Rome in 1968, Schumacher's *Small is Beautiful* in 1974, the Brundtland Commission's *Our Common Future* in 1987, and the Rio Earth Summit in 1992.

## Land Resources

Land supports vegetation, wildlife, settlements, farming, industry, transport and communication. It is limited, so every use needs careful planning.

India's relief share is about:

- 43 per cent plains, useful for agriculture and industry
- 30 per cent mountains, important for rivers, tourism and ecology
- 27 per cent plateaus, rich in minerals, fossil fuels and forests

## Land Utilisation

The textbook groups land use into forests; land not available for cultivation; other uncultivated land; fallow land; and net sown area.

Net sown area is the physical area on which crops are sown and harvested. Gross cropped area is net sown area plus the area sown more than once in the same agricultural year.

## Land Use Pattern in India

Physical factors such as relief, climate and soil, and human factors such as population, technology, culture and traditions shape land use.

India's total geographical area is 3.28 million square kilometres, but the textbook notes that land-use data covers about 93 per cent. Net sown area is over 80 per cent in Punjab and Haryana but below 10 per cent in Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Manipur and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

## Land Degradation and Conservation Measures

Land degradation means a fall in the quality and productive capacity of land.

| Main cause | Textbook region/example | Matching control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deforestation due to mining | Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha | Control mining and restore damaged land |
| Overgrazing | Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra | Manage grazing and grow protective vegetation |
| Over-irrigation | Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh | Improve drainage and avoid waterlogging, salinity and alkalinity |
| Mineral-processing dust | Cement and ceramic areas | Control dust and manage waste |
| Industrial effluents | Industrial and suburban areas | Treat waste before discharge |

Afforestation, shelter belts, thorny bushes for sand-dune stabilisation and careful wasteland management also help.

## Soil as a Resource

Soil is a living system and the main medium of plant growth. A few centimetres may take millions of years to form. Relief, parent rock, climate, vegetation, living organisms and time all take part in soil formation.

## Classification of Soils

| Soil | Main area and formation | Key properties and crops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alluvial | Northern plains, river valleys and eastern deltas | Very fertile; contains potash, phosphoric acid and lime; supports sugarcane, paddy, wheat, cereals and pulses |
| Black | Deccan Trap and the Godavari-Krishna valleys | Fine and clayey, holds moisture, develops cracks; ideal for cotton |
| Red and Yellow | Low-rainfall parts of the eastern and southern Deccan | Red due to iron diffusion; yellow in hydrated form |
| Laterite | High-rainfall tropical and subtropical areas | Strong leaching, acidic and nutrient-poor; with conservation supports tea, coffee and cashew |
| Arid | Western dry regions | Sandy, saline, low in humus and moisture; can become cultivable with irrigation |
| Forest | Hilly and mountainous areas | Texture changes with altitude; valley terraces can be fertile |

Old alluvium is called Bangar and contains more *kankar*. New alluvium is called Khadar and is finer and more fertile.

## Soil Erosion and Soil Conservation

Soil erosion is the removal and washing away of the soil cover. Running water may cut deep gullies and create bad land; in the Chambal basin these are called ravines. Sheet erosion removes topsoil over a broad slope. Wind erosion blows loose soil from flat or sloping land.

Control methods include contour ploughing, terrace cultivation, strip cropping and shelter belts. Each method slows water or wind and protects topsoil.

## NCERT exercise questions and answers

### 1. Multiple choice questions

**(i) Which one of the following is the main cause of land degradation in Punjab?**

*Answer:* (c) Over irrigation.

**(ii) In which one of the following states is terrace cultivation practised?**

*Answer:* (d) Uttarakhand.

**(iii) In which of the following states black soil is predominantly found?**

*Answer:* (b) Maharashtra.

### 2. Answer the following questions in about 30 words

**(i) Name three states having black soil and the crop which is mainly grown in it.**

*Answer:* Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat have extensive black soil. Cotton is the main crop associated with this moisture-holding soil.

**(ii) What type of soil is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast? Give three main features of this type of soil.**

*Answer:* Alluvial soil occurs in the eastern deltas. It is made of sand, silt and clay, is very fertile, and contains useful amounts of potash, phosphoric acid and lime.

**(iii) What steps can be taken to control soil erosion in the hilly areas?**

*Answer:* Plough along contour lines, cut slopes into terraces and keep protective vegetation. These steps slow runoff and stop topsoil from being washed away.

### 3. Answer the following questions in about 120 words

**(i) Explain land use pattern in India and why has the land under forest not increased much since 1960-61?**

*Answer:* India's land is used for forests, cultivation, settlements, transport, industry, grazing and fallow land. Physical factors such as relief, climate and soil and human factors such as population, technology and culture shape this pattern. Forest area has not risen much because land faces competing demands from farming, settlements, roads, industry and other non-agricultural uses. Degraded and fragmented forest land also takes time to restore. The textbook comparison shows only a small change, so conservation and planned land use remain necessary.

**(ii) How have technical and economic development led to more consumption of resources?**

*Answer:* Better technology makes it possible to extract, process and transport resources on a much larger scale. Economic growth raises production, incomes and demand for goods, energy, transport and construction. Industries then use more minerals, water, forests and fuels. If this use is uncontrolled, resources are depleted and pollution grows. Technology and development must therefore be joined with planning, recycling and conservation.

## Common mistakes

- Calling every natural object a resource without the three conditions of access, feasibility and acceptance.
- Mixing up stock with reserves or renewable with unlimited.
- Writing only a cause of degradation without its state example and matching control.
- Confusing Bangar with Khadar.
- Treating sheet erosion and gully erosion as the same process.

## Board tips

- Use basis, type and example for resource-classification answers.
- For a soil answer, connect formation or area, property and crop.
- In land-degradation questions, present cause, affected region and remedy together.

## One-minute revision

- Resource = useful + accessible + feasible + culturally acceptable.
- Sustainable development protects present and future needs.
- India needs planning because resources are unevenly distributed.
- Land degradation controls must match the cause.
- Bangar is old alluvium; Khadar is new and more fertile.
- Contours, terraces, strips and shelter belts protect soil.
