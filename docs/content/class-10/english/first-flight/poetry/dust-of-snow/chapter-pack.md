# Dust of Snow

## chapter goal

Understand how a tiny natural incident changes the speaker's mood.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "A Letter to God" in `jeff101.pdf`. It avoids copying poem lines and focuses on theme, symbols, and answer points.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Situation.
2. Mood change.
3. Symbols.
4. Literary devices.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) situation

The speaker is in a low mood. A crow shakes snow from a hemlock tree.

That small fall of snow changes the speaker's mood and saves part of the day.

## 2) theme

The poem shows that small moments in nature can change our feelings.

Even things usually seen as dark or unlucky can bring a positive turn.

## 3) symbols

The crow and hemlock tree are unusual choices for a happy change.

This contrast makes the mood shift surprising and memorable.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The poem is short but has a clear mood change.
- Nature creates the turning point.
- Negative-looking symbols produce a positive effect.
- The tone moves from sadness to relief.

## common mistakes students make

1. Calling the crow purely unlucky.
2. Missing the contrast between symbols and effect.
3. Writing a long story instead of a short poetic answer.

## board tips

- Mention mood before and after the incident.
- Use the word contrast when explaining symbols.
- Keep answers simple and precise.

## short answer practice

1. What changes the speaker's mood?
2. Why are the crow and hemlock tree surprising symbols?
3. What does the poem suggest about small moments?

## mini quiz with answers

1. The speaker's mood changes after snow falls.
2. Nature causes the change.
3. The crow and hemlock create contrast.
4. The poem values small positive moments.
5. The tone ends with relief.
