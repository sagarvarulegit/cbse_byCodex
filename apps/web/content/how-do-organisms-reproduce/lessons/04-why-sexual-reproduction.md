# 7.3 Sexual Reproduction

Sexual reproduction combines DNA from two individuals. This produces new
combinations of variations more rapidly than relying only on occasional copying
changes in one lineage.

## Main advantage

Two parents have accumulated different variations. Combining their DNA creates
a unique combination in the offspring. Greater variation raises the chance that
some members of a population will survive a changing environment.

## The chromosome-number problem

If two ordinary body cells combined, chromosome number and DNA amount would
double in every generation. Organisms solve this using specialised germ cells:

1. **Meiosis** forms gametes with half the chromosome number and half the DNA.
2. Male and female gametes fuse during fertilisation.
3. The zygote regains the species' normal chromosome number.

`diploid parent cell --meiosis--> haploid gametes`

`male gamete + female gamete --fertilisation--> diploid zygote`

In humans: `23 + 23 = 46 chromosomes`.

## Specialised gametes

- The male gamete is usually smaller and motile.
- The female gamete is usually larger and contains food reserves.

These differences help fertilisation and early development.

## Asexual versus sexual reproduction

| Asexual | Sexual |
| --- | --- |
| one parent | usually two parents |
| no gamete fusion | male and female gametes fuse |
| offspring very similar to parent | offspring receive DNA from both parents |
| variation arises mainly from copying changes | greater variation through recombination |
| usually rapid | usually more complex and energy-demanding |

## Gamete versus zygote

| Gamete | Zygote |
| --- | --- |
| reproductive cell | first cell of the new individual |
| has half the chromosome number | has the restored full chromosome number |
| sperm or egg | formed by sperm-egg fusion |
| carries DNA from one parent | combines DNA from both parents |

## Board questions

### Why do sexually reproducing parents and offspring have the same chromosome number?

Meiosis halves the chromosome number during gamete formation. Fertilisation
combines two half sets, restoring the original species-specific number in the
zygote.

### Why is sexual reproduction advantageous?

It combines variations from two parents to create novel combinations. The
resulting diversity improves the species' chance of surviving environmental
change and supplies material for evolution.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying fertilisation halves chromosome number.
2. Calling the zygote haploid.
3. Claiming every sexual offspring is better adapted.
4. Confusing variation with a guaranteed advantage to one individual.

## Memory trick

**Meiosis halves; fertilisation restores; variation combines.**
