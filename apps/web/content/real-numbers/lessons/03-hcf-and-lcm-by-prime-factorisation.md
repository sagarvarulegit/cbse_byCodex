# HCF and LCM by prime factorisation

This lesson uses `prime factorisation` to find `HCF` and `LCM`. Students usually mix them up, so we will keep the method clean, slow, and friendly.

## first remember the words

### meaning of `HCF`
`HCF` means Highest Common Factor.
It is the biggest `factor` that divides the given numbers exactly.

### meaning of `LCM`
`LCM` means Lowest Common Multiple.
It is the smallest `multiple` that is exactly divisible by the given numbers.

## one idea that changes everything

When you use `prime factorisation`:
- `HCF` takes the common `prime factors` with the smallest powers
- `LCM` takes all `prime factors` with the greatest powers

If a student remembers only this one line, half the work is done.

## solved example 1
Find the `HCF` and `LCM` of `26` and `91`.

Prime factor forms:
- `26 = 2 × 13`
- `91 = 7 × 13`

### `HCF`
Common `prime factor` is only `13`.

So,
`HCF = 13`

### `LCM`
Take all `prime factors` with greatest powers:
- `2`
- `7`
- `13`

So,
`LCM = 2 × 7 × 13 = 182`

## solved example 2
Find the `HCF` and `LCM` of `135` and `225`.

Prime factor forms:
- `135 = 3³ × 5`
- `225 = 3² × 5²`

### `HCF`
Take common `primes` with smaller powers:
- for `3`, smaller power is `3²`
- for `5`, smaller power is `5¹`

So,
`HCF = 3² × 5 = 45`

### `LCM`
Take all `primes` with bigger powers:
- for `3`, bigger power is `3³`
- for `5`, bigger power is `5²`

So,
`LCM = 3³ × 5² = 675`

## solved example 3
Find the `HCF` and `LCM` of `12` and `15`.

Prime factor forms:
- `12 = 2² × 3`
- `15 = 3 × 5`

### `HCF`
Common `prime factor` is only `3`.
So,
`HCF = 3`

### `LCM`
Take all `prime factors`:
`LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60`

## check rule for two numbers

For two numbers only:

`HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers`

Let us check for `135` and `225`:

`45 × 675 = 30375`
`135 × 225 = 30375`

So the answer is correct.

## very important warning

This check rule is safe for two numbers.
Do not blindly use it for three numbers.

## easy comparison in words

Think like this:
- `HCF` asks: what part is common?
- `LCM` asks: what full set is needed?

## how to avoid confusion

### for `HCF`
Ask:
What do both numbers share?
Then take the smaller power.

### for `LCM`
Ask:
What complete set of `prime factors` will cover both numbers?
Then take the bigger power.

## common mistakes

1. Taking bigger powers in `HCF`
2. Taking only common factors in `LCM`
3. Forgetting a `prime factor` that appears in only one number
4. Writing `HCF` and `LCM` answers without `factorising` first
5. Using the product rule for more than two numbers without care

## memory trick

`HCF` hugs the overlap.
`LCM` loads everything.

## board tip

Always write the `prime factor` forms first. If you skip that step, silly mistakes happen very fast.

## one more solved question

Find the `HCF` and `LCM` of `96` and `404`.

Prime factor forms:
- `96 = 2⁵ × 3`
- `404 = 2² × 101`

### `HCF`
Common `prime factor` is `2`.
Smaller power is `2²`.

So,
`HCF = 4`

### `LCM`
Take all `primes` with greater powers:
`LCM = 2⁵ × 3 × 101 = 9696`

## quick practice

1. Find the `HCF` and `LCM` of `18` and `24`.
2. Find the `HCF` and `LCM` of `20` and `30`.
3. Find the `HCF` and `LCM` of `14` and `35`.
4. Check whether your `HCF` and `LCM` satisfy the product rule.

## answer check

### 1) `18` and `24`
- `18 = 2 × 3²`
- `24 = 2³ × 3`
- `HCF = 2 × 3 = 6`
- `LCM = 2³ × 3² = 72`

### 2) `20` and `30`
- `20 = 2² × 5`
- `30 = 2 × 3 × 5`
- `HCF = 2 × 5 = 10`
- `LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60`

### 3) `14` and `35`
- `14 = 2 × 7`
- `35 = 5 × 7`
- `HCF = 7`
- `LCM = 2 × 5 × 7 = 70`

## one-minute revision

- `HCF` means Highest Common Factor
- `LCM` means Lowest Common Multiple
- `HCF` uses common factors with smaller powers
- `LCM` uses all `prime factors` with bigger powers
- for two numbers, `HCF × LCM = product`

## comic recap

- `HCF`: “I only take what both numbers share.”
- `LCM`: “I carry the full bag. Nothing left behind.”
- `Smaller power`: “Call me for `HCF`.”
- `Greater power`: “Call me for `LCM`.”

## remember this line

`HCF` picks the common part. `LCM` builds the full part.
