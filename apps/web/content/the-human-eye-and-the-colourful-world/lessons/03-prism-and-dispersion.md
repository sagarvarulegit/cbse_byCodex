# 10.3 Refraction through a Prism

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-10-4-prism-refraction" />

A prism has two triangular bases and three rectangular lateral faces. The angle
between its two refracting faces is the angle of the prism.

In Figure 10.4:

- `PE`: incident ray, angle `i`;
- `EF`: refracted ray, angle `r`;
- `FS`: emergent ray, angle `e`;
- `A`: prism angle;
- `D`: angle of deviation between original direction and emergent ray.

Unlike a rectangular slab, a prism's refracting faces are inclined, so the
emergent ray is deviated rather than parallel to the incident ray.

Activity 10.1 traces the prism path using four vertical pins, normals at both
faces, and measurements of `i`, `r`, `e`, `A`, and `D`.

# 10.4 Dispersion of White Light

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-10-5-10-6-dispersion" />

Dispersion is splitting white light into its component colours. The colour band
is the spectrum:

`Violet Indigo Blue Green Yellow Orange Red` — VIBGYOR.

Different colours refract by different amounts:

- red deviates least;
- violet deviates most.

Newton passed the spectrum through a second identical inverted prism. The
colours recombined into white light, showing sunlight contains all spectrum
colours.

## Rainbow

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-10-7-10-8-rainbow" />

A rainbow is a natural spectrum produced by water droplets after rain.
Sunlight undergoes refraction and dispersion on entering a droplet, internal
reflection, and refraction on leaving. A rainbow is seen opposite the Sun.

## Common errors

- A prism does not create colours; it separates colours already in white light.
- VIBGYOR is the sequence; deviation is greatest for violet and least for red.
- Dispersion and scattering are different phenomena.
