# The Trees

## chapter goal

Understand the poem's image of trees moving out from confined indoor spaces into the forest.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "Glimpses of India" in `jeff105.pdf`.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Confined trees.
2. Movement outward.
3. Nature and freedom.
4. Imagery and personification.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) confined nature

The poem imagines trees inside a house, away from the forest where they belong.

This creates a feeling of confinement.

## 2) movement

The trees move out toward the forest.

Their movement suggests return, freedom, and the power of nature.

## 3) imagery

The poem uses strong visual and sound images.

Personification makes the trees seem alive and determined.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The trees represent nature seeking freedom.
- The house suggests confinement.
- The forest suggests natural belonging.
- Imagery and personification are important.

## common mistakes students make

1. Explaining the poem only as literal gardening.
2. Missing the idea of freedom.
3. Forgetting personification.

## board tips

- Use nature, freedom, confinement, and personification.
- Explain movement as meaningful.
- Keep answers linked to the poem's images.

## short answer practice

1. What does the house suggest?
2. Why do the trees move out?
3. How does personification help the poem?

## mini quiz with answers

2. The forest is their natural place.

5. Imagery is central to the poem.
