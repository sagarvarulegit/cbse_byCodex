# What You Have Learnt

- Carbon is a versatile element and forms the basis of living organisms and
  many useful materials.
- Its large variety of compounds comes from tetravalency and catenation.
- A covalent bond forms when atoms share electrons and complete their
  outermost shells.
- Carbon forms covalent bonds with carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, sulphur, nitrogen
  and chlorine.
- Carbon compounds may contain single, double or triple bonds and may form
  straight chains, branched chains or rings.
- A homologous series has the same functional group on carbon chains of
  different lengths; successive members differ by `CH2` and 14 u.
- Alcohol, aldehyde, ketone and carboxylic-acid functional groups give
  characteristic properties.
- Carbon compounds are major fuels; ethanol and ethanoic acid are important in
  daily life.
- Soap and detergent molecules contain a hydrophilic part and a hydrophobic
  part. This lets them emulsify oily dirt and remove it.

## Questions after 4.1

### 1. What would be the electron-dot structure of carbon dioxide, `CO2`?

**Answer:** Carbon shares two pairs of electrons with each oxygen atom:

`O = C = O`

Each `C=O` is a double covalent bond. Carbon and both oxygen atoms complete
their octets; each oxygen also retains two lone pairs.

### 2. What would be the electron-dot structure of an `S8` molecule?

**Answer:** Eight sulphur atoms form a closed ring. Each sulphur atom shares one
pair with each of its two neighbours, so every sulphur has two single
covalent bonds and two lone pairs.

## Questions after 4.2

### 1. How many structural isomers can you draw for pentane?

**Answer:** Three:

1. n-pentane: `CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH3`
2. 2-methylbutane: `CH3-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH3`
3. 2,2-dimethylpropane: `C(CH3)4`

All have molecular formula `C5H12` but different carbon skeletons.

### 2. Which two properties of carbon produce its huge number of compounds?

**Answer:** **Catenation** and **tetravalency**. Carbon forms strong bonds with
itself to make chains, branches and rings, and its valency four lets it bond
with carbon and many other elements.

### 3. Give the formula and electron-dot structure of cyclopentane.

**Answer:** Formula `C5H10`. Five carbon atoms form a ring; each carbon forms
two `C-C` single bonds and two `C-H` single bonds. Every carbon therefore has
four shared pairs and a complete octet.

### 4. Draw the structures of ethanoic acid, bromopentane, butanone and hexanal.

**Answer:**

- Ethanoic acid: `CH3-COOH`
- One valid bromopentane: `CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2-Br`
- Butanone: `CH3-CO-CH2-CH3`
- Hexanal: `CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2-CHO`

Structural isomers are possible for bromopentane because bromine can occupy
different positions and the five-carbon skeleton itself can be branched.

### 5. Name the source structures.

**Answer:**

1. `CH3-CH2-Br`: **bromoethane**
2. `H-CHO`: **methanal**
3. Six-carbon terminal-alkyne structure: **hexyne** (more precisely,
   hex-1-yne)

## Questions after 4.3

### 1. Why is ethanol-to-ethanoic-acid conversion an oxidation reaction?

**Answer:** Ethanol gains oxygen (or loses hydrogen) to form ethanoic acid:

`CH3CH2OH + 2[O] → CH3COOH + H2O`

Alkaline potassium permanganate or acidified potassium dichromate supplies the
oxygen and is the oxidising agent.

### 2. Why is oxygen mixed with ethyne for welding instead of air?

**Answer:** Ethyne burns completely in pure oxygen and gives a very hot,
clean flame suitable for welding. Air contains only about 21% oxygen and much
nitrogen, so ethyne burns less completely, gives a sooty flame and does not
reach the required temperature.

## Questions after 4.4

### 1. How can an alcohol and a carboxylic acid be distinguished experimentally?

**Answer:** Add sodium hydrogen carbonate or sodium carbonate. A carboxylic
acid gives brisk carbon-dioxide effervescence; an alcohol does not. The gas
turns lime water milky. A carboxylic acid also turns blue litmus red, whereas
ethanol is neutral to litmus.

### 2. What are oxidising agents?

**Answer:** Substances that add oxygen to, or remove hydrogen from, another
substance are oxidising agents. Alkaline `KMnO4` and acidified `K2Cr2O7` are
examples used to oxidise ethanol.

## Questions after 4.5

### 1. Can detergent be used to check whether water is hard?

**Answer:** No. Detergents lather in both soft and hard water because their
charged ends do not form insoluble precipitates with calcium and magnesium
ions. Soap is the useful comparison because it forms scum in hard water.

### 2. Why is agitation necessary while washing clothes?

**Answer:** Soap surrounds oily dirt in micelles. Beating, scrubbing or machine
agitation helps detach the grease-filled micelles from cloth fibres and keeps
them suspended so rinse water can carry them away.

# NCERT Exercises

## 1. Ethane, `C2H6`, has how many covalent bonds?

(a) 6  (b) 7  (c) 8  (d) 9

### Answer

**(b) 7.** Ethane has six `C-H` bonds and one `C-C` bond.

## 2. Butanone is a four-carbon compound with which functional group?

(a) carboxylic acid  (b) aldehyde  (c) ketone  (d) alcohol

### Answer

**(c) ketone.** Butanone contains the `>C=O` group within its carbon chain:
`CH3-CO-CH2-CH3`.

## 3. A cooking vessel blackens outside when

(a) food is not cooked completely  
(b) fuel is not burning completely  
(c) fuel is wet  
(d) fuel is burning completely

### Answer

**(b).** Insufficient oxygen causes incomplete combustion and produces soot,
which deposits on the vessel.

## 4. Explain the covalent bond in `CH3Cl`.

### Answer

Carbon has four valence electrons. It shares one electron with each of three
hydrogen atoms and one electron with chlorine, forming three `C-H` bonds and
one `C-Cl` bond. Carbon completes its octet, each hydrogen completes its duet,
and chlorine completes its octet while retaining three lone pairs. No electron
is completely transferred.

## 5. Draw electron-dot structures for ethanoic acid, `H2S`, propanone and `F2`.

### Answer

- Ethanoic acid: `H3C-C(=O)-O-H`; the carbonyl oxygen and hydroxyl oxygen each
  retain two lone pairs.
- `H2S`: `H-S-H`; sulphur has two lone pairs.
- Propanone: `CH3-C(=O)-CH3`; oxygen has two lone pairs.
- `F2`: `F-F`; each fluorine has three lone pairs.

Count shared pairs and lone pairs so every non-hydrogen atom has an octet.

## 6. What is a homologous series? Explain with an example.

### Answer

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with the same functional
group and general formula, similar chemical properties, and consecutive
members differing by `CH2` or 14 u. For example, the alcohols `CH3OH`,
`C2H5OH`, `C3H7OH` and `C4H9OH` all contain `-OH`.

## 7. Differentiate ethanol and ethanoic acid using physical and chemical properties.

### Answer

| Test/property | Ethanol | Ethanoic acid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smell | Characteristic alcoholic smell | Vinegar-like smell |
| Melting point | 156 K | 290 K; pure acid may freeze in cool weather |
| Litmus | Neutral | Turns blue litmus red |
| `NaHCO3`/`Na2CO3` | No `CO2` effervescence | Brisk `CO2`; gas turns lime water milky |

## 8. Why do micelles form in water? Will they form in ethanol?

### Answer

In water, the ionic hydrophilic heads of soap remain in contact with water
while the hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails avoid it and turn inward around grease.
This produces a micelle. In ethanol, the hydrocarbon tail is also soluble, so
the same water-driven clustering does not occur and normal soap micelles are
not formed.

## 9. Why are carbon and its compounds used as fuels?

### Answer

Most carbon compounds release a large amount of heat and light on complete
combustion. Many are easy to store and transport, and controlled burning
provides useful energy.

## 10. Explain scum formation in hard water.

### Answer

Soap reacts with calcium and magnesium ions in hard water to form insoluble
calcium/magnesium salts of fatty acids. These curdy precipitates are scum.
Soap is consumed in forming scum, so less remains to produce lather.

## 11. What happens when soap solution is tested with red and blue litmus?

### Answer

Soap solution is alkaline. Red litmus turns blue; blue litmus remains blue.

## 12. What is hydrogenation? Give its industrial application.

### Answer

Hydrogenation is the addition of hydrogen across a carbon-carbon double or
triple bond in the presence of a catalyst such as nickel:

`Unsaturated oil + H2  --Ni-->  saturated fat`

It is used to convert vegetable oils into solid or semi-solid fats such as
vanaspati ghee.

## 13. Which undergo addition: `C2H6`, `C3H8`, `C3H6`, `C2H2`, `CH4`?

### Answer

`C3H6` and `C2H2`, because they are unsaturated and contain a multiple bond.
The alkanes `C2H6`, `C3H8` and `CH4` do not normally undergo addition.

## 14. Give a test for saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.

### Answer

Add bromine water or alkaline potassium permanganate:

- an unsaturated hydrocarbon decolourises the reagent by addition/oxidation;
- a saturated hydrocarbon does not decolourise it under ordinary conditions.

State the reagent, observation and inference for full marks.

## 15. Explain the cleaning action of soap.

### Answer

1. The hydrophobic tail of each soap molecule enters oily dirt.
2. The hydrophilic ionic head remains in water.
3. Many molecules surround the grease with tails inward and heads outward,
   forming a micelle.
4. Agitation lifts these grease-filled micelles from the cloth.
5. Like-charged heads keep micelles suspended, and rinsing carries them away.

# Group Activity - making soap

With teacher supervision, warm about 20 mL of a vegetable oil with 30 mL of
20% sodium hydroxide while stirring until it thickens. Add 5-10 g common salt,
stir and cool. Soap separates and can be shaped after setting.

**Safety:** Sodium hydroxide is corrosive. This is a supervised laboratory
activity, not a home experiment.

# Full-chapter equation bank

- Complete combustion:
  `CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O + heat and light`
- Ethanol oxidation:
  `CH3CH2OH + 2[O] → CH3COOH + H2O`
- Hydrogenation:
  `CH2=CH2 + H2 --Ni--> CH3-CH3`
- Substitution:
  `CH4 + Cl2 --sunlight--> CH3Cl + HCl`
- Sodium with ethanol:
  `2Na + 2C2H5OH → 2C2H5ONa + H2`
- Dehydration:
  `C2H5OH --conc. H2SO4, 443 K--> C2H4 + H2O`
- Esterification:
  `CH3COOH + C2H5OH ⇌ CH3COOC2H5 + H2O`
- Saponification:
  `CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa + C2H5OH`
- Ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide:
  `CH3COOH + NaOH → CH3COONa + H2O`
- Ethanoic acid with sodium carbonate:
  `2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 → 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2`
- Ethanoic acid with sodium hydrogen carbonate:
  `CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2`
