# Our Environment

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain ecosystem components
- understand food chains and food webs
- describe energy flow and trophic levels
- explain ozone depletion
- distinguish biodegradable and non-biodegradable wastes
- suggest responsible waste management

## 1) ecosystem

An ecosystem includes living organisms and their physical environment interacting as a system.

Components:
- biotic components: plants, animals, microorganisms
- abiotic components: air, water, soil, sunlight, temperature

Organisms can be grouped as:
- producers
- consumers
- decomposers

## 2) producers, consumers, decomposers

Producers make their own food.

Example:
Green plants.

Consumers depend on other organisms for food.

Types:
- herbivores
- carnivores
- omnivores

Decomposers break down dead plants and animals and recycle nutrients.

Examples:
Bacteria and fungi.

## 3) food chain

A food chain shows who eats whom.

Example:
grass -> deer -> tiger

Each step is a trophic level.

Producers form the first trophic level.

## 4) food web

In nature, organisms usually have more than one food source.

Many connected food chains form a food web.

Food webs are more stable than single food chains.

## 5) energy flow

Energy flow in an ecosystem is one-directional.

Plants capture only a small part of sunlight.

Only about 10 percent of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next.

This is why food chains usually have limited steps.

## 6) ozone layer

Ozone `O3` in the upper atmosphere protects life by absorbing harmful ultraviolet radiation.

Ozone depletion is mainly caused by chemicals like CFCs.

Damage to ozone layer can increase:
- skin cancer risk
- eye damage
- harm to plants and animals

## 7) garbage management

Biodegradable wastes can be broken down by microorganisms.

Examples:
vegetable peels, paper, cotton.

Non-biodegradable wastes do not break down easily.

Examples:
plastics, some metals, glass.

Better waste management:
- reduce
- reuse
- recycle
- compost biodegradable waste
- avoid single-use plastics
- segregate waste

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Ecosystem has biotic and abiotic components.
- Producers make food.
- Decomposers recycle nutrients.
- Food chain shows feeding relationship.
- Food web is a network of food chains.
- About 10 percent energy moves to the next trophic level.
- Ozone layer blocks harmful UV radiation.

## common mistakes students make

1. Calling decomposers consumers without explaining their recycling role.
2. Forgetting arrows in food chains show energy flow.
3. Saying all sunlight is trapped by plants.
4. Mixing ozone at ground level with protective ozone layer.
5. Treating biodegradable waste as automatically harmless in any amount.

## board tips

- Draw food chains with arrows in the correct direction.
- Mention trophic levels in energy questions.
- For ozone questions, write CFCs and UV radiation.
- In garbage questions, give practical examples.

## solved practice set

### question 1
Why are decomposers important?

Answer:
They break down dead organisms and recycle nutrients back into the ecosystem.

### question 2
Why are food chains short?

Answer:
Only about 10 percent energy passes to the next trophic level, so energy becomes too low at higher levels.

### question 3
What protects Earth from harmful UV radiation?

Answer:
The ozone layer in the upper atmosphere.
