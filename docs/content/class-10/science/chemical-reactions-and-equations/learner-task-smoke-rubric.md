# Chemical Reactions and Equations Learner-Task Smoke Rubric

## Purpose and limitation

This is a reproducible non-student learning smoke check. It tests whether the
chapter supports transfer beyond copied examples after one complete learning
pass.

It does **not** prove that a real Class 10 student learned independently. Keep
real-student validation deferred until suitable students are available.

## Administration contract

- Time limit: 15 minutes.
- Materials: blank paper and pen only.
- Notes, chapter pack, quiz review, search, calculator, and hints are not
  allowed during the attempt.
- The reviewer may explain the response format but may not explain chemistry.
- Use the equations and order below unchanged so results remain comparable.
- Record the first answer. Do not award marks for corrections made after the
  expected answer is shown.
- The person running the smoke must complete one uninterrupted study pass
  through the platform chapter before attempting this holdout.

## Student-facing task sheet

### Part A - Balance four equations

Use coefficients only. Do not change any formula.

1. `Ca + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂`
2. `KClO₃ → KCl + O₂`
3. `Fe + Cl₂ → FeCl₃`
4. `C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O`

### Part B - Classify eight reactions

Choose the most specific taught reaction type: combination, decomposition,
displacement, or double displacement. Add `precipitation` when an insoluble
solid is formed.

1. `Ca + Cl₂ → CaCl₂`
2. `2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂`
3. `Mg + CuO → MgO + Cu`
4. `CaCl₂ + Na₂CO₃ → CaCO₃ + 2NaCl`
5. `2HgO → 2Hg + O₂`
6. `Ca + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂`
7. `CuSO₄ + 2NaOH → Cu(OH)₂ + Na₂SO₄`
8. `2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl`

### Part C - Explain the ideas

1. In `3Mg + N₂ → Mg₃N₂`, name the reactants and the product.
2. Name two observations that may indicate a chemical reaction has taken place.
3. Why must subscripts not be changed while balancing an equation?
4. What is the difference between displacement and double displacement?
5. In `CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O`, identify the oxidised substance and the reduced
   substance. Explain using oxygen.
6. Give one example of corrosion and two ways to slow rancidity.
7. Open the platform only after Parts A-C are collected. Find one exact NCERT
   in-text question, attempt it before revealing the answer, and show the
   reviewer where the answer reveal is located.

### Part C

1. Magnesium and nitrogen are the reactants. Magnesium nitride is the product.
2. Accept any two source observations: change in state, change in colour,
   evolution of a gas, or change in temperature.
3. A subscript is part of a substance's fixed chemical formula. Changing it
   changes the identity or composition of the substance. Balancing changes only
   the number of particles by placing coefficients before formulae.
4. In displacement, one element replaces another element in a compound. In
   double displacement, two compounds exchange ions or groups of atoms to form
   two new compounds.
5. Hydrogen is oxidised because it gains oxygen to form water. Copper(II) oxide
   is reduced because it loses oxygen to form copper.
6. Accept one correct corrosion example such as rusting of iron or the green
   coating on copper. Accept any two chapter-supported rancidity controls:
   antioxidants, airtight containers, or replacing oxygen-rich air with
   nitrogen.
7. Pass when the reviewer observes all three behaviours: the learner finds an
   in-text question, attempts it before opening the answer, and uses the answer
   reveal without help.

## Scoring

### Part A - 8 marks

Award 2 marks per equation:

- 2: balanced and in the smallest whole-number ratio
- 1: atom totals balance but coefficients are a reducible whole-number multiple
- 0: unbalanced, a formula/subscript was changed, or no answer

### Part B - 8 marks

Award 1 mark per reaction:

- B4 and B7 require `double displacement`; omitting the additional word
  `precipitation` does not remove the classification mark, but record the
  omission as a diagnostic note.
- Do not accept `redox` alone because this task asks for the most specific
  reaction type from the four listed choices.

### Part C - 12 marks

- C1: 2 marks for identifying both reactants and the product; 1 mark for
  identifying only one side correctly.
- C2: 2 marks for two valid observations; 1 mark for one valid observation.
- C3: 2 marks for both identity/composition and coefficients-only reasoning; 1
  mark for a correct but incomplete rule.
- C4: 2 marks for correctly explaining one-element replacement and two-compound
  ion/group exchange; 1 mark for a correct distinction without both mechanisms.
- C5: 2 marks for both substances and correct gain/loss-of-oxygen reasoning; 1
  mark for correct substances without reasoning.
- C6: 1 mark for a correct corrosion example and 1 mark for two correct
  rancidity controls; award no second mark for only one control.
- C7 is an interaction gate and is recorded separately, not added to the
  28-mark content score.

## Fixed pass gate

The smoke passes only when all conditions are met:

- at least 3 of 4 balancing equations receive 2 marks
- at least 6 of 8 classifications are correct
- C1 receives 2 marks
- C2 receives 2 marks
- C3 receives 2 marks
- C4 receives 2 marks
- C5 receives 2 marks
- C6 receives 2 marks
- C7 interaction gate passes
- total content score is at least 24 of 28

This conjunctive gate prevents a strong total in easy recall items from hiding a
critical conceptual failure.

## Failure handling

If the smoke fails:

1. Record the first failed concept and the exact lesson or interaction expected
   to teach it.
2. Repair that narrow gap without adding the holdout equation or answer directly
   to the lesson, quiz, or a workbench.
3. Use a clean attempt sheet and rerun the full fixed rubric.
4. Keep both results. Do not overwrite the failed evidence.

If the holdout leaks into teaching or assessment content, replace the leaked
item here before using the rubric again and record the change in the evidence
index.
