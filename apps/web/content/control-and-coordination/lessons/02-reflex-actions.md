# 6.1.1 What happens in Reflex Actions?

Sometimes the body must react so quickly that it cannot wait for the full thinking process. That is where reflex actions help.

## 6.1.1 What happens in Reflex Actions?

A reflex action is a quick, automatic response to a stimulus. A clear
protective example is pulling a hand away from a flame or hot object.

If the brain had to think through every urgent movement, the response might come too late. The body solves this by using a reflex arc.

### Reflex arc

The reflex arc is the connection between the input nerve and the output nerve at the point where they first meet in the spinal cord. The information still reaches the brain, but the quick action can happen earlier through the spinal cord.

Reflex arc path:

`stimulus -> receptor -> sensory neuron -> relay neuron in spinal cord -> motor neuron -> effector -> response`

## Solved examples

### Example 1

Question:
Why do we pull our hand back from a hot object reflexly?

Answer:
The body needs a fast protective response, so the reflex arc sends the message through the spinal cord and the hand moves back immediately.

### Example 2

Question:
Does the brain know about a reflex action?

Answer:
Yes. The information also reaches the brain, but the quick movement can happen before conscious thinking finishes.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying reflex action is the same as slow thinking.
2. Forgetting the spinal cord in the reflex arc.
3. Thinking the brain is useless in all reflexes.
4. Leaving out the effector, such as a muscle or gland.

## Board tips

- In answers, write the full reflex arc in order.
- Mention that reflexes are automatic and protective.
- If asked why reflexes are fast, say the spinal cord handles the shortcut.
