# 12.4 Domestic Electric Circuits

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-12-15-domestic-circuit" />

NCERT describes Indian mains as `220 V, 50 Hz AC`.

Textbook colour convention:

- live: red;
- neutral: black;
- earth: green.

Follow current certified wiring standards in real installations; for the board,
state the NCERT convention when asked from this chapter.

## Supply path and parallel branches

Mains -> electricity meter -> main fuse -> main switch -> separate household
circuits. Typical NCERT ratings:

- 5 A circuit: bulbs, fans, lower-power loads;
- 15 A circuit: geysers, air coolers, higher-power loads.

Appliances connect in parallel across live and neutral, each with a separate
switch. Each gets 220 V and operates independently.

## Earth wire

Connected to appliance metal body and to a metal plate deep in earth. It offers
a low-resistance leakage path, keeps body near earth potential, and causes a
large fault current that lets protective devices disconnect supply, reducing
shock risk.

## Fuse, overload, short circuit

- Fuse is in live wire and melts by Joule heating when current is excessive.
- Short circuit: live and neutral directly contact due damaged insulation/fault;
  current rises abruptly.
- Overload: current exceeds safe rating due too many/high-power appliances or a
  supply fault.

Avoid overload by not connecting many high-power devices to one socket/circuit
and by using correctly rated fuse/MCB and sound wiring.

## In-text numerical

`P=2kW`, `V=220V`, so `I=P/V=2000/220≈9.1A`.
This exceeds a 5 A circuit rating; fuse/MCB should operate, otherwise wiring may
overheat.

## Safety measures

Correct fuse/MCB and earthing are two standard answers. Never improvise mains
wiring; use a qualified electrician.
