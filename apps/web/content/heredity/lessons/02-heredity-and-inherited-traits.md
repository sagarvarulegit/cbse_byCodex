# 8.2 Heredity

**Heredity** is the transmission of traits from parents to offspring. A
**trait** is a recognisable feature, such as seed shape or plant height.

The rules of heredity explain:

1. why offspring resemble their parents;
2. why they are not identical to either parent; and
3. how some traits appear while others remain hidden.

# 8.2.1 Inherited Traits

An inherited trait is controlled by genetic information received through germ
cells. Each offspring receives contributions from both parents.

## Activity 8.1: free and attached earlobes

Observe the earlobes of classmates and their parents. Record free or attached
earlobes and look for a relationship. The activity introduces inherited
variation, but a small class survey alone cannot prove the complete inheritance
rule.

## Vocabulary that earns marks

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Gene | Unit of hereditary information; a DNA segment controlling a trait | Gene influencing pea height |
| Alleles | Alternative forms of a gene | `T` and `t` |
| Genotype | Allele combination | `TT`, `Tt`, `tt` |
| Phenotype | Visible expression | Tall or short |
| Homozygous | Two identical alleles | `TT`, `tt` |
| Heterozygous | Two unlike alleles | `Tt` |
| Dominant | Expressed in the heterozygous condition | `T` |
| Recessive | Expressed only when both alleles are recessive | `tt` |

## Inherited versus acquired

An acquired change that affects only body cells is not passed through germ-cell
DNA. For example, muscles developed through exercise are not directly inherited.
This chapter focuses on inherited traits; never use an acquired trait as proof
of Mendelian inheritance.

## Board trap: “looks like a parent”

Similarity alone does not show whether a trait is dominant. Dominance is found
from controlled crosses and the pattern in generations, not by counting which
trait appears more often in one family.

## Easy memory trick

`gene combination = genotype`

`visible result = phenotype`

`Tt looks tall, but carries t`
