# Board Exam Practice

These questions cover the distinct Control and Coordination demand families
found in the local 2023-2026 CBSE Science paper archive. Attempt each answer
before reading its marking points.

# Latest verified case pattern: 2026

## 1. Plant movements case study - 4 marks

(a) Why is hydrotropism essential for plants?  
(b) State one role of auxin in plant growth.  
(c) Give two differences between sensitive-plant movement and tropic movement.

**Full-mark answer:**

- Hydrotropism makes roots grow towards available water, allowing absorption of
  water and dissolved minerals.
- Auxin promotes cell elongation and regulates tropic bending, such as a stem
  bending towards light.
- Sensitive-plant movement is non-directional, fast, and growth-independent.
  Tropic movement is directional, slow, and growth-related. Write any two
  paired differences.

**OR for part (c):**

- roots: positive geotropism and negative phototropism
- shoots: negative geotropism and positive phototropism

The official 2026 scheme awards separate marks for hydrotropism, auxin, and two
paired differences.

## 2. Cognitive-change case: brain and muscle action - 4 marks

(a) Define voluntary actions.  
(b) Which brain part gives precision to voluntary actions?  
(c) Explain either the role of the medulla or how animal muscles move.

**Full-mark answer:**

- Voluntary actions are actions performed under conscious control after a
  decision, such as writing or climbing stairs.
- The **cerebellum** provides precision and maintains posture and balance.
- Medulla option: it controls involuntary actions such as blood pressure,
  salivation, vomiting, and breathing.
- Muscle option: a motor impulse reaches the muscle; special proteins change
  shape and arrangement; the muscle cell shortens and produces movement.

## 3. Electrical impulses or tendril - 2 marks

### Option A: State two limitations of electrical impulses.

1. They reach only cells connected through nervous tissue.
2. After transmitting an impulse, a cell needs time to reset and cannot generate
   impulses continuously.

### Option B: How does a pea tendril climb?

Touch produces unequal growth. The part touching the support grows more slowly,
while the part away from it grows faster. The tendril bends and coils around
the support.

## 4. Nephron versus neuron; sensory versus motor

| Pair | Complete distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Nephron | Structural and functional unit of the kidney; filters blood and forms urine |
| Neuron | Structural and functional unit of nervous tissue; receives and transmits impulses |
| Sensory neuron | Carries an impulse from receptor to CNS |
| Motor neuron | Carries an instruction from CNS to muscle or gland |

## 5. Reflex assertion-reason

**Assertion:** Reflex actions do not require conscious thinking.  
**Reason:** Most reflex actions are coordinated through the spinal cord.

**Answer:** Both are true, and the reason correctly explains the assertion. The
short spinal pathway produces an immediate response, although information also
reaches the brain.

# Nervous-system patterns

## 6. Define reflex action and show the path of sneezing - 3 marks

A reflex action is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus without conscious
thought.

`dust stimulus -> receptor in nose -> sensory neuron -> relay neuron in CNS -> motor neuron -> respiratory muscles -> sneeze`

Write arrows and identify the receptor, sensory neuron, CNS connection, motor
neuron, and effector.

## 7. Hot-object reflex arc

`heat -> skin receptor -> sensory neuron -> relay neuron in spinal cord -> motor neuron -> arm muscle -> hand withdrawn`

Reflex arcs evolved because conscious thinking is too slow for urgent danger.
They remain efficient even in animals with complex brains. The brain is
informed, but the spinal response happens first.

## 8. What happens at a synapse? - 2 marks

The electrical impulse reaching an axon ending releases chemicals. These
chemicals cross the synaptic gap and start a new electrical impulse in the
dendrite of the next neuron. Mention both the chemical crossing and the new
electrical impulse.

## 9. How is an impulse created and carried?

A stimulus at the dendritic tip produces a chemical reaction that creates an
electrical impulse. The dendrite carries it towards the cell body; the axon
carries it away from the cell body to its ending.

## 10. Brain parts and functions

| Demand | Scoring answer |
| --- | --- |
| thinking, memory, sensory interpretation, voluntary command | fore-brain or cerebrum |
| precision of voluntary action, posture, equilibrium | cerebellum |
| blood pressure, salivation, vomiting, breathing | medulla |
| hind-brain components | pons, cerebellum, medulla |
| CNS | brain and spinal cord |
| PNS | cranial nerves from brain and spinal nerves from spinal cord |

Never write "brain" alone when the question asks for a part.

## 11. Voluntary, involuntary, and reflex actions

- **Voluntary:** conscious decision; fore-brain; example writing.
- **Involuntary:** no conscious choice; often mid-/hind-brain centres; example
  blood pressure.
- **Reflex:** immediate automatic response through a short reflex arc; example
  hand withdrawal.

Reflex and involuntary are not synonyms.

## 12. Gustatory and olfactory receptors

Gustatory receptors on the tongue detect taste. Olfactory receptors in the nose
detect smell. When the nose is blocked, olfactory input falls, so the combined
perception of flavour becomes weaker.

# Plant-coordination patterns

## 13. Auxin and unidirectional light - 2 or 3 marks

Auxin is synthesised at the shoot tip. With light from one side, it moves to the
shaded side. Cells there elongate more, causing the shoot to bend towards the
light. This is positive phototropism.

## 14. Tendril growth around a support - 3 marks

The tendril is touch-sensitive. Contact slows growth on the touching side;
auxin-supported growth is faster away from the support. Unequal growth curves
the tendril around the support. The response is directional growth due to touch,
or thigmotropism.

## 15. Name the tropism and give an example

| Stimulus | Tropism | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Phototropism | shoot grows towards light |
| Gravity | Geotropism | root grows downwards |
| Water | Hydrotropism | root grows towards moisture |
| Chemicals | Chemotropism | pollen tube grows towards the ovule |
| Touch | Thigmotropism | tendril coils around support |

## 16. Sensitive plant versus shoot towards light - 2 marks

Sensitive-plant movement is fast, non-directional, growth-independent, and
caused by changes in cell water content. A shoot's phototropic movement is slow,
directional, growth-related, and caused by unequal cell elongation.

## 17. Plant hormones

| Hormone | Board-ready function |
| --- | --- |
| Auxin | cell elongation and tropic bending |
| Gibberellin | stem growth |
| Cytokinin | cell division; abundant in rapidly dividing fruits and seeds |
| Abscisic acid | inhibits growth; may cause leaf wilting |

Do not say cytokinin "opens stomata" or that abscisic acid promotes growth.

# Animal-hormone patterns

## 18. Insulin and feedback - verified 2023 pattern

Insulin is secreted by the pancreas and lowers or regulates blood-sugar level.
Insufficient effective insulin causes high blood sugar and may lead to diabetes.
When blood sugar rises, pancreatic cells make more insulin. As it falls, insulin
secretion is reduced. This negative feedback keeps the level within a suitable
range.

## 19. Adrenaline in a scary situation - 2 or 3 marks

The adrenal glands secrete adrenaline directly into blood. It:

- increases heart rate and oxygen supply
- increases breathing rate
- constricts small arteries in the skin and digestive system
- diverts more blood to skeletal muscles

These coordinated changes prepare the body to fight or run.

## 20. Iodine, thyroxin, and goitre

The thyroid gland needs iodine to make thyroxin. Thyroxin regulates
carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism for balanced growth. Iodine deficiency
can reduce thyroxin production and cause goitre, often seen as a swollen neck.

## 21. Hormone imbalance and gland - 2023 five-mark family

| Imbalance | Disorder or consequence | Gland |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Excess growth hormone in childhood | Gigantism | Pituitary |
| Oestrogen deficiency | Delay in puberty | Ovary |
| Low thyroxin, often due to iodine deficiency | Goitre | Thyroid |
| Low effective insulin | Diabetes or raised blood sugar | Pancreas |

The local official 2023 scheme specifically expects **delay in puberty** for
oestrogen deficiency, not a guessed disorder.

## 22. Nervous versus hormonal control - 3 marks

Write three or four matched pairs:

| Nervous | Hormonal |
| --- | --- |
| electrical impulses | chemical hormones |
| carried through neurons | carried through blood |
| rapid response | slower response |
| short-lived response | longer-lasting response |
| reaches connected effectors | can reach many tissues, but acts on target cells |

## 23. Why is chemical communication needed?

Electrical impulses reach only connected nerve cells and cannot be produced
continuously because neurons need reset time. A hormone can travel through blood
to many target tissues and can be released steadily. This is useful for
widespread, coordinated, persistent responses such as growth and emergency
preparation.

## 24. Hormone-source-function rapid drill

- pituitary -> growth hormone -> body growth
- thyroid -> thyroxin -> metabolism for growth
- pancreas -> insulin -> blood-sugar regulation
- testes -> testosterone -> male puberty changes
- ovaries -> oestrogen -> female puberty changes and menstrual-cycle regulation
- adrenal -> adrenaline -> emergency preparation
- hypothalamus -> releasing hormones -> stimulate pituitary hormone release

## 25. Complete five-mark synthesis

(a) Give three differences between nervous and hormonal control.  
(b) Explain auxin-mediated phototropism.

**Full-mark plan:** Draw a two-column comparison with signal, route, speed, and
duration; then write the four-link auxin chain:

`one-sided light -> auxin to shade -> greater elongation on shade -> shoot bends towards light`

### OR

(a) Give the gland and consequence for excess growth hormone, oestrogen
deficiency, and low thyroxin.  
(b) Explain feedback with insulin.

**Full-mark plan:** Pituitary/gigantism; ovary/delayed puberty;
thyroid/goitre. Then write:

`blood sugar rises -> pancreas releases more insulin -> blood sugar falls -> insulin release reduces`.
