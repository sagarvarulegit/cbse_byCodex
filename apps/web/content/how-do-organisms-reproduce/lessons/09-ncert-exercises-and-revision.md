## 7.1 in-text questions

### 1. Importance of DNA copying

It transfers hereditary information, maintains the species' basic body design,
and produces small variations because copying is not perfectly accurate.

### 2. Why variation benefits the species, not necessarily an individual

When conditions change, a few variants may survive and maintain the population.
A particular variation may be neutral or harmful to its present carrier, so it
does not guarantee individual benefit.

## 7.2 in-text questions

### 1. Binary versus multiple fission

Binary fission makes two daughters from one parent, as in Amoeba and Leishmania.
Multiple fission makes many daughters simultaneously, as in Plasmodium.

### 2. Benefit of spores

Numerous, light spores disperse widely. Thick walls protect them through
unfavourable conditions, and they germinate on a suitable moist surface.

### 3. Why complex organisms cannot reproduce through regeneration

Their specialised cells are arranged into tissues, organs, and organ systems in
fixed positions. A random piece cannot normally reconstruct the complete
organisation, and most cells cannot produce every required cell type.

### 4. Why vegetative propagation is practised

It rapidly preserves desirable parental traits, gives earlier flowering and
fruiting, and propagates seedless or poor-seed plants such as banana, rose, and
jasmine.

### 5. Why DNA copying is essential

It transmits the hereditary instructions required to make a similar body and
provides the variation on which survival and evolution can act.

## 7.3.3 in-text questions

### 1. Pollination versus fertilisation

Pollination transfers pollen from anther to stigma. Fertilisation is fusion of a
male germ cell with the egg in an ovule, producing a zygote.

### 2. Seminal vesicles and prostate gland

Their secretions add fluid to sperms, making transport easier and supplying
nutrition. Together with sperms, this fluid forms semen.

### 3. Changes in girls at puberty

Breasts develop, skin around nipples darkens, menstruation begins, hair grows in
armpits and the genital region, and skin may become oily with pimples. Changes
occur gradually and vary normally between individuals.

### 4. Embryo nourishment

The placenta embedded in the uterine wall has embryonic villi surrounded by
maternal blood spaces. Its large area permits glucose and oxygen to pass to the
embryo and wastes to pass to the mother's blood.

### 5. Does Copper-T protect against STIs?

No. It helps prevent pregnancy inside the uterus but does not form a barrier to
infectious body fluids. Condoms help reduce transmission of many STIs.

## What you have learnt

- Reproduction is not required to maintain one individual's life.
- DNA copying plus additional cellular apparatus precedes cell division.
- Body design determines the reproductive mode.
- Asexual reproduction uses one parent; sexual reproduction combines two
  parental DNA contributions.
- Fission, fragmentation, regeneration, budding, vegetative propagation, and
  spore formation are distinct processes.
- Sexual reproduction creates greater variation.
- Flower reproduction follows pollination, pollen-tube growth, fertilisation,
  embryo/seed formation, and fruit formation.
- Puberty signals reproductive-tissue maturation.
- Testes produce sperms; ovaries produce eggs; fertilisation occurs in an
  oviduct; implantation occurs in the uterus.
- Contraception includes condoms, oral pills, Copper-T, and surgical methods.

## NCERT Exercises

### Exercise 1

**Asexual reproduction takes place through budding in Amoeba, yeast,
Plasmodium, or Leishmania?**

**Answer:** Yeast. Amoeba and Leishmania show binary fission; Plasmodium shows
multiple fission.

### Exercise 2

**Which is not part of the female reproductive system: ovary, uterus, vas
deferens, or fallopian tube?**

**Answer:** Vas deferens; it is a male reproductive duct.

### Exercise 3

**The anther contains what?**

**Answer:** Pollen grains.

### Exercise 4

**Advantages of sexual over asexual reproduction**

Sexual reproduction combines DNA and accumulated variations from two parents,
creating new combinations. The greater variation improves the population's
chance of surviving environmental change and supplies material for evolution.

### Exercise 5

**Functions of the testes**

They produce male germ cells or sperms and secrete testosterone. Testosterone
helps regulate sperm formation and brings about several puberty changes in boys.

### Exercise 6

**Why does menstruation occur?**

The uterus prepares a thick, spongy, blood-rich lining each month for a possible
embryo. If the egg is not fertilised, no implantation occurs, so the unnecessary
lining breaks down and leaves through the vagina as blood and mucus.

### Exercise 8

**Different methods of contraception**

- barrier: condoms prevent sperm from reaching egg and reduce many STI risks
- hormonal: oral pills alter hormone balance and prevent egg release
- intrauterine: loop/Copper-T placed in uterus
- surgical: vasectomy blocks vas deferens; tubectomy blocks fallopian tubes

### Exercise 9

**How do modes differ in unicellular and multicellular organisms?**

In a unicellular organism, division of the one cell creates complete new
individuals, so fission or budding can work. Simple multicellular organisms may
fragment, regenerate, bud, or form spores. Complex multicellular organisms have
specialised tissues and organs, so they use specialised reproductive cells and
organs, commonly involving gametes.

### Exercise 10

**How does reproduction stabilise species populations?**

Reproduction replaces individuals that die and preserves the basic body design
through reliable DNA copying, maintaining the population in its niche.
Variations also help some members survive environmental change, supporting
long-term stability.

### Exercise 11

**Reasons for adopting contraception**

To avoid unwanted pregnancy, space children, protect parental health, plan a
family responsibly, and—with condoms—reduce transmission of many STIs.
