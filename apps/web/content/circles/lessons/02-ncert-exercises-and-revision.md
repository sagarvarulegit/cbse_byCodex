# NCERT Exercises and Chapter Revision

## Exercise 10.1

The four questions check:

- number of tangents from a point on a circle
- tangent, secant and point-of-contact vocabulary
- tangent length from an external point using radius and centre distance
- a true/false statement comparing tangents at the endpoints of a diameter

For the numerical question, draw the radius to the point of contact and use Pythagoras in the right triangle.

## Exercise 10.2

The thirteen questions use Figures 10.11-10.14 and several proof configurations. They include:

- multiple-choice angle questions
- proving parallel tangents at a diameter's endpoints
- perpendicular-through-centre and angle-bisector results
- tangent length from radius and centre distance
- equal sums of tangent segments in a circumscribed quadrilateral
- the right angle between two tangent radii
- angle between two tangents
- a circumscribed parallelogram and triangle
- supplementary opposite angles of a circumscribed quadrilateral

### Tangent-length bookkeeping

At each external vertex, name the two equal tangent segments. For a quadrilateral, adding the pairs gives

$$AB+CD=AD+BC.$$

## Common mistakes

- assuming a chord is tangent because it appears to touch the circumference
- skipping the right angle before applying Pythagoras
- pairing tangent segments from different external points

## Board-answer checklist

- Radius and point of contact named
- Theorem quoted at the exact step where it is used
- Equal tangents grouped by their external point
- Required angle or length stated with units

## One-minute revision

Draw the radii to every point of contact; the hidden right triangles then appear.

## Comic recap

- Contact point: "Every tangent theorem passes through me."
- Right angle: "I unlock Pythagoras and congruence."
- Equal pair: "Both tangents must start at one external point."
- Proof: "Name the theorem before using the result."
