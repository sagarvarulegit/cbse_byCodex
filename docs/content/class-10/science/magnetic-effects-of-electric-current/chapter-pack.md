## 1) magnetic field

Magnetic field is the region around a magnet where magnetic force can be detected.

Field lines show direction and strength of magnetic field.

Properties:
- outside a magnet, field lines go from north pole to south pole
- inside a magnet, they go from south to north
- field lines never intersect
- closer lines mean stronger field

## 2) magnetic field due to current

An electric current produces a magnetic field.

A compass needle deflects near a current-carrying wire because of this field.

For a straight conductor, magnetic field lines are concentric circles around the wire.

## 3) right-hand thumb rule

Hold the current-carrying conductor in your right hand with thumb pointing in current direction.

Curled fingers show direction of magnetic field lines.

## 4) circular loop and solenoid

In a circular loop, magnetic field lines become stronger at the centre.

A solenoid is a coil of many circular turns.

Magnetic field inside a solenoid is strong and nearly uniform.

A solenoid carrying current behaves like a bar magnet.

## 5) force on current-carrying conductor

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.

The direction of force depends on:
- direction of current
- direction of magnetic field

Fleming's left-hand rule:
- forefinger: magnetic field
- middle finger: current
- thumb: force or motion

## 6) electric motor

An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.

Principle:
A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences force and rotates.

Main parts:
- coil
- magnet
- split-ring commutator
- brushes
- axle

Split-ring commutator reverses current after each half rotation so the coil keeps rotating in the same direction.

## 7) domestic electric circuits

Domestic supply in India is about `220 V`, `50 Hz`.

Wires:
- live wire
- neutral wire
- earth wire

Fuse protects a circuit by melting when current becomes too high.

Earthing protects users from electric shock.

Overloading and short-circuiting can cause fire.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Current produces magnetic field.
- Field lines never intersect.
- Right-hand thumb rule gives field direction around conductor.
- Solenoid behaves like a bar magnet.
- Fleming's left-hand rule gives direction of force.
- Electric motor converts electrical energy to mechanical energy.
- Fuse and earthing are safety devices.

## common mistakes students make

1. Mixing right-hand thumb rule with Fleming's left-hand rule.
2. Saying magnetic field lines intersect at poles.
3. Forgetting the role of split-ring commutator.
4. Confusing live and neutral wires.
5. Saying fuse is connected in parallel.

## solved practice set

### question 1
Why do magnetic field lines not intersect?

Answer:
If they intersected, the magnetic field would have two directions at one point, which is impossible.

### question 2
What is the function of split-ring commutator?

Answer:
It reverses current in the coil after every half rotation to maintain rotation in one direction.

### question 3
What does an electric motor convert?

Answer:
Electrical energy into mechanical energy.
