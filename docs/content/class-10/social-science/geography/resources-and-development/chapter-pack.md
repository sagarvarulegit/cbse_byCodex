# Resources and Development

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- define resources and classify them

## 1) what is a resource?

Anything in the environment becomes a resource when it can satisfy human needs and is technologically accessible, economically feasible, and culturally acceptable.

Resources are not only gifts of nature. Human skill, technology, and institutions help turn natural materials into useful resources.

## 2) classification of resources

Resources can be classified in four main ways:

- Origin: biotic and abiotic
- Exhaustibility: renewable and non-renewable
- Ownership: individual, community, national, and international
- Status of development: potential, developed, stock, and reserves

Biotic resources come from living things. Abiotic resources are non-living things like rocks and metals.

Renewable resources can be renewed naturally, but careless use can still damage them. Non-renewable resources take millions of years to form.

## 3) development of resources

Resource use has created problems like depletion, unequal accumulation, and environmental damage.

Sustainable development means development should meet present needs without damaging the environment or reducing the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

The 1992 Rio Earth Summit and Agenda 21 stressed global cooperation for sustainable development.

## 4) resource planning in India

Resource planning means identifying resources, making a planning structure, and matching resource development with national development goals.

India needs resource planning because resources are unevenly distributed. Some regions have minerals, some have fertile soil, some have water, and some have forests.

## 5) land resources and land use

Land supports agriculture, forests, settlements, transport, industries, and wildlife.

Land use depends on physical factors like topography, climate, and soil, and human factors like population, technology, and culture.

Important land use categories include forests, land not available for cultivation, fallow land, net sown area, and pasture land.

## 6) land degradation and conservation

Land degradation means loss of land quality. Causes include deforestation, overgrazing, mining, over-irrigation, and industrial waste.

Conservation methods include afforestation, controlled grazing, shelter belts, proper mining management, and treatment of industrial effluents.

## 7) soil as a resource

Soil is a renewable natural resource, but it forms slowly. It supports plant growth and food production.

Major Indian soils:

- Alluvial soil: fertile, common in northern plains and river valleys
- Black soil: good for cotton, found in Deccan trap areas
- Red and yellow soil: formed from crystalline rocks, often less fertile
- Laterite soil: found in high rainfall areas, needs manure and fertilisers
- Arid soil: sandy and saline, found in dry regions
- Forest and mountain soil: varies with altitude and vegetation

## 8) soil erosion and conservation

Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by water, wind, or human misuse.

Types include sheet erosion, gully erosion, and wind erosion.

Conservation methods:

- contour ploughing
- terrace farming
- strip cropping
- shelter belts
- checking overgrazing

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Resource = useful, accessible, feasible, acceptable.
- Sustainable development protects present and future needs.
- Resource planning is essential because resources are unevenly distributed.
- Land degradation is caused by overuse and poor management.
- Alluvial soil is very fertile; black soil is linked with cotton.
- Soil erosion removes topsoil and lowers productivity.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing that all natural things are resources.
2. Confusing stock with reserves.
3. Treating renewable resources as unlimited.
4. Mixing up soil types and their regions.
5. Forgetting human causes of land degradation.

## board tips

- In classification questions, mention the basis first.
- For soil answers, connect feature + region + crop.
- In conservation answers, write practical methods, not just "save soil".
