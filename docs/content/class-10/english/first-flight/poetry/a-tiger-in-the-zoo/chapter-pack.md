# A Tiger in the Zoo

## chapter goal

Understand the contrast between the tiger's natural freedom and his caged life.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "Nelson Mandela - Long Walk to Freedom" in `jeff102.pdf`.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Caged tiger.
2. Tiger in the forest.
3. Contrast.
4. Theme of freedom.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) caged tiger

The tiger in the zoo moves inside a small cage. His strength is trapped.

The cage makes him silent, angry, and helpless.

## 2) forest tiger

In the forest, the tiger belongs to open space, grass, shadows, and natural hunting.

The poem reminds us that wild animals have a natural home.

## 3) contrast

The poem compares what the tiger is with what he should be.

This contrast creates sympathy for the animal.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The caged tiger represents lost freedom.
- The forest shows natural power and dignity.
- Contrast is the main method of the poem.
- The poem questions captivity.

## common mistakes students make

1. Describing the tiger only as dangerous.
2. Ignoring the sympathy created by the poem.
3. Missing the contrast between zoo and forest.

## board tips

- Use words like contrast, captivity, freedom, and dignity.
- Explain how setting changes the tiger's image.
- Keep answers tied to the poem, not general wildlife facts only.

## short answer practice

1. How does the cage affect the tiger?
2. What does the forest represent?
3. Why does the poem use contrast?

## mini quiz with answers

1. The cage limits the tiger's freedom.
2. The forest is his natural home.
3. The poem creates sympathy.
4. Contrast is important.
5. Captivity weakens the tiger's dignity.
