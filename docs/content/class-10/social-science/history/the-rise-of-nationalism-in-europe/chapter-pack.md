# The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain nationalism and the idea of the nation-state
- describe the French Revolution's role in nationalism
- understand liberalism, conservatism, and revolutionaries
- explain the unification of Germany and Italy
- analyse nationalism, imperialism, and the Balkans

## 1) nation-state and nationalism

Nationalism is the feeling that people sharing history, culture, territory, or political hopes should form a nation.

In Europe, nationalism helped replace empires and dynastic states with nation-states.

## 2) French Revolution and nation

The French Revolution promoted ideas of citizenship, equality before law, common symbols, and a united people.

Measures such as a common flag, national assembly, hymns, and uniform laws helped create a sense of collective identity.

Napoleon introduced administrative reforms, but his rule also created resentment because of conquest and control.

## 3) liberal nationalism

For the middle classes, liberalism meant freedom for the individual, equality before law, representative government, and private property.

Economically, liberals wanted free movement of goods, people, and capital.

However, early liberal nationalism often limited political rights to property-owning men.

## 4) conservatism after 1815

After Napoleon's defeat, European powers tried to restore monarchies and maintain traditional authority.

The Congress of Vienna redrew boundaries to create a conservative order.

Conservatives supported monarchy, church, social hierarchy, and stability, but some accepted limited modern reforms.

## 5) revolutionaries and revolutions

Revolutionaries like Giuseppe Mazzini believed in united republics and national freedom.

The 1830s and 1848 saw revolts, demands for constitutions, and struggles for national unity.

Romanticism used folk songs, language, and culture to build national feeling.

## 6) Germany

Germany was divided into many states. Prussia led unification through diplomacy and wars.

Otto von Bismarck used the Prussian army and bureaucracy. After wars with Denmark, Austria, and France, the German Empire was proclaimed in 1871.

## 7) Italy

Italy was divided among dynastic states and foreign rule.

Mazzini inspired republican nationalism, Cavour used diplomacy, Garibaldi led armed volunteers, and Victor Emmanuel II became king of united Italy.

## 8) visualising the nation

Artists personified nations as female figures. Examples include Marianne for France and Germania for Germany.

Symbols such as flags, crowns, swords, broken chains, and oak leaves helped make abstract ideas visible.

## 9) nationalism and imperialism

Nationalism later became tied to competition between powerful states.

The Balkans became a region of intense conflict because of ethnic diversity, declining Ottoman control, and rivalry among European powers.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- French Revolution helped create modern political nationalism.
- Liberalism meant political rights, law, and economic freedom.
- Congress of Vienna restored conservative order.
- Germany unified under Prussian leadership in 1871.
- Italy unified through Mazzini, Cavour, Garibaldi, and Victor Emmanuel II.
- The Balkans show how nationalism could become explosive.

## common mistakes students make

1. Treating nationalism as always democratic.
2. Confusing Mazzini, Cavour, and Garibaldi.
3. Forgetting Bismarck's role in Germany.
4. Mixing Marianne and Germania.

## board tips

- Use timelines for Germany and Italy.
- In visual-source questions, identify symbol and meaning.
- For liberalism, separate political and economic meanings.
