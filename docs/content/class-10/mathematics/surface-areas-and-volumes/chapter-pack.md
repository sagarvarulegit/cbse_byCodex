# Surface Areas and Volumes

This is an original teaching draft for Class 10 Mathematics, Chapter 12. Students usually know the formulas partly, but they lose marks by choosing the wrong solid or wrong surface.

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- identify the required solid from the question
- use correct formulas for surface area and volume
- solve combination-of-solids questions
- handle conversion of one solid into another
- work with a frustum of a cone

## 1) solid-shape refresher

Main solids in this chapter:
- cube
- cuboid
- cylinder
- cone
- sphere
- hemisphere
- frustum of a cone

### core idea
Surface area measures covering.
Volume measures space inside.

## 2) important formulas

Students must know:

- cylinder volume = `pi r^2 h`
- cone volume = `1/3 pi r^2 h`
- sphere volume = `4/3 pi r^3`
- hemisphere volume = `2/3 pi r^3`

- curved surface area of cylinder = `2pi rh`
- curved surface area of cone = `pi rl`
- surface area of sphere = `4pi r^2`
- curved surface area of hemisphere = `2pi r^2`
- total surface area of hemisphere = `3pi r^2`

For a cone:
`l = sqrt(r^2 + h^2)`

## 3) combination of solids

Some questions join two or more solids.

Examples:
- a hemisphere on a cylinder
- a cone on a cylinder
- a toy made from multiple parts

### method
1. separate the figure into known solids
2. find each area or volume
3. add them
4. do not count joined circular faces when surface is internal

That last point is very important.

## 4) conversion of solids

Sometimes one solid is melted and recast into another.

In such cases:

`Volume before = Volume after`

Example idea:
A sphere is melted to form small cones.
Total volume remains the same.

### memory trick
Melted shape changes outside, not amount of material.

## 5) frustum of a cone

A frustum is the part left when the top of a cone is cut off by a plane parallel to the base.

Important formulas:

- volume = `1/3 pi h (R^2 + r^2 + Rr)`
- curved surface area = `pi l (R + r)`

where:
- `R` = bigger radius
- `r` = smaller radius
- `h` = vertical height
- `l` = slant height

## 6) exam-friendly summary notes

### must-know facts
- Volume measures capacity, surface area measures covering.
- Different questions ask for curved surface area or total surface area, not always both.
- Joined surfaces inside a combined solid are not counted in outer surface area.
- Recasting means equal volumes.
- Frustum formulas must be remembered carefully.

### one-line revision points
- identify solid first
- choose CSA or TSA correctly
- volume stays same in melting problems

## 7) common mistakes students make

1. Using total surface area when only curved surface area is asked.
2. Counting hidden joined surfaces.
3. Mixing slant height and vertical height in cones.
4. Forgetting equal volumes in conversion questions.
5. Using wrong radius for hemisphere or frustum.

## 8) board tips

- Draw a small side sketch if the figure is mixed.
- Write the formula name before substitution.
- Check whether the top or bottom circular face is exposed.
- Keep unit conversion consistent, especially `cm`, `m`, and `cm^3`.

## 9) how to remember the chapter

### memory box 1
Area is outside, volume is inside.

### memory box 2
Combined solid means split first.

### memory box 3
Melted and recast means volume stays same.

### memory box 4
Cone needs careful attention to `r`, `h`, and `l`.

## 10) solved practice set

### question 1
Find the volume of a cylinder with `r = 7 cm` and `h = 10 cm`.

Answer:
`Volume = pi x 7^2 x 10 = 490pi cm^3`

### question 2
Find the curved surface area of a cone with `r = 3 cm` and `l = 5 cm`.

Answer:
`CSA = pi x 3 x 5 = 15pi cm^2`

### question 3
Why are internal joined surfaces not counted in outer surface area?

Answer:
Because they are not visible or exposed on the outside of the solid.

## 12) mini quiz with answers

### quiz
1. Volume of a cone is:
   - a) `pi r^2 h`
   - b) `1/3 pi r^2 h`
   - c) `2pi rh`
   - d) `4pi r^2`

2. Total surface area of a hemisphere is:
   - a) `2pi r^2`
   - b) `3pi r^2`
   - c) `4pi r^2`
   - d) `pi r^2`

3. In recasting problems, which stays same?
   - a) shape
   - b) radius
   - c) volume
   - d) height

4. Hidden joined surfaces in a solid are:
   - a) added twice
   - b) always exposed
   - c) not counted in outside area
   - d) counted as volume

5. Frustum comes from:
   - a) stretching a sphere
   - b) cutting the top of a cone
   - c) joining two cylinders
   - d) folding a rectangle

### answer key
1. b
2. b
3. c
4. c
5. b
