# 3.3 How do Metals and Non-metals React?

Noble gases react very little because their outermost shells are completely
filled. Other atoms tend to reach a stable noble-gas configuration by losing,
gaining or sharing electrons. In this chapter, a metal transfers electrons to
a non-metal.

## Figure 3.5 — formation of sodium chloride

[[demo:figure-3-5-sodium-chloride-formation]]

Sodium loses its one valence electron:

`Na (2,8,1) → Na⁺ (2,8) + e⁻`

Chlorine gains that electron:

`Cl (2,8,7) + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (2,8,8)`

The sodium nucleus still has 11 protons but the atom now has 10 electrons, so
it has a +1 charge. Chlorine has 17 protons and 18 electrons after the transfer,
so it has a −1 charge.

`Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl`

Oppositely charged ions are held by strong **electrostatic forces of
attraction**. Sodium chloride does not exist as separate NaCl molecules; it
exists as a large aggregate of oppositely charged ions.

## Figure 3.6 — formation of magnesium chloride

[[demo:figure-3-6-magnesium-chloride-formation]]

Magnesium loses two valence electrons:

`Mg (2,8,2) → Mg²⁺ (2,8) + 2e⁻`

Each of two chlorine atoms gains one electron:

`2Cl (2,8,7) + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ (2,8,8)`

`Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → MgCl₂`

The cation is Mg²⁺ and the anion is Cl⁻.

## Key definitions

- **Cation:** positively charged ion formed here when a metal loses electrons.
- **Anion:** negatively charged ion formed here when a non-metal gains
  electrons.
- **Ionic/electrovalent compound:** compound formed by electron transfer and
  electrostatic attraction between the resulting ions.
- **Electrovalent bond:** the electrostatic force holding oppositely charged
  ions together.

## Four properties with reasons

### (i) Physical nature

Ionic compounds are solids and somewhat hard because strong forces hold
positive and negative ions together. They are generally brittle and break when
pressure is applied.

### (ii) Melting and boiling points

They have high melting and boiling points because considerable energy is
needed to overcome strong inter-ionic attraction.

### (iii) Solubility

They are generally soluble in water and insoluble in organic solvents such as
kerosene and petrol.

### (iv) Conduction of electricity

- **Solid:** no conduction because ions are locked in a rigid structure.
- **Molten:** conducts because heat overcomes enough attraction for ions to
  move.
- **Aqueous:** conducts because dissolved ions move to opposite electrodes.

The charge carriers in molten and aqueous ionic compounds are **ions**, not
free electrons.

# NCERT Questions — after 3.3

## 1. (i) Write the electron-dot structures for sodium, oxygen and magnesium.

### Answer

Valence-electron representations:

- Sodium: `Na•`
- Oxygen: six valence electrons around O, represented as `:O:••`
- Magnesium: `•Mg•`

The dots show only outer-shell electrons: Na has 1, O has 6 and Mg has 2.

## 1. (ii) Show the formation of Na₂O and MgO by transfer of electrons.

### Na₂O

Two sodium atoms each lose one electron; one oxygen atom gains both:

`2Na → 2Na⁺ + 2e⁻`

`O + 2e⁻ → O²⁻`

`2Na⁺ + O²⁻ → Na₂O`

### MgO

`Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻`

`O + 2e⁻ → O²⁻`

`Mg²⁺ + O²⁻ → MgO`

## 1. (iii) What ions are present in these compounds?

### Answer

- Na₂O contains Na⁺ and O²⁻ ions.
- MgO contains Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions.

## 2. Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?

### Answer

Ionic compounds contain oppositely charged ions held by strong electrostatic
forces. A large amount of energy is required to overcome these strong
inter-ionic attractions, so their melting points are high.

## Board answer builder

For an ionic-bond question:

1. write the electronic configurations;
2. show which atom loses and which gains electrons;
3. show charges on the resulting ions;
4. balance the number of transferred electrons;
5. state electrostatic attraction;
6. write the correct formula.

## Common mistakes

- Writing `MgCl` instead of `MgCl₂`.
- Calling NaCl a molecule in this context.
- Saying solid NaCl has no ions; it has ions, but they cannot move.
- Giving “strong bond” without naming electrostatic attraction.
- Showing oxygen gaining only one electron in Na₂O or MgO.

## One-minute revision

Metals lose electrons to form cations; non-metals gain them to form anions.
Electrostatic attraction forms ionic compounds. Their strong inter-ionic
forces explain hardness and high melting points. They conduct only when ions
are free to move in molten or aqueous states.
