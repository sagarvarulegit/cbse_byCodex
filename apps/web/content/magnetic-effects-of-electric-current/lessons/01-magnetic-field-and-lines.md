# 12.1 Magnetic Field and Field Lines

Activity 12.1: current in a wire deflects a nearby compass; reversing current
reverses deflection. Electric current therefore produces a magnetic field.

A compass needle is a small magnet. Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.
It deflects near a bar magnet because the magnet's field exerts torque on it.

<ActivityIllustration demoId="figure-12-4-bar-magnet-field" />

## Magnetic field

Region around a magnet in which magnetic force can be detected. It has
magnitude and direction; direction at a point is the direction a free north
pole/compass north end would move.

## Field-line properties

1. Outside a magnet, lines emerge from N and enter S.
2. Inside, they go S to N; hence they form closed curves.
3. Tangent at a point gives field direction.
4. Closer lines show stronger field; crowding is greatest near poles.
5. Lines never intersect, because one point cannot have two field directions.

Activities 12.2–12.3 use iron filings to reveal the pattern and a compass to
trace individual lines. Iron filings align along the local field.

## Uniform field

Represent a uniform magnetic field by straight, parallel, equally spaced lines
with arrowheads in one direction.

## Drawing checklist

Mark N/S, draw smooth closed curves, put outside arrows N→S, show greater
density near poles, and never cross lines.
