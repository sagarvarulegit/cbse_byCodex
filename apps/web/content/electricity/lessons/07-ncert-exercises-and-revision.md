# Chapter 11 NCERT Answer Lab

## In-text question blocks

- Circuit: a continuous closed conducting path.
- `1A=1C/s`; one coulomb is about `6×10^18` electrons.
- Cell/battery maintains potential difference; `1V=1J/C`; a 6 V battery gives
  6 J to each coulomb.
- Resistance depends on length, area, material and temperature. A thick wire
  carries more current because its resistance is lower. Halving V at constant R
  halves I. Alloy heater coils have high resistivity/melting point and resist
  oxidation. Iron is a better conductor than mercury; silver is the best
  conductor in NCERT Table 11.2.

For three 2 V cells with `5Ω,8Ω,12Ω` in series:
`V=6V`, `R=25Ω`, so ammeter reads `0.24A`; voltmeter across 12 Ω reads
`2.88V`.

Parallel estimates: `1Ω||10^6Ω` is just below `1Ω`; adding `10^3Ω` keeps it
just below 1 Ω (`≈0.999Ω`).

For 100 Ω lamp, 50 Ω toaster and 500 Ω filter at 220 V, total current is
`2.2+4.4+0.44=7.04A`. An iron drawing the same current has
`R=220/7.04=31.25Ω`.

With `2Ω,3Ω,6Ω`: `(3||6)+2=4Ω`; all three parallel give `1Ω`.
For `4Ω,8Ω,12Ω,24Ω`, maximum (all series) is `48Ω`; minimum (all parallel)
is `2Ω`.

Heater cord does not glow because its resistance is far below the element's.
`Q=96000C`, `V=50V` gives `H=VQ=4.8×10^6J`.
An iron `R=20Ω`, `I=5A`, `t=30s` gives `H=15000J`.

A 220 V motor drawing 5 A has `P=1100W`; in 2 h it uses `2.2kWh`
`=7.92×10^6J`.

1. Five equal pieces in parallel: each `R/5`, equivalent `R/25`; `R/R'=25`,
   **(d)**.
2. Not a power expression: **(b) `IR²`**.
3. 100 W bulb at 110 V: **(d) 25 W**.
4. Same V, equal wires, series:parallel heat = **(c) 1:4**.
5. Voltmeter connects in parallel.

## 6. Copper wire

`d=0.5mm`, `r=0.25×10^-3m`, `A=πr²=1.963×10^-7m²`.
`l=RA/ρ≈122.7m`. Doubling diameter makes area fourfold, so resistance becomes
`2.5Ω`, a decrease of `7.5Ω`.

## 18. Reasons

- tungsten: very high melting point and high resistivity;
- heater alloy: high resistivity, high melting point, resists oxidation;
- no domestic series: voltage division, no independence, one failure breaks all;
- resistance varies inversely with cross-sectional area;
- copper/aluminium: low resistivity, good conductivity, economical/practical.
