# Board Exam Practice - Verified Questions

## Source boundary

Lessons 1-5 follow the current NCERT textbook. This is a separate,
unnumbered exam-practice supplement; it is not an NCERT section.

The 11 PYQs below were checked against their original English board-paper pages
and the matching official CBSE marking-scheme pages on 26 July 2026. The
answers are labelled **Platform marking points** because they paraphrase the
official value points and add student-friendly reasoning.

Historical questions show useful demand patterns, but they do not predict a
future paper.

## The full-mark answer method

For a multi-part answer:

1. Copy the same `(a)`, `(b)`, `(i)` and `(ii)` labels.
2. Begin with the direct answer.
3. Add the scientific reason, observation or definition requested.
4. Write a balanced equation with condition and states when known.
5. Count the requested examples or uses before stopping.

[REMEMBER: A correct fact can still lose a mark if the question also asks for
an equation, reason, pH value, electrode, observation, or second example.]

### Official SQP Q28 - Neutralisation data and reasoning - 4 marks

Sara adds phenolphthalein to dilute sodium hydroxide and then adds dilute
sulphuric acid drop by drop until the pink solution becomes colourless. Her
results are:

| Dilute NaOH | Dilute H2SO4 |
| ---: | ---: |
| 2 mL | 20 drops |
| 3 mL | 30 drops |
| 4 mL | 40 drops |

(A) If concentrated sulphuric acid replaces dilute sulphuric acid, will the
required number of drops be `40`, less than `40`, or more than `40`? Justify.

(B) Twenty drops of the dilute acid occupy `1 mL`. If `3 mL` acid causes the
colour change, calculate the initial volume of sodium hydroxide.

**OR**

Acid containing phenolphthalein is colourless. Predict the change when sodium
hydroxide is added until neutralisation and explain it.

(C) Write the balanced equation. Choose and justify the correct
classification: neutralisation and double displacement; neutralisation and
precipitation; precipitation and double displacement; or all three.

## Answer check

### PYQ 1

**Platform marking points:**

- (a)(i) Groups A and B have pH below `7`.
- (a)(ii) The basic members of Group C have pH above `7`; sodium chloride is
  neutral.
- (b) Use pH paper and a universal indicator.
- (c) The copper vessel is tarnished by a basic copper compound. Acidic lemon
  juice reacts with that coating to form soluble salt, which is washed away.
- OR: any two complete applications, such as optimum pH for digestion, tooth
  decay below pH `5.5`, defence by stings, or difficulty for aquatic life when
  river pH falls.

### PYQ 2

- (i) Tomato juice tastes slightly sour because pH `4.6` is acidic and acids
  taste sour.
- (ii) A strong acid produces more `H+` or `H3O+` ions; a weak base produces
  fewer `OH-` ions in water.
- (iii) Aquatic animals survive best around pH `7.0-7.8`. Acid rain has pH
  below `5.6` and lowers river pH, making survival difficult.

### PYQ 3

- (i) Plaster of Paris; calcium sulphate hemihydrate,
  `CaSO4·1/2H2O`.
- (ii) `CaSO4·2H2O --373 K--> CaSO4·1/2H2O + 1 1/2H2O`
- (iii) Any two: making toys, decorative materials, smoothing surfaces, or
  supporting fractured bones.

### PYQ 4

- (a) Parent acid: hydrochloric acid, `HCl`; parent base: sodium hydroxide,
  `NaOH`.
- (b) Cation `Ca2+`; anion `SO4 2-`.
- (c) Both sodium chloride and washing soda contain `Na+`, so they belong to
  the same family of salts.
- OR: pH is a scale for measuring hydrogen-ion concentration. Potassium
  sulphate, `K2SO4`, is neutral and has pH about `7`.

### PYQ 5

- Strongly acidic: B; strongly alkaline: C; weakly acidic: A; neutral: D;
  weakly alkaline: E.
- Increasing `H+` concentration: `C < E < D < A < B`.
- Acidic salt: ammonium chloride, `NH4Cl`; parent acid `HCl`, parent base
  `NH4OH`.
- Basic salt: sodium carbonate, `Na2CO3`; parent acid `H2CO3`, parent base
  `NaOH`.

### PYQ 6

- (i) Hydrogen chloride, `HCl`, gas.
- (ii) Dry blue litmus shows no colour change because dry HCl does not produce
  `H+` ions without water.
- Wet blue litmus turns red because HCl ionises in water.
- The aqueous solution is acidic.

### PYQ 7

- Chlor-alkali:
  `2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) --Electricity--> 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g) + Cl2(g)`.
- Chlorine forms at the anode; hydrogen forms at the cathode.
- Chlorine uses: preparation of bleaching powder and disinfecting drinking
  water.
- Hydrogen uses: manufacture of ammonia/fertilisers and use in fuels or
  hydrogenation to make margarine.
- OR preparation of washing soda:
  `NaCl + NH3 + CO2 + H2O -> NaHCO3 + NH4Cl`
  `2NaHCO3 --Heat--> Na2CO3 + CO2 + H2O`
  `Na2CO3 + 10H2O -> Na2CO3·10H2O`
- Uses: glass, soap and paper industries; manufacture of borax; domestic
  cleaning; removing permanent hardness of water.

### PYQ 8

- (a)(i)
  `2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) --Electricity--> 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g) + Cl2(g)`
- (a)(ii)
  `NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 -> NaHCO3 + NH4Cl`
- OR: connect two nails through a bulb, switch and battery and dip the nails
  into each solution. The bulb glows in dilute acid but not in glucose or
  alcohol. Acids form ions in water; glucose and alcohol do not.

### PYQ 9

- **Option 4:** Assertion is false but Reason is true.
- Safe rule: add concentrated acid slowly to water with constant stirring.
  Never add water to concentrated acid because dilution is highly exothermic;
  sudden local heating can cause splashing and burns.

### PYQ 10

- (a) The process forms chlorine and sodium hydroxide, so it is called
  chlor-alkali.
- `2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) --Electricity--> 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g) + Cl2(g)`
- Anode: chlorine; cathode: hydrogen.
- OR:
  - baking soda:
    `NaCl + H2O + NH3 + CO2 -> NaHCO3 + NH4Cl`
  - bleaching powder, current NCERT representation:
    `2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2 -> Ca(ClO)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O`
  - Plaster of Paris:
    `CaSO4·2H2O --373 K--> CaSO4·1/2H2O + 1 1/2H2O`
- Older board sources may show `CaOCl2`; follow the chemical representation
  required by the source used in the question.

### PYQ 11

- (a) Universal indicator.
- (b) Solution A, pH `3`, has the highest `H+` concentration.
- (c) A strong acid forms more `H+` ions in water; a weak acid forms fewer.
- OR: acid rain has pH below `5.6`. Acetic acid and formic acid are weak acids.

### Official SQP Q28

- (A) Less than `40` drops because concentrated sulphuric acid supplies more
  `H+` ions per drop than the dilute acid.
- (B) `3 mL` acid = `60` drops. The table shows 10 acid drops per `1 mL`
  NaOH, so the starting NaOH volume was `6 mL`.
- OR: colourless to pink because phenolphthalein is colourless in acid and
  pink in basic solution.
- (C)
  `2NaOH(aq) + H2SO4(aq) -> Na2SO4(aq) + 2H2O(l)`
- It is neutralisation because acid and base form salt and water. It is double
  displacement because ions exchange partners. It is not precipitation
  because sodium sulphate is soluble and no insoluble solid forms.

## Common mistakes

- Treating pH `7` as acidic or basic instead of neutral.
- Saying a lower pH means fewer `H+` ions.
- Confusing strength with concentration.
- Writing that dry HCl is not acidic without explaining the absence of water
  and ions.
- Interchanging chlorine at the anode and hydrogen at the cathode.
- Forgetting water of crystallisation in washing soda or POP.
- Giving one use when the question asks for two or four.
- Adding water to concentrated acid.
- Calling every double-displacement reaction a precipitation reaction.

## Board tips

- For a pH order, write “lower pH -> higher `H+` concentration” first.
- For a salt, use the four-part pattern: formula -> ions -> parent acid/base ->
  expected pH.
- For an industrial compound, use: raw material -> balanced equation ->
  condition -> uses.
- For a lab question, write setup -> observation -> ion/reaction reason.
- Before ending, count equations, examples and uses against the command words.

## One-minute revision

- Strong acid means more ionisation, not merely more concentrated.
- Dry HCl needs water before it shows acidic behaviour.
- Add acid to water, never water to acid.
- Low pH means high `H+`.
- Chlor-alkali: chlorine at anode, hydrogen at cathode, NaOH remains in
  solution.
- POP is `CaSO4·1/2H2O`; washing soda is `Na2CO3·10H2O`.
- A soluble salt product means no precipitate.
