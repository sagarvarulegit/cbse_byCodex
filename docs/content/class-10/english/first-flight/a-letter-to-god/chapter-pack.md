# A Letter to God

## chapter goal

By the end of this unit, a student should be able to:
- retell Lencho's story and explain the irony
- describe Lencho, the postmaster, and post office employees
- explain the themes of faith, innocence, kindness, and suspicion
- revise the poem "Dust of Snow" and the poem "Fire and Ice"
- answer value-based and inference questions in simple English

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack follows `jeff101.pdf`: "A Letter to God", money order/post office context, "Dust of Snow", and "Fire and Ice".

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Story situation and setting
2. Lencho's faith and loss
3. The postmaster's kindness
4. The ending and irony
5. Poem: "Dust of Snow"
6. Poem: "Fire and Ice"
7. Exam practice and recap

## 1) story situation and setting

Lencho is a hardworking farmer living in a lonely house in a valley. His crop needs rain, and he expects a good harvest.

The rain begins as a blessing, but it turns into a hailstorm. The hail destroys his cornfield and leaves the family helpless.

## 2) Lencho's faith

Lencho has complete faith in God. He believes God sees everything and will help honest people.

He writes a letter asking God for money so he can sow again and survive until the next crop.

His faith is powerful, but it is also simple and unquestioning.

## 3) the postmaster and employees

The postmaster is first amused by the letter, then moved by Lencho's faith.

He collects money from employees and friends and sends it to Lencho in the name of God.

This act shows human kindness and compassion.

## 4) ending and irony

Lencho receives less money than he asked for because the postmaster could not collect the full amount.

Instead of thanking the post office workers, Lencho thinks they stole the missing money.

The irony is that the people he calls dishonest are the same people who helped him.

## 5) poem: Dust of Snow

This short poem shows how a small natural moment changes the speaker's mood.

A crow shakes snow from a hemlock tree, and the falling snow saves part of the speaker's day.

Theme: even ordinary or unexpected things can heal a sad mood.

## 6) poem: Fire and Ice

The poem presents two destructive human emotions: desire and hatred.

Fire suggests greed, uncontrolled desire, and passion. Ice suggests hatred, coldness, and cruelty.

Theme: both desire and hatred can destroy the world of human relationships.

## character notes

Lencho: hardworking, hopeful, deeply faithful, innocent, but suspicious at the end.

Postmaster: kind, sensitive, practical, and respectful of faith.

Post office employees: ordinary people who become part of an act of generosity.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The story is built on faith and irony.
- Lencho's crop is destroyed by hail.
- The postmaster sends money to protect Lencho's faith.
- Lencho mistakes helpers for thieves.
- "Dust of Snow" shows mood change through nature.
- "Fire and Ice" warns against desire and hatred.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing that God directly sent Lencho the money.
2. Ignoring the irony of the ending.
3. Calling Lencho greedy instead of desperate and faithful.
4. Mixing the symbols of fire and ice.

## board tips

- In story answers, mention both faith and irony.
- In poem answers, connect image + meaning.
- Use short character evidence instead of retelling the whole chapter.

## short answer practice

1. Why did Lencho write a letter to God?
2. Why did the postmaster decide to help Lencho?
3. What is ironic about Lencho's second letter?
4. How does nature change the speaker's mood in "Dust of Snow"?
5. What do fire and ice symbolise?

## mini quiz with answers

1. Lencho was a farmer.
2. Hail destroyed Lencho's crop.
3. The postmaster collected money for Lencho.
4. "Dust of Snow" uses a crow and a hemlock tree.
5. In "Fire and Ice", fire suggests desire and ice suggests hatred.
