# NCERT Exercises and Chapter Revision

Try each printed exercise before opening its answer. A proof earns marks for its reasoned chain, not only its final line.

## Exercise 1.1

### 1. Express each number as a product of its prime factors

**Answer:**

- $140=2^2\times5\times7$
- $156=2^2\times3\times13$
- $3825=3^2\times5^2\times17$
- $5005=5\times7\times11\times13$
- $7429=17\times19\times23$

### 2. Find the LCM and HCF of the following pairs and verify that LCM x HCF equals the product

**Answer:**

- $26,91$: HCF $=13$, LCM $=182$
- $510,92$: HCF $=2$, LCM $=23460$
- $336,54$: HCF $=6$, LCM $=3024$

### 3. Find HCF and LCM by prime factorisation

**Answer:**

- $12,15,21$: HCF $=3$, LCM $=420$
- $17,23,29$: HCF $=1$, LCM $=11339$
- $8,9,25$: HCF $=1$, LCM $=1800$

### 4. Given HCF$(306,657)=9$, find the LCM

**Answer:**

$$\operatorname{LCM}=\frac{306\times657}{9}=22338.$$

### 5. Check whether $6^n$ can end with digit $0$

**Answer:** No. $6^n=2^n3^n$ has no prime factor $5$.

### 6. Explain why each expression is composite

**Answer:**

- $7\times11\times13+13=13(7\times11+1)$
- $7\times6\times5\times4\times3\times2\times1+5=5(7\times6\times4\times3\times2\times1+1)$

Each has a non-trivial factor.

### 7. Circular path meeting time

**Answer:** The friends take $18$ and $12$ minutes. They meet together at the starting point after LCM$(18,12)=36$ minutes.

## Exercise 1.2

### 1. Prove that $\sqrt5$ is irrational

**Answer:** Assume $\sqrt5=a/b$ in lowest form. The equation $a^2=5b^2$ forces $5\mid a$ and then $5\mid b$, contradicting lowest form.

### 2. Prove that $3+2\sqrt5$ is irrational

**Answer:** If it were rational, subtracting $3$ and dividing by $2$ would make $\sqrt5$ rational, a contradiction.

### 3. Prove that the listed numbers are irrational

**Answer:**

- $1/\sqrt2$: otherwise its reciprocal $\sqrt2$ would be rational.
- $7\sqrt5$: otherwise division by $7$ would make $\sqrt5$ rational.
- $6+\sqrt2$: otherwise subtraction of $6$ would make $\sqrt2$ rational.

## 1.4 Summary

- Every composite number has one prime factorisation apart from order.
- If prime $p$ divides $a^2$, then $p$ divides $a$.
- The contradiction proof shows $\sqrt2$, $\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt5$ are irrational.

### Note to the Reader

For three positive integers, the textbook gives separate HCF and LCM relations. Do not extend the two-number product rule without checking the stated formula.

## Common mistakes

- multiplying before cancelling the HCF in an LCM calculation
- treating the optional support topics as current numbered sections
- giving only a one-line claim for an irrationality proof

## Board-answer checklist

- Have you shown prime factors clearly?
- Have you used the correct HCF/LCM powers?
- Have you stated "lowest form" and the final contradiction in every proof?

## One-minute revision

Prime factorisation controls the arithmetic. Lowest-form contradiction controls the proofs.

## Comic recap

- Prime factors: "We form one unique fingerprint."
- HCF: "Take the shared smallest powers."
- LCM: "Take all largest powers."
- Irrational proof: "A shared factor breaks lowest form."
