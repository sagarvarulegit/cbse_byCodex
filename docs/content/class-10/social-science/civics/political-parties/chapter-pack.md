# Political Parties

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- define political party

- compare party systems
- identify national and state parties
- discuss challenges and reforms

## 1) why parties are needed

Political parties are essential in modern democracies because they organise opinions, contest elections, form governments, and link people with the state.

Without parties, representative democracy would become unstable and unorganised.

## 2) meaning of political party

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in government.

Parties usually have leaders, active members, and followers.

They agree on some policies and programmes for society.

## 3) functions of parties

Political parties:

- contest elections
- put forward policies and programmes
- make laws through elected representatives
- form and run governments
- play opposition role
- shape public opinion
- provide access to government machinery and welfare schemes

## 4) party systems

One-party system: only one party effectively controls politics. This is not considered democratic competition.

Two-party system: two main parties compete for power.

Multi-party system: several parties compete and may form coalitions.

India has a multi-party system because of its social and regional diversity.

## 5) national and state parties

National parties have a presence in several states and are recognised by the Election Commission based on performance criteria.

State parties are influential in one or a few states and often raise regional issues.

Regional parties have strengthened federal politics and coalition governments.

## 6) challenges

Major challenges include:

- lack of internal democracy
- dynastic succession
- money and muscle power
- limited meaningful choice because parties may look similar on some issues

## 7) reforms

Reforms include anti-defection law, affidavits about candidates' criminal and financial records, internal elections, transparent funding, and citizen pressure.

People can improve parties by joining them, questioning them, voting responsibly, and supporting clean politics.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- A political party contests elections and tries to form government.
- Parties link citizens with government.
- India has a multi-party system.
- National and state parties are recognised by the Election Commission.
- Parties face challenges of internal democracy, dynastic politics, and money power.

## common mistakes students make

1. Defining party only as an election group.
2. Forgetting opposition as a function.
3. Calling India's system a two-party system.
4. Writing reforms without explaining citizen role.

## board tips

- In function answers, give at least five clear functions.
- In challenges, explain each term briefly.
- Mention Election Commission recognition for national and state parties.
