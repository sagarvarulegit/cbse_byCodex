# 5.5 Excretion

Excretion removes metabolic wastes from the body. In humans, nitrogenous wastes are removed mainly by kidneys through nephrons. Plants use different strategies because their body design and waste products are different.

## 5.5.1 Excretion in human beings

The human excretory system includes:
- kidneys
- ureters
- urinary bladder
- urethra

Kidneys filter blood and remove soluble nitrogenous wastes such as urea.

Ureters carry urine from kidneys to urinary bladder.

Urinary bladder stores urine until it is passed out through the urethra.

## Nephron structure

Nephrons are the functional units of kidneys.

Each nephron has:
- a cluster of capillaries called glomerulus
- Bowman's capsule around the glomerulus
- a long tubule
- connection to a collecting duct

Bowman's capsule collects the filtrate formed from blood.

## Urine formation

Urine formation has three main ideas:

1. filtration
Blood is filtered under pressure in the glomerulus. Water and small dissolved substances enter Bowman's capsule.

2. selective reabsorption
Useful substances such as glucose, amino acids, salts, and much of the water are reabsorbed from the tubule back into blood.

3. urine collection
The remaining waste-rich fluid becomes urine and passes into the collecting duct, ureter, bladder, and urethra.

The amount of urine depends on:
- how much excess water is in the body
- how much dissolved waste must be removed
- hormone regulation and body needs

## Artificial kidney

Kidney failure can allow poisonous wastes to accumulate in the body.

An artificial kidney removes nitrogenous wastes from blood by dialysis.

In dialysis:
- blood passes through tubes with a semi-permeable lining
- tubes are surrounded by dialysing fluid
- wastes diffuse from blood into the fluid
- purified blood returns to the patient

Important difference:
Dialysis is similar to kidney filtration, but it does not perform selective reabsorption like a natural nephron.

## 5.5.2 Excretion in plants

Plants use different methods to remove or manage wastes.

Plant excretion methods:
- oxygen from photosynthesis can be treated as a waste product
- excess water is removed by transpiration
- many wastes are stored in cellular vacuoles
- wastes may be stored in leaves that fall off
- some wastes are stored as resins and gums, especially in old xylem
- some waste substances are excreted into surrounding soil

Do not write only "plants release waste through stomata." That is too narrow. The textbook gives several methods.

## Questions and answers

Question:
Describe the structure and functioning of nephrons.

Answer:
Each nephron has a cluster of capillaries called the glomerulus, a cup-shaped Bowman's capsule, a long tubule, and a collecting duct. Blood is filtered in the glomerulus, filtrate enters Bowman's capsule, useful substances are selectively reabsorbed from the tubule, and the remaining waste-rich fluid becomes urine.

Question:
What are the methods used by plants to get rid of excretory products?

Answer:
Plants get rid of wastes by releasing oxygen from photosynthesis, removing excess water by transpiration, storing wastes in vacuoles or leaves that fall off, storing resins and gums in old xylem, and excreting some wastes into the surrounding soil.

Question:
How is the amount of urine produced regulated?

Answer:
The amount of urine depends on how much excess water is present in the body and how much dissolved waste must be excreted. The kidney tubules selectively reabsorb water according to the body's need.

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Nephron

Question:
Describe the structure and functioning of nephrons.

Answer:
A nephron has a glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, tubule, and collecting duct. Blood is filtered in the glomerulus, filtrate enters Bowman's capsule, useful substances are selectively reabsorbed from the tubule, and remaining wastes form urine.

### Example 2: Urine regulation

Question:
How is the amount of urine produced regulated?

Answer:
It depends on the amount of excess water in the body and the amount of dissolved waste to be removed. The body reabsorbs more or less water according to its need.

### Example 3: Plant excretion

Question:
What methods do plants use to remove excretory products?

Answer:
Plants remove wastes by transpiration, storing wastes in vacuoles or falling leaves, storing resins and gums in old xylem, and excreting some substances into soil.

### Example 4: Dialysis

Question:
How does an artificial kidney help?

Answer:
It removes nitrogenous wastes from blood by diffusion through semi-permeable tubes into dialysing fluid.

## Common mistakes

1. Saying kidneys remove undigested food.
2. Forgetting selective reabsorption.
3. Writing that all filtrate becomes urine.
4. Saying dialysis is exactly the same as natural kidney function.
5. Giving only one plant excretion method.

## One-minute revision

Human excretion uses kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, and urethra. Nephrons filter blood in the glomerulus, collect filtrate in Bowman's capsule, reabsorb useful substances, and form urine. Dialysis can remove nitrogenous wastes when kidneys fail, but it is not identical to natural kidney function. Plants remove wastes through transpiration, storage in vacuoles or falling leaves, resins and gums, and release into soil.
