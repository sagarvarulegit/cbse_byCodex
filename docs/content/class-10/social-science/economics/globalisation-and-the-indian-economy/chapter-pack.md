# Globalisation and the Indian Economy

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- explain globalisation in economic terms
- describe MNCs and production across countries
- explain foreign trade and market integration
- identify factors enabling globalisation
- discuss liberalisation, WTO, and impacts in India

## 1) production across countries

Companies may spread production across countries to reduce cost, access markets, get skilled labour, or use local resources.

This links economies through investment, trade, technology, and services.

## 2) multinational corporations

An MNC owns or controls production in more than one country.

MNCs set up factories and offices where production is cheap and markets are large.

## 3) interlinking production

MNCs can invest directly, buy from local producers, place orders with small producers, or partner with local companies.

This can bring technology, jobs, and markets, but may also create pressure on small producers and workers.

## 4) foreign trade and markets

Foreign trade connects markets of different countries.

Imports give consumers more choices. Exports allow producers to reach wider markets.

Trade leads to integration of markets because prices, quality, and competition become linked across countries.

## 5) what is globalisation?

Globalisation is the process of rapid integration and interconnection between countries through trade, investment, technology, and movement of people and services.

## 6) factors enabling globalisation

Important factors include:

- improvement in transport
- information and communication technology
- liberalisation of trade and investment
- expansion of MNCs
- faster financial transactions

## 7) liberalisation and WTO

Trade barriers are restrictions on imports and exports, such as taxes on imports.

Liberalisation means reducing barriers and allowing easier foreign trade and investment.

The World Trade Organization aims to liberalise international trade, but developing countries often demand fairer rules.

## 8) impact in India

Benefits include more consumer choice, improved quality, new jobs in some sectors, foreign investment, and growth of some Indian companies.

Problems include pressure on small producers, job insecurity, unequal benefits, and tough competition from imports.

## 9) fair globalisation

Fair globalisation means benefits should reach workers, small producers, consumers, and weaker countries, not only powerful companies.

Government can protect labour rights, support small producers, negotiate fair trade rules, and invest in education and infrastructure.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- MNCs control production in more than one country.
- Foreign trade integrates markets.
- Globalisation links economies through trade, investment, and technology.
- Liberalisation reduces trade barriers.
- Globalisation has mixed effects; fair rules are needed.

## common mistakes students make

1. Treating globalisation as only imports.
2. Writing only benefits and ignoring small producers.
3. Confusing foreign trade with foreign investment.
4. Forgetting the role of technology.

## board tips

- Use MNC examples in globalisation answers.
- Explain both integration of production and integration of markets.
- In impact questions, write balanced points.
