# NCERT Exercises and Chapter Revision

## Exercise 3.1

This exercise contains seven graphical and consistency tasks. It asks you to:

1. form pairs of equations from two situations and solve graphically
2. compare $a_1/a_2$, $b_1/b_2$ and $c_1/c_2$
3. classify several pairs as intersecting, parallel or coincident
4. decide consistency and solve where possible
5. form the pencil-and-pen cost equations
6. form the rectangular-garden equations
7. draw two lines and shade the enclosed triangular region

For every graph, make a value table, plot at least two reliable points per line, label the intersection, and write its meaning.

## Exercise 3.2

### Substitution practice

The exercise includes six direct pairs, a parameter question, and five word problems. Use this answer pattern:

1. define the variables
2. write both equations
3. isolate the easiest variable
4. substitute and solve
5. check the real-life condition

Key textbook outcomes include:

- difference of two numbers $=26$ and one is three times the other: $39$ and $13$
- two supplementary angles differ by $18^\circ$: $99^\circ$ and $81^\circ$
- cricket bat and ball costs: bat Rs 500 and ball Rs 50
- fixed taxi charge: Rs 50; charge per kilometre: Rs 10
- fraction with denominator $9$ greater than numerator: $3/12=1/4$
- present ages in Aftab's family: Aftab $42$ years and daughter $12$ years

## Exercise 3.3

The elimination exercise includes four direct pairs and six situation-based problems. High-value checks are:

- adding $1$ to both numerator and denominator gives $1/2$, while adding $1$ only to the denominator gives $1/3$: fraction $=1/5$
- five years ago Nuri was three times Sonu; ten years later Nuri will be twice Sonu: Nuri $50$, Sonu $20$
- two-digit number has digit sum $9$ and is nine times the number obtained by reversing its digits: $81$
- bank withdrawal using Rs 50 and Rs 100 notes: $25$ notes in all and Rs 2000 gives $10$ fifty-rupee notes and $15$ hundred-rupee notes

## Common mistakes

- drawing a graph from only one point
- using a solution that makes a count or age negative
- forgetting that an identity represents infinitely many solutions
- reporting reversed digits without checking the original condition

## Board-answer checklist

- Variables defined with units
- Two correct equations
- Method shown, not only the result
- Final answer translated back to the situation

## One-minute revision

Translate, classify, solve, verify, interpret.
