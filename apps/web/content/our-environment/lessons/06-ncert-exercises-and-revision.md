# Chapter 13 NCERT Answer Lab

## 1–3 MCQs

1. Only biodegradable groups: **(a), (c), and (d)**; (b) contains plastic.
2. Food chain: **(b) grass -> goat -> human**.
3. Environment-friendly practices: **(d) all of the above**.

## 4. Kill one trophic level

Predators lose food, prey populations may rise, competition changes, and
energy flow/ecological balance is disrupted; linked food-web effects spread.

## 5. Does impact differ? Can a level be removed safely?

Yes, consequences vary with position and food-web links. No complete trophic
level can be removed without damage because levels are interdependent.

## 6. Biological magnification

Progressive increase in concentration of persistent non-biodegradable
substances at successive trophic levels. Yes; it is lowest near entry/producers
and highest in top consumers.

## 7. Non-biodegradable waste problems

It persists and accumulates, pollutes soil/water, may injure organisms or
release toxins, and persistent chemicals can biomagnify.

## 8. If all waste were biodegradable

There would still be impact if quantity exceeded decomposition capacity:
odour, pathogens, methane, oxygen depletion, and local pollution. Proper
segregation, composting, and controlled disposal remain necessary.

## 9. Ozone concern and steps

Stratospheric ozone absorbs harmful UV; depletion increases UV-related damage
such as skin cancer and ecosystem harm. CFC restrictions/phasing out,
CFC-free technology, international agreements, leak control, and safe recovery
limit damage.

## In-text questions

- Trophic level: each feeding step; label a valid chain from producer upward.
- Decomposers: clear dead matter and recycle nutrients.
- Biodegradability depends on decomposer enzymes and environmental conditions.
- Ozone: protective upper-atmosphere O3; ground-level/high concentration is
  harmful.
- Reduce waste through reduction, reuse, segregation, composting, and recycling.
