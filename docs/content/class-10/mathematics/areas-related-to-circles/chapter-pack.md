# Areas Related to Circles

This is an original teaching draft for Class 10 Mathematics, Chapter 11. The chapter is formula-based, but the real skill is choosing the correct part of the figure before calculating.

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- find arc length of a sector
- find the area of a sector
- understand a segment of a circle
- find area of a segment
- solve combination-of-figures questions involving circles

## 1) circle refresher

Important formulas:
- circumference = `2pi r`
- area = `pi r^2`

These are the base formulas used in the entire chapter.

## 2) sector and arc length

A sector is the part of a circle bounded by two radii and the corresponding arc.

If the central angle is `theta`, then arc length is:

`theta/360 x 2pi r`

### example
If `r = 14 cm` and `theta = 90 deg`:

Arc length = `90/360 x 2pi x 14 = 7pi cm`

## 3) area of a sector

Area of sector:

`theta/360 x pi r^2`

### example
If `r = 7 cm` and `theta = 60 deg`:

Area = `60/360 x pi x 49`

`= 49pi/6 cm^2`

## 4) segment of a circle

A segment is the region between a chord and its corresponding arc.

To find area of a segment:

`Area of segment = Area of sector - Area of triangle`

This is one of the most tested ideas in the chapter.

### important note
First identify whether the segment is minor or major.
Most textbook questions use the minor segment.

## 5) combined figures

Many questions involve figures made by joining:
- semicircles
- quadrants
- rectangles
- triangles

### method
1. split the figure into known parts
2. find area of each part
3. add or subtract as needed

### common example
Find the area of a region inside a sector but outside a triangle.

That means:
sector area minus triangle area

## 6) exam-friendly summary notes

### must-know facts
- Arc length uses `theta/360 x 2pi r`.
- Sector area uses `theta/360 x pi r^2`.
- Segment area is usually sector minus triangle.
- Combined figures need careful splitting.

### one-line revision points
- angle controls the part of the circle
- sector uses radii
- segment uses chord plus arc

## 7) common mistakes students make

1. Mixing circumference and area formulas.
2. Using degrees wrongly in the fraction part.
3. Forgetting to subtract the triangle for a segment.
4. Confusing sector and segment.
5. Using wrong radius from the figure.

## 8) board tips

- Write the formula before substitution.
- Mark the central angle clearly from the figure.
- In combined-figure questions, state what you are adding or subtracting.
- Keep units like `cm` and `cm^2` correct.

## 9) how to remember the chapter

### memory box 1
Sector is a slice with two radii.

### memory box 2
Segment is a curved cap made by a chord and an arc.

### memory box 3
Segment area usually means sector minus triangle.

## 10) solved practice set

### question 1
Find the area of a sector of radius `14 cm` and angle `180 deg`.

Answer:
`180/360 x pi x 14^2 = 98pi cm^2`

### question 2
What is the formula for arc length?

Answer:
`theta/360 x 2pi r`

### question 3
How do you find area of a segment?

Answer:
Area of sector minus area of triangle.
