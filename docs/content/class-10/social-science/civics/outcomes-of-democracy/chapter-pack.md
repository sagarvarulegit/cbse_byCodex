# Outcomes of Democracy

## chapter goal

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:
- assess democracy through expected outcomes
- explain accountable, responsive, and legitimate government
- analyse democracy and economic growth
- discuss inequality, social diversity, dignity, and freedom
- understand why democracy is preferred despite limitations

## 1) assessing democracy

Democracy should be assessed by realistic expectations.

It is not a magic solution for all problems, but it creates conditions for participation, accountability, rights, and correction of mistakes.

## 2) accountable, responsive, legitimate

Democratic governments are accountable because citizens can question, criticise, and replace them through elections.

They are responsive when they consider people's needs and demands.

They are legitimate because they are based on consent of citizens.

Democracy may be slower because it requires discussion, consultation, and procedures.

## 3) economic growth

Democracy does not guarantee faster economic growth than dictatorship.

Economic growth depends on many factors: population, global conditions, resources, policies, technology, education, and investment.

However, democracy allows debate about development priorities and public welfare.

## 4) inequality and poverty

Democracies are expected to reduce inequality, but inequalities can continue.

Political equality through one person, one vote does not automatically remove economic inequality.

Citizens must use democratic rights to demand fair policies.

## 5) social diversity

Democracy can accommodate social diversity by allowing negotiation, representation, and peaceful resolution.

This works when majority rule does not become majority domination and minority rights are respected.

## 6) dignity and freedom

Democracy promotes dignity of citizens by recognising equal political status.

It supports the dignity of women, disadvantaged groups, and individuals by allowing rights, representation, and public debate.

Freedom of expression helps people raise injustice and demand reform.

## 7) strengths and limits

Democracy's strength is that it can correct its own mistakes through public discussion, elections, courts, movements, and media.

Its limits include delays, corruption, inequality, and unmet expectations, but it remains preferable because citizens are not passive subjects.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- Democracy is accountable, responsive, and legitimate.
- It may be slow because it follows procedures.
- Democracy does not automatically guarantee economic equality.
- It can accommodate diversity if minority rights are respected.
- Democracy promotes dignity and freedom.

## common mistakes students make

1. Writing democracy solves every problem automatically.
2. Ignoring accountability and legitimacy.
3. Saying democracy always gives fastest growth.
4. Forgetting dignity as an outcome.

## board tips

- For outcomes, use headings: political, economic, social, dignity.
- In economic answers, write balanced points.
- Mention correction of mistakes as a key democratic strength.
