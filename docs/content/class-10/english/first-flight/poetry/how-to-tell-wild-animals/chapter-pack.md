# How to Tell Wild Animals

## chapter goal

Revise the poem's comic method for identifying wild animals through exaggerated danger.

## teacher note on textbook alignment

This pack supports the poem paired with "Two Stories about Flying" in `jeff103.pdf`.

## chapter flow for the platform

1. Comic situation.
2. Animal descriptions.
3. Exaggeration and irony.
4. Rhyme and humour.
5. Exam answer points.

## 1) comic situation

The poem pretends to give practical advice for identifying wild animals.

The advice is funny because it often becomes dangerous for the person trying to identify them.

## 2) animal descriptions

Each animal is described through a striking feature or action.

The descriptions are playful, not serious zoology.

## 3) humour

The humour comes from exaggeration, surprise, rhyme, and impossible advice.

The speaker sounds calm while describing risky situations.

## exam-friendly summary notes

- The poem is humorous and exaggerated.
- It gives mock instructions.
- Animal features are described playfully.
- Irony makes the advice funny.

## common mistakes students make

1. Treating the poem like a science chapter.
2. Missing the irony in the instructions.
3. Forgetting the role of rhyme and exaggeration.

## board tips

- Use the words humour, exaggeration, and irony.
- Give one animal feature as evidence.
- Keep tone answers light but clear.

## short answer practice

1. Why is the poem funny?
2. How does exaggeration work in the poem?
3. Why are the instructions not practical?

## mini quiz with answers

1. The poem uses mock advice.
2. Exaggeration creates humour.
3. The speaker's calm tone adds irony.
4. The animal descriptions are playful.
5. It is not a serious field guide.
